## What it is
**Description:** Slide a marker along a strip of the whole day, watch the hour hand go round twice, write a.m. or p.m., and hop hours across noon and midnight.
**Why:** Tell the two halves of a day apart, write a.m. or p.m. after a time, and count hours forward or back across noon and midnight.
**Look:** A strip from one midnight to the next with a sun on the noon post, a clock whose hour hand turns with a marker, and a tray of day cards.
**Context:** Grade 4, and the first place in the chapter where one clock time can mean two times of day. An adult sees a.m. and p.m. written after times, which is quickly learnt as a pair of labels. What is actually being settled is what they label: the half of the day before noon and the half after it. That decides the answer when a count of hours passes 12 noon or 12 midnight, where the label changes. A child who carries the old label writes a time twelve hours out. The printed exercise counts hours across noon both ways and forward from just after midnight.
**For the child:** Slide the marker along the day. Watch the hour hand go round twice. Drop each card in its half. Hop one hour at a time. Read the label at every landing.
**Teaching notes:** Lay a paper strip across the board marked midnight, noon and midnight again, and have the class place cards for the things they do in a day before the tool is opened. The page asks whether a clock showing nine means morning or night, and answers it with a.m. and p.m. in the next lines. Keep the answer tied to the halves of the strip and not to the size of the number, because one in the afternoon is p.m. and eleven in the morning is a.m. The twelve o'clock times need saying. 12 noon is the post between the halves, 12:05 p.m. is just after noon, and 12:05 a.m. is just after midnight, at the start of a new day. The exercise counts hours across noon both ways and forward from just after midnight, so hop an hour at a time and read the label at every landing. The hop that crosses a post is where the label changes.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Twice round in a day
**Screen:** the clock above the day strip, with the marker on the left-hand midnight post and the lap counter at nought
**Does:** slides the marker along the whole strip, counts the laps of the hour hand, then finds the two places on the strip where the clock shows the same time
**Win:** the lap counter reaches two, and two places light on the strip where the clock shows the same time, one in each half
**Feels:** the whole strip is two laps of the same face, one in each half of the day
**Goal:** a day has twenty-four hours and a clock shows twelve, so the hour hand goes round twice a day
**Observe:** The lap counter reached two as the marker crossed from one midnight to the next, and two places lit on the strip where the clock looked the same.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- says the hour hand goes round once a day, so a clock showing seven can mean only one time. → the marker slides from the left-hand midnight post, and at the noon post the lap counter reads one lap done with half the strip still ahead. The marker goes on to the right-hand post and the counter reaches two. The hour hand is then set at seven, and two places light on the strip, one in each half. The child's answer stays in the slot, nothing marked.
- says the hour hand goes round twenty-four times a day, once for each hour. → the marker crosses one hour block and the hour hand moves on by one number, a twelfth of a lap and not a whole one. The marker is run on block by block, and the lap counter reaches its first lap only when the hand has passed all twelve numbers. The child's twenty-four stays in the slot beside the counter.
**Misconception to break:** Thinking the hour hand goes round once a day, so seven o'clock can mean only one time.

### 2 · Before noon and after noon
**Screen:** the day strip with its a.m. and p.m. halves, and a tray of day cards whose times carry no label
**Does:** drops each card into the half of the day where it happens, then writes a.m. or p.m. after its time
**Win:** every card settles in its half, and a.m. or p.m. is written after its time
**Feels:** the half of the day decides the label, and the number on the clock does not
**Goal:** times after midnight and before noon are a.m., and times after noon and before midnight are p.m.
**Observe:** Each card settled in the half of the strip where its activity happens, and its label matched the colour under it.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes 1:15 a.m. for lunch, because one is a small number and small numbers seem to come in the morning. → the card settles where the child put it, an hour and a quarter after the left-hand midnight post, near the moon in the sky blue half. The same time after the noon post lights beside it in the amber half, and the card's word, lunch, is read aloud. The child's label stays on the card, nothing marked.
- writes 11:40 p.m. for a snack in the late morning, because eleven is a big number and big numbers seem to come late. → the card settles where the child put it, twenty minutes before the right-hand midnight post, near the moon in the amber half. The same time twenty minutes before the noon post lights beside it in the sky blue half, under the sun, and the card's words are read aloud. The child's label stays on the card.
**Misconception to break:** Writing 1:15 a.m. for lunch because one is a small number, as if small numbers meant morning.

### 3 · Counting past noon
**Screen:** the marker at a morning time, the hopper set to a number of hours, and the noon post lit
**Does:** hops the marker forward one hour at a time, reading the label at each landing, then hops back from an afternoon time
**Win:** the label changes from a.m. to p.m. on the hop that crosses the noon post, and the end time is written
**Feels:** the label holds steady hop after hop in the morning, and turns over on the hop that crosses the sun
**Goal:** a count of hours that passes 12 noon changes a.m. to p.m., and a count back past noon changes p.m. to a.m.
**Observe:** The label on the tag turned from a.m. to p.m. on the hop that crossed the noon post, and on none of the hops before it.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- keeps the a.m. label after the count passes noon, and writes 2:25 a.m. for six hours after 8:25 a.m. → the hopper runs the six hops again one at a time, and the tag reads each landing aloud: 9:25 a.m., 10:25 a.m. and 11:25 a.m., then the fourth hop arcs over the noon post, which lights, and lands on 12:25 p.m. in the amber half. Two more hops land on 1:25 p.m. and 2:25 p.m. A pale pin shows the child's 2:25 a.m. near the moon in the sky blue half, and the child's answer stays in the slot.
- writes 12:25 a.m. for the landing one hour after 11:25 a.m., waiting for one o'clock before changing the label. → the hop from 11:25 a.m. arcs over the noon post, which lights, and lands twenty-five minutes into the amber half, where the tag reads 12:25 p.m. A pale pin shows the child's 12:25 a.m. twenty-five minutes after the left-hand midnight post, at night, and the child's answer stays in the slot.
- keeps p.m. when counting four hours back from 2:35 p.m., and writes 10:35 p.m. → the hopper turns and hops back one hour at a time, to 1:35 p.m. and 12:35 p.m., then over the noon post, which lights, to 11:35 a.m. in the sky blue half, and one more to 10:35 a.m. A pale pin shows the child's 10:35 p.m. late in the amber half, and the child's answer stays in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Keeping the a.m. label after the count passes noon, and writing 2:25 a.m.

### 4 · Just after midnight
**Screen:** the marker near the right-hand end of the strip late at night, with the next day's strip waiting
**Does:** hops the marker past midnight into the next day, reads the new label, then hops back across midnight
**Win:** the next day's strip slides in, the day counter goes up by one, and the label reads twelve and some minutes a.m.
**Feels:** one hop over the midnight post turns the night into a new day and the label back to a.m.
**Goal:** the a.m. hours start at midnight, so twelve and a few minutes a.m. is just after midnight, at the start of a new day
**Observe:** One hop past the right-hand midnight post brought in the next day's strip and a tag reading twelve and some minutes a.m.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- keeps p.m. after passing midnight, and writes 1:35 p.m. for two hours after 11:35 p.m. → the hopper runs the two hops again. The first arcs over the right-hand midnight post, which lights, the next day's strip slides in, the day counter goes up by one, and the tag reads 12:35 a.m. The second lands on 1:35 a.m. near the moon. A pale pin shows the child's 1:35 p.m. just past the sun on the new day's strip, and the child's answer stays in the slot.
- reads 12:25 a.m. as twenty-five minutes after noon, because twelve o'clock sounds like the middle of the day. → the marker is set at 12:25 a.m. and stands twenty-five minutes after the left-hand midnight post, near the moon, in the sky blue half. A pale pin stands twenty-five minutes after the noon post, where the tag would read 12:25 p.m., and both stay lit side by side with the child's answer in the slot.
- counts one hour back from 12:40 a.m. and writes 11:40 a.m., keeping the a.m. label. → the hopper turns and hops back over the left-hand midnight post, which lights, the strip of the day before slides in from the left, and the tag reads 11:40 p.m. in the amber half, near the moon. A pale pin shows the child's 11:40 a.m. just before the sun, and the child's answer stays in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Keeping the p.m. label after passing midnight, and writing 1:35 p.m.

## Delight
The first phase, when the marker reaches the noon post. The hour hand has come back to the twelve with one lap done, and a thread is drawn from each morning hour block to the afternoon block the hour hand points the same way for, twelve threads arching over the sun. As the marker goes on into the afternoon each thread lights in turn when the marker reaches its far end, and the clock face above looks exactly as it did twelve hours before. It can be run as often as a finger likes, nothing is marked, and the voice says nothing at all while it happens.

**General miss line:** Hop one hour at a time, and read the label at every landing.

## Answer key
A day has twenty-four hours and a clock shows twelve, so the hour hand goes round twice a day. Times after midnight and before noon are a.m., short for ante meridiem, and times after noon and before midnight are p.m., short for post meridiem. Twelve o'clock in the middle of the day is 12 noon, and twelve o'clock at night is 12 midnight. A count of hours that passes 12 noon changes a.m. to p.m., and a count back past noon changes p.m. to a.m. A count that passes 12 midnight changes p.m. to a.m. and moves into the next day. A time such as 12:05 a.m. is five minutes after midnight, and 12:05 p.m. is five minutes after noon.

## Student controls
- **Day strip**: runs from one midnight to the next, the a.m. half before the noon post and the p.m. half after it
- **Marker**: slides along the strip carrying its time with a.m. or p.m.
- **Geared clock**: turns its hour hand with the marker, twice round for the whole strip
- **Lap counter**: says whether the hour hand is on its first or its second lap of the day
- **Hopper**: moves the marker one whole hour forward or back, drawing each hop over the strip
- **Next day strip**: slides in past the right-hand midnight post and adds one to the day counter
- **Day cards**: each an activity with a time, dropped into the half of the day where it happens
