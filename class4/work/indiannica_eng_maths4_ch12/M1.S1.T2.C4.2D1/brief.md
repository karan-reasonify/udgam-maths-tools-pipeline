## What it is
**Description:** Read and set an analog clock to the exact minute. Count in fives to the last number the minute hand has passed, then the marks, and read the hour the hour hand has passed.
**Why:** Read an analog clock to the exact minute, and set its two hands for any time, knowing where each hand has to point.
**Look:** A large clock face with sixty marks and two hands, a digital readout beside it, and a ring of fives round the face that can be shown or hidden.
**Context:** Grade 4, the first working topic of the time chapter. An adult sees a clock being read, which a paper plate clock does well. What is actually being settled is where the minutes are counted from and which hour is read. The chapter's own clocks are drawn up to a mark away from the times the text gives them, and the book's key reads one exercise face a minute off. So this tool draws every hand exactly, and the plan accepts a reading of the printed faces within one minute. The hour hand is the other trap: late in an hour it is almost on the next number, and the hour is still the one it has passed.
**For the child:** Count in fives to the last number the minute hand has passed. Then count the marks after it. Read the hour the hour hand has passed. Drag the hands to show a time.
**Teaching notes:** Make paper plate clocks before the tool is opened, with sixty marks and two card hands of different lengths, and set times on them in pairs. The section is called time to the exact minute, but its clocks are not drawn to the exact minute. Its worked clock says forty-eight minutes with the hand drawn nearer the forty-seventh mark, and each exercise face sits up to a mark from the minute the key gives it. The book's key reads the first exercise face as 5:08, where the drawn hand is nearer 5:09. It also draws the hour hand of 9:06 just before the nine instead of just past it. So accept any reading of the printed faces within one minute, and use this tool's clocks, which are drawn exactly, to settle where a hand belongs. Two moves need saying aloud every time. Count the minutes in fives to the last number the minute hand has passed, and never read that number as that many minutes. And read the hour the hour hand has passed: late in an hour it is almost on the next number, and the hour is still the one before. The recap's words name the next hour only when they say to, as in a quarter to four, which is 3:45.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Digital and analog
**Screen:** the analog clock and the digital readout side by side, the readout showing a time and the clock's hands waiting at twelve
**Does:** reads the figures, drags the hands until the clock with hands shows the same time, then uncovers the words card
**Win:** the two clocks agree, and the time is written under both in figures and in words
**Feels:** two clocks tell the same moment, one in figures and one by where its hands point
**Goal:** a digital clock shows the time in figures and an analog clock shows it with hands, and both can show the same time to the exact minute
**Observe:** The clock with hands settled on the same time as the figures, and the words card read it aloud.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- points the minute hand at the five for 9:05, taking the five in the figures as the number the hand should point at. → the words card reads the child's hands back, twenty-five past nine, under the readout's five past nine. The fives ring shows its numbers, five beside the one and twenty-five beside the five, and a pale ghost of the minute hand settles on the one. The child's hand stays on the five beside it, nothing marked.
- draws the hour hand for 9:05 exactly on the nine, or just short of it, as if nine o'clock had not quite come. → the pale ghost of the hour hand settles two and a half degrees past the nine, and the hour finder rings the nine as passed and outlines the ten. The minute hand is swept back to the twelve and on again to the one, and the ghost is seen to leave the nine as it goes. The child's hour hand stays where it was set, beside the ghost.
**Misconception to break:** Pointing the minute hand at the five for five minutes, or drawing the hour hand just before the nine, as if nine o'clock had not come.

### 2 · Each small mark is a minute
**Screen:** the magnifier over the space between the one and the two, with the minute hand resting on the one
**Does:** counts the spaces between the two numbers, then steps the minute hand on one mark at a time while a count of minutes rises
**Win:** five spaces light between each pair of numbers, and sixty light round the whole face as one hour
**Feels:** the space between two numbers is five small steps, and every step takes one minute
**Goal:** the space between two numbers has five equal parts, and the minute hand takes one minute to move from one mark to the next
**Observe:** Five spaces lit between the one and the two, and sixty lit round the whole face as the minute hand went once round.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- counts the four small lines between two numbers as four minutes, and says the hand takes twelve minutes from the four to the seven. → the magnifier spreads the space between the four and the five, and its five spaces light and are numbered one to five, the last ending on the long mark at the five. The minute hand then steps from the four to the seven one mark at a time while the count of minutes climbs by one at each mark, to fifteen, and the child's twelve stays in the slot beside it.
- says three minutes from the four to the seven, because the hand passed three numbers. → the magnifier opens on each stretch in turn, four to five, five to six and six to seven, and each lights five spaces. The three stretches stand side by side as three blocks of five, fifteen spaces in all, and the child's three stays in the slot beside the count.
**Misconception to break:** Counting the four small lines between two numbers as four minutes, or saying three minutes because three numbers were passed.

### 3 · Counting on from the last number
**Screen:** the minute hand set a few marks past a number, with the fives ring hidden
**Does:** counts in fives round to the last number the hand has passed, counts the marks after it, then sets the minute hand for a written time
**Win:** the fives light up to that number, the marks after it light one by one, and the minutes are written
**Feels:** the numbers on the face stand for fives, and only the marks after the last one count in ones
**Goal:** the minutes are counted in fives to the last number the minute hand has passed, and then mark by mark
**Observe:** The arc lit in fives up to the six, thirty, and then one mark at a time to thirty-four.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- reads the six as six minutes, adds the four marks after it, and answers ten minutes past the hour. → the fives ring shows its numbers, and thirty stands beside the six. The counting arc runs from the twelve in blocks of five, five, ten and on to thirty at the six, then lights the four marks after it one at a time to thirty-four. The child's ten stays in the slot beside the arc's count.
- counts in fives up to the seven, which the hand is heading for, adds the four marks, and answers thirty-nine. → the counting arc stops its fives at the six, the last number the hand has passed, and the fives ring lights thirty there. The stretch from the six to the seven is lit mark by mark, and it stops at the fourth mark, one short of the seven. The child's thirty-nine stays in the slot beside it.
- writes 3:09 for a clock whose minute hand is on the nine and whose hour hand is three quarters of the way from the three to the four. → the fives ring lights forty-five beside the nine, and the counting arc runs round in nine blocks of five to meet the hand. The words card reads a quarter to four, the readout's cover slides back on 3:45, and the child's 3:09 stays in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Reading the six as six minutes and answering ten, instead of counting the six as thirty minutes.

### 4 · The hour the hand has passed
**Screen:** a clock late in an hour, with the hour hand almost on the next number
**Does:** reads the minutes, taps the number the hour hand has passed, then drags both hands to draw a written time
**Win:** the number behind the hour hand lights, the time is written with that hour, and the words card reads minutes to the next hour
**Feels:** late in an hour the hour hand is nearly at the next number, and it still has not arrived
**Goal:** the hour is the number the hour hand has passed, not the number it is moving towards
**Observe:** At fifty-eight minutes past three the hour hand stood one degree short of the four, and the hour finder rang the three.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- reads the hour as four because the hour hand is almost on the four, and writes 4:58. → the hour finder rings the three as the number passed and outlines the four, and the magnifier spreads the space between the three and the four, where the hour hand's tip is seen one degree short of the four. The minute hand is run on two marks to the twelve, and only then does the hour hand touch the four. The child's 4:58 stays in the slot, nothing marked.
- writes 6:45 for a quarter to six, taking the hour named in the words as the hour the hand has passed. → the clock is set to a quarter to six, the minute hand on the nine and the hour hand three quarters of the way from the five to the six. The hour finder rings the five and outlines the six, and the words card reads fifteen minutes to six. The child's 6:45 is set as a pale second pair of hands, whose hour hand sits three quarters of the way past the six, and both stay side by side.
- draws the hour hand for 8:50 right on the eight, where it stands at eight o'clock. → the pale ghost of the hour hand settles five degrees short of the nine, five sixths of the way from the eight. The minute hand is swept back from the ten to the twelve and the ghost slides back to the eight with it, so the fifty minutes are seen to have carried the hour hand along. The child's hand stays on the eight beside the ghost.
**Misconception to break:** Reading the hour as four because the hour hand is almost on the four, and writing 4:58.

## Delight
The fourth phase, when the minute hand is dragged once all the way round. The hour hand creeps after it the whole time, so slowly that it seems to stand still, and the hour finder's filled ring stays on the number behind it through fifty-nine minutes. The moment the minute hand reaches the twelve, the hour hand touches the next number and the filled ring hops onto it, not a moment before. Dragging back sends both hands back together. It can be tried as often as a finger likes, nothing is marked, and the voice says nothing at all while it happens.

**General miss line:** Count the fives again, up to the last number the minute hand has passed.

## Answer key
A digital clock shows the time in figures, and an analog clock shows it with hands. On an analog clock the space between two numbers is split into five equal parts by four small lines. The minute hand takes one minute to move from one mark to the next, so the sixty marks round the face make one hour. To read the minutes, count in fives to the last number the minute hand has passed, then count the marks after it. The hour is the number the hour hand has passed, not the one it is moving towards; exactly on the hour, the hour hand is on the number. As the minutes pass, the hour hand moves from one number towards the next. It is a little way past the number early in the hour, and nearly at the next number late in the hour. A quarter past is fifteen minutes past the hour and half past is thirty. A quarter to is fifteen minutes before the next hour, which is forty-five minutes past the hour before it.

## Student controls
- **Minute hand**: drags round the face and snaps to a minute mark
- **Hour hand**: drags between the numbers, with a ghost showing where it belongs for the minutes set
- **Digital readout**: shows the same time in figures, and can be covered
- **Fives ring**: numbers the long marks five, ten, fifteen and on to sixty, shown or hidden
- **Counting arc**: lights the fives to the last number passed, then each mark after it
- **Magnifier**: spreads the space between two numbers apart so its five spaces can be counted
- **Hour finder**: lights the number the hour hand has passed and outlines the one it is moving towards
- **Words card**: reads the time as minutes past the hour or minutes to the next hour
