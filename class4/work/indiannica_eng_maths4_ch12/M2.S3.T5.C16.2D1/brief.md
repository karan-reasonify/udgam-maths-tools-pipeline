## What it is
**Description:** Slide a marker along the day's timeline and read each time two ways: as a 12-hour time, and as four digits counted from midnight.
**Why:** Write a time as four digits counted from midnight, with the twelve o'clock hours right, and change a 24-hour time back to a.m. or p.m.
**Look:** The page's timeline drawn large, nought to twenty-four hours above the 12-hour times, a marker with two readouts, and a departure board beside it.
**Context:** Grade 4, and where the chapter drops a.m. and p.m. for four digits. An adult sees a rule, add twelve in the afternoon, which works for most of the day. What is actually being settled is what the four digits count: the hours and minutes since midnight. Read that way, the two twelve o'clock hours stop being exceptions. 12 noon is twelve hours after midnight, 1200 hours, and a time just after midnight has had no whole hour yet, so it starts with 00. The page's notes add twelve only to times from one o'clock in the afternoon on, and show no time in the hour after noon or after midnight. Its exercise tests noon and midnight, so the timeline is what teaches them.
**For the child:** Slide the marker to the time. Read the four digits: hours, then minutes. After one in the afternoon, add twelve. Noon is 1200 hours. Just after midnight starts with 00.
**Teaching notes:** Read a real timetable in 24-hour time with the class before the tool is opened. The page gives the rule only by example, twelve and one make thirteen and twelve and six make eighteen. It adds twelve only to times from one o'clock in the afternoon on. Its exercise then asks for 12 noon and 12 midnight, and other exercises in the chapter use 12:05 a.m. and 12:15 a.m. A child following the examples adds twelve to noon and writes 2400 hours, a name the page keeps for midnight at the end of a day. So teach what the four digits count, the hours and minutes since midnight, and read the twelve o'clock hours off the timeline. Noon is 1200 hours, and a time just after midnight starts with 00. The page says midnight is 0000 hours and that it can also be written 2400 hours, which names the end of a day. Say that 0000 hours starts a day, and accept either for the exercise that asks for midnight. The page's railway note writes the same times with a colon, 13:45, and this plan writes four digits and the word hours. The box about a slow clock asks for its time in both forms, and the book's key does not answer it: the clock shows 3:00 p.m., which is 1500 hours.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Four digits, no a.m. or p.m.
**Screen:** the timeline with the marker at a morning time and both readouts showing
**Does:** slides the marker through the morning, reading each time in four digits and filling a nought before a one-digit hour
**Win:** each morning time is written in four digits with the word hours, and none of them carries a.m.
**Feels:** the four digits count the whole day from midnight, so a morning time and an evening time never share a number
**Goal:** the 24-hour clock counts from midnight and writes four digits, two for the hours and two for the minutes, with a nought filling a one-digit hour
**Observe:** Each morning time came out in four digits, with a nought in front of a one-digit hour, and none of them carried a.m.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes 705 hours for 7:05 a.m., with three digits. → the four-digit readout under the marker shows all four places drawn, 0705, and its first place, the nought, lights in vermilion as part of the hours. The child's 705 is laid under it place by place from the left, so its hours would read seventy and its last place stands empty. The child's answer stays in the slot, nothing marked.
- writes 0705 a.m., keeping the label on a four-digit time. → the marker is slid on to seven in the evening, and the four-digit readout changes to 1905 hours while the 12-hour readout reads 7:05 p.m. It is slid back, and the readout returns to 0705 hours. Each set of four digits names one moment of the day, so the label has nothing left to say, and the child's answer stays in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Writing 705 hours with only three digits, or dropping the nought in the minutes and writing 075.

### 2 · Afternoon hours gain twelve
**Screen:** the marker just past one o'clock in the afternoon, with the add twelve gate open
**Does:** moves the marker through the afternoon and evening, adding twelve to each hour and writing the four digits
**Win:** the hour digits jump by twelve as the marker passes into each hour, and the four digits settle
**Feels:** the hours keep counting past twelve, so an afternoon hour is twelve more than its 12-hour name
**Goal:** from one o'clock in the afternoon to just before midnight, twelve is added to the hour
**Observe:** From one o'clock in the afternoon on, the hour digits read twelve more than the 12-hour hour.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes 0925 hours for 9:25 p.m., as if afternoon and evening hours kept their numbers the way morning hours do. → the marker is slid to 9:25 p.m., far along the amber part of the bar, and the four-digit readout reads 2125 hours with the add twelve tag riding beside it. A pale pin shows the child's 0925 in the sky blue part, at 9:25 a.m., and both stay on the bar with the child's answer in the slot.
- adds twelve to the minutes instead of the hour, and writes 0937 hours for 9:25 p.m. → the marker crosses the gate, and the minute digits under it stay at 25 while the hour digits jump by twelve, from nine to twenty-one. A pale pin shows where 0937 hours stands, in the morning, and the child's answer stays in the slot beside the readout's 2125.
- gives the true time, 1320 hours, when the question asks what a clock that gains three minutes an hour shows four hours after it is set right at 9:20 a.m. → the clock bay runs both clocks from 9:20 a.m. for four hours at walking pace, and each hour the gaining clock's minute hand runs three marks further ahead, three, six, nine and twelve minutes in all. The true clock stops at 1:20 p.m. and the gaining clock at 1:32 p.m., and the marker slides to the gaining clock's time, reading 1332 hours. The child's 1320 stays in the slot beside it.
**Misconception to break:** Writing 0925 hours, as if p.m. times kept their hour the way a.m. times do.

### 3 · The twelve o'clock hours
**Screen:** the marker on the noon post with the add twelve gate shut, and the left-hand midnight post lit
**Does:** sets the marker on noon and just after it, then just after midnight, and tries the gate each time
**Win:** noon reads 1200 hours with the gate shut, and the time just after midnight begins with two noughts
**Feels:** the digits count from the midnight post, so noon is twelve hours along and just after midnight no hour has passed
**Goal:** 12 noon is 1200 hours with nothing added, and a time just after midnight starts with 00
**Observe:** On the noon post the digits read 1200 with the gate shut, and just after the left-hand post they began with 00.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- adds twelve to 12 noon, as for the afternoon hours, and writes 2400 hours, which names midnight at the end of a day. → the marker stands on the noon post with the gate shut, and the four-digit readout reads 1200 hours: twelve hours have passed since the left-hand midnight post, and the twelve on the bar above the post says so. A pale pin shows where the child's number belongs, on the right-hand post, whose card names 2400 hours as midnight at the end of a day. The child's answer stays in the slot.
- adds twelve to 12:35 p.m. and writes 2435 hours. → the marker stands thirty-five minutes past the noon post, short of the gate, which stays shut, and the readout reads 1235 hours. The bar ends at twenty-four, at the right-hand midnight post, and no place on it reads 2435; the child's number is written small past the end of the bar, and it stays in the slot.
- writes 1235 hours for 12:35 a.m., keeping the twelve of its 12-hour name. → the marker is slid to 12:35 a.m., thirty-five minutes after the left-hand midnight post, and the readout reads 0035 hours: no whole hour has passed since midnight, so the hour places hold two noughts. A pale pin shows the child's 1235 hours thirty-five minutes past the noon post, and the child's answer stays in the slot.
- writes 2435 hours for 12:35 a.m., counting on from 2400 hours, the name for midnight at the end of a day. → the marker stands thirty-five minutes after the left-hand midnight post, and the readout reads 0035 hours. The card by the right-hand post is lit, naming 2400 hours as midnight at the end of a day, and the post itself reads 0000 hours, where the new day starts again. The child's answer stays in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Adding twelve to the twelve and writing 2435 hours for the afternoon time, or writing 1235 hours for the time just after midnight.

### 4 · Back to the 12-hour clock
**Screen:** the departure board of 24-hour times, with the take twelve control beside it
**Does:** reads each board time, takes twelve from an hour of thirteen to twenty-three, and writes the 12-hour time with a.m. or p.m.
**Win:** each board time gets its 12-hour twin with a.m. or p.m., and the marker lands on both at once
**Feels:** the take twelve control lights from one o'clock in the afternoon on, the same place the gate opened
**Goal:** an hour of 13 to 23 has twelve taken away and is p.m., minutes past 00 are twelve a.m., and minutes past 12 stay twelve p.m.
**Observe:** Each board time found its 12-hour twin: an hour from thirteen on lost twelve and took p.m., an hour of 12 kept its twelve, and 0026 hours became 12:26 a.m.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- drops the first digit of 16 and writes 6:50 p.m. for 1650 hours, instead of taking twelve away. → the take twelve control lights under the board row, and sixteen less twelve is written in vermilion: four. The marker slides to 1650 hours on the bar and its 12-hour readout reads 4:50 p.m., and a pale pin shows the child's 6:50 p.m. two hours further along. The child's answer stays in the slot.
- takes twelve from 1219 hours and writes 0:19 p.m. → the take twelve control stays unlit for an hour of twelve, and the marker slides to nineteen minutes past the noon post, where the 12-hour readout reads 12:19 p.m. No hour on the 12-hour row under the bar is called nought, and the child's answer stays in the slot.
- writes 0:26 a.m. for 0026 hours, copying the two noughts as the hour. → the marker slides to twenty-six minutes after the left-hand midnight post, and the 12-hour readout reads 12:26 a.m. The 12-hour row under the bar starts at 12 midnight and goes on to 1 a.m., so the hour just after midnight is called twelve. The child's answer stays in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Dropping the first digit of 16 and writing 6:50 p.m., instead of taking twelve away.

## Delight
The second phase, when the marker is swept slowly from the late morning into the afternoon. The four-digit readout climbs in even steps, 1158, 1159, 1200, and on through 1259 to 1300 without a jump, while the 12-hour readout climbs to 12:59 p.m. and then drops back to 1:00 p.m. At that moment the gate swings open and its add twelve tag slides under the marker, carrying exactly the twelve hours the 12-hour readout set aside when it began again from one. It can be swept back and forth as often as a finger likes, nothing is marked, and the voice says nothing at all while it happens.

**General miss line:** Slide the marker back to the time, and read both readouts together.

## Answer key
The 24-hour clock counts the hours and minutes since midnight and writes them as four digits and the word hours, with no a.m. or p.m. The first two digits are the hours and the last two the minutes. From 1 a.m. to just before noon the hour keeps its number, written with two digits, so 7:05 a.m. is 0705 hours. From 1 p.m. to just before midnight, twelve is added to the hour, so 9:25 p.m. is 2125 hours. 12 noon is 1200 hours, and a time from noon to just before one keeps its twelve: 12:35 p.m. is 1235 hours. A time from midnight to just before one starts with 00: 12:35 a.m. is 0035 hours. Midnight is 0000 hours at the start of a day, and 2400 hours names the same midnight at the end of a day. To change back, 0000 hours is midnight and 1200 hours is 12 noon. A time with an hour of 00 and minutes past it is twelve and those minutes a.m., and 01 to 11 keep their hour and are a.m. A time with an hour of 12 and minutes past it stays twelve and is p.m., and for hours 13 to 23, take twelve away and write p.m.

## Student controls
- **Timeline**: runs from nought to twenty-four hours, with the 12-hour times written beneath it
- **Marker**: slides along the timeline carrying both readouts
- **Twelve-hour readout**: shows the time with a.m. or p.m.
- **Four-digit readout**: shows the hours and the minutes since midnight, two digits each, and the word hours
- **Add twelve gate**: adds twelve to the hour from one o'clock in the afternoon, and stays shut in the hour after noon
- **Take twelve control**: turns an hour of thirteen to twenty-three back into a p.m. hour
- **Departure board**: lists times to change from one clock to the other
- **Clock bay**: runs a true clock beside one that gains or loses minutes every hour
