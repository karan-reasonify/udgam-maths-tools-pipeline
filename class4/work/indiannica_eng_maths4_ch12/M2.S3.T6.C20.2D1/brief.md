## What it is
**Description:** Add and take away times in columns of hours, minutes and seconds, carrying at sixty or more and breaking an hour into sixty minutes, even across two empty columns.
**Why:** Add and take away times written in hours, minutes and seconds, carrying at sixty and breaking an hour into sixty minutes.
**Look:** A column frame headed hours, minutes and seconds, with a sixty gate between each pair of columns and a check control under the answer.
**Context:** Grade 4, and the chapter's written arithmetic of time. An adult sees column addition and subtraction, and a child who is good at them is the one most likely to go wrong, because these columns do not carry at ten. Sixty minutes make an hour and sixty seconds a minute, so a column carries when it reaches sixty, and a broken hour brings sixty minutes, not a hundred. The printed summary box converts minutes only once they go past sixty and says nothing of exactly sixty; exactly sixty carries too, so this tool's gate opens at sixty or more. The hardest printed step breaks an hour across two empty columns at once, and the page shows it only in a grid.
**For the child:** Put each part under its heading. Add from the right. Sixty or more carries one to the left. If the top is too small, break one from the left. Check by adding back.
**Teaching notes:** Log real reading or practice times on a sheet with hours and minutes columns, and add three days of them by hand before the tool is opened. The page says time is added and taken away the same way as whole numbers. That is true of the columns and false of the carrying: a column of time carries at sixty, not at ten or a hundred. The summary box after the worked examples converts minutes only once they go past sixty, and exactly sixty minutes is an hour too, so say sixty or more every time. The worked subtraction from twenty-two hours breaks an hour across two empty columns, into fifty-nine minutes and sixty seconds. The page shows that only in its grid, never in words, so work it aloud. One exercise writes a race time as two hours thirty-five seconds with the minutes left out. It means two hours, no minutes and thirty-five seconds, and read any other way the new record would be slower than the old one. Another exercise prints its first line with the words run together, so read it aloud to the class.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · A column for each unit
**Screen:** two times written in a line, and the empty frame headed hours, minutes and seconds
**Does:** drags each part of each time under its own heading, then adds the columns from the right
**Win:** every number sits under its heading, and the columns are added one at a time from the seconds
**Feels:** each column holds one kind of time, so a part is placed by its name and not by where the line ends
**Goal:** each unit of a time is written in its own column, and the columns are added from the right, smallest unit first
**Observe:** Every part settled under its own heading, and the columns were added one at a time from the seconds.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- lines up two hours fifteen minutes thirty seconds and one hour twenty seconds by their right-hand ends, so the one hour stands under the fifteen minutes, and gets two hours sixteen minutes fifty seconds. → the one hour card springs back from the minutes column to the line of times with its word hour lit, then settles under the hours heading, and the minutes of that line fill with 00. The columns are added again from the seconds, and the answer line reads three hours fifteen minutes fifty seconds beside the child's answer in the slot, nothing marked.
- writes five hours seven minutes forty-two seconds with the seven in the first place of the minutes column, so the minutes read seventy. → the minutes column's two places are both drawn, and the seven in the first place is read aloud as seventy minutes, an hour and ten minutes, while the gate beside it lights. The seven then moves to the second place with a nought in front of it, 07, the gate goes dark, and the child's line stays drawn beside the new one.
**Misconception to break:** Adding along a line as if the time were one number, or writing the seconds under the minutes.

### 2 · Sixty or more carries
**Screen:** a sum in the frame whose minutes add up to sixty or more, with the sixty gate waiting
**Does:** adds the minutes, sends sixty through the gate as one hour, writes what is left, and adds the hours with the carry
**Win:** one hour sits in a circle over the hours column, the minutes left are written with two digits, and the hours total takes in the carry
**Feels:** the gate lets sixty through the moment the column reaches it, and not one minute later
**Goal:** sixty or more minutes carry an hour, and sixty or more seconds carry a minute, with exactly sixty carrying too
**Observe:** When the minutes came to sixty or more the gate lit, one hour rolled into the carry circle, and what was left went down in two places.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes eight hours eighty-five minutes for six hours forty-eight minutes and two hours thirty-seven minutes, carrying nothing because eighty-five is less than a hundred. → the minutes column adds to eighty-five and the sixty gate lights: sixty cells lift out, roll up into one hour and drop into the carry circle over the hours, and twenty-five go down. The hours then add with the carry, six and two and one, and the answer line reads nine hours twenty-five minutes beside the child's answer in the slot.
- writes three hours sixty minutes for one hour thirty minutes and two hours thirty minutes, because sixty did not seem to be past sixty. → the minutes column adds to exactly sixty and the gate lights all the same: all sixty cells lift out and roll up into one hour in the carry circle, and the minutes left, none, go down as 00. The hours add with the carry to four, and the child's three hours sixty minutes stays in the slot beside four hours.
- carries only one hour when three sets of minutes add to one hundred and twenty, and writes four hours sixty minutes. → the gate lights and one lot of sixty cells rolls into the carry circle, and the sixty left in the column are still sixty or more, so the gate lights again and a second hour rolls in beside the first. The minutes left, none, go down as 00, the carry circle reads two, and the hours add to five beside the child's answer.
**Misconception to break:** Writing 8 hours 85 minutes, or waiting for a hundred minutes before carrying, as in whole numbers.

### 3 · Borrowing an hour
**Screen:** a taking-away in the frame whose top minutes are fewer than the minutes below them
**Does:** breaks one hour into sixty minutes, adds them to the top minutes, and takes away column by column
**Win:** the hours digit goes down by one, sixty minutes join the top minutes, and the difference is written
**Feels:** the broken hour does not vanish, it crosses into the minutes as sixty
**Goal:** when the minutes on top are too few, one hour is broken into sixty minutes and the hours go down by one
**Observe:** The hours digit on top went down by one, and sixty cells poured into the minutes on top.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- takes the smaller number from the larger in the minutes column, fifty less twenty-five, and writes five hours twenty-five minutes for eight hours twenty-five minutes take away three hours fifty minutes. → the minutes cell stays shut on the twenty-five on top, and the child's twenty-five is written small beside it. The break control strikes through the eight and writes seven above it in wine, and sixty cells in six rows of ten pour into the minutes on top, which are rewritten eighty-five. Eighty-five less fifty is taken away, and the answer line reads four hours thirty-five minutes beside the child's answer in the slot.
- breaks an hour into a hundred minutes, writes the top minutes as one hundred and twenty-five, and gets four hours seventy-five minutes. → the cell panel lays out the broken hour as it really opens, six rows of ten, sixty cells, and the child's hundred is laid beside it as ten rows of ten, four rows too many. The top minutes are rewritten twenty-five and sixty, eighty-five, and the take-away runs again, the child's answer staying in the slot.
- breaks the hour into sixty minutes but leaves the eight hours on top as they were, and gets five hours thirty-five minutes. → the eight on top is lit with nothing struck through it, beside sixty minutes that came from nowhere, and the whole top line is counted in minutes to show it has grown by an hour. The eight is then struck through and seven written above it in wine, and the hours take away again, the child's answer staying in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Taking the smaller number from the larger in the minutes column, 50 less 25, and writing 5 hours 25 minutes.

### 4 · Borrowing across an empty column
**Screen:** a whole number of hours on top, with hours, minutes and seconds to take away below it
**Does:** breaks one hour into sixty minutes, then one of those minutes into sixty seconds, and takes away column by column
**Win:** the top line reads one hour fewer, fifty-nine minutes and sixty seconds, each column takes away, and the check rebuilds the top
**Feels:** one hour has to fill two empty columns, so one of its minutes is opened again into seconds
**Goal:** when the minutes and seconds on top are both empty, one hour is broken into fifty-nine minutes and sixty seconds
**Observe:** With both columns on top empty, one hour opened into fifty-nine minutes and sixty seconds, and the check rebuilt the whole hours.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes six hours as five hours sixty minutes sixty seconds, which is one minute too many, and answers three hours twenty-four minutes fifteen seconds. → the top line the child wrote is counted in seconds beside six hours, and it runs sixty seconds, one whole minute, past it. The double break then opens one hour into sixty minutes and one of those minutes into sixty seconds, so the minutes on top read fifty-nine, and the take-away runs again, the child's answer staying in the slot.
- brings the thirty-six minutes and forty-five seconds straight down under the empty columns, and writes four hours thirty-six minutes forty-five seconds. → the seconds cell stays shut over the empty seconds on top, and the child's numbers are written small beside the frame. The double break strikes through the six and writes five above it in wine, and the top line fills to fifty-nine minutes and sixty seconds from the cell panel. Each column then takes away, and the child's answer stays in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Writing 6 hours as 5 hours 60 minutes 60 seconds, which is one minute too many, and answering 3 hours 24 minutes 15 seconds.

## Delight
The fourth phase, the moment the check control runs on six hours take away two hours thirty-six minutes forty-five seconds. The answer, three hours twenty-three minutes fifteen seconds, slides down onto the smaller time, and the seconds make exactly sixty: the gate lights, a minute rolls into its circle, and 00 goes down. With the carry the minutes make exactly sixty too, that gate lights, an hour rolls into its circle, and 00 goes down again. The hours add to six, and the check strip reads six hours with both empty columns back, beside the top line it came from. It can be run as often as a finger likes, nothing is marked, and the voice says nothing at all while it happens.

**General miss line:** Work the column on the right first, and watch the gate at sixty.

## Answer key
Times are added and taken away in columns, one for each unit, with the hours on the left, then the minutes, then the seconds. Work from the right, the smallest unit first. When a column adds up to sixty or more, take out sixty as one of the next unit and carry it left. Write what is left with two digits; exactly sixty carries one and leaves 00. When the top number in a column is too small to take the bottom one from, break one of the next unit. One hour gives sixty minutes, one minute gives sixty seconds, and the column on the left goes down by one. When the minutes and seconds on top are both empty, one hour breaks into fifty-nine minutes and sixty seconds. Adding the answer back to the smaller time gives the larger one.

## Student controls
- **Column frame**: headed hours, minutes and seconds, with a line for each time and an answer line
- **Drag to heading**: places each part of a time under its own heading, and refuses the wrong column
- **Sixty gate**: lights when a column reaches sixty or more, and carries one to the left
- **Carry circle**: holds the carried hour or minute at the top of the column on the left
- **Break control**: opens one hour into sixty minutes, or one minute into sixty seconds, and rewrites the column on the left
- **Double break**: opens one hour into fifty-nine minutes and sixty seconds when both columns on top are empty
- **Check control**: adds the answer back to the smaller time and rebuilds the larger one
