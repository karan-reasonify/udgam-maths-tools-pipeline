## What it is
**Description:** Watch the second hand go round once, set the exchanges between days, hours, minutes and seconds, and step tiles down the ladder to change a time into a smaller unit.
**Why:** Know how many of each unit of time make the next, and change days, hours or minutes into a smaller unit, with the part already there added on.
**Look:** A ladder of four rungs, day, hour, minute and second, with tiles on the rungs, and a clock with a thin second hand beside it.
**Context:** Grade 4, and the chapter's topic on changing one unit of time into another. An adult sees a multiplication, which it is. What is actually being settled is which number to multiply by, twenty-four for days and sixty for hours and for minutes, and what happens to the part already in the smaller unit. A child who multiplies every number in sight turns three hours forty minutes into far too many minutes, and one who drops the forty answers one hundred and eighty. The printed questions name the unit they want, so the unit is part of the answer.
**For the child:** Watch the second hand go round once. Fill in the numbers between the rungs. Step each tile down and multiply. Add on the part that was already there. Write the unit.
**Teaching notes:** Before the tool is opened, watch a real second hand go round for a whole minute with the class counting aloud. Let them see the minute hand step on one mark as it finishes. The page names three units, the hour, the minute and the second, and uses the day as well, twenty-four hours. Say the three exchanges every time: twenty-four for days, sixty for hours and sixty for minutes. The worked examples change one unit at a time and then two units into one, and the second kind is where the added part is lost. Watch the exercise that asks for seventy-five minutes in seconds: it wants seconds, not an hour and fifteen minutes. The match in the On Your Own box is played for at most six hours a day. The page asks for its total minutes, so work it as five days of six hours. One exercise sets its conversion in a breathing exercise held for a long time. This plan does not reuse that setting, because breath-holding is not a safe thing for children to practise.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · The second hand
**Screen:** the clock with three hands, the thin second hand at the twelve, and a count of ticks at nought
**Does:** starts the second hand and counts its ticks for one whole lap, watching the minute hand
**Win:** sixty ticks are counted, and the minute hand steps on exactly one mark
**Feels:** one lap of the thin hand is sixty ticks, and the long hand creeps one mark for it
**Goal:** the second hand moves one mark each second and goes round once a minute, so one minute is sixty seconds
**Observe:** Sixty ticks were counted in one lap, and the minute hand stepped on exactly one mark.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- counts the twelve numbers as the second hand passes them, and says one lap is twelve seconds. → the second hand is run again from the twelve, and the tick count beside the clock climbs by one at every mark, reaching five at the one, ten at the two, and sixty back at the twelve. The minute hand steps on one mark as the lap ends, and the child's twelve stays in the slot beside the count.
- says one lap of the second hand moves the minute hand on to the next number, five minutes. → the second hand runs one lap and the tick count writes sixty, and the minute hand's move is lit: one small mark and no further. A second lap steps it one more mark, and the child's answer stays in the slot beside the two marks.
**Misconception to break:** Counting the twelve numbers instead of the sixty marks, and saying one lap is twelve seconds.

### 2 · Sixty, sixty and twenty-four
**Screen:** the ladder with its four rungs in a jumble and the three exchange tags blank
**Does:** orders the rungs from the largest unit at the top to the smallest at the bottom, then fills each exchange tag
**Win:** the rungs settle as day, hour, minute and second, and twenty-four, sixty and sixty fill the tags
**Feels:** each unit is a bundle of the one below it, and the day's bundle is a different size from the others
**Goal:** sixty seconds make a minute, sixty minutes make an hour and twenty-four hours make a day, with the second the smallest and the hour the largest of the three
**Observe:** The rungs settled with the day at the top and the second at the foot, and the tags read twenty-four, sixty and sixty.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- uses sixty for every step, and says a day is sixty hours. → the child's sixty is set on the tag between day and hour, and a day tile is stepped down: its cells fill two rows of ten and four more, and the rung stops there at twenty-four, with the child's tag still reading sixty beside the count. The child's answer stays in the slot, nothing marked.
- puts the second above the minute, because sixty seconds is a bigger number than one minute. → one minute tile is set on the minute rung and stepped down, and it opens into sixty second cells, each far smaller than the tile. The sixty cells are laid back over the minute tile and fill it exactly, and the child's order stays set out beside the ladder.
**Misconception to break:** Using sixty for every step, and saying a day is sixty hours.

### 3 · Larger to smaller: multiply
**Screen:** a row of tiles on one rung, with the rung below empty
**Does:** steps the tiles down, watches each open into the smaller unit, and writes the multiplication for the whole row
**Win:** every tile opens into the rung below, and the multiplication and the total are written beside it
**Feels:** every tile on the rung opens into the same number of cells, so a row of tiles is one multiplication
**Goal:** a larger unit is changed into a smaller one by multiplying by the number between their rungs
**Observe:** Each tile on the rung opened into the same number of cells, and the multiplication beside the rung gave the total.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- multiplies nine days by sixty and answers five hundred and forty hours. → the nine day tiles step down one at a time, and each opens into two rows of ten hour cells and four more, twenty-four and no more. The multiplication beside the rung is written nine times twenty-four, and the total two hundred and sixteen. The child's five hundred and forty stays in the slot beside it.
- writes three hours as three hundred minutes, as if an hour were a hundred minutes the way a metre is a hundred centimetres. → the three hour tiles step down, and each opens into a bar of sixty minute cells in six rows of ten, not ten rows of ten. The three bars make one hundred and eighty. The child's three hundred is laid beside them in bars of sixty and runs to five bars, which is five hours, and the child's number stays in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Multiplying by sixty because hours and minutes use sixty, and answering 540 hours.

### 4 · Two units into one
**Screen:** a time loaded as hour tiles and minute tiles, with the minute tiles waiting in the side bay
**Does:** steps the hour tiles down, slides the side bay's minutes in, and writes the total with its unit
**Win:** the two groups of minutes join into one row, and the total is written in minutes
**Feels:** the hours need changing and the minutes do not, so the two parts are treated differently
**Goal:** a time in two units is changed into the smaller unit by changing the larger part and adding the part already there
**Observe:** The side bay's minutes slid in beside the opened hours and were added, not opened.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- leaves out the eighteen minutes and answers three hundred minutes for five hours eighteen minutes. → the five hour tiles open into five bars of sixty, three hundred cells, and the side bay's eighteen minute tiles glow and slide in beside them. The add bar writes three hundred and eighteen, and the child's three hundred stays in the slot beside it.
- writes one hour thirty minutes as one hundred and thirty minutes, reading the time as a whole number. → the hour tile opens into one bar of sixty, and the thirty from the side bay slide in beside it: ninety. The child's one hundred and thirty is laid beside it in bars of sixty, two full bars and ten more, which is two hours ten minutes, and the child's number stays in the slot.
- for eighty-five minutes in seconds, writes one hour twenty-five minutes, changing the time up the ladder instead of down. → the child's answer is built first: sixty of the eighty-five minute cells gather into one hour tile that rises a rung, and the twenty-five left over stay on the minute rung as minutes, which is what going up the ladder gives. The unit strip then lights seconds, the unit the question asks for, and the eighty-five minutes step down instead, each opening into sixty second cells: eighty-five times sixty, five thousand one hundred seconds. The child's answer stays in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Leaving out the 18 minutes and answering 300, or multiplying them by sixty as well.

## Delight
The third phase, when one day tile is stepped down twice. It opens into twenty-four hour cells, and stepping those down opens each into a bar of sixty minute cells, until the minute rung is lined with folded stacks and the multiplication beside it reads twenty-four times sixty, one thousand four hundred and forty minutes, all out of one small olive tile. It can be run as often as a finger likes, nothing is marked, and the voice says nothing at all while it happens.

**General miss line:** Open one tile and count what is inside it.

## Answer key
Sixty seconds make one minute, sixty minutes make one hour, and twenty-four hours make one day. The second is the smallest of the three clock units and the hour the largest, and a day is larger still. The second hand moves one mark each second and goes round once a minute. To change a larger unit into a smaller one, multiply: days by twenty-four to get hours, hours by sixty to get minutes, and minutes by sixty to get seconds. For a time in two units, change the larger unit and then add the part already in the smaller unit. So one hour twenty-five minutes is sixty minutes and twenty-five minutes, eighty-five minutes. Give the answer in the unit the question asks for.

## Student controls
- **Ladder**: four rungs, day, hour, minute and second, from the top down
- **Exchange tags**: sit between the rungs and are filled with twenty-four, sixty and sixty
- **Tiles**: load a time onto its rungs, one tile for each unit
- **Step down**: opens every tile on a rung into cells of the rung below and writes the multiplication
- **Side bay**: holds the tiles already on the lower rung until they are slid in and added
- **Add bar**: adds the opened cells and the side bay into one total
- **Unit slot**: takes an answer only with the unit the question asks for
- **Second hand clock**: ticks one mark a second and steps the minute hand on at the end of each lap

A day is twenty-four hours, an hour is sixty minutes and a minute is sixty seconds, so a time is changed into a smaller unit by multiplying and adding on what is left. One minute.
