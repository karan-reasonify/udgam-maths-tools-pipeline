## What it is
**Description:** Add the answer back onto the number you took away, and hold the total against the number you started from.
**Why:** Find out whether a difference is right without working the same subtraction over again, by putting back what was taken away.
**Look:** Two bays joined down the middle, the subtraction above and the check below it, with a true and false rail along the foot of the stage.
**Context:** Grade 4, and this is the topic that stops a wrong answer from travelling. An adult sees an extra sum done after the real one. What is actually being taught is that subtraction carries its own test inside it, worked with different steps: difference plus subtrahend rebuilds the minuend. Reading an answer over again repeats whatever slip produced it, and a child who has only ever read an answer over has no way of finding out that it is wrong.
**For the child:** Work the subtraction in the upper bay. Then drop your answer and the number you took away into the bay below. Add them and hold the total against the number you started from. If the two match, the working is right. If they do not, go back and work it again.
**Teaching notes:** The chapter's own worked check is the one to read aloud before anything is opened: taking 56,986 from 98,975 leaves 41,989, and putting 41,989 back onto 56,986 rebuilds 98,975. Say it as putting back what was taken away, not as a second sum, because that is the whole reason it catches anything. Insist that the check is written out in columns. A child who reads the answer over again will repeat the same slip and feel surer afterwards than before. The verdict rail is worth the slowest work: hand over statements that are already written out, some right and some not, and let the class discover that a wrong answer looks exactly like a right one until it is tested. When a statement fails, stop and ask what the check does and does not tell them. It says the working is wrong; it does not say which column. Finish on the turn it round control, so that addition and subtraction are held as one another undone rather than as two separate chapters. The dice game at the end of the chapter is this whole topic as a race, and it works best with the roles swapped every round.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · The answer and the number taken away rebuild the start
**Screen:** a finished subtraction above an empty check bay
**Does:** drops the answer and the number taken away into the bay and adds them
**Win:** the total lifts, stands beside the number on top and the match light joins them
**Feels:** putting back what was taken away lands on the number you set out from, every single time
**Goal:** difference plus subtrahend lands on the minuend
**Observe:** The check was a different sum from the one it was testing, and that is the only reason it caught anything.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- works the subtraction a second time instead of adding anything back, because doing it again is what checking has always meant. → the second subtraction runs column by column beside the first and lands on exactly the same difference, with every column agreeing. Both runs are left standing side by side, the number the working set out from stays untouched above them, and the shelf stays empty, because nothing has been built that could be stood on it.
- adds the difference to the number on top instead of to the number that was taken away. → that addition runs in the lower bay and its total is lifted to the shelf, where it stands plainly taller than the number beside it and the match light stays dark. The three rows in the upper bay are then read out by their names, and the two rows that rebuild the top one light together while the child's pair stays drawn exactly where it was put.
**Misconception to break:** Subtracting a second time instead of adding back, which just repeats the same slip.

### 2 · A check worked all the way through
**Screen:** the check bay open with the columns empty and the plus already drawn
**Does:** adds the check column by column from the ones and reads the total out
**Win:** every column of the check is filled and its total is held against the number on top
**Feels:** the check is a sum in its own right and it has to be worked like one
**Goal:** the check is written out in columns rather than glanced at
**Observe:** The total the check built was stood beside the number the working set out from, not compared from memory.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- looks at the two numbers, says the answer seems about right and gives a verdict without the check bay being touched. → the check runs anyway at walking pace, column by column from the ones, with each column filling in green as it is added. The child's verdict stays in the answer slot the whole time, and the total the columns build is lifted to the shelf beside the number the working set out from so that the judgement and the check stand together.
- writes the number from the top of the upper bay into the check's total, because that is what the check is supposed to come to. → the copied total stays exactly where it was put. The check is then added column by column from the ones with every carried one drawn small above its column, and the total the columns actually build stands beside the copied one with the first column from the right where the two part lit and staying lit.
**Misconception to break:** Saying the answer looks about right and never writing the addition down at all.

### 3 · A printed answer can be wrong
**Screen:** a statement already written out, with the verdict rail waiting below it
**Does:** runs the check, reads the match light and sends the statement to one end
**Win:** the statement lands on the end the check earned and the true difference stands beside it
**Feels:** a wrong answer looks exactly like a right one until it has been added back
**Goal:** a written statement is judged by the check and not by how it looks
**Observe:** When the check failed it said that the working was wrong and never once said which column was wrong.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- sends the statement to the true end because it is neatly written and its columns line up. → the drag springs back from the true end with nothing marked and the statement left where it began. The check then runs of its own accord in the lower bay, adding 52,011 on to 21,340, and its total of 73,351 is lifted to the shelf beside 73,451 with the match light dark and the hundreds column, which is where the two part, left ringed in amber.
- sends the statement to the false end and moves on without ever working out what it should have said. → the statement stays at the false end where the child put it. The working in the upper bay is then opened again column by column from the ones until it builds 52,111, that difference is dropped into the check bay and added on to 21,340, and the total is lifted to the shelf, where it lands on 73,451 and the match light fills.
**Misconception to break:** Believing a written answer because it is written, and never adding it back.

### 4 · Addition and subtraction check each other
**Screen:** an addition handed over in the upper bay with the turn it round control lit
**Does:** takes one of the two numbers from the total and looks for the other
**Win:** the other number is left exactly, and the two bays read as one another undone
**Feels:** the two workings undo each other, so either one can be used to test the other
**Goal:** an addition can be tested by taking one of its numbers away
**Observe:** A written statement that was wrong looked exactly like one that was right.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- adds the two numbers together a second time to check the addition. → the second addition runs beside the first and lands on the same total, column for column and carry for carry, with both runs left standing. The turn it round control then lights on its own, and the lower bay opens with a minus drawn in it and one of the two numbers already waiting underneath the total.
- takes the smaller of the two numbers away from the greater one instead of taking either of them away from the total. → that subtraction runs in the lower bay and leaves 8,793, which is none of the three numbers on the bench, and it is left standing. The three numbers are then named out loud as two parts and a total with the total lit, the check is laid again taking one part away from the total, and both runs stay on the stage together.
**Misconception to break:** Adding the two numbers again to check the addition, which repeats the same steps.

## Delight
The moment hold against is pressed. The total lifts out of the lower bay and travels up the drawn path to the shelf, where it comes to rest beside the number the working set out from. The two are then read off digit for digit from the right, one pair at a time, with each pair lighting as it is read, and the match light between them fills only once the last pair on the left has agreed. Where they part, the reading stops at the first column from the right that parts them, that column keeps a small amber ring, and the light stays dark with nothing else marked anywhere.

**General miss line:** Send them down again and add the whole check out, column by column.

## Answer key
To check a subtraction, add the difference to the subtrahend. The total has to be the minuend, the number the working started from. If it is not, something in the working is wrong, although the check does not yet say where. The check has to be written out in full, because reading an answer again repeats whatever slip made it. The same tie works the other way: to check an addition, take one of the two numbers away from the total and the other one should be left.

## Student controls
- **Upper bay**: holds the subtraction, set out in columns
- **Drop to check**: sends the difference and the subtrahend down into the lower bay
- **Add it back**: adds the two in the lower bay and lifts the total
- **Hold against**: stands the total beside the number the working started from
- **Match light**: joins the two when they read the same and stays dark when they do not
- **Verdict rail**: takes a written statement to the true end or the false end
- **Turn it round**: hands over an addition and checks it by taking one number away
- **Dice bay**: builds a number from rolled digits, takes it away and adds it back

## Textbook (this topic)
**Pages:** 30
**Key terms:** difference, subtrahend, minuend, checking
A difference is checked by adding it to the number that was taken away, which must rebuild the number you started with. On the number you started with, every time. Taking away and putting back undo each other. So difference plus subtrahend gives the minuend. The difference is 41,989. Adding 41,989 to 56,986 gives 98,975, which is where you began. The check has to be written out, not just looked at. No. Adding 10,000 back to 23,244 gives 33,244, not 34,244. The true difference is 11,000. The page prints that error on purpose, so that checking is what catches it. Take one of the two numbers away from the total. The other one should be left. Each of the two undoes the other, so either can check the other.
