## What it is
**Description:** Run a number through the rules that need no columns, and find the one thing subtraction will not let you do.
**Why:** Find out which things you are allowed to do to a subtraction, and the one thing that addition allows and this does not.
**Look:** A rail with a number card riding on it, rule cards waiting underneath, and a two lane order gate standing at its left hand end.
**Context:** Grade 4, and the chapter stops working here to ask what subtraction will and will not allow. An adult sees four shortcuts and a box about order. What is actually at stake is a rule carried over from the chapter before. There, two numbers being added could be swapped freely and the answer did not care. Here they cannot, and a child who assumes otherwise writes the smaller number on top and then takes the bigger digit from the smaller one column by column, which is the tidiest way there is of getting every column wrong.
**For the child:** Put a number on the rail and drop a rule card onto it. Say which digit will move before it moves. Then send a pair through the order gate both ways round and see which lane jams. Finish at the fact bay and write both subtraction facts from one addition.
**Teaching notes:** Start at the order gate and start it as a question, not as a rule. The chapter before this one taught that two numbers being added may be swapped over and nothing changes, so every child in the room arrives expecting the same here, and telling them otherwise is much weaker than letting the second lane jam in front of them. Run the same pair as an addition straight afterwards, while the jam is still on the stage, so the two rules are held apart by having been seen side by side. For the rules that need no columns, make them say the answer before the card drops every single time, because a rule used after the fact is only a check and a rule used before it is a rule. Watch for the child who counts places from the left hand end: with a five digit number they will be right often enough to keep doing it, and a six digit number then catches them out. The fact bay is worth ending on. Three cards, one addition and two subtractions, and the two subtractions both take a part from the total rather than a part from a part. The vocabulary quiz works best with the word pad turned towards a partner and the five terms asked in a different order each time.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · The order cannot be swapped
**Screen:** the order gate with the same pair loaded into both of its lanes
**Does:** runs both lanes, watches one jam, then loads the pair as an addition
**Win:** one lane jams for want of enough to take, and both addition lanes land together
**Feels:** one of the two lanes simply has nothing left in it to take from
**Goal:** the greater number has to go first, unlike two numbers being added
**Observe:** The same pair ran into both lanes together and only one of the two lanes came out with an answer.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- swaps the two numbers over so that the subtraction reads the easier way round, because addition allowed exactly that. → the swapped pair is sent through the gate as it stands and the lane jams at the first column with less in it than is being asked for, with the pair left standing and nothing marked. The swap control then loads the identical two numbers as an addition, both lanes run to the end and their totals land together, and the two runs are left on the stage one above the other so that the rule that holds and the rule that does not can be read side by side.
- keeps the smaller number in front and takes the smaller digit from the greater one in each column, so that the lane runs after all. → the lane is run again at walking pace with each column read out in the order it is written, the leading number's digit named first, and every column the child turned round is ringed amber and read again as it stands. The same pair is then run the proper way round in the lane above, and the two answers stand at their openings together with the check by addition run on each of them.
**Misconception to break:** Carrying the swapping rule over from addition and writing the smaller number on top.

### 2 · One, nothing, and the number itself
**Screen:** a number on the rail with the nothing, one and itself cards waiting
**Does:** drops each of the three in turn and says what moved before it moves
**Win:** the card stands unchanged, steps back one, and empties, in that order
**Feels:** three answers, and not one column worked between them
**Goal:** three rules that need no columns at all
**Observe:** Loaded as an addition, that very same pair ran in both lanes and the two totals landed together.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes nothing at all as the answer when nothing has been taken away, because the two rules were learnt in the same breath. → the nothing card runs again at walking pace with the place chart behind the number and every digit pinned, and not one of them moves; the card that leaves the rail is read out place by place as the same number it arrived as. The itself card is then run on a second card beside it and that one empties, and the two cards are left standing together for comparison.
- steps the card forward to the number just after instead of back to the number just before. → a short strip of the numbers either side of the card is drawn under the rail with the card sitting on its own place, and the step is animated along it; the number just before lights and the number just after is left plain. The child's answer stays on the strip exactly where it was put, one place past the card, and both stay showing.
**Misconception to break:** Writing 0 for 45,208 - 0, by reading the rule for taking a number from itself.

### 3 · Ten, a hundred and a thousand each touch one digit
**Screen:** the rail with the place chart behind the number and every digit pinned
**Does:** names the digit that will drop, then drops the card and checks
**Win:** the named digit drops by one, the rest stay pinned and the chart agrees
**Feels:** one digit moves and none of the others cares
**Goal:** a round amount drops the digit of its own place and no other
**Observe:** Taking a round amount moved one digit and left every other digit standing exactly where it was.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- counts the places from the left hand end of the number, so the digit that drops is the wrong one. → the place chart slides in behind the number and is read out from the right hand end, with the place the card names lit the whole way. The digit the child dropped keeps its own mark where it was put, and the same rule card is then run on 4,37,912, where 4,36,912 and 4,27,912 stand one under the other and the two ways of counting plainly part.
- drops the named digit and nudges the digits beside it as well, as though the whole number had to shift along. → hold the rest pins every other digit and the card is run again with each pinned digit drawn holding its place as the movement passes it. The number is then read out place by place before and after, the two readings standing one under the other, and only the single named place differs between them.
**Misconception to break:** Counting places from the left hand end of the number instead of the right.

### 4 · Every addition fact holds two subtraction facts
**Screen:** the fact bay holding one addition fact on three cards
**Does:** turns the fact over and writes both subtraction facts from the same cards
**Win:** both subtraction facts stand under the addition, built from the same three cards
**Feels:** three cards, and every one of the three facts uses all three of them
**Goal:** one addition fact turns over into two subtraction facts
**Observe:** Three number cards produced three facts, and two of the three were subtractions.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes a subtraction that takes one of the two parts away from the other part. → the three cards are read again as two parts and a total with the total card lit, and the child's line is worked anyway in the lane beside it, landing on 13,069, which is not written on any of the three cards. Both lines stay on the stage, with the lines that begin from the total lit and the one that does not left plain.
- writes one subtraction fact and reports the fact bay finished. → the fact bay keeps the line the child wrote and lifts the part card that line took away, leaving the other part standing on its own and unused; the bay then asks for a line that begins at the total and ends on that card, and both subtraction facts finish standing under the addition, all three built from the same three cards.
**Misconception to break:** Writing 41,203 - 26,150 as a subtraction fact, which takes one part from another part.

## Answer key
The two numbers in a subtraction cannot be swapped: the greater one has to go first, and that is not a choice. Two numbers being added may be swapped freely, which is the rule this one is easily mistaken for. Taking one away gives the number just before it, its predecessor. Taking nothing away leaves a number exactly as it was. Taking a number away from itself leaves nothing. Taking ten drops the tens digit by one, taking a hundred drops the hundreds digit and taking a thousand drops the thousands digit, with every other digit standing still. Every addition fact holds two subtraction facts, one for each number that was added.

## Student controls
- **Number rail**: carries one number card across the stage for the rules to act on
- **Rule cards**: takes nothing, one, the number itself, ten, a hundred or a thousand
- **Place chart**: names the place of the digit that moved, behind the number
- **Hold the rest**: pins every other digit visibly still while one drops
- **Order gate**: runs the same pair with the greater number first and with the smaller first
- **Swap control**: loads the same pair as an addition, where both lanes run
- **Fact bay**: turns one addition fact over into the two subtraction facts inside it
- **Word pad**: holds the chapter terms for a partner to be quizzed on

## Textbook (this topic)
**Pages:** 31, 32
**Key terms:** order, predecessor, subtraction fact, rule
Order is fixed, one and zero behave predictably, and every addition fact holds two subtraction facts. Not at all. Eighteen take ten is eight, but ten take eighteen is not eight. In subtraction the greater number has to go first, and that is not a choice you get to make. Taking one gives the number just before it, its predecessor. Taking nothing leaves the number exactly as it was. Taking the number away from itself leaves nothing at all. Only the tens digit, which went down by one. Taking a hundred drops the hundreds digit instead, and taking a thousand drops the thousands digit. The rest of the number stands still. Two. Take either number away from the total and the other one is left. A line holding a plus and a minus can be rearranged too, adding first and taking away after.
