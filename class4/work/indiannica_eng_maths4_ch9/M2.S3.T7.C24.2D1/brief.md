## What it is
**Description:** Fold a figure along a line to test whether its halves match, count its lines of symmetry, and place a mirror image the same distance behind an upright mirror line.
**Why:** Fold a figure to find out whether its halves match, and stand it by a mirror to see where its image goes.
**Look:** A paper figure on a table with a fold line across it, a tally beside it, and a mirror bay on squared paper at the right.
**Context:** Grade 4, and the chapter's opening question comes back here. An adult sees paper being folded. What is actually being settled is that a line of symmetry is tested by folding and not guessed by looking, which is why a rectangle's diagonal, which looks as though it should work, is a trap: fold along it and the corners stick out. ## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Folding so the halves match
**Screen:** a paper figure on the table with the fold line lying across it
**Does:** sets the fold line, folds, and looks for any part that sticks out
**Win:** the halves lie exactly together, the fold sets hard, and the line is kept as a line of symmetry
**Feels:** a fold keeps only if nothing at all sticks out
**Goal:** a fold is a line of symmetry only when one half fits exactly over the other
**Observe:** A fold set hard only when nothing stuck out past the other half.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- calls a line across a rectangle near its top a line of symmetry, because it runs right across the figure. → the fold is made along the child's line, the thin top strip turning down onto the rest, and the long part below sticks out past it and glows sky blue. The line stays solid, the paper opens back out, and the true fold across the middle is then tried and kept, drawn dotted. The child's answer stays in the slot, nothing marked.
- folds a capital N down its middle and calls the fold a line of symmetry, because each half has one upright stroke. → the fold is made down the middle of the N, and the slanting stroke of the turned half lands crossing the other half's slant instead of lying on it, both glowing sky blue where they miss. The line stays solid, the paper opens back out, and the child's answer stays in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Judging by eye that a line looks central, without checking that every part fits.

### 2 · More than one line, or none
**Screen:** a square, a long rectangle and a capital letter F side by side, each with an empty tally
**Does:** tries folds down, across and corner to corner on each figure and reads the tallies
**Win:** the square's tally stops at four, the rectangle's at two with its diagonal turned away, and the letter's at none
**Feels:** a figure keeps every fold that matches and no others, and some keep none at all
**Goal:** a figure can have one line of symmetry, several, or none, and each is found by folding
**Observe:** The square kept four folds, the long rectangle two, and the capital F none.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- counts a long rectangle's diagonal as a line of symmetry and says it has four, like a square. → the fold is made along the diagonal and the turned triangle lands with two corners sticking out past the other half, both glowing sky blue, and the line stays solid. The square beside it folds along its diagonal and matches, so the two diagonal folds are seen side by side. The rectangle's tally stays at two and the child's four stays in the slot.
- stops after the upright and across folds and misses the slanting ones. → the fold line is turned a step at a time round the square, and at each slanting position where the halves meet, the fold sets and is kept, the tally climbing past two to four. The child's count stays in the slot beside the tally, nothing marked.
**Misconception to break:** Stopping at the upright and across folds and missing the two slanting ones.

### 3 · Behind the mirror line
**Screen:** a shape on squared paper beside an upright mirror line, with the distance strings coiled
**Does:** runs a string from each corner to the mirror line, carries it on the same distance, and places the image
**Win:** every corner of the image lands at the end of its string and the image stands turned the other way round
**Feels:** every corner goes as far behind the line as it stood in front, so the near side stays near and the far side stays far
**Goal:** an image sits the same distance behind the mirror line as the object, turned the other way round
**Observe:** Every string ran the same number of squares behind the mirror line as in front of it.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- draws the image right up against the mirror line, or at a different distance from it than the shape. → a string runs from each corner of the shape straight across to the mirror line and on by the same number of squares beyond it, the squares counted aloud on each side. The image is drawn at the ends of the strings in sky blue, and the child's image stays where it was placed, its corners short of the string ends.
- slides the shape across the line without turning it round, so the image faces the same way as the shape. → the strings run from each corner, and the corner nearest the mirror lands nearest it on the other side while the farthest lands farthest, so the image comes out turned the other way round. The child's slid copy stays beside it, and the corner that stood nearest the mirror is lit in it, now sitting farthest from the line.
**Misconception to break:** Placing the image right up against the mirror line, or the same distance from the edge of the paper.

### 4 · Letters that look the same in a mirror
**Screen:** the letter tray beside the upright mirror, with the lying mirror set aside
**Does:** places letters beside the upright mirror one at a time and sorts them into same and turned, then tries the lying mirror
**Win:** the sorted letters settle in two rows, and the lying mirror moves some of them from one row to the other
**Feels:** which letters match depends on which way the mirror stands
**Goal:** a letter looks the same in an upright mirror only if it has an upright line of symmetry
**Observe:** Some letters moved from one row to the other when the lying mirror took the upright mirror's place.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- names the letters that look the same without saying which mirror, mixing B and C, which match in a mirror lying across, with A and M, which match in an upright one. → each of the child's letters is set beside the upright mirror and then above the lying mirror, and its two images are drawn side by side. The letters settle into two rows headed upright and lying across, A and M in one and B and C in the other, and the child's list stays in the slot with the mirror keys lit beside it.
- says N looks the same in an upright mirror, because it looks the same turned half way round. → N is set beside the upright mirror and its image is drawn behind the line point for point, facing the other way. The child's N turned half way round is drawn beside it, the two stand plainly different, and nothing is marked.
**Misconception to break:** Answering without saying which mirror, when B and D look the same in a mirror lying across.

## Delight
The second phase, the moment the fold line is turned onto the square's diagonal. The paper turns over along it, one triangle lying down exactly onto the other with not a hair showing, the fold sets and the line turns from solid to dotted. Drag the same line onto the long rectangle's diagonal and fold, and two corners swing out past the other half and glow, the line stays solid, and the paper opens back out by itself. The two diagonals can be tried one after the other as often as a finger likes, nothing is marked, and the voice says nothing at all while it happens.

**General miss line:** Open it out and fold again slowly, watching for anything that sticks out.

## Answer key
A figure is symmetric if folding it along a line makes one part fit exactly over the other, and that line is a line of symmetry. Some figures have one line of symmetry, some have more than one, and some have none. A rectangle has two, down its middle and across its middle, and its diagonal is not one; a square has four. A mirror image appears on the other side of the mirror line, the same distance from it as the object, turned the other way round, and the mirror line is a line of symmetry between them. A letter looks the same in an upright mirror only if it has an upright line of symmetry: A, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X and Y in plain capitals. In a mirror lying across under the letter, the letters with a line of symmetry across their middle look the same instead.

## Student controls
- **Fold line**: drags and turns to any position on the figure
- **Fold**: lays one side over the other and keeps the fold only if the halves match
- **Overhang glow**: lights any part that sticks out past the other half
- **Symmetry tally**: keeps every fold that matched
- **Upright mirror**: stands a mirror line straight up on squared paper
- **Distance strings**: run from each corner straight across to the mirror line and on to the image
- **Letter tray**: sets capital letters beside the mirror
- **Lying mirror**: sets a second mirror across under a letter, beside the upright one
