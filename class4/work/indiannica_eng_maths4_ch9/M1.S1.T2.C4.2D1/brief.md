## What it is
**Description:** Stamp points, draw straight strokes through them, cap every end with a dot or an arrowhead, and find the two rays that start at a point on a line.
**Why:** Put points down and name them, draw straight strokes through them, and let the ends of each stroke say whether it stops or carries on.
**Look:** A drawing board with a point stamp, a ruler that turns to any slope, a tray of dots and arrowheads, and a tape measure coiled at one corner.
**Context:** Grade 4, the first working topic of the geometry chapter. An adult sees dots and strokes being drawn, which a pencil and a ruler do perfectly well. What is actually being settled is what the ends of a stroke say: an arrowhead says it carries on, a dot says it stops, and only a stroke that stops at both ends has a length. The printed exercise then asks for two rays in a figure that draws only one, and the second is hidden inside the line: a point on a line is the start of two rays, one going each way, which the chapter never says but its own definition gives.
**For the child:** Stamp a point and give it a capital letter. Draw a straight stroke with the ruler. Cap each end with a dot or an arrowhead. Run the tape along it. Tap a point on a line to find its two rays.
**Teaching notes:** Draw with a real ruler and a sharp pencil before the tool is opened, and make the class say what each end of every stroke means. An arrowhead says it carries on, and a dot says it stops. Two sentences on the page need care. It calls a point the smallest shape in geometry; say instead that a point has no size at all, which its own next sentence says. It says a line runs usually along a straight path and then that a line always means a straight one; keep the second, and leave out its example of a road, whose two edges are two separate things, each with ends, and so not a line at all. The exercise then asks for two rays in a figure that draws only one. Before anyone hunts for a second drawn ray, stand a child on a string line and ask how many ways they could walk off along it without turning. Two, so a point on a line is the start of two rays. Name every ray from its end point first, and let the class hear that ray FE and ray EF are not the same ray.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · A point marks a place
**Screen:** an empty drawing board with the point stamp, the letter tray and the magnifier waiting
**Does:** stamps points, names each one with a capital letter, passes the magnifier over one, then joins them in order with the curve pen
**Win:** the paper spreads apart under the magnifier while the dot stays one small mark, and the curve runs through every named point in order
**Feels:** the dot can grow as big as the ink likes, and the place it marks never grows at all
**Goal:** a point marks a position, has no size, and is named by a capital letter
**Observe:** The paper spread apart under the magnifier and the dot stayed one small mark.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- calls the point drawn as a fat dot the bigger point. → the magnifier passes over each dot in turn, and under the glass the paper spreads apart while the exact place each dot marks is found at its middle and held there as one small mark. The two places are restamped side by side at the board's one small size, the fat ink left faintly behind the second, and the child's answer stays in the slot with nothing marked.
- gives a second point the letter already standing on the first, so one letter names two places. → the letter tray holds that capital dimmed, and the two points the child meant to share it are lit one after the other while the dimmed letter pulses. A free capital is offered beside the second point, and the child's name stays written in the strip beside it until another is chosen.
**Misconception to break:** Thinking a bigger dot is a bigger point, or naming a point with a small letter or a number.

### 2 · A line goes on for ever both ways
**Screen:** two named points on the board with the ruler lying across them and both end caps waiting
**Does:** draws a straight stroke through both points, caps both ends with arrowheads, turns the ruler to draw lines across, upright and slanting, then runs the tape along one
**Win:** the tape runs off both edges of the board without finding an end, and the line is named by its two points
**Feels:** the arrowheads are not where the line ends, they are the line saying it does not end
**Goal:** a line is straight, has an arrowhead at each end, and has no length that can be measured
**Observe:** The tape ran off both edges of the board along the line and never gave a reading.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- caps a line with a dot at each end, so what was meant to be line CD is drawn as a stroke that stops. → the name strip writes line segment under the stroke, not line, and the tape runs along it and stops at the second dot with a reading. Beside it the ruler draws the same stroke again with an arrowhead at each end, and the tape along that one runs off both edges of the board with its reading blank. Both strokes stay drawn with their names under them, nothing marked.
- measures from one arrowhead to the other and gives that as the length of the line. → the tape is laid from arrowhead to arrowhead and keeps unrolling past each one, the board sliding to show the line carrying on beyond where it was drawn, while the reading stays blank. The child's number stays in the slot, and the key reading no length is lit on the pad beside it.
**Misconception to break:** Measuring from one arrowhead to the other and giving that as the length of the line.

### 3 · A segment has two ends, a ray has one
**Screen:** two strokes side by side, one capped with a dot at each end and one with a dot and an arrowhead
**Does:** runs the tape along each stroke and reads the one length it finds
**Win:** the tape stops at the second dot of the segment and reads its length, and on the ray it unrolls off the board
**Feels:** a dot says stop and an arrowhead says keep going, and the tape believes them both
**Goal:** a line segment has two end points and a length, and a ray has one end point and goes on in one direction
**Observe:** Swapping one dot for an arrowhead took the reading off the tape.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- gives ray MN a length, reading the ruler from M to the arrowhead. → the tape unrolls from the dot at M, passes N and reaches the arrowhead, and keeps on unrolling past it off the board with its reading blank. The segment beside it is measured again and its tape stops at the second dot with a number. The child's length stays in the slot beside the no length key, nothing marked.
- names the ray from its arrowhead end, writing ray NM for the ray that starts at M. → the splitter draws the ray the child's name describes, starting at N and running back through M and on past it in sky blue, a different ray pointing the other way. The drawn ray stays lit starting at M, the two stand side by side with their names under them, and nothing is marked.
**Misconception to break:** Calling a ray measurable because the part drawn on the page looks short.

### 4 · Two rays start at every point on a line
**Screen:** a line through three named points, with a fourth point off the line joined to the middle one as a ray
**Does:** taps the middle point, watches the splitter light each way in turn, and names every ray that starts there
**Win:** the line lights one way and then the other from the tapped point, and three ray names settle, each starting with its letter
**Feels:** a point on a line has the line running away from it both ways, and each way is a ray of its own
**Goal:** a point on a line is the start of two rays, one going each way, each named from that point first
**Observe:** The splitter lit two rays from a point where only one ray had been drawn.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- names only ray LP, the one drawn as a stroke of its own, and says there is no second ray to find. → the splitter lights the line from L one way, out through M to its arrowhead, and holds it lit beside the drawn ray, then lights the other way through K in sky blue. The names ray LM and ray LK write themselves into the strip under the child's one name, which stays where it was written, and nothing is marked.
- names the two rays along the line with the starting point last, writing ray KL and ray ML. → the splitter draws the rays the child's names describe, one starting at K and one starting at M, each in sky blue and each running on through L. The two rays that start at L are lit beside them in deep blue, all four names stand in the strip, and nothing is marked.
**Misconception to break:** Finding only one ray because only one was drawn separately, or naming a ray with its end point last.

## Delight
The fourth phase, the moment the middle point is tapped. The line lights from that point one way, out through the next letter to the arrowhead at the edge, and holds; then it lights the other way in sky blue and holds too, so the one line is seen as two rays pointing apart from one dot. Their names write themselves into the strip, each beginning with the tapped letter, beside the name of the ray that was drawn all along. The tap can be made on any point of the line as often as a finger likes, nothing is marked, and the voice says nothing at all while it happens.

**General miss line:** Run the tape along it again, and read what each end says.

## Answer key
A point marks a position and has no size. It is shown by a dot and named by a capital letter. A line is straight, goes on for ever in both directions, is drawn with an arrowhead at each end and has no length that can be measured; it is named by any two points on it, and it can run across, upright or slanting. A line segment is part of a line with two end points and a length that can be measured. A ray starts at one end point and goes on for ever in one direction, so it cannot be measured, and it is named from its end point first. A point on a line is the start of two rays, one going each way along the line.

## Student controls
- **Point stamp**: puts a point down with one tap
- **Letter tray**: names a point with a capital letter, never the same letter twice on one board
- **Magnifier**: spreads the paper under a point apart while the dot stays one small mark
- **Curve pen**: joins named points in order with freehand curves
- **Ruler**: draws a straight stroke through two points, turned to any slope
- **End caps**: puts a dot or an arrowhead on each end of a stroke
- **Tape**: measures along a stroke, stopping at a dot and running off at an arrowhead
- **Ray splitter**: lights the two rays that start at a tapped point on a line

**Key terms:** point, line, line segment, ray, end point
A point marks a place, a line goes on for ever both ways, a line segment has two end points, and a ray starts at one and goes on in one direction. It has no size at all. A point only marks a position, and it is named with a capital letter. Nowhere. The arrowheads say it carries on in both directions, so a line has no end points and no length you can measure. The segment, because it starts and stops. A ray starts at its end point and goes on for ever in one direction. Two, one going each way. So a line with a point marked on it holds two rays, even when no ray is drawn on its own.
