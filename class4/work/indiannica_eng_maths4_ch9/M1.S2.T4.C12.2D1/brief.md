## What it is
**Description:** Walk round a figure side by side adding every length, find a side with no label from the sides facing it, and find a missing side from the perimeter.
**Why:** Walk all the way round a figure adding every side once, and find any side that is missing before the walk can finish.
**Look:** A figure with its sides labelled, a small walker at one corner, a string coiled beside it, and a running sum at the top left of the canvas.
**Context:** Grade 4, and the first topic in the chapter that measures anything. An adult sees sides being added up, which any child with a pencil can do. What is actually being taught is that the perimeter is every side of the boundary, including a side the figure does not label. One printed figure, an L-shape, leaves a side unlabelled, and a child who adds only the numbers printed on it is out by the whole of that side. The page never shows how to find such a side, but its own fully labelled stepped figures show the rule: the sides running one way along one side of a stepped shape add up to the side facing them.
**For the child:** Walk round the figure one side at a time. Add each side as you go. If a side has no label, find it from the sides facing it. Every side counts. For a missing side, take the other sides off the perimeter.
**Teaching notes:** Measure something real before the tool is opened, a desk or a book, and lay a string round it too, so the class sees the perimeter as one length before it becomes a sum. Then use the walker, because it adds every side once and cannot skip one. The worked triangle on the page is drawn with a square corner and labelled six, seven and eight centimetres, which no triangle with a square corner can be; its perimeter, twenty-one, is still right, so use the numbers and not the drawing. The one figure that needs real care is the L-shape with a side left unlabelled. Adding the printed numbers gives forty-five, and the perimeter is fifty-two. The page never shows how to find such a side, but its own fully labelled stepped figures show the rule, and this tool teaches only that: the sides running one way along one side of a stepped shape add up to the side facing them, across and upright alike. When the exercise says equilateral, say what it means, a triangle with all three sides equal, because the chapter never does. The Critical Thinking cloth asks for a perimeter and not an area; halve the length to get the width first.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Add every side
**Screen:** a rectangle with two of its sides labelled, the walker at one corner and the sum empty
**Does:** lights the opposite sides, walks the walker round side by side, then lays the string
**Win:** the walker gets home, the sum stops at the perimeter, and the straightened string reaches the same mark on the ruler
**Feels:** the walk goes round once, and every side takes one turn in the sum
**Goal:** the perimeter is every side of the boundary added once
**Observe:** The walker's sum and the straightened string reached the same mark on the ruler.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- adds only the two labelled sides of the rectangle, giving forty for a frame twenty-five by fifteen. → the walker walks the first two sides and the sum stands at forty with the walker only half way round, at the opposite corner. The opposite-sides control lights the two unlabelled sides with the same lengths, the walker walks on, and the sum reaches eighty as it gets home. The child's forty stays in the slot beside the half way mark.
- counts the squares inside a shape on squared paper when asked how far it is round the edge, giving five for a shape whose boundary is twelve. → the walker goes round the outside one square's edge at a time, each edge lighting amber as the sum climbs to twelve, while the five squares inside are lit green and left alone. The child's five stays in the slot beside the green squares, and the twelve stands beside the amber edge.
**Misconception to break:** Adding only the two sides that are labelled, which gives forty, half the perimeter.

### 2 · Four equal sides
**Screen:** a square with one side labelled and the times-four key waiting
**Does:** walks the square once, adding, then presses times four and compares
**Win:** the adding and the times-four answer land side by side on the same number
**Feels:** four equal sides is one side said four times
**Goal:** a figure with four equal sides has a perimeter of four times its side
**Observe:** The times-four key and the walker's adding landed on the same number for every square.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- gives a square's perimeter as its side times two, writing eighteen for a tile of side nine. → the walker walks the square side by side, each nine dropping into the sum, and it stands at eighteen with the walker only half way round, at the opposite corner. It walks on and the sum climbs to thirty-six as it gets home, and the times-four key writes four nines beside it. The child's eighteen stays in the slot beside the half way mark.
- adds four to the side instead of multiplying by four, writing thirteen for a square of side nine. → the times-four key shows its adding: nine, and nine, and nine, and nine, written one under another beside the square's four sides, each side lighting as its nine is written. Nine and four is written beside the stack, one side and a little more, and both stay drawn with the child's answer in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Giving the side, nine, or multiplying by two instead of four.

### 3 · A side with no label still counts
**Screen:** an L-shape with every side labelled but one upright side, the walker stopped beside it
**Does:** opens the facing-sides bracket, reads the missing length off, then lets the walker finish
**Win:** the missing length drops onto the side, the walker walks on, and the sum takes all six sides
**Feels:** a side with no number on it is still a side, and the sides facing it know how long it is
**Goal:** every side belongs to the perimeter, and a side with no label is found from the sides facing it
**Observe:** The walker would not go on at the side with no label, and the bracket found it from the sides facing it.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- adds only the printed numbers on the L-shape and stops, giving twenty-nine for a boundary of thirty-four. → the walker stops at the side with no label and will not step along it; the sum holds at the sides already walked, and the unlabelled side stays lit with its empty label box. The facing-sides bracket opens and lays the three and the empty side end to end above the eight they face, and the missing five is read off the space left. Only then does the walker go on, and the sum reaches thirty-four beside the child's twenty-nine, both standing.
- takes the missing upright side from sides that run across, writing three, which is nine less six. → the missing side is lit, upright, and the bracket lights only the sides that run the same way as it, the upright three on the right and the eight on the left facing it, while the sides running across dim. Laid end to end above the eight, the upright three and the empty bar leave a space of five. The child's three stays in the slot, and the sides across are then stacked on their own to show three and six making nine, a true fact about a different pair of sides.
**Misconception to break:** Adding only the printed numbers, which gives twenty-nine and leaves a whole side out.

### 4 · A side from the perimeter
**Screen:** a figure with its perimeter written in the middle and one side left blank
**Does:** takes each given side off the perimeter in the missing-side bay until one length is left
**Win:** one length is left in the bay, it slides onto the blank side, and the walker's sum matches the middle
**Feels:** the whole way round is known, so the missing piece is whatever the others leave
**Goal:** a missing side is the perimeter less every side that is given
**Observe:** Taking the given sides off the perimeter left exactly the blank side's length.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- adds the given sides to the perimeter instead of taking them away, writing eighty-seven for a field of fifty. → the bay lays the perimeter as one long bar of fifty and lays the three given sides along it end to end, ten, fifteen and twelve, leaving a gap at the end that measures thirteen. The child's eighty-seven is drawn as a bar beside it, far longer than the whole boundary, and both stay drawn, nothing marked.
- takes away only one of the given sides, writing forty for a field whose other two sides were given too. → the forty is slid onto the blank side and the walker walks the whole boundary: ten, fifteen, twelve and forty make seventy-seven, and the fifty written in the middle stands unmatched beside the sum. The bay then takes off the other two given sides one at a time, and the length left shrinks to thirteen, with the child's forty still in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Adding the perimeter to the given sides, or taking away only one of them.

## Delight
The third phase, the moment the bracket closes on the side with no label. The short sides lift off the figure as amber bars and lay themselves end to end, the long side facing them lies down in the row beneath, and the empty bar at the end of the top row stretches until its end sits exactly over the end of the long bar, where it stops by itself with its length written in. Then it flies back to the figure and drops onto the unlabelled side, and the walker, which has been waiting there, steps along it and on round to home. It can be replayed as often as a finger likes, nothing is marked, and the voice says nothing at all while it happens.

**General miss line:** Walk it again from the same corner, and say each side as you add it.

## Answer key
The perimeter is the total length of the boundary, found by adding the lengths of all the sides. Opposite sides of a rectangle are equal, so its perimeter is its length and its breadth added twice. All four sides of a square are equal, so its perimeter is four times its side, and a triangle with all three sides equal has its one side added three times. Every side belongs to the perimeter whether it is labelled or not. In a stepped shape, the sides running one way along one side add up to the side facing them, so a missing side is that facing side less the others beside it. When the perimeter is given, a missing side is the perimeter less all the sides that are given.

## Student controls
- **Walker**: walks one side per tap and adds its length to the sum
- **Running sum**: adds each walked side at the top left of the canvas
- **String**: lays a string round the boundary and straightens it against a ruler
- **Opposite sides**: lights the side opposite a labelled side of a rectangle
- **Times four**: multiplies the side of a figure with four equal sides, showing the adding it replaces
- **Facing-sides bracket**: stacks the sides running one way along one side beside the side facing them
- **Missing-side bay**: takes the given sides off a given perimeter one by one
