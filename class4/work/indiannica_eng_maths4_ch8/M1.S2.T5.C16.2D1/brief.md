## What it is
**Description:** Drop a number into the chart past the ones, then give a digit its place and its worth in two separate slots.
**Why:** Carry your place chart past the ones, and then say two different things about one digit: where it sits, and how much it is worth there.
**Look:** A ruled chart with a heading over every column, a dot column between the ones and the tenths, two answer slots under it and a rebuild bar along the foot.
**Context:** Grade 4, and this topic carries two words that look like one word. The place of a digit is where it sits, and it is a name. The place value of a digit is what it is worth sitting there, and it is an amount. The chapter's own worked example asks for place values and then answers every line with the word place, so a class reading it cold will learn the two as one and answer a place value question with the word tenths, which names the column again and never says how much.
**For the child:** Put one digit in each column. Count the columns from the dot to find a place. Write the name of the place in the first slot. Write what the digit is worth in the second slot. Add all the worths up to check.
**Teaching notes:** Draw the chart by hand first, with the dot given a column of its own, and write every heading in before a single digit goes anywhere. The chapter's own worked example drops 367.912 into the chart a digit at a time, and that one is worth doing together before the tool is opened. Then take the two words apart and keep them apart. Ask for the place first and the worth second, in that order, every single time, and never accept one for the other. A class reading the chapter cold will learn them as one word, because the worked example that goes through 12.563 digit by digit asks for place values and then answers every one of its five lines with the word place. The five worths themselves are right: ten, two, five tenths, six hundredths and three thousandths, and they add back to 12.563 exactly. Use that adding as the check on every place value question you set. A total that does not rebuild the number says a digit was given the wrong column, and it says so without anybody having to mark anything. The digit card lab at the end of the chapter is this whole topic standing up. Put the point card in the middle before the holders arrange themselves. As printed, they stand in any order, and a point at either end builds something the chapter has not taught and the step after it cannot be answered about.

## Opening (first frame of every mount)
Every mount comes up already furnished for its phase with nothing in front of it. On the first phase the familiar chart stands ending at the ones column with plain ruled ground to its right and no headings on it. On the second a number is already dropped into the full chart with the headings turned face down, so the columns are there and unnamed. On the third one digit in the chart is lit and the two answer slots under it stand empty, one headed place and one headed worth. On the fourth every digit has been given its worth and the rebuild bar lies empty along the foot with its plus signs already drawn. The running count at the top left holds the digits settled against the digits the chart is holding, and the answer slot and the pad stand empty down the right. One thing moves, once: the empty ground past the ones, or the face down heading over the lit digit, or the slot headed place, or the first worth waiting to come down, gives one slow pulse while the voice asks what could come after the ones. That pulse is the only invitation, and a finger on it is the whole way in.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · The chart carries on past the point
**Screen:** the familiar chart ending at the ones with empty ground to its right
**Does:** grows the chart past the ones and names each new column as it appears
**Win:** the dot column and the three decimal columns settle, each one named over its head
**Feels:** the chart does not stop, it carries on getting smaller past the ones
**Goal:** the chart the child already knows grows rightwards past the ones, through a dot
**Observe:** The chart did not stop at the ones; it carried on rightwards through a column kept for the dot.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- reads the columns after the point leftwards, the way the whole number columns are read, so tenths ends up at the far right. → the chart is grown again from the ones outwards with each new column named as it appears and a piece of the right size drawn under it, the amber tenth first, the rose hundredth next and the sky blue thousandth last, so the pieces get smaller as the columns travel right. The child's headings stay exactly where they were placed, and the two readings of the chart stand one above the other.
- puts the dot inside the ones column instead of giving it a column of its own. → the chart redraws with the narrow dot column ruled between the ones and the tenths and the dot standing in it, and count from the point is run twice: once from the child's chart, where the count has to start inside a column that is already holding a digit, and once from the chart with the dot column, where it starts on empty ground. Both charts are left standing.
**Misconception to break:** Reading the columns after the point leftwards, the way the whole number columns are read.

### 2 · Counting places from the point
**Screen:** a number dropped into the chart with its columns still unnamed
**Does:** counts rightwards from the dot and names the column a chosen digit sits in
**Win:** the counted columns light one at a time and the chosen column takes its name
**Feels:** the column decides, and the digit standing in it has no say in the matter
**Goal:** position alone decides the place, whatever the digit happens to be
**Observe:** The counting of places always began at the dot, never at either end of the number.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- gives the larger digit the larger place, so an eight is put ahead of a four whatever order they stand in. → count from the point runs along the columns one at a time with each heading lighting as it is passed, and the digits are read out in the order they are standing rather than by size. The child's answer stays in the slot, the two digits are then swapped over in the chart with their places read again, and both readings are left on the stage.
- counts the decimal places from the left hand end of the number instead of from the point. → the counting runs twice with both runs left drawn and numbered over the columns: once from the left hand end of the number, which is still inside the whole number part when it stops, and once from the dot outwards. A second number with three digits in front of the point is then dropped into the chart, where the two counts plainly part, and the child's count stays written where it was made.
**Misconception to break:** Giving the larger digit the larger place, so the 8 is put ahead of the 4.

### 3 · Place and place value are not the same word
**Screen:** one digit lit in the chart with the place slot and the worth slot both empty
**Does:** fills the place slot with a name and the worth slot with an amount
**Win:** both slots fill with answers of the right kind and the lit column holds the two together
**Feels:** two questions about one digit, and the answers are different kinds of thing
**Goal:** the place is a name and the place value is an amount
**Observe:** The slot headed worth would not take the word tenths, and the slot headed place would not take an amount.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- answers a place value question with the word tenths, which names the column again and never says how much. → the word the child gave stays exactly where it was dropped and the slot headed place lights, because that is the slot it belongs in. The lit column is read out twice over, once as the name written above it and once as the size of the piece it counts, with that piece drawn underneath at its real size, and the slot headed worth is left open beside the answer already given.
- writes the digit itself into the worth slot, so the six in a number is said to be worth six. → the digit is carried down out of its column into the rebuild bar as the bare number the child wrote, and the row is added with it: the total lands nowhere near the number the chart started from and is left standing beside it with the first pair that parts ringed in amber. The column is then read again with its heading lit and the piece it counts drawn under it.
**Misconception to break:** Answering a place value question with the word tenths, which names the column again and never says how much.

### 4 · Adding the worths back up
**Screen:** every digit given its worth, with the rebuild bar empty along the foot
**Does:** carries every worth down into the bar, adds them, and reads the total
**Win:** the total settles beside the number the chart started from and the two read the same
**Feels:** the worths add back to the number they came out of, exactly, with nothing left over
**Goal:** the place values of every digit add back to the number they came from
**Observe:** The worths added back to the number the chart started from, digit for digit.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- adds the digits themselves instead of what each one is worth in its own column. → the bare digits come down into the rebuild bar exactly as the child sent them, the row adds itself and the small total stands beside the number the chart started from with the two read digit for digit and the first pair that parts ringed. The worths are then carried down into a second row underneath, added the same way, and both rows are left standing together.
- leaves the digits after the point out of the sum, because they are too small to make a difference. → the worths of the whole number part alone are added in the bar and the total stands beside the number the chart started from, where it lands on a whole number and the reading parts at the dot column, which is left ringed. The three worths that were left out are then walked down into the row one at a time, each one changing the total as it lands, and the row is added again with the child's first total still standing above it.
**Misconception to break:** Adding the digits themselves instead of what each one is worth in its own column.

## Delight
The fourth phase, when the rebuild bar is added. The worths come down out of the chart one at a time along drawn paths, each one leaving its column lit behind it, and settle into the row in the order their columns stand in. The row adds itself from the right with the plus signs drawn between, and the total lifts across and stands beside a copy of the number the chart started from, where the two are read digit for digit from the left with each pair lighting as it is read. Move one digit into the wrong column and run it again: the row is built the same way, the total lands somewhere else, and the first pair that parts is left ringed in amber with nothing else marked.

**General miss line:** Count the columns again from the dot, and answer the two slots in order.

## Answer key
The place value chart carries on to the right of the ones place, past a dot, as tenths, hundredths and thousandths, and those three are the decimal places. The first digit after the point sits in the tenths place, the second in the hundredths and the third in the thousandths, and position alone decides it. The PLACE of a digit is where it sits, and it is a name, such as the tenths place. The PLACE VALUE of a digit is what it is worth there, and it is an amount, such as 5/10. Adding every digit's place value together rebuilds the number, and a total that does not come back means some digit was given the wrong column.

## Student controls
- **Drop a digit**: puts one digit in one column and refuses a second digit in the same column
- **Grow the chart**: extends the columns past the ones, through the dot, to tenths, hundredths and thousandths
- **Count from the point**: counts the columns rightwards from the dot and names each one
- **Place slot**: takes the name of the column a digit sits in
- **Worth slot**: takes the amount that digit is worth there, and refuses a name
- **Rebuild bar**: adds every worth given and stands the total beside the number the chart started from
- **Whole number bay**: asks how many tens, hundreds or thousands sit inside a long whole number
- **Card lane**: hands out digit cards and one point card, and will not let the point stand first or last
