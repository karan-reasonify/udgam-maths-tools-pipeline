## What it is
**Description:** Lift one part off the strip, split it into ten again, and again, and watch the places being made.
**Why:** Take one tenth off the strip, split it into ten, and find out what the next two places are called and where they came from.
**Look:** A strip of ten on the left, a magnifier on the right holding the piece being split, and a name ladder running down the side.
**Context:** Grade 4, and the whole system of this chapter is made on this topic. An adult sees a second and a third name being learnt. What is actually happening is one move being repeated: split by ten, and the piece you get is a tenth of the piece you started with. A child who learns the three names as a list has to remember which name goes with which position; a child who has watched the splitting happen has nothing to remember.
**For the child:** Lift one piece off the strip. Split it into ten again. Say what the new piece is called. Count the shaded cells, then count the whole grid. Write the fraction first, then the decimal.
**Teaching notes:** Do the second split with the first one still on the desk. Cut a strip into ten, then cut one of those pieces into ten again, and lay the small pieces back along the tenth they came from so that ten of them are seen to rebuild it. That one picture is the whole of this topic and the whole of the place chart that follows it. The tool's job is to keep the first split standing while the second one happens, which two sheets of paper cannot do. Count shaded grids in tens and not one square at a time, because a child counting singly loses the place and then distrusts an answer that is right. The chapter's own worked figure is a hundred grid with sixty-one cells shaded, written as 61/100 and 0.61, and it is worth reading off together before anything is shaded here: seven in the top row and six in each of the rows under it. In the words bay, make the class say the last word of the phrase aloud before writing anything down. Thousandths means a thousand underneath, and hearing that word is the whole of the step. The eight vocabulary words on the closing list are all highlighted in the body of the chapter, and five of the eight are settled here and on the topic before it, so quiz them now rather than leaving them all to the last lesson.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Splitting a tenth again
**Screen:** the strip of ten with one piece lifted and waiting in the magnifier
**Does:** splits the lifted piece into ten and counts the pieces back into the tenth
**Win:** ten pieces fill the magnifier and the ladder writes that one tenth is ten hundredths
**Feels:** the small pieces fill the piece they came out of, and there are nine more pieces of the strip still to go
**Goal:** a hundredth is one tenth split into ten, so a tenth is ten hundredths
**Observe:** The tenth kept its place on the strip while the piece cut out of it stood in the glass.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- says a hundredth must be bigger than a tenth, because a hundred is a bigger number than ten. → one tenth and one hundredth are laid on the stage side by side at their real sizes, the tenth from the strip and the hundredth from the glass, with the small one standing on top of the large one so the ten that fit can be counted. The child's answer stays in the slot, and the ladder writes the tie between the two places on its own rung without a word being said about which answer was given.
- counts the ten small pieces as making the whole strip, not the one piece they came out of. → the ten small pieces walk back to their real size and lay themselves along the gap in the strip, where they fill one piece and go no further, and the other nine pieces of the strip stay drawn beside them untouched. The whole strip is then counted along its length as ten pieces of ten, and the count is written on the ladder as it finishes.
**Misconception to break:** Thinking a hundredth is bigger than a tenth because the word hundred is the bigger word.

### 2 · One more split, and the pattern is clear
**Screen:** a single cell of the hundred grid lifted into the magnifier
**Does:** splits it again and says what the new piece will be called before the ladder writes it
**Win:** the third name lands on the ladder with its tie to the place above it
**Feels:** the same cut a third time, and the name could be said before the pieces were seen
**Goal:** a thousandth is one hundredth split into ten, and the pattern carries on
**Observe:** Ten of the small pieces laid back along the tenth filled it exactly, with none left over.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- waits for a new rule, because a third name feels like a third thing to learn rather than the same cut made again. → the ladder is walked from the top with each rung read out as the split that made it, and the same split control that made the second name is the one that makes the third. The child's answer stays in the slot and the glass replays both cuts one after the other with nothing else on the stage moving.
- names the new piece a ten hundredth, joining the count of pieces to the name of the piece they came from. → the ten new pieces are counted back into the hundredth they came out of, and then the whole is counted along the ladder rung by rung: ten tenths, a hundred hundredths, and a thousand of these. The name lands on the ladder from that count, and the child's name stays written beside it on the same rung so the two can be read together.
**Misconception to break:** Expecting a new rule for the third place instead of the same split made again.

### 3 · Reading a shaded grid
**Screen:** a ten by ten grid with some cells shaded and both writing slots empty
**Does:** counts the shaded cells, writes the fraction, then writes the decimal
**Win:** both slots fill and the shaded cells light in tens as the count is read back
**Feels:** the bottom number is the whole grid and the top number is what is coloured, and counting in tens keeps both of them
**Goal:** a shaded figure is read by counting the shaded parts and the total parts
**Observe:** The third name arrived without a thousand pieces ever being drawn.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- counts the shaded squares and writes them over ten, because the grid has ten rows. → the whole grid is counted out in rows of ten with the running count climbing at the top left until it reaches a hundred, and the count stays written under the grid. The child's fraction stays in its slot, a strip of ten is drawn beside the grid with the child's fraction shaded on it, and the two pictures stand together with plainly different amounts coloured in.
- counts the shaded squares one at a time and loses the place, then distrusts a count that was right. → the grid is counted again in rows, each full row lighting as a ten with the tens written along the left edge and the part row counted on at the end, and the running count at the top left climbs in tens and then in ones. The child's number stays in the slot the whole time and the count is not compared with it or marked against it.
**Misconception to break:** Counting the shaded squares and writing them over ten because the grid has ten rows.

### 4 · From words straight to a decimal fraction
**Screen:** an amount said in words, with the words bay open and its last word waiting
**Does:** picks out the last word, writes the denominator from it, then writes the top number
**Win:** the denominator lands from the lit word and the fraction settles above it
**Feels:** the last word is not the tail of the sentence, it is the word that says how big the pieces are
**Goal:** the last word of a spoken amount names the denominator
**Observe:** Changing the last word of the spoken amount changed the bottom number and nothing else.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes the number heard over ten, whatever word the amount happened to end on. → the last word of the spoken amount is lit in the words bay and read out on its own, and the ladder lights the rung that word names. The child's fraction stays written where it was put, the bottom box is opened again beside it, and the amount is said once more with the last word held.
- hears the word hundred inside two hundred five thousandths and writes a hundred underneath. → the phrase is laid out word by word along the words bay and the words that build the top number are lit together as one group, with the last word left lit on its own at the end. The child's denominator stays in its box, the two readings are stood one above the other, and the grid and the ladder are asked which of the two sizes of piece is being counted.
**Misconception to break:** Writing the number heard over ten whatever the last word happens to say.

## Delight
The first phase, at the moment the split lands. The piece rides up into the glass and the gap it left along the strip stays open behind it, and the cut runs through it in one movement into ten pieces that are plainly of one size. Drag them back out and they walk down to their real size on the way, and lay themselves along the gap one at a time; the tenth one closes it and the strip is whole again, with the ten small pieces still drawn inside the space of the one they came from. Lift and split the same piece again and it all runs once more, as often as a finger likes, and the voice says nothing while it happens.

**General miss line:** Put the pieces back beside the one they came from and count them into it again.

## Answer key
One whole split into a hundred equal parts gives hundredths, and a hundredth is what you get by splitting one tenth into ten. One whole split into a thousand equal parts gives thousandths, and a thousandth is one hundredth split into ten. So one tenth is ten hundredths and one hundredth is ten thousandths, and every place is a tenth of the place on its left. To read a shaded figure, count the shaded parts for the top number and the total parts for the bottom one. When an amount is said in words, the last word names the denominator: hundredths means a hundred underneath and thousandths means a thousand.

## Student controls
- **Lift a part**: takes one piece off the strip and carries it into the magnifier
- **Split by ten**: cuts whatever is in the magnifier into ten equal pieces
- **Build the grid**: lays the hundred pieces out as a ten by ten grid of the whole
- **Name ladder**: writes each new place as it is made, with its tie to the place above
- **Shade cells**: colours cells by tapping, or a whole row by dragging
- **Counting bay**: runs the count of shaded cells at the top left of the canvas
- **Words bay**: hands over an amount in words and lights the word that names the denominator
- **Word pad**: holds the chapter's eight highlighted terms for a partner to quiz from
