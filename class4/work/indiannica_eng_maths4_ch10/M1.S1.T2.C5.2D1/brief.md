## What it is
**Description:** Find the group that repeats, or the move made at every step, and carry a picture pattern on until it comes round again.
**Why:** Find the group that repeats, or the move made at every step, and carry a picture pattern on until it comes round to the start again.
**Look:** A long strip of cells with the last few empty, a turn dial under it, and a ring of numbered places at one side of the stage.
**Context:** Grade 4, the first working topic of the patterns chapter, and the place where the chapter's whole idea is first made to do some work. An adult sees shapes being copied into blanks. What is actually being settled is that a pattern is a start and a rule, and that the rule is found by looking for what stays the same from one step to the next: a group that repeats, a turn of the same size, one side fewer. Every row of the recap's matching question alternates two shapes, so a child can fill each blank by copying the cell two places back without ever finding a group, and that habit fails on the first pattern with a group of three.
**For the child:** Find the shapes that come round again. Put a bracket over them. Turn the arrow the same way each time. Count the sides of each shape. After the last number, go round to the first.
**Teaching notes:** Make one repeating row with real things on a desk before the tool is opened, beads or caps or pencils, and ask for the smallest group that repeats rather than for the next thing. Give a group of three early. Every row of the recap's matching question alternates two shapes, and a child can fill all four blanks by copying the cell two places back without having found anything, so a group of three is the first place the habit is tested. Do the quarter turn with a paper arrow held flat on the desk and turned the way a clock's hands move, and count the turns aloud until four brings it home. The row of shapes that loses a side is worth a question the page does not ask: why does it start again after the triangle? Because a closed shape with straight sides needs at least three. The closing task on the last page is an order that goes round, four performers in the order of their numbers starting from a number drawn by lot. Name nobody; call each performer by their number. Say out loud that one draw sets the start for both teams in an event, because the page implies it and never states it, and a child who draws again for the second team fills a different table.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · The group that repeats
**Screen:** a strip of shapes with its last two cells empty and the bracket waiting
**Does:** draws a bracket over the group they think repeats, watches its copies light, then fills the blanks
**Win:** every copy of the group lights along the strip and the blanks fill with the matching shapes
**Feels:** the row is one group laid down again and again, and a blank is only the next place in the group
**Goal:** the smallest group that repeats fills every blank in a row
**Observe:** The copies lit all along the strip only when the bracket covered the smallest group that repeats.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- copies the cell two places back into every blank, which works while two shapes take turns and finds no group at all. → the child's shapes stay in the blanks, and a bracket is drawn over the two cells they were copied from. Copy light runs that pair along the strip. On a group of three the copies stop matching at the third cell, which is left unlit and ringed in reddish purple. A bracket over three cells is then offered beside it, and every copy of that one lights.
- brackets a run one cell too long, taking in the start of the next copy, and fills the blank from it. → copy light runs the long bracket along the strip, and each copy drifts one cell further until one does not match. That copy is left unlit with its breaking cell ringed. The child's bracket stays drawn, and it is then shortened one cell at a time, with the copies lit again after each.
**Misconception to break:** Copying the last bead, which gives blue for the first place, because no group was found.

### 2 · A quarter turn at every step
**Screen:** a row of arrows, each pointing a different way, with the dial under the first empty cell
**Does:** turns the dial until the new arrow repeats the move between the cells before it
**Win:** the arrow settles and the turn ring closes when four quarter turns bring it home
**Feels:** the same small turn every time, and after four of them the arrow is back where it began
**Goal:** the same quarter turn is made at every step, and four of them make a full turn
**Observe:** Four quarter turns closed the ring and brought the arrow back to where it began.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- turns the arrow against a clock's hands, so a row that went up, right, down is carried on the other way round. → the dial replays the move between the last two cells one quarter at a time, drawing it as a vermilion arc the way a clock's hands go. The child's arrow stays in its cell with its own arc drawn beside it, running the other way. The two arcs stand together, and nothing is marked.
- makes a half turn at every step, so up is followed by down and the arrow never points right or left. → the turn ring shows the child's move as two quarters filled, where the row made one. The dial then turns from the last cell one quarter at a time, and the arrow lands on a direction the child's row skipped. The child's arrow and the dial's stand side by side.
**Misconception to break:** Turning the arrow the other way round, or by half a turn, and so skipping a direction.

### 3 · One side fewer, then start again
**Screen:** a row of shapes with their side counts written under them and one cell empty
**Does:** reads the side counts, then chooses the shape for the empty cell
**Win:** the next shape lands and its count starts the row of numbers again from the top
**Feels:** the counts go down one at a time until they cannot go any lower, and then the row starts over
**Goal:** a row can lose one side at each step and then start again from its first shape
**Observe:** The side counts went down to three and then went back to the first count.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- looks for a shape with two sides after the triangle, and draws two straight lines meeting at a point. → the child's two lines are drawn in the empty cell as they were made. The side counter writes two under them and rings the open end in reddish purple, where no side closes the shape. The strip then shows the row's first shape again after the triangle, and the counts under it start again from the top.
- reads the first shape coming back after the triangle as a slip in the row, and carries on losing sides. → the bracket is drawn over the run from the first shape to the triangle, and copy light lights the second copy, starting at the shape that came back. The side counts under the row are drawn as two runs, the second starting under that shape. The child's answer stays in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Looking for a shape with two sides after the triangle, when a closed shape with straight sides needs at least three.

### 4 · After the last comes the first
**Screen:** numbered places round a ring, with the draw waiting and an empty order line beside it
**Does:** draws the starting number, then reads the order off the ring into the line
**Win:** the line fills round the ring and an arc joins the last number back to the first
**Feels:** the order has a start and no end, and the draw decides where the start is
**Goal:** an order that goes round comes back to the first number after the last
**Observe:** The order line bent round into a ring, and the last number sat beside the first.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- stops the order at the last number, leaving out every player whose number comes before the one drawn. → the order line keeps the child's numbers and draws the places still to fill as empty outlined boxes. The ring is then walked from the drawn place, each number lighting as it is passed. The walk goes past the last number and on round to the first, and the arc joining them is drawn in vermilion.
- writes the drawn number first and then the rest in counting order from one, so a draw of four out of five gives four, one, two, three, five. → the ring is walked from the drawn place, and each player's neighbour on the ring is lit beside them. The child's line stands under the order the ring gives. The one place where a neighbour changed, four and then one, is ringed in reddish purple.
- makes a new draw for the second team, so the two teams start from different numbers in one event. → two rings stand side by side and read one draw together, and both order lines fill from the same start. The child's second order stays beside its ring, and the drum shows the single number drawn for the event.
**Misconception to break:** Stopping after 5 and leaving out the children whose numbers come before the one drawn.

## Delight
The fourth phase, when the last place of the order line fills. The line lifts off and bends itself round into a ring, and its two ends meet, so the last number sits beside the first. The drawn start carries a small flag. Drag the flag to any other number and the ring unrolls into a new line from there, with every neighbour kept. It runs as often as a finger likes, nothing is marked either way, and the voice says nothing at all while it happens.

**General miss line:** Look again at what stays the same from one cell to the next.

## Answer key
A pattern is continued by finding its rule and using it at every step. In a repeating pattern the rule is the smallest group that repeats, and each blank takes the cell one whole group before it. In a turning pattern the rule is the move made at every step; a quarter turn clockwise each time brings the shape back to where it began after four turns. In a row that loses one side at each step, the shapes go down to three sides and then start again, because a closed shape with straight sides needs at least three. In an order that goes round, whoever is drawn goes first and the rest follow in the order of their numbers, and after the last number comes the first again. When one draw sets the start for two teams, both are read from the same number.

## Student controls
- **Bracket**: draws a bracket over the cells the child thinks repeat
- **Copy light**: lights every copy of the bracketed group along the strip
- **Turn dial**: turns an arrow or a dot one quarter turn clockwise at a time
- **Turn ring**: counts the quarter turns made and closes at four
- **Side counter**: writes how many straight sides each shape has under its cell
- **Ring bay**: lays a numbered order round a circle
- **Draw**: picks the number that goes first
- **Two rings**: sets one draw for two teams at once

**Key terms:** pattern, repeat, quarter turn, clockwise
A picture pattern is continued by finding the group that repeats, or the change made at every step, and seeing where it comes back to the start. Find the smallest group that repeats, or the move made at every step, and use it again. Four quarter turns bring an arrow back to where it started. After the last shape in a row, or the last number in a ring, the pattern starts again from the first. A square. The two shapes take turns, so the pair repeats. Find the smallest group that repeats, and every blank fills itself. Up again. Each step is a quarter turn, the way a clock's hands move. Four quarter turns make a full turn, so the arrow comes back to the start. A dot moving round a shape works the same way. The page starts again with six sides. A closed shape with straight sides needs at least three, so the row cannot go any lower. It goes back to the start and repeats. Player two. The order goes round like a circle: three, four, one, two. Wherever it starts, each player is still followed by the same neighbour.
