## What it is
**Description:** Build a number tower up from its bottom row by adding or by multiplying, then check every box against one operation.
**Why:** Build a number tower up from the bottom by adding or by multiplying, and check every box to decide whether a tower is correct.
**Look:** A tower of boxes, each resting on two below, with an operation switch at its foot and a check walk that climbs it box by box.
**Context:** Grade 4. An adult sees towers of sums and products. What is actually being settled is what makes a tower correct. The page shows one tower that adds all the way up and one that multiplies all the way up, and then asks which of six towers are correct without ever saying that a tower keeps to one operation. Mixed box by box, every one of the six printed towers works. The tool states the rule the page's own two towers follow, one operation for every box, and makes checking every box against it the job.
**For the child:** Choose adding or multiplying first. Tap an empty box. Use the two boxes under it. Write what they make. Check every box, from the bottom row up. Keep one sign for every box.
**Teaching notes:** Build one adding tower and one multiplying tower out of paper cups and sticky notes before the tool is opened, and make the class say, for each box, the two boxes it rests on. Then say the rule out loud, and say where it comes from. The page's own two towers each keep to one operation all the way up, and a tower is correct when one operation works for every box. The page never states it. It shows its two towers and then says towers can be made with as many numbers as we like, which a careful child can read as allowing both signs in one tower. Mixed box by box, every one of the six printed towers in Exercise 10.1 works, and two of them even keep to one operation within each row, one multiplying below and adding on top and the other the other way round. So without the rule the question has no answer, and with it the answer is the three towers that keep one sign all the way up. Check box by box from the bottom, and name the two boxes being tested each time. A child who checks only the top box will pass a tower whose top happens to add up.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Two neighbours make the box above
**Screen:** an adding tower with its bottom row filled and every box above it empty
**Does:** taps an empty box, adds the two boxes it rests on, and works up one row at a time
**Win:** each row fills in turn and the top box lands last
**Feels:** every box stands on its own two, and the tower is built one row at a time from the bottom
**Goal:** each box is made from the two boxes side by side beneath it
**Observe:** Each box lit exactly two boxes when it was tapped, and never the box beside it.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- adds the whole bottom row and writes that total in the top box, thirty for six, nine, four and eleven. → the top box is tapped and lights the two boxes it rests on, not the bottom row. The child's thirty stays written beside the tower. The rows are then built up one at a time with the working written in the ledger, and the top lands on fifty-six.
- fills a box from the wrong pair, sliding one box along, so the box over nine and four gets six add nine again. → tapping the box lights the two it rests on, nine and four, and the child's fifteen is handed back with those two still lit. The struts from nine and four up to the box light as well, and the ledger writes nine add four.
**Misconception to break:** Adding a box to the box beside it in the same row, instead of using the two it rests on.

### 2 · A tower that multiplies
**Screen:** the same tower with the switch turned to multiplying and a new bottom row filled
**Does:** multiplies each pair of neighbours and fills the tower up to the top
**Win:** the times sign shows between every pair and the top box fills
**Feels:** the same tower and the same pairs, and only the sign in the gaps has changed
**Goal:** a multiplying tower has the same shape and multiplies in every box
**Observe:** The multiplying tower had the same shape and the same struts, and only the sign in every gap changed.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- adds the bottom pair out of habit, writing four above three and one, and multiplies only higher up. → the box slot hands the four back with the times sign drawn between three and one, and the sign rail draws the times sign again in every gap of every row. The ledger writes three times one. The child's four stays beside the box.
- writes one above three and one, taking times one to leave only the one. → the ledger lays out three groups of one counter each, and they are counted aloud. The child's one stays beside the box, and the count stands next to it.
**Misconception to break:** Adding in the first row out of habit and then multiplying higher up.

### 3 · Checking a tower box by box
**Screen:** a tower that arrives already filled, with one box that does not follow
**Does:** runs the check walk box by box and stops on the box that breaks the rule
**Win:** the walk lights each box in turn and one box keeps its light, its working written beside it
**Feels:** one box at a time, the two below it named every time, and the top box last of all
**Goal:** a tower is tested by checking each box against the two below it, one at a time
**Observe:** The walk left one box lit after passing it, while the top box above it still added up.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- checks only the top box, finds that eight add ten is eighteen, and calls the whole tower correct without testing each box. → the check walk climbs from the bottom row and tests each box in turn, writing its working in the ledger. The box holding ten keeps its reddish purple light, because two add five is seven. The top box's working is written too, and it adds, so both stand together.
- tests every tower by adding, so a tower that multiplies all the way up is called broken at its first box. → the walk with adding lights the first box, because two add three is five, not six. Other operation then runs the same walk with the times sign, and every box follows. Both walks stay written side by side in the ledger.
**Misconception to break:** Checking only the top box, which adds up, and calling the whole tower right.

### 4 · One operation all the way up
**Screen:** a tower that multiplies in one row and adds in the next, with both walks waiting
**Does:** walks the tower with adding, then with multiplying, and decides whether either works all the way up
**Win:** each walk runs the whole tower, and the boxes its sign cannot make stay lit
**Feels:** one sign, set once at the foot, and every box in the tower answering to it
**Goal:** a tower is correct only when one operation works for every box, from the bottom row to the top
**Observe:** On the tower that switched signs, each walk left a different box lit, and neither walk ran clean.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- accepts a tower whose middle row multiplies and whose top adds, because each box works with some operation. → both walks run the whole tower. With adding, five add one is six, not five, and that box stays lit. With multiplying, five times three is fifteen, not eight, and the top stays lit. Neither walk runs clean, and both stay in the ledger.
- calls the tower page's multiplying tower mixed, because two and two make four by adding and by multiplying. → both walks run on that tower, and the box holding four stays unlit in both, because it follows either sign. The adding walk lights box after box, starting beside it, where two add one is three, not two. The multiplying walk runs clean all the way to sixty-four at the top.
**Misconception to break:** Accepting the tower because each box works with some operation, when it switches from multiplying to adding.

## Delight
The first phase, once the top box has landed. Any number in the bottom row can then be tapped and changed, and the tower rebuilds itself upward from it. Only the boxes resting on that number, directly or through the rows between, make themselves again, one row at a time; every box it cannot reach stays still. The top box lands last with its new number. It runs as often as a finger likes, nothing is marked, and the voice says nothing while it happens.

**General miss line:** Tap the box again, and look at the two boxes holding it up.

## Answer key
Each box in a number tower rests on two boxes side by side, and it is made from those two. In an adding tower it is their sum; in a multiplying tower it is their product. The page's two towers each keep to one operation all the way up, so a tower is correct when one operation works for every box, from the bottom row to the top. To check a tower, test each box against the two below it, one at a time, with the same operation throughout. One box that does not follow makes the tower not correct, even when every other box does, and a tower that adds in one row and multiplies in the next fits neither kind. A missing box is found from the boxes it rests on or from the box it helps to make.

## Student controls
- **Operation switch**: sets the tower to adding or to multiplying before any box is filled
- **Sign rail**: draws the chosen sign between every pair of neighbours in every row
- **Rest lights**: lights the two boxes a box rests on when it is tapped
- **Box slot**: takes the number for the lit box and keeps it only if the operation makes it
- **Check walk**: tests a filled tower box by box from the bottom row up
- **Other operation**: runs the same walk with the other sign
- **Missing box**: leaves a box blank at any level to be found from the boxes around it
- **Own bottom row**: takes the child's own numbers for the bottom row
