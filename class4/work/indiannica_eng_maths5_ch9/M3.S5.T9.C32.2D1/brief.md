## What it is
**Description:** Sort other units by what they measure, compare each with a metric unit, estimate by adding a chart number a few times, and say about with every answer.
**Why:** Know what each other unit measures, compare it with a metric unit using the chart, estimate a measure by adding a chart number a few times, and say about with every answer.
**Look:** Seven chart cards along the top, three bins, pairs of bars laid side by side, stacks of one card, and an About stamp.
**Context:** Grade 5, the page of other units in everyday use. An adult sees a conversion chart. What the page is doing is meeting units used elsewhere, and the one thing a child must carry away is that every answer made from a rounded chart is about, not exact.
**For the child:** Sort the units. Lay a card's bars side by side. Stack a card and add. Stamp every answer About.
**Teaching notes:** Say about with every answer made from the chart: its numbers are rounded. Where a question needs a few lots of a chart number, add them rather than multiply a decimal, which has not been taught. Compare sizes before any number: a mile is a little longer than one and a half kilometres, and a kilogram is a little more than two pounds. Leave out the chart's line from centimetres to feet, which is cut short.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Units used elsewhere
**Screen:** seven unit cards and three bins labelled length, weight and capacity
**Does:** drags each unit card into the bin of what it measures
**Win:** every card sits in its bin, and the three bins light together
**Feels:** the three bins lighting together once every card is home
**Goal:** miles, yards, feet and inches measure length; pounds and ounces measure weight; gallons measure capacity
**Observe:** Every card went into one of only three bins.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- drops the gallon card into the weight bin → the card stays in the weight bin, and the capacity bin beside it is lit amber with a drawn jug in ink above it
- drops the ounce card into the capacity bin → the card stays where it was put, and the weight bin is lit amber with a drawn balance in ink above it
**Misconception to break:** Thinking every unit on the chart measures length, when the chart mixes units of length, weight and capacity.

### 2 · Reading the chart
**Screen:** the chart cards along the top and two empty bar rails
**Does:** taps a card and compares the two bars it lays side by side
**Win:** the longer bar glows, and the chart number is read with the word about
**Feels:** two bars sliding out side by side and one plainly longer
**Goal:** read the chart's rounded number to tell whether a unit is bigger or smaller than a metric unit
**Observe:** On some cards the other unit's bar was longer than the metric bar, and on some it was shorter.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- answers that the yard is longer than the metre, because a yard sounds like a big open space → the two bars are laid side by side on the rails, and the longer bar glows while the child's answer stays
- answers that the pound is heavier than the kilogram, because the number beside the pound on the kilogram card is bigger → the two bars are laid side by side, the kilogram's bar plainly longer, and the card's words are read aloud in the caption
**Misconception to break:** Taking the smaller unit to be the gallon because the word is unfamiliar, without reading the chart card.

### 3 · Adding the chart's numbers
**Screen:** one chart card and a Stack button, with a question about a few of that unit
**Does:** stacks the card as many times as asked and adds the metric numbers
**Win:** the stack meets the given length, and the sum turns green
**Feels:** copies of one card stacking up and their numbers adding into one estimate
**Goal:** for a few lots of a unit, add the chart number that many times
**Observe:** Stacking a card a few times and adding its numbers gave the estimate.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- adds the chart number one time too few → the child's answer is drawn as a bar beside the stacked copies, the two lengths seen apart, and the missing copy's place is outlined amber at the end of the stack
- lines up the last digits of the chart numbers instead of their points when adding → the child's answer is drawn as a bar beside the stacked copies, and the numbers under the bars are shown again with their points in one line
**Misconception to break:** Adding the 3 to the chart number once, instead of adding the chart number three times.

### 4 · About, not exact
**Screen:** an estimate waiting in the answer slot and the About stamp beside it
**Does:** builds the estimate, presses the About stamp onto it, and says why
**Win:** the About stamp lands, and the answer turns green
**Feels:** the About stamp landing and the answer turning green
**Goal:** every answer made from the chart is about, not exact, because its numbers are rounded
**Observe:** No answer could be taken until the About stamp was on it.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- offers the answer without pressing the About stamp onto it → the answer waits in the slot uncoloured, and the About stamp beside it is lit amber
- stamps About on a card's metric number and leaves the answer unstamped → the stamp mark stays where it landed, and the answer in the slot is lit amber, waiting for its own stamp
**Misconception to break:** Saying the parcel is exactly 6.6 pounds, when the chart's 2.2 is itself rounded.

## Textbook (this topic)
**Pages:** 144
**Key terms:** mile, yard, foot, inch, gallon, pound, ounce, rounded, about
Miles, yards, feet, inches, gallons, pounds and ounces are linked to metric units by a rounded chart, so every answer made from it is about, not exact. Some places also use other units in daily life. Miles, yards, feet and inches measure length. Pounds and ounces measure weight, and gallons measure capacity. A chart links them to metric units, and its numbers are rounded. Yes, a mile is a little longer than one and a half kilometres. The chart also gives 1 kg as about 2.2 pounds. So a kilogram is a little more than 2 pounds. The chart gives 1 inch as about 2.54 cm. Add three of them: 2.54 + 2.54 + 2.54 = 7.62. So the shell is about 7.62 cm long. The chart's numbers are rounded, so each answer made from them is close, not exact. The chart gives 1 yard as about 0.914 m. 0.914 + 0.914 = 1.828, so 2 yards are about 1.828 m.
