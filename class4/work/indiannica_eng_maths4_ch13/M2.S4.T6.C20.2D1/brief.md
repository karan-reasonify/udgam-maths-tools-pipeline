## What it is
**Description:** Multiply a price by a count, paise first, carrying each hundred paise as one rupee and writing the paise left after the dot, keep the count free of any dot, and follow a cost through to the change or the money left.
**Why:** Multiply a price by a count, paise first, put the dot in its place, and follow a cost through to the change or the money left.
**Look:** A multiplying frame in the middle of the canvas under a row of the things being bought, a carry circle over the rupees, and a two-step card with a change bench at the right.
**Context:** Grade 4, the multiplying half of the chapter's last section. An adult sees a price multiplied, which looks like long multiplication with a dot in it. What is actually being settled is where the dot goes. The page says to put it at the appropriate place and never says where, and its only multiplication worked in columns comes out to whole rupees. The same example prints its multiplier with a dot in front of it, under the paise. The multiplier counts things, so it has no dot and no paise, and this tool has nowhere to put one.
**For the child:** Multiply the paise first. Carry each hundred paise as one rupee. Write the paise left after the dot. The count has no dot. Then find the change or the money left.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Paise first, then rupees
**Screen:** a price on the top line, a count on the second, and the carry circle empty
**Does:** multiplies the paise by the count, rolls each hundred into the carry circle, then multiplies the rupees and adds the carry
**Win:** the hundreds roll up into the carry circle, and the rupees and the carry add into the cost
**Feels:** the paise of every thing bought pile up, and every full hundred of them becomes a rupee
**Goal:** money is multiplied paise first, each hundred paise carried as one rupee, and then the rupees
**Observe:** The paise times the count rolled up into the carry circle a hundred at a time, and the rupees took the carry.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- multiplies the rupees and the paise apart and writes Rs 138.300, leaving the carried rupees in the paise. → the paise box shows three hundred paise, and three full strips fill one after another, each rolling up into a rupee and dropping into the carry circle, which reads three. Two zeros go down after the dot in the paise column, the rupees product takes the three, and the child's Rs 138.300 stays in the slot beside the new cost.
- multiplies the rupees and brings the fifty paise down unchanged, writing Rs 138.50, as if only the rupees were bought six times. → the six notebooks in the row light one at a time, and a copy of the price, rupees and paise together, slides down under each. The six lots of fifty paise are counted together in the paise box, three hundred paise, and roll up into three rupees in the carry circle. The rupees product takes them, and the child's Rs 138.50 stays in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Multiplying the rupees and the paise apart and writing Rs 138.300, with the carried rupees left in the paise.

### 2 · The number multiplied by is a count
**Screen:** a frame where the count stands with a dot in front of it under the paise, and a row of the things above
**Does:** counts the things one by one, lifts the count out of the paise column, and sets it in the count box with no dot
**Win:** the count settles in its box with no dot, and the row of things lights one by one to match it
**Feels:** the number multiplied by is how many things, and things come whole
**Goal:** the number multiplied by counts things, so it is written without a dot and has no paise
**Observe:** Once the count was lifted into its own box and lost its dot, the row of things lit one by one to match it.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- copies a layout that puts a dot in front of the nine under the paise, and reads the nine as nine paise. → the nine pens in the row stay dark while the count stands under the paise. The count is then lifted out: its dot falls away, it settles in the sky blue count box beside the times sign, and the pens light one at a time up to nine. The price is multiplied by the count, and the child's reading stays written in the slot.
- multiplies only the digits standing over the count, the fifty paise, carries four rupees and writes Rs 18.50, leaving the rupees unmultiplied. → the count moves into its box beside the times sign, and a near-black bracket reaches from the box across both columns of the price. The paise are multiplied, four hundred and fifty, and the rupees too, one hundred and twenty-six, and with the four carried the cost comes to Rs 130.50. The child's Rs 18.50 stays in the slot beside it.
**Misconception to break:** Copying a layout that puts a dot in front of the count under the paise, and reading the 9 as 9 paise.

### 3 · Paise left over go after the dot
**Screen:** a price whose paise times the count is not a whole number of hundreds, with the dot waiting in the answer line
**Does:** multiplies the paise, carries the whole rupees, writes the paise left after the dot, then finishes the rupees
**Win:** the paise left land as two digits after the dot, and the carried rupees join the rupees
**Feels:** whatever the hundreds leave behind is still paise, and it still takes two places
**Goal:** when the paise do not come to whole rupees, the paise left are written as two digits after the dot
**Observe:** When the paise product was not a whole number of hundreds, what was left went after the dot as two digits.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes the five paise left from a hundred and five as one digit after the dot, Rs 49.5 for Rs 49.05. → the child's Rs 49.5 is read back on the answer line with the second place after the dot empty, which says fifty paise, and fifty cells shade on a strip. A zero is then set in the first place after the dot and the five moves to the second, and the shading falls back to five cells. Both answers stand side by side, the child's still in the slot.
- forgets the carried rupee and writes Rs 168.40 for four cups at Rs 42.35. → the carry circle over the rupees glows amber with its one still in it, and the rupees product, one hundred and sixty-eight, is lit beside it. The circle's rupee travels down into the product, making one hundred and sixty-nine, and the child's Rs 168.40 stays in the slot beside the new cost.
**Misconception to break:** Writing all of 140 after the dot, which gives Rs 168.140, or writing 4 after the dot instead of 40.

### 4 · The cost, then the change
**Screen:** a two-step card with the money held, a price and a count, and the answer slot asking for the money left
**Does:** finds the cost by multiplying, sets it under the money held, takes it away, and fills the answer slot
**Win:** the change or the money left lands in the answer slot, and the cost stays in its box as a step
**Feels:** the cost is on the way to the answer, not the answer itself
**Goal:** a two-step question finds the cost first and then the change or the money left, which is the answer
**Observe:** The cost stayed in its box as a step while the answer slot took the money left.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- stops at the cost, Rs 593.25, when the question asks how much money is left. → the child's number is set in the cost box on the bench, where it matches, and the answer slot's label, money left, lights in reddish purple. The cost is then set under Rs 700 on the change bench, a rupee is broken into a hundred paise because there are zero paise on top, and the money left is taken away. The child's Rs 593.25 stays in the slot beside the cost box.
- takes the cost from Rs 700 without breaking a rupee, bringing the twenty-five paise straight down, and writes Rs 107.25. → the paise cell stays shut over the grey zeros of the Rs 700, and the child's twenty-five is written small beside it. One rupee is broken into a hundred paise, seven hundred struck through and six hundred and ninety-nine written above it, and a hundred less twenty-five gives seventy-five. The rupees then take away, and the child's Rs 107.25 stays in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Stopping at the cost, Rs 593.25, when the question asks for the money left.

## Delight
The second phase, the moment the count is lifted out of the paise column. Its dot falls away and rolls off the frame, the count settles into its own sky blue box beside the times sign, and the things in the row light one at a time. As each one lights, a copy of the price card slides down beside the frame, until the copies stand in a stack, one for each thing, and the stack adds up to the same cost the frame gives by multiplying. It can be done as often as a finger likes, nothing is marked, and the voice says nothing at all while it happens.

**General miss line:** Multiply the paise again, and watch where each hundred goes.

## Answer key
When each thing costs the same, the cost of several is the price of one multiplied by the number of things. Multiply the paise first. The paise times the count is a number of paise; each hundred of them is carried as one rupee, and the paise left are written as two digits after the dot. Then multiply the rupees and add the rupees carried. The number multiplied by counts things, so it has no dot and no paise, and it is written beside the times sign, outside the rupees and paise columns. Some questions take two steps: find the cost, then take it from the money held or paid, breaking one rupee into a hundred paise if the paise on top are too few. The answer is the one the question asks for, the change or the money left, and the cost is a step.

## Student controls
- **Price line**: holds the price of one thing in a rupees column and a paise column
- **Thing row**: draws the things along the top and lights one for each counted
- **Counter**: sets the number of things, whole things only, with no dot key
- **Multiply**: works the paise column first, then the rupees
- **Paise box**: shows the paise times the count before anything is carried
- **Carry circle**: takes one rupee for each hundred paise
- **Two-step card**: holds the money held or paid in a two-step question
- **Change bench**: takes the cost from that money in columns
