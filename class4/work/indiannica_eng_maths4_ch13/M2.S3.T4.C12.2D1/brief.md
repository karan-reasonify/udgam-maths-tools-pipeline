## What it is
**Description:** Set prices on a bill with every dot in one line, carry each hundred paise as one rupee, fill a whole-rupee price's paise column with two zeros, and total the bill at its foot.
**Why:** Put prices on a bill with every dot in one line, carry each hundred paise as one rupee, and total the bill at its foot.
**Look:** A bill slip down the middle of the canvas with its dotted line between rupees and paise, a shop shelf of price cards beside it, and a carry circle over the rupees column.
**Context:** Grade 4, the adding half of the chapter's middle section. An adult sees a shop bill added up, which looks like ordinary column addition. What is actually being settled is where each digit goes: the dots in one line, so rupees stand under rupees and paise under paise, and a hundred paise carried as one rupee. The first summary box says two zeros go after the dot whenever there are no paise, and the later box puts them in the paise column; the pages themselves write whole amounts with no dot. This tool uses the later form, so two zeros appear only in column working, where they keep a whole-rupee price's rupees in the rupees column.
**For the child:** Put each price on its own line with the dots in one line. Add the paise first. Carry each hundred paise as one rupee. Write the total at the foot.
**Teaching notes:** Set up a class shop with priced packets and write one bill by hand before the tool is opened, one thing to a line and the dots under each other. Then use the tool for the two moves that slip on paper: every dot on one line, and each hundred paise rolled up into the rupees as one rupee. Say what a bill is, because the chapter highlights the word and never says: a bill lists the things bought and their prices, with the total at the foot. The chapter gives the rule for amounts with no paise twice. The first box says two zeros are written after the dot, and the later box puts them in the paise column. The pages themselves write forty-three rupees and three thousand rupees in running text with no dot, so teach the later form: two zeros belong in column working, where they keep a whole-rupee price's rupees in the rupees column. One printed bill has its words run together with no spaces, so read it aloud before anyone copies it.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Rupees under rupees, paise under paise
**Screen:** an empty bill slip with its dotted line, and two price cards on the shelf
**Does:** drags each price onto a line so its dot lands on the dotted line, then adds the paise column and the rupees column
**Win:** the dots stand in one straight line, and each column adds on its own
**Feels:** once every dot sits on one line, each column holds only one kind of money
**Goal:** amounts are added in columns with the dots in one straight line, so rupees sit under rupees and paise under paise
**Observe:** Every card snapped with its dot on the dotted line, and each column added on its own.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- starts both prices at the left edge, so the five of fifty-two stands under the three of three hundred and fourteen and the dots do not line up. → the Rs 52.60 card springs back from where the child set it, and the dotted line lights, with a pale outline left standing where the card was, its dot one place left of the line. The card then snaps in with its dot on the line, fifty-two under the fourteen of three hundred and fourteen, and the outline stays standing beside it.
- adds every column correctly and writes the total with no dot, 36685, which reads as thirty-six thousand six hundred and eighty-five rupees. → the dotted line runs on down through the answer line and lights there, and the child's digits are laid across it: the eight and the five fall to the right of the line in green, and three hundred and sixty-six stands to its left in deep blue. The vermilion dot drops onto the line between them, and the child's number stays in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Starting both amounts at the left edge, so the 5 of 52 stands under the 3 of 314 and the dots do not line up.

### 2 · A hundred paise carry one rupee
**Screen:** two prices on the slip whose paise add to more than a hundred, with the carry circle empty
**Does:** adds the paise, sees the sum pass a hundred, carries one rupee, writes the rest, and adds the rupees with the carry
**Win:** a hundred paise roll up into the carry circle as one rupee, and the paise column shows only what is left
**Feels:** a hundred paise are too many for the paise column, so they go and join the rupees
**Goal:** when the paise add to a hundred or more, each hundred is carried as one rupee and only the paise left are written
**Observe:** Each time the paise passed a hundred, a full strip rolled into the carry circle as one rupee.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes all of one hundred and forty in the paise column and gets Rs 175.140. → the paise box shows one hundred and forty paise, and a hundred of its cells gather into a full strip that rolls up into one rupee and drops into the carry circle, leaving forty below. The child's three places after the dot are laid on the answer line, and the third has no place to stand. The rupees are added again with the carry, and the child's answer stays in the slot.
- carries the rupee from one hundred and forty paise but forgets to add it, and writes Rs 175.40. → the carry circle over the rupees column glows amber with its one still in it, and the rupees column is added again with the circle's rupee travelling down into the sum: one hundred and twenty-eight and forty-seven make one hundred and seventy-five, and one more makes one hundred and seventy-six. The child's answer stays in the slot beside it.
**Misconception to break:** Writing all of 140 in the paise column, which gives Rs 175.140, a price with three places after the dot.

### 3 · Two zeros when there are no paise
**Screen:** a price with paise already on the slip, and a whole-rupee price card waiting on the shelf
**Does:** places the whole-rupee price, fills its paise column with two zeros, and adds
**Win:** two zeros fill the paise column, the rupees stay in line, and the sum comes out right
**Feels:** the grey zeros hold the paise column open, so the rupees cannot slip across the dot
**Goal:** in column working, an amount with no paise gets two zeros in its paise column, so its rupees stay in the rupees column
**Observe:** The whole-rupee card came in with grey zeros under the paise, and its rupees stayed under the rupees.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes the twenty-nine of Rs 29 under the paise and adds twenty-nine paise, so rupees are added to paise and the sum comes out as Rs 63.74. → the Rs 29 card springs back from under the paise, its outline left standing there, and snaps in again with grey zeros filling its paise column and its twenty-nine under the sixty-three. The paise column adds forty-five and nothing, the rupees add to ninety-two, and the child's Rs 63.74 stays in the slot beside the new line.
- adds the rupees and stops at Rs 92, leaving off the forty-five paise because the whole-rupee price seemed to have no paise to add. → the two zeros in the whole-rupee price's paise column light grey, and the paise column is added with them, forty-five and nothing making forty-five, which goes down into the answer line in green. The answer line then reads rupees, dot and paise all filled, and the child's Rs 92 stays in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Writing the 29 under the paise and adding 29 paise, which gives Rs 63.74.

### 4 · Making a bill
**Screen:** a shop shelf of three or four priced things and an empty bill slip
**Does:** writes each thing and its price on a line of the bill, adds the columns with every carry, and writes the total at the foot
**Win:** the total lands at the foot of the bill under the rule
**Feels:** a bill is a column of prices with one sum at its foot, however many lines it has
**Goal:** a bill lists each thing bought with its price, and the total is added at the foot
**Observe:** The total landed under the rule, with each carry still in its circle above it.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- forgets the rupee carried from the paise and writes Rs 270.65 at the foot of the bill. → the paise box shows one hundred and sixty-five paise, a full strip rolls up into the carry circle, and sixty-five goes down. The rupees column is then added with the circle's rupee travelling down into it, and the Total line reads the new sum beside the child's Rs 270.65, which stays in the slot.
- carries one rupee when the paise of four prices come to two hundred and eighty, and writes Rs 298.80. → the paise box shows two hundred and eighty paise, and two full strips fill one after the other, each rolling up into a rupee, so the carry circle reads two. Eighty goes down into the paise column, the rupees are added with both carried rupees, and the child's answer stays in the slot beside the new total.
**Misconception to break:** Forgetting the carried rupee and writing Rs 270.65 at the foot of the bill.

## Delight
The second phase, the moment the paise box passes a hundred. The cells of the paise sum pour into a strip, filling it block by block until it holds exactly a hundred, and the full strip curls up into one rupee that rises and drops into the carry circle, while the paise left slide down into the paise column as two green digits. When the sum passes two hundred, two strips fill and curl up one after the other, and the carry circle reads two. The add can be undone and run again as often as a finger likes, nothing is marked, and the voice says nothing at all while it happens.

**General miss line:** Lay the prices again with the dots in line, and add the paise first.

## Answer key
To add money, write the amounts one under another with the dots in one straight line, so rupees stand under rupees and paise under paise. Add the paise column first. When the paise come to a hundred or more, each hundred is carried as one rupee to the rupees column, and only the paise left are written, as two digits. Then add the rupees with anything carried. In column working, an amount with no paise is written with two zeros in its paise column, so its rupees stay in the rupees column; in running text a whole amount is written with no dot. A bill lists each thing bought with its price, one to a line, and the total is added at its foot.

## Student controls
- **Shop shelf**: holds price cards for the things on sale
- **Bill slip**: takes one thing and its price on each line
- **Dotted line**: snaps every price so its dot sits on one line
- **Paise box**: shows the paise column's sum before anything is carried
- **Carry circle**: takes one rupee for each hundred paise
- **Add**: adds the paise column first, then the rupees with the carry
- **Total line**: writes the total at the foot of the bill
