## What it is
**Description:** Take one amount from another in columns, paise first, break one rupee into a hundred paise when the paise on top are too few, and find the change as the money given less the bill.
**Why:** Take one amount from another in columns, break a rupee into a hundred paise whenever the paise on top are too few, and find the change a payment brings back.
**Look:** A shop counter with the money given on the left, the bill beside it, a column frame between them, and a change tray at the right.
**Context:** Grade 4, the taking-away half of the chapter's middle section. An adult sees change being counted out. What is actually being settled is where paise come from when there are too few on top: one rupee is broken into a hundred paise, and the rupees go down by one. The chapter works that once, with paise on both lines. The exercise then asks for change from whole-rupee payments, which takes paise from zero paise, and the one worked example that does so prints only its answer. It is the same move, a rupee broken into a hundred paise, and this tool works it in full every time. The exercise asks for the amount returned, so the total of the bill is a step and not the answer.
**For the child:** Line up the dots. Take away the paise first. If the paise on top are too few, break one rupee into a hundred paise. The change is the money given less the bill. Add it back to check.
**Teaching notes:** Play shopkeeper and shopper with play notes and hundred-cell strips before the tool is opened: a price in rupees and paise, a large note, and the change counted out. Then use the tool for the one move paper lets a child skip, breaking a rupee. The chapter's regrouping example breaks one rupee into a hundred paise when there are too few paise on top, and the rupees go down by one. That example has paise on both lines. Both bills in the exercise are paid in whole rupees and come to rupees and paise, so paise are taken from zero paise, and the chapter's only worked example of that prints the answer with no working. It is the same move: break one rupee into a hundred paise, the rupees go down by one, and the paise on top become a hundred. Work it in full on the board, more than once. The exercise asks for the amount returned; the bill's total is a step, and a child who stops there has answered a different question. Adding the change back to the bill checks every answer.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Paise first, then rupees
**Screen:** the column frame holding a larger amount above a smaller one, with enough paise on top
**Does:** takes away the paise column, then the rupees column
**Win:** the paise answer lands, then the rupees answer beside it across the dot
**Feels:** each column takes away on its own, and the paise go first
**Goal:** amounts are taken away in columns with the dots in a line, the paise first and then the rupees
**Observe:** The paise answer landed first, then the rupees answer beside it across the dot.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- takes away the rupees only and writes Rs 45, leaving the paise out. → the paise column lights green and its take-away runs, forty less fifteen, and twenty-five lands on the answer line after the dot. The answer line then reads rupees, dot and paise, and the child's Rs 45 stays in the slot beside it, nothing marked.
- sets Rs 23.15 on the top line because it was named first in take Rs 23.15 from Rs 68.40. → the frame sets the child's order and the take-away tries the rupees: twenty-three rupees on top cannot give sixty-eight, and the rupees cell stays shut. The word from in the question lights, the two lines change places so that the amount after it stands on top, and the child's first frame stays drawn beside the new one.
**Misconception to break:** Taking away the rupees only and writing Rs 45, leaving the paise out.

### 2 · Breaking a rupee into paise
**Screen:** a take-away whose paise on top are fewer than the paise below, with the paise cell refusing
**Does:** breaks one rupee, watches the rupees go down by one and the paise on top grow by a hundred, then takes away
**Win:** the rupee opens into a hundred cells, the paise on top grow by a hundred, and the take-away goes through
**Feels:** the rupee does not vanish, it moves across the dot as a hundred paise
**Goal:** when the paise on top are too few, one rupee is broken into a hundred paise and the rupees on top go down by one
**Observe:** The paise cell stayed shut until a rupee was broken, and then the rupees on top showed one less.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- takes the smaller paise from the larger, eighty-five less thirty, whichever line each stands on, and writes Rs 288.55. → the paise cell stays shut on the thirty on top, and the child's fifty-five is written small beside it. The break control then strikes through four hundred and twenty-six and writes four hundred and twenty-five above it, and a strip of a hundred cells joins the thirty to make a hundred and thirty. The paise take away, a hundred and thirty less eighty-five, and the child's Rs 288.55 stays in the slot.
- breaks the rupee but writes a hundred paise in place of the thirty on top, instead of adding them, and gets Rs 287.15. → the strip of a hundred cells slides across and lies beside the thirty shaded cells on top, and the two are counted together, a hundred and thirty, before anything is taken. The paise take away, a hundred and thirty less eighty-five, and the child's Rs 287.15 stays in the slot beside the new answer.
**Misconception to break:** Taking the smaller paise from the larger, 85 less 30, and writing Rs 288.55.

### 3 · Taking paise from zero paise
**Screen:** a whole-rupee amount written with two zeros in its paise column, above an amount with paise
**Does:** breaks one rupee, sees a hundred paise appear on top, takes the paise away, then the rupees
**Win:** a hundred paise appear on top, the rupees go down by one, and both columns take away
**Feels:** with no paise on top, the break makes all of them, and the rupees pay for it
**Goal:** from zero paise, one rupee is broken into a hundred paise, so the rupees go down by one and the paise on top become a hundred
**Observe:** From zero paise, the strip from the broken rupee alone made the paise on top, a hundred paise.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes the thirty-five paise from the bottom line straight down into the answer, and gets Rs 243.35. → the paise cell stays shut over the grey zeros, and the child's thirty-five is written small beside it. The break control then strikes through three hundred and writes two hundred and ninety-nine above it, and a full strip of a hundred cells slides across to stand over the zeros, a hundred paise on top. A hundred less thirty-five is taken away, and the child's Rs 243.35 stays in the slot.
- breaks a rupee into a hundred paise but leaves the three hundred as it was, and gets Rs 243.65. → the three hundred on top is lit with nothing struck through it, beside a hundred paise that came from nowhere, and the whole top line is counted in paise to show it has grown by a rupee. The three hundred is then struck through and two hundred and ninety-nine written above it, and the take-away runs again, the child's Rs 243.65 staying in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Writing the 35 paise from the bottom, which gives Rs 243.35, or breaking the rupee and not taking it off the rupees, which gives Rs 243.65.

### 4 · Change from a payment
**Screen:** a bill slip with its total, the notes paid, and the change tray empty
**Does:** sets the bill under the money given, takes it away, counts the change into the tray, then adds it back to check
**Win:** the change fills the tray, and the change and the bill add back to the money given
**Feels:** the change is what the money given still holds once the bill has been paid out of it
**Goal:** the change is the money given less the bill, and adding the change back to the bill checks it
**Observe:** The change slid back onto the bill, and the two made exactly the money given.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- gives the bill, Rs 736.40, as the answer, when the question asks how much change comes back. → the child's number is set against the bill slip's total, where it matches, and the answer slot's label, change, lights in reddish purple. The bill is then set under the money given, eight hundred rupees, and taken away, and the change tray fills. The child's Rs 736.40 stays in the slot beside the bill's own total.
- adds the bill to the money given and writes Rs 1536.40. → the check control lays the child's sum beside the notes paid, and it stands far taller than the eight hundred rupees on the counter, more than the shopper ever had. The bill is then set under the money given and taken away, a rupee broken into a hundred paise at the paise column, and the change fills the tray. The child's number stays in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Giving the bill, Rs 736.40, as the answer, or adding the bill to the money given.

## Delight
The fourth phase, the moment the check control is pressed on a bill with paise that was paid in whole rupees. The change slides back across the counter onto the bill, and its shaded cells pour onto the bill's paise and fill exactly one strip, not a cell over and not a cell short. The full strip curls up into a rupee and joins the rupees, and the whole stack settles level beside the notes that were paid, rupee for rupee. It can be run as often as a finger likes, nothing is marked, and the voice says nothing at all while it happens.

**General miss line:** Look at the paise on top again. Are there enough to take from?

## Answer key
To take one amount from another, write the smaller under the larger with the dots in one line, and take away the paise first, then the rupees. When the paise on top are fewer than the paise below, break one rupee into a hundred paise: the rupees on top go down by one and the paise on top grow by a hundred. From zero paise the same move gives a hundred paise on top, so Rs 500, written 500.00 in column working, becomes Rs 499 and 100 paise. The change is the money given less the bill. Adding the change back to the bill gives the money given, which checks it.

## Student controls
- **Money given**: shows the notes paid and their amount
- **Bill slip**: shows the bill and its total
- **Column frame**: sets the bill under the money given with the dots in one line
- **Take away**: works the paise column first, then the rupees
- **Break a rupee**: takes one rupee off the rupees on top and adds a hundred paise to the paise on top
- **Change tray**: fills with the change, rupees as notes and coins and paise as cells on a strip
- **Check**: adds the change back to the bill and lays the sum beside the money given
