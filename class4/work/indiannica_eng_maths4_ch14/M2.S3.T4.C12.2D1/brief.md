## What it is
**Description:** Turn the key card to see what one symbol stands for, read each row as its symbols times the key, compare the rows, and light only the row a question asks about.
**Why:** Read what one symbol stands for from the key, turn each row into a number, compare the rows, and answer a question from the row it asks about.
**Look:** A pictograph board with a title strip, a key card that starts face down, a compare rail beside it and a row lamp.
**Context:** Grade 4, the heart of the chapter. An adult sees pictures being counted. What is actually being learnt is that a picture stands for a number only through the key, and that a question about one row is answered from that row alone. The book's own answer key gets exactly that wrong once: for the monuments row of the exercise, three heads at three students each, it prints the total of every row on the pictograph. The kites pictograph in the same section draws every kite yellow whatever its row; this tool draws each row in its own colour.
**For the child:** Turn the key card first. Count the symbols in a row. Use the key for each symbol. Line the rows up to compare them. Read only the row the question asks about.
**Teaching notes:** Pin a pictograph of books read by four groups on the wall before the tool is opened, with a title and a key, and have pairs answer question cards from it. Ask which cards needed one row and which needed every row. That is the question the book's own answer key gets wrong once: the exercise asks how many students visited the monuments, a row of three heads at three students each, which is nine, and the key prints the total of every row. Mark nine right, and have the class say what the other number counts. Keep the count of symbols and the number apart in every answer, and say the unit. The kites pictograph in the section draws every kite yellow, even in the rows for red, blue, green and purple kites, so read it by its row names; in the tool each row is drawn in its own colour. The worked conclusion under it is printed as the number of less red kites sold than green; say how many fewer red kites were sold than green, which is four.

## Opening (first frame of every mount)
Every mount comes up already furnished for its phase with nothing in front of it. On the first phase the pictograph board stands with its title strip showing, its key card face down, and one row lit. On the second the key card is turned, and one row is lit with its tag an empty outlined box. On the third every row is read and numbered, and the compare rail stands empty beside the board. On the fourth the whole pictograph stands with every tag empty, a question card naming one row sits in the slot, and the all-rows control waits under the rail. The running count at the top left holds the symbols counted in the row being read, with the rows read beside it. The answer slot and the pad stand empty down the right. One thing moves, once: the face-down key card, or the lit row, or the largest number, or the row the question names, gives one slow pulse while the voice asks what one picture stands for. That pulse is the only invitation, and a finger on it is the whole way in.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · The key says what one symbol stands for
**Screen:** a pictograph with its title strip showing and its key card face down, and one row lit
**Does:** tries to give the lit row a number, then turns the key card and reads the title
**Win:** the key card turns and every row can now take a number, with the title telling what it counts
**Feels:** the pictures are only pictures until the key card turns, and then every row can become a number
**Goal:** the key says what one symbol stands for, and the title says what the data is about
**Observe:** With the key face down, the row could be counted in symbols, and its tag stayed empty.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- gives the number of symbols as the answer, three apples, while the key card is still face down. → the running count holds the three symbols, and the row's tag stays an empty outlined box. The key card gives one slow pulse. Once it is turned, the row reader takes its value under each symbol and the tag fills. The child's three stays in the slot.
- reads the key's number as every row's number, so a key of ten makes every row ten. → the row reader takes the ten under the first symbol only, and the tag reads ten. It then carries on under each symbol in turn, and the tag climbs to thirty for a row of three. The child's ten stays beside it.
- reads a chart of books read by four groups as a count of children, because the rows are groups of children. → the title strip lights, and its words are set beside the child's answer. Each row's tag writes its unit, books, after its number, and the child's answer stays in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Reading both rows as 3 apples because the pictures look the same, without reading either key.

### 2 · Symbols times the key
**Screen:** the pictograph with its key card turned and one row lit, its number box empty
**Does:** counts the symbols in the row, then takes the key once for each symbol
**Win:** the key's value is added once under each symbol and the row's number lands at the end of the row
**Feels:** the same amount under every symbol, climbing along the row to the number at its end
**Goal:** a row is its symbols counted and the key taken that many times
**Observe:** The key's value was written once under each symbol, and the row's number was the last of them.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- adds the key to the count, giving thirteen for seven umbrellas at six each. → the row reader takes six under the first symbol, then six more under the second, and on down the row, the running sum written under each symbol in amber. It reaches forty-two at the seventh symbol. The child's thirteen stays in the slot.
- counts the key card's own symbol as one of the row's, so seven symbols are read as eight. → the row reader rings each of the row's symbols as it counts and stops at the row's last one. The key card's symbol is never ringed. The running count holds seven, and the tag fills with forty-two beside the child's forty-eight.
**Misconception to break:** Adding the key to the count, 7 + 6 = 13, instead of taking 6 seven times.

### 3 · Most, least and the same
**Screen:** every row read and numbered, with the compare rail empty beside the board
**Does:** stands the rows on the compare rail and names the most, the least and the two that are the same
**Win:** the rows line up in order on the rail and the two equal rows stand level
**Feels:** with one key for every row and one size for every symbol, a longer row is a bigger number, and the rail puts them in order
**Goal:** with one key for every row, the rows are compared by their numbers or by their symbols
**Observe:** Two rows with the same number of symbols shared one place on the rail.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- gives nine as the most visitors, the number of symbols, instead of ninety. → the rows stand on the rail by their numbers, ninety at the top. The running count shows that row's nine symbols, and its tag shows ninety. The child's nine stays in the slot.
- calls the row that reaches furthest the most, on a chart drawn by hand with the pictures of one row drawn larger. → the card's rows are redrawn on the board at one size from the same edge, and the row of five large pictures comes out shorter than the row of seven. The rail puts the seven row's number above the five row's. The card stays beside the board.
- compares rows from two charts with different keys by their symbols, calling six symbols more than four. → each row is read with its own key, six fives and four tens. Thirty and forty take their places on the rail, forty above. The two key cards stand beside their rows.
**Misconception to break:** Giving 9 as the most visitors, the number of symbols, instead of 90 from the key.

### 4 · Read only the row asked
**Screen:** the whole pictograph, a question card naming one row, the row lamp and the all-rows control
**Does:** lights the row the question names, reads it, and sees what the all-rows control gives instead
**Win:** the answer lands beside the one lit row, and the all-rows sum sits apart under the board as the total
**Feels:** one row lit and the rest dimmed, and the sum of every row kept in its own place under the board
**Goal:** a question about one row is answered from that row, and every row added gives the total for all of them
**Observe:** The lit row's number landed in its tag, and the sum of every row sat apart under the board.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- adds every row and gives one hundred and five when the question asks how many chose chess. → the row lamp lights the chess row alone and dims the rest. Its three faces are counted and read with the key, and fifteen lands in its tag. The all-rows sum, one hundred and five, sits apart in the strip under the board, labelled as the total for all the rows. The child's answer stays in the slot.
- reads the row next to the one the question names. → the row lamp lights the named row. The row the child read is outlined, and its name is set beside the name on the question card. The lit row is then read, and its number lands in its tag.
- gives one club's number when the question asks how many children are in the four clubs together. → the question card's word together is underlined. The all-rows control adds every row, twelve, eighteen, fifteen and nine, and fifty-four lands in the strip, labelled as the total for all the rows. Each child here is in one club, so the sum counts each child once.
**Misconception to break:** Adding every row and giving 105, the total for all four sports, when the question asks about chess alone.

## Delight
The second phase, once a row's number has landed. Any symbol in that row can then be tapped. It opens like a paper packet to show what it stands for inside, the key's worth of small amber counters, then folds shut again. A half symbol opens to show half as many. It runs as often as a finger likes, nothing is marked either way, and the voice says nothing at all while it happens.

**General miss line:** Count the symbols again, and take the key once for each one.

## Answer key
A pictograph shows data with symbols. The key says what one symbol stands for, and the title says what the data is about. A row is read by counting its symbols and taking the key that many times. When every row uses the same key, the rows can be compared by their symbols: the row with the most symbols stands for the most. A question about one row is answered from that row only. Adding every row gives the total for all the rows, which answers a different question.

## Student controls
- **Key card**: starts face down and turns to show one symbol and what it stands for
- **Title strip**: says what the data in the pictograph is about
- **Row reader**: counts a row's symbols and takes the key that many times
- **Row number**: writes each row's number, with its unit, at the end of the row
- **Compare rail**: stands the rows' numbers side by side from the largest to the smallest
- **Row lamp**: lights only the row a question names and dims the rest
- **All rows**: adds every row and labels the sum as the total for all the rows
