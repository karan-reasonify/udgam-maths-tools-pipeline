## What it is
**Description:** Fold a symbol down its middle to see what half of it stands for, read rows that end in a half, add the rows for a total, and light a shared thing in every row it sits in.
**Why:** Read what half a symbol stands for, read rows that end in a half, add the rows for a total, and see when a sum counts one thing more than once.
**Look:** A bench with a key card that folds, a pictograph board in the middle, a total bar under it, and a small route map at one side.
**Context:** Grade 4. An adult sees half pictures and a sum. What is actually being learnt is that half a symbol stands for half of what the key says, and that adding a pictograph's rows answers how many in all only when nothing sits in two rows. The exercise this tool serves adds the interchange stations of five metro lines; an interchange station is where lines meet, so a station shared by two of those lines is in both rows, and the sum counts stations on lines, not separate stations. The chapter never shows a pictograph being totalled and never says how a half symbol is drawn.
**For the child:** Fold the symbol to see what half of it stands for. Read the whole symbols first. Then add the half. Add every row for the total. Tap a shared stop to see every row that counts it.
**Teaching notes:** Cut paper symbols in half before the tool is opened, and let groups show an odd count, such as the pencils in a tin, with a key of two and one half symbol at a row's end. The chapter never says how a half symbol is drawn: draw the left half of the whole symbol, cut down its middle, as the chapter's own keys show it, and put it last in its row. Use a half symbol only with a key that halves into whole things, such as two, four or ten, because half of a key of three is not a whole number of anything. The chapter asks for a total three times and never shows a pictograph being totalled; it is only adding. The exercise this tool serves adds the interchange stations of five metro lines. An interchange station is where lines meet, so a station shared by two of those lines sits in both rows, and adding the rows counts it twice. Say that the sum counts stations on lines, not separate stations, and that the pictograph cannot say how many separate stations there are. Draw two routes crossing at one stop on the board and let the class find that stop in both rows.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Half a symbol
**Screen:** the key card with one whole symbol and what it stands for, and the fold waiting
**Does:** folds the symbol down its middle and says what the half stands for
**Win:** the symbol folds, its left half stands alone, and half the key is written beside it
**Feels:** the symbol folds like paper, and its worth splits into two equal heaps along the same crease
**Goal:** half a symbol stands for half of what the key says
**Observe:** The folded symbol stood as its left half, and half the key was written beside it.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- reads half a bottle picture as one bottle, as though any piece of a picture stood for one thing. → the fold runs again, and the line of six counters under the symbol splits down the crease into two heaps of three. The half keeps its three, and the key card writes three beside it in amber. The child's one stays in the slot.
- reads the half symbol as a whole one, six bottles, because it is the same picture. → the whole symbol is drawn beside the half at the same height and twice as wide. The fold closes the whole down its crease onto the half, and the two edges meet. Three is written under the half, and six under the whole.
- asks for a half symbol on a key of three children. → the fold control stays shut. The key card lays its three counters under the symbol and draws the crease through the middle counter, which no fold can share between two halves. The key card stays as it was.
**Misconception to break:** Reading half a picture as 1 bottle, as though any piece of a picture stood for one thing.

### 2 · A row that ends in a half
**Screen:** a pictograph row of whole symbols with a half symbol at its end, the key card beside it
**Does:** reads the whole symbols with the key, then adds what the half stands for
**Win:** the two parts are written under the row and their sum lands at its end
**Feels:** the whole symbols first, then the half, two parts written under the row and added
**Goal:** a row ending in a half is its whole symbols times the key, add half the key
**Observe:** The parts line wrote what the whole symbols stand for and what the half stands for, and the row's sum was the two added.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- counts the half as a whole symbol, and writes forty for four whole bus pictures and a half. → the parts line writes the four whole symbols as thirty-two and the half as four, and the row's sum lands as thirty-six. The running count shows four and a half, never five. The child's forty stays in the slot.
- leaves the half out and writes thirty-two, because only the whole symbols match the key card. → the parts line writes thirty-two under the whole symbols, then lights the half and writes four under it. The two parts are added to thirty-six. The child's thirty-two stays beside it.
**Misconception to break:** Counting the half picture as a whole one and writing 40, or leaving it out and writing 32.

### 3 · Adding the rows for a total
**Screen:** a pictograph of three rows, some ending in a half, with every row read and the total bar empty
**Does:** adds every row into the total bar, then pairs two halves into one whole to check it
**Win:** the total fills the bar, and two halves slide together into one whole symbol
**Feels:** every row added once into the bar, and two halves from different rows making one whole
**Goal:** a pictograph's total is every row added once, when nothing sits in two rows
**Observe:** Two halves from different rows clicked together into one whole in the tray, and the total did not change.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- counts each half as a whole symbol, and writes sixty-four for rows of five and a half, three, and six and a half at four each. → each row's parts line is written again: twenty and two, twelve, and twenty-four and two. The total bar adds the rows, twenty-two, twelve and twenty-six, and sixty lands. The two halves then pair up in the tray into one whole. The child's sixty-four stays in the slot.
- adds the symbols and gives fifteen as the saplings, forgetting the key. → the running count shows the fifteen symbols once the two halves have paired up. The total bar adds the rows' numbers, and sixty lands, labelled saplings in all. The child's fifteen stays beside the running count, where symbols are counted.
- adds only the whole symbols and drops both halves, writing fifty-six. → the pair-up control slides the two halves into the tray, where they click into one whole symbol, worth four. The total bar adds it to the fifty-six, and sixty lands. The child's fifty-six stays in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Counting each half picture as a whole one and writing 64.

### 4 · Counted twice
**Screen:** the route map with two routes crossing at one stop, and the pictograph of stops on each route
**Does:** taps the shared stop on the map and watches it light in each row that counts it
**Win:** the shared stop lights in both rows, and the total bar is relabelled as stops on routes
**Feels:** the shared stop is tagged beside each row that counts it, and the sum is seen counting it twice
**Goal:** when one thing sits in two rows, adding the rows counts it twice
**Observe:** The shared stop's tag hung beside both rows, and the total bar changed its label to stops on routes.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- gives eleven as the number of separate stops, when the stop the two routes share sits in both rows. → the share finder lights the shared stop on the map, and a reddish purple tag with its name hangs beside both rows. The total bar is relabelled stops on routes. The child's eleven stays in the slot.
- leaves the shared stop out of one route's row, so that the rows add up to separate stops. → that route's line on the map is lit, and its stop dots are counted one at a time: five. Its row holds four, so the row no longer says how many stops the route has. The dot with no symbol is ringed, and the child's row stays as it was.
- will not add the rows of a pictograph whose rows share nothing, sure that any sum counts something twice. → the saplings board has no route map, and the share finder finds nothing to light, because each sapling sits in one class's row only. The total bar adds the rows and keeps its label, saplings in all.
**Misconception to break:** Giving 11 as the number of separate stops, when the stop the two routes share is counted twice.

## Delight
The first phase, when the fold closes. Under the whole symbol the key's worth of small amber counters stands in a line. As the symbol folds, the line splits down the same crease into two equal heaps, one under each half. The left half keeps its heap, and the right half folds away with the other. It runs as often as a finger likes on any key that halves into whole things, nothing is marked either way, and the voice says nothing at all while it happens.

**General miss line:** Read the whole symbols first, then the half, and put the two together.

## Answer key
Half a symbol stands for half of what the key says, so a half symbol is used only with a key that halves into whole things. A row that ends in a half is its whole symbols times the key, add half the key, and two half symbols together stand for one whole symbol's worth. The total a pictograph shows is found by adding every row, and it counts each thing once only when nothing sits in two rows. When one thing sits in several rows, adding the rows counts it once for every row it is in, so the sum counts things in rows, not separate things.

## Student controls
- **Key card**: shows one whole symbol and what it stands for
- **Fold**: folds the key's symbol down its middle and writes what the half stands for
- **Row reader**: reads the whole symbols and a half at the end, writing both parts and their sum
- **Pair-up**: slides two half symbols together into one whole
- **Total bar**: adds every row once and writes the sum under the board
- **Route map**: shows each route's stops as dots on its own line
- **Share finder**: lights a shared stop in every row that counts it
