## What it is
**Description:** Try keys against every number of a data set, see what each one leaves over and how many symbols it needs, choose one that fits, and ask the finished pictograph what its data can answer.
**Why:** Choose a key that turns every number into whole symbols, or whole symbols and a half, without needing too many, then draw the pictograph and ask it what its data can answer.
**Look:** A data card and a key dial at the left, a checker board that lays each number out in symbols, a symbol count at the top, and question cards, a can do list and word cards along the foot.
**Context:** Grade 4, the chapter's recap. An adult sees a key being picked. What is actually being learnt is how a key is chosen, which the chapter asks for twice and never teaches. The route taken is the one the chapter's own pictographs show: every key on its pages turns its data into whole symbols, or whole symbols and a half, and each number is checked against the key in turn. The opening page's airport data and the closing box's points both find a key that way, and the closing box's three questions about the game are replaced by the questions its points can answer.
**For the child:** Pick a key. Check it on every number. Read what is left over. A half is fine. Count the symbols it needs. Draw the pictograph with a title and a key. Ask it what its data can answer.
**Teaching notes:** Give each group a data card before the tool is opened and let them try three keys, writing how many symbols each row would need, then draw the pictograph with the key they choose, and its title. ## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · A key that fits the data
**Screen:** a data card of four numbers, the key dial set to a trial key, and the checker board empty
**Does:** sets a trial key and watches each number laid out in whole symbols, checking every row
**Win:** every row lights as whole symbols and the set key control unlocks
**Feels:** the key is laid against every number in turn, and the set key control waits until every row lights
**Goal:** a key that turns every number into whole symbols fits the data
**Observe:** Three rows lit under a trial key and two stayed unlit, each with a loose leftover in its tag.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- tries a key of three hundred on the first and largest number, sees fifteen hundred make five whole symbols, and sets it. → the checker lays out every row, not only the first. Fifteen hundred, nine hundred and six hundred light. Five hundred stands at one symbol with two hundred loose in its tag, and four hundred at one with one hundred loose, and both stay unlit. The set key control stays locked.
- chooses the smallest number in the data as the key, one hundred and fifty points, so that player gets one symbol. → the checker lays out every row. Three hundred and one hundred and fifty light. The two rows of two hundred leave fifty loose, two hundred and fifty leaves one hundred, and three hundred and fifty leaves fifty. Half of one hundred and fifty is seventy-five, so all four stay unlit.
**Misconception to break:** Checking only the first number, 30, and stopping, when every number must be checked.

### 2 · When a half is needed
**Screen:** a data card where one number leaves something over, with the checker holding it at the row's end
**Does:** compares what is left over with half the key, and closes the row with a half symbol when they match
**Win:** the leftover folds into a half symbol and the row lights, and a leftover that is not a half stays loose
**Feels:** what is left over is laid against half the key, and either it matches or it stays loose
**Goal:** a key may leave a half symbol, when exactly half the key is left over
**Observe:** A leftover of exactly half the key folded into a half symbol, and a leftover of any other size stayed loose.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- drops the five left over from thirty-five and draws three symbols for a key of ten. → the checker lays three tens and stands the five at the row's end. The five is laid against the key's symbol folded down its middle, and the two match. A half symbol closes the row, which lights, and the running count shows three and a half. The child's three stays in the slot.
- draws a half for any leftover, so eighty with a key of thirty ends in a half. → a half symbol is laid at the end of eighty's row, and the checker writes what it stands for, fifteen, beside the loose twenty. The two do not match, so the half comes back off and the twenty stays loose. The row stays unlit.
- turns down a key that leaves exactly half over, sure that a key that fits gives whole symbols only. → the leftover is laid against the folded symbol, and the two match. The half closes the row and the row lights. With every row lit, the set key control unlocks, and the child's choice stays on the dial.
**Misconception to break:** Drawing 35 as 3 symbols and dropping the 5 left over, or as 4 whole symbols.

### 3 · Too many symbols
**Screen:** a data card of large numbers with a small trial key, the rows running off the edge of the board
**Does:** reads the symbol count, then turns the dial to a larger key that still fits every number
**Win:** the rows shrink back inside the board and the symbol count drops to a number worth drawing
**Feels:** the rows shrink as the key grows, and the count of symbols falls to something a hand could draw
**Goal:** too small a key gives too many symbols to draw, even when it fits
**Observe:** The rows ran off the board under a small key and drew back inside it when the dial turned to a larger key that still fitted.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- chooses the smallest key that fits, five, for three hundred, five hundred and four hundred and fifty visitors. → the checker lays out sixty, one hundred and ninety symbols, and every row runs off the board's right edge. The symbol count reads two hundred and fifty. The child's key stays on the dial.
- turns straight to a large key, two hundred, and does not check it. → the checker lays out every row. Three hundred and five hundred each end in a half and light. Four hundred and fifty stands at two symbols with fifty loose, which is not half of two hundred, and stays unlit. The set key control stays locked.
**Misconception to break:** Choosing the smallest key that fits because it gives whole symbols, and then drawing a hundred symbols in one row.

### 4 · Asking the pictograph questions
**Screen:** the finished pictograph with its title and key, a stack of question cards, and the can do list
**Does:** answers each card from the pictograph, sets aside a card the data cannot answer, and ticks the can do list
**Win:** each answer lands on its card, the card the data cannot answer is set aside, and the list is ticked
**Feels:** each card either lights what answers it on the pictograph, or finds nothing there and is set aside
**Goal:** a finished pictograph answers questions about its own data, and nothing else
**Observe:** The cards about the most, the least and the same were answered, and one card went to the tray headed not in this data.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- tries to answer how many players a team has from the pictures. → the card is laid on the pictograph and nothing on it lights: the title says points, and the key says points. The card slides into the tray headed not in this data, with those words across it.
- gives seven as the most, the number of symbols, when the question asks for points. → the row is read with the key, seven times twenty-five, and one hundred and seventy-five lands in its tag. The running count shows the seven symbols, and the child's seven stays in the slot.
- sets aside a card asking how many more points one team scored than another, thinking how many more is not in the data. → the card is laid on the pictograph, and both rows light. The stretch by which the longer row passes the shorter, four symbols, is banded sky blue and read with the key: one hundred. The card is answered and stays out of the tray.
**Misconception to break:** Trying to answer a question the data does not hold, such as how many players a team has, from the pictures.

## Delight
The third phase, while the dial turns. With each new key every row is laid out afresh in one quick sweep from its left edge. The rows are seen shrinking as the key grows and stretching as it shrinks. A row whose leftover stops fitting lets its loose tag drop from its end, and catches it up again when a key fits it. It runs as often as a finger likes, nothing is marked either way, and the voice says nothing at all while it happens.

**General miss line:** Check every number again, and read what the key leaves over.

## Answer key
A key fits the data when every number becomes whole symbols, or whole symbols and a half, as the chapter's own pictographs show. To check a key, take each number in turn: if the key goes into it exactly, it is whole symbols; if half the key is left over, the row ends in a half symbol; if anything else is left over, the key does not fit that data. A key can fit and still be too small, needing more symbols than anyone would draw, so choose a key that fits and keeps the rows short enough to draw. A finished pictograph answers questions about its own data, such as the most, the least, the same and how many more, and nothing else.
