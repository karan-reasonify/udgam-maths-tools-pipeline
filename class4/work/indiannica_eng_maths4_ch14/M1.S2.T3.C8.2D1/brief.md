## What it is
**Description:** Draw one tally mark for each answer as it comes, draw every fifth mark across the four before it, read a tally in fives, and compare the rows of a tally chart.
**Why:** Record each answer with one tally mark as it comes, draw every fifth mark across the four before it, read a tally in fives, and compare the rows of a tally chart.
**Look:** A tally chart drawn as a board, with a belt of answer cards along its top, a pen for each row, and a compare bar at its foot.
**Context:** Grade 4. An adult sees marks on a chart. What is actually being learnt is that a tally records each answer the moment it comes, so each answer gets one mark and none is left to count later, and that bundles of five make a count quick to read. The page defines tally marks as vertical bars, when the fifth mark of each bundle is drawn across the four before it, and one question in the exercise that follows is worded as though a list of favourites could say which children prefer one flavour to another. This tool draws the fifth mark across every time, and asks how many more children chose one thing than another.
**For the child:** Tap the row each answer belongs to. Draw one mark for each answer. Draw the fifth mark across the four. Count the bundles in fives. Then count the marks left over. Line two rows up to find how many more.
**Teaching notes:** Tally something real before the tool is opened: groups stand at the school gate or a window for ten minutes and mark each car, bike, bus and person walking as it passes. The page defines tally marks as vertical bars. Say instead that each thing counted is one short mark, and that every fifth mark is drawn across the four before it, so a bundle is five and a count is read in fives. The page's own worked tally and every chart in the chapter draw the fifth mark that way. Tally chart is used from the first worked Example on and never defined: it is a chart with each name beside its tally marks, and usually its number. One question in the exercise that follows is worded as though a list of favourites could say which children prefer one flavour to another. It cannot. Ask how many more children chose one than the other, and find it by taking the smaller count from the larger. A tally chart's total is the number of children only when each child gave one answer, so say so whenever the class adds one up.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · One mark for each
**Screen:** an empty tally chart with a row for each answer and a belt of answer cards along the top
**Does:** taps the row for each card as it arrives and watches its mark drawn and the card turn over
**Win:** the belt empties, every card is turned, and the marks in the rows match the cards
**Feels:** one card, one tap, one mark, and a marked card lies face down on the belt
**Goal:** one mark is drawn for each answer as it comes, so each answer gets one mark and none is left to count later
**Observe:** Every card on the belt turned over as its mark was drawn, and the marks in the rows came to the cards on the belt.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- goes back through the list once for each pet, misses a cat on the way, and gives the cat row one mark. → the belt runs the six cards again one at a time, and each card turns face down as its mark is redrawn in its row. The cat row comes to two marks beside the child's one, and the cat card that was passed over lifts a little as it turns.
- copies a list of nine answers into the chart and loses one, so the bus row has three marks where the list has four. → the list is laid on the belt as nine cards, and each turns face down as its mark is drawn. The bus row comes to four marks, and the running count comes to nine, one for each card. The child's row of three stays beside it.
**Misconception to break:** Going back through the list once for each pet and missing or repeating an answer, instead of marking each answer once as it comes.

### 2 · The fifth mark crosses the four
**Screen:** a row with four marks standing and a fifth answer card arriving on the belt
**Does:** draws the fifth mark across the four, then carries on marking into a new bundle
**Win:** the mark falls across the four, the bundle closes with a ring, and the next mark starts a new bundle
**Feels:** four marks standing and the fifth lying across them, closed off and easy to see as five
**Goal:** every fifth mark is drawn across the four before it, making a bundle of five
**Observe:** The fifth mark in every bundle lay across the other four, and the next mark started a new bundle.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- draws the fifth mark upright beside the four, so the row is a line of single marks with no bundles. → the child's upright fifth mark stays where it was drawn. Beside the row the pen draws the same five again, four standing and the fifth laid across them in vermilion, and the bundle closes with its ring. The two stand side by side.
- draws five upright marks and then crosses them with a sixth, so each bundle holds six. → the counting finger reads the child's first bundle mark by mark and reaches six, writing it over the bundle. The pen then draws a bundle of five beside it, four standing and one across, and the finger reads that one as five.
- starts the next mark inside the closed bundle, with no gap, so two bundles run together. → the pen redraws the row with a small gap before each new bundle, and the child's row stands above it with its marks run together. The finger then reads the pen's row in fives.
**Misconception to break:** Drawing the fifth mark as one more standing mark, so the marks never fall into fives and must be counted one by one.

### 3 · Reading a tally in fives
**Screen:** a finished tally chart with its number column empty and the counting finger waiting
**Does:** runs the counting finger along each row, counting the bundles in fives and then the single marks
**Win:** each bundle lights as it is counted and the row's number drops into the number column
**Feels:** the bundles go by in fives, and the last few marks are counted on in ones
**Goal:** a tally is read by counting its bundles in fives and then the marks left over
**Observe:** The finger said five, ten, fifteen over the bundles, then counted the single marks in ones.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- counts each bundle as four, reading only the upright marks, so four bundles and three read as nineteen. → the counting finger lights each bundle whole, the fifth mark with it, and writes five, ten, fifteen, twenty over the bundles. It then counts on the single marks to twenty-three. The child's nineteen stays in the slot.
- carries on counting in fives over the single marks, so two bundles and three read as twenty-five. → the finger lights the two bundles with five and ten, then stops. It lights each single mark in ones, eleven, twelve, thirteen, and the row's number drops in as thirteen beside the child's twenty-five.
- counts a long row one mark at a time, loses the place, and counts one bundle twice, so three bundles and one read as twenty-one. → the finger lights each bundle once, leaving a small tick over it as it passes, and says five, ten, fifteen, then sixteen for the single mark. No bundle is lit twice. The child's twenty-one stays beside the sixteen.
**Misconception to break:** Counting each bundle as 4, because four marks stand upright, and missing the mark drawn across them.

### 4 · A tally chart and how many more
**Screen:** a tally chart with its numbers written, a question card, and the compare bar at the foot
**Does:** lines two rows up on the compare bar, counts the marks one row has beyond the other, then totals the chart
**Win:** the marks beyond the shorter row light, their count lands in the answer slot, and the total fills
**Feels:** two rows lined up from the same edge, and the difference sitting in plain view at the end of the longer one
**Goal:** how many more is the smaller count taken from the larger, and the total adds every row
**Observe:** In the compare bar the shorter row ran out, and the marks the longer row had left were banded and counted.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- answers how many more children chose cricket than kabaddi with sixteen, the number who chose cricket. → the two rows are laid in the compare bar, bundle under bundle. The kabaddi lane runs out at nine, and the seven marks the cricket lane has beyond it are banded sky blue and counted. The child's sixteen stays in the slot.
- adds the two rows for how many more, giving twenty-five for sixteen and nine. → the compare bar lines the two rows up, and only the marks beyond the shorter one are banded. Seven is written on the band, and the child's twenty-five stays beside it.
- adds a chart in which each child named two games, and calls the forty answers forty children. → the total cell adds the number column and is labelled answers. The belt's cards are then grouped by the child who gave them, two to each child, and the children come to twenty.
**Misconception to break:** Answering 16, the number who chose cricket, when the question asks how many more chose cricket than kabaddi.

## Delight
The second phase, whenever a bundle closes. For a moment the five marks of that bundle lift and fan out into the five answer cards that made them, face up, each with its name. Then they fold back down into the bundle. On a finished row a tap on any bundle does the same. It runs as often as a finger likes, nothing is marked either way, and the voice says nothing at all while it happens.

**General miss line:** Count the bundles in fives again, then count on the marks left over.

## Answer key
A tally mark is drawn for each thing counted, as it comes. Every fifth mark is drawn across the four before it, making a bundle of five, and the next mark starts a new bundle. A tally is read by counting the bundles in fives and then the marks left over in ones. A tally chart sets each name beside its marks and its number. How many more is found by taking the smaller count from the larger, and when each child gave one answer, the numbers add up to the number of children.

## Student controls
- **Answer belt**: brings answer cards one at a time along the top of the board
- **Row tap**: draws one mark in the row an answer belongs to and turns its card over
- **Fifth mark**: draws every fifth mark in a row across the four before it
- **Counting finger**: reads a row in fives, then the marks left over in ones
- **Number column**: writes each row's count beside its marks
- **Compare bar**: lines two rows up, bundle under bundle, to show how many more
- **Total**: adds the number column when each child gave one answer
