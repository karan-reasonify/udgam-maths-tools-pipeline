## What it is
**Description:** Sort things into grams or kilograms, level a kilogram against a thousand grams, and change weights between the two by a thousand only, keeping the remainder as grams.
**Why:** Sort things into grams or kilograms, see that a thousand grams make a kilogram, and change weights both ways by a thousand only.
**Look:** A pan balance with a shelf of things at the left, a converter with one number on it at the right, and a place board below.
**Context:** Grade 4, and the first time the chapter's pattern comes round again. An adult sees the length work done with a new unit. What is actually being taught is that this pair has one number only. The page's summary box after the weight conversions is the length box copied word for word: multiply or divide by 10, 100 or 1000. For grams and kilograms the only number is a thousand, and a child who follows the box writes three kilograms as three hundred grams.
**For the child:** Lift a thing onto the pan. Send it to grams or kilograms. Level the kilogram with gram weights. Multiply or divide by a thousand only. The remainder stays in grams.
**Teaching notes:** Let the class lift things in their hands and sort them into heavy and light before the tool is opened. Then balance a kilogram packet against smaller packets on a real pan balance if the school has one. The page pictures five weighing instruments and asks for no reading from any of them, and the one captioned as a digital scale is drawn with a dial. The tick table asks whether four things are weighed in kilograms or grams. The summary box after Examples 11 and 12 copies the length box word for word and offers ten, a hundred or a thousand; for grams the only number is a thousand. A child who follows the box writes three kilograms as three hundred grams. Say a thousand, and only a thousand, every time. The page writes a division by a thousand that leaves a remainder as equal to a weight in two units; the quotient is the kilograms and the remainder stays as grams. Example 11 starts from seven point three two five kilograms. Seven point three two five is seven and three hundred and twenty-five thousandths, and a thousandth of a kilogram is a gram, so it is seven kilograms three hundred and twenty-five grams. Ask nothing more with decimals. Exercise 11.3 has sixty-five kilograms forty-five grams, which is sixty-five thousand and forty-five grams, with a zero in the hundreds. Say weight throughout, the vocabulary box's word.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Grams or kilograms
**Screen:** the shelf of things, the grams tray and the kilograms tray
**Does:** lifts each thing onto the pan and sends it to the tray for its unit
**Win:** each thing settles in its tray, the light ones in grams and the heavy ones in kilograms
**Feels:** the balance tips toward the heavier thing, and heavy things go with kilograms
**Goal:** heavy things are weighed in kilograms and light things in grams
**Observe:** The keys' pan rode up against the kilogram weight, and the keys went to grams with the spoon and the letter.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- reads the pan holding the keys, riding high, as the heavier one, and sends the keys to kilograms. → the beam is drawn again with the small near-black arrow under it lit, pointing to the pan that has gone down, the kilogram's. The keys go to the grams tray beside the spoon and the letter, and the child's choice stays lit on the word pad, nothing marked.
- gives the sack of onions a weight of twenty grams, because twenty is a small, easy number. → twenty grams is set on the other pan as one small green weight, and the sack's pan stays down and does not stir. Kilogram weights then go on one at a time, and the sack's pan stays down under several of them, and the kilograms tray lights. The child's twenty grams stays on the pad beside it.
**Misconception to break:** Choosing the unit by the size of the number to be written, not by how heavy the thing is.

### 2 · A thousand grams make a kilogram
**Screen:** a kilogram weight on one pan and a pile of hundred gram weights beside the other
**Does:** adds hundred gram weights until the pans level, counts the grams, then passes a card in kilograms through the converter
**Win:** the pans level at a thousand grams, and the card comes out in grams
**Feels:** a kilogram and a thousand grams balance exactly, so there is one number to use
**Goal:** a kilogram is a thousand grams, so kilograms become grams by multiplying by a thousand, and only by a thousand
**Observe:** The beam levelled when the hundred gram weights on one pan came to a thousand grams.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes nine hundred grams for nine kilograms, multiplying by a hundred. → the converter's one number, a thousand, lights on its front. Nine kilogram weights go on one pan and nine hundred grams of hundred gram weights on the other, and the beam stays tipped hard toward the kilograms. The card then passes through, nine times a thousand, nine thousand grams, and the child's nine hundred stays in the slot, nothing marked.
- multiplies the kilograms and leaves the grams behind, writing four thousand grams for four kilograms three hundred and seventy-five grams. → the three hundred and seventy-five grams stay lit on the card at the converter's top after the four thousand have come out. They then drop through after it, already grams, and join the four thousand, and the card reads four thousand three hundred and seventy-five grams. The child's four thousand stays in the slot beside it.
**Misconception to break:** Multiplying by a hundred and writing 900 g, because the summary box offers ten, a hundred or a thousand; for grams the only number is a thousand.

### 3 · Grams into kilograms and grams
**Screen:** a card in grams alone, and the empty kilograms and grams slots
**Does:** passes the card back through the converter and reads the two slots
**Win:** the kilograms and the grams stand in their slots and rebuild the card together
**Feels:** the whole kilograms come out, and the grams that cannot make another kilogram stay grams
**Goal:** divide by a thousand; the quotient is the kilograms and the remainder stays as grams
**Observe:** Passed back through the converter, the whole kilograms landed in one slot and the grams left over stayed in the other.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- divides by a hundred and writes fifty-two kilograms forty grams for five thousand two hundred and forty grams. → the converter's one number, a thousand, lights, and the card passes back through it: five whole kilograms land in the kilograms slot and two hundred and forty grams stay in the grams slot. The child's fifty-two kilograms is set beside it as fifty-two kilogram weights, a heap far larger than the card, and the child's answer stays in the slot.
- writes five kilograms and stops, leaving the two hundred and forty grams out. → the grams slot holds two hundred and forty in green, lit, beside the five kilograms. The two slots are put back together through the converter, five thousand grams and two hundred and forty grams, and the card is whole again. The child's five kilograms stays in the slot, two hundred and forty grams short.
**Misconception to break:** Dividing by a hundred and writing 63 kg 85 g, as if grams joined kilograms the way centimetres join metres.

### 4 · A zero that holds a place in grams
**Screen:** a card in kilograms and a small number of grams, and the place board with four empty boxes
**Does:** passes the card into grams and writes it on the place board, one digit to a box
**Win:** a zero drops into every empty box and the grams read correctly
**Feels:** the empty places do not vanish; each one waits for a zero
**Goal:** when the grams do not fill three places, a zero holds each empty one
**Observe:** Six grams went into the ones box, and zeros dropped into the hundreds and the tens.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes five thousand six hundred grams for five kilograms six grams, setting the six straight after the five and filling the end with zeros. → the place board takes the five in its thousands box and the six in its ones box, because six grams are six ones, and grey zeros drop into the hundreds and the tens at full size: five thousand and six. The child's number is laid on a second board beside it, five hundred and ninety-four grams more, and both stay drawn.
- writes three hundred and forty grams for three kilograms forty grams, setting the digits side by side as they are said. → the child's three hundred and forty is laid on the place board, the three standing in the hundreds box, three hundred grams, less than one kilogram. The card then passes through, three thousand grams and forty more, and a grey zero drops into the empty hundreds box. The two boards stand side by side, two thousand seven hundred grams apart.
**Misconception to break:** Writing 79 g, or 7900 g, with no zeros holding the empty places.

## Delight
The second phase, the moment the last hundred gram weight goes on. The beam, tipped hard toward the kilogram, stays tipped as each weight is added, and on the last one it lifts, wobbles and settles dead level, and the counter under the pan reads one thousand grams. Then the kilogram weight and the pile of gram weights swap pans in one slow arc, and the beam stays level. It can be tried again as often as a finger likes, nothing is marked, and the voice says nothing at all while it happens.

**General miss line:** Lift it again and watch the balance, or read the one number on the converter.

## Answer key
Heavy things are weighed in kilograms and light things in grams. A thousand grams make a kilogram, and a thousand is the only number that joins grams and kilograms. Kilograms become grams by multiplying by a thousand, with any grams added on. Grams become kilograms and grams by dividing by a thousand: the quotient is the kilograms and the remainder stays as grams. Beside kilograms, grams take three places, and a zero holds any place the grams leave empty, so 8 kg 50 g is 8050 g.

## Student controls
- **Thing shelf**: holds the everyday things to be weighed
- **Pan balance**: levels a kilogram weight against gram weights added a hundred grams at a time
- **Grams and kilograms trays**: take the light things and the heavy things
- **Converter**: joins grams and kilograms by a thousand only
- **Weight card**: a weight in kilograms and grams, or in grams alone
- **Kilograms and grams slots**: take the quotient and the remainder after dividing by a thousand
- **Place board**: shows the grams in four places, with a zero in every empty one
