## What it is
**Description:** Fill containers to find their capacity, see why a count of glasses changes with the glass, and change capacities between litres and millilitres by a thousand only.
**Why:** Find out what capacity is, see why a count of glasses depends on the glass, and change capacities both ways by a thousand only.
**Look:** A tap over a row of containers at the left, a jug and two sets of glasses in the middle, and a converter with one number on it at the right.
**Context:** Grade 4, and the chapter's pattern for the third time. An adult sees the weight work done with a new unit. What is actually being taught is what capacity is, and why a count of glasses is not an exact one. The page pours a full jug into six glasses and calls that an estimated capacity. It is a measure in glasses, and a different glass gives a different count, so the exact capacity is measured in litres and millilitres. The page's summary box after Example 15 repeats the length box's 10, 100 or 1000, and for millilitres and litres the only number is a thousand.
**For the child:** Fill a container to the top. Pin on its capacity. Pour the jug into glasses and count. Change litres and millilitres by a thousand only. The remainder stays in millilitres.
**Teaching notes:** Pour a jug of water into small cups and then into large ones before the tool is opened, and write both counts on the board. ## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · What capacity is
**Screen:** the tap and the row of empty containers, with a height tag, a weight tag and a capacity tag waiting
**Does:** fills each container to the brim and pins the capacity tag on it, leaving the other two tags aside
**Win:** each full container wears its capacity tag, in millilitres or litres
**Feels:** a container's capacity is what fills it to the brim, whatever its shape
**Goal:** capacity is the quantity of liquid a container can hold, measured in millilitres and litres
**Observe:** Only the capacity tag would pin onto a full container, and the height and weight tags slid back.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- pins the height tag on the bucket as its capacity, because it is the biggest number on offer. → the height tag slides back to its place, and the words of the question, how much it holds, light under the slot. The bucket is held under the tap and fills with pale blue water to its brim, and the capacity tag, in litres, pins onto it. The child's height tag stays lit beside the row, nothing marked.
- says the tall thin bottle holds more than the short wide jug, because it is taller. → the bottle is filled to its brim and poured into the empty jug, and the water fills the jug only partway, its level stopping well short of the brim line. The jug is then filled from the tap to its brim, and both capacity tags pin on. The child's choice stays lit on the pad beside them.
**Misconception to break:** Taking the jug's height or weight as its capacity, because all three are measures of the same jug.

### 2 · Counting in glasses
**Screen:** one full jug with a row of small glasses and a row of large glasses
**Does:** pours the jug into the small glasses and counts, refills it, pours it into the large glasses and counts again
**Win:** the two counts stand side by side under one jug, and the measuring jug gives one reading in millilitres
**Feels:** the jug never changed; only the glasses did, and the count went with them
**Goal:** a count of glasses is a capacity in glasses, and glasses differ
**Observe:** The same full jug filled twelve small glasses and six large ones, and poured back it reached the same brim line both times.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- says the jug held more water the first time, because it filled twelve glasses then and only six the second time. → the glasses of both rows are tipped back into the empty jug in turn, and each time the water climbs to the same brim line and stops there. The two counts stand side by side under the one jug, and the child's choice stays lit on the word pad, nothing marked.
- writes that the jug holds twelve litres, because it filled twelve glasses. → the measuring jug takes one small glass and reads a hundred and fifty millilitres. Twelve such glasses are laid out as twelve bars of a hundred and fifty millilitres, one thousand eight hundred millilitres in all, which is one litre eight hundred millilitres, and the child's twelve litres stays in the slot beside it.
**Misconception to break:** Saying the kettle holds 4 or 8 as if that were litres, or that it held more water the second time.

### 3 · A thousand millilitres make a litre
**Screen:** a one litre bottle beside the measuring jug, and a card in litres and millilitres at the converter
**Does:** pours the litre into the measuring jug and reads it, then passes the card through the converter
**Win:** the measuring jug reads a thousand millilitres, and the card comes out in millilitres
**Feels:** a litre bottle fills the measuring jug to one line, and that line says a thousand
**Goal:** a litre is a thousand millilitres, so litres become millilitres by multiplying by a thousand, and only by a thousand
**Observe:** The litre bottle, poured into the measuring jug, stopped exactly on the thousand millilitres line.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- multiplies the litres by a hundred and writes one thousand and fifty millilitres for four litres six hundred and fifty millilitres. → the converter's one number, a thousand, lights. The litre bottle is poured into the measuring jug and stops on the thousand line, and four such bottles make four thousand millilitres. The six hundred and fifty are added on, four thousand six hundred and fifty, and the child's answer stays in the slot, nothing marked.
- multiplies the litres and leaves the millilitres behind, writing four thousand millilitres. → the six hundred and fifty millilitres stay lit on the card at the converter's top after the four thousand have come out. They then drop through after it, already millilitres, and join the four thousand. The child's four thousand stays in the slot beside four thousand six hundred and fifty millilitres.
- writes three thousand four hundred and fifty millilitres for three litres forty-five millilitres, setting the forty-five straight after the three. → the place board takes the three in its thousands box and the forty-five in its tens and ones, and a grey zero drops into the empty hundreds box at full size: three thousand and forty-five. The child's number is laid on a second board beside it, four hundred and five millilitres more, and both stay drawn.
**Misconception to break:** Multiplying the litres by a hundred and writing 900 mL and 340 mL, 1240 mL in all; for litres the only number is a thousand.

### 4 · Millilitres into litres and millilitres
**Screen:** a card in millilitres alone, and the empty litres and millilitres slots
**Does:** passes the card back through the converter and reads the two slots
**Win:** the litres and the millilitres stand in their slots and rebuild the card together
**Feels:** the whole litres come out, and what cannot make another litre stays in millilitres
**Goal:** divide by a thousand; the quotient is the litres and the remainder stays as millilitres
**Observe:** Passed back through the converter, the whole litres landed in one slot and the millilitres left over stayed in the other.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes eighty-six litres for eight thousand six hundred millilitres, dividing by a hundred. → the converter's one number, a thousand, lights, and the card passes back through it: eight whole litres land in the litres slot and six hundred millilitres stay in the millilitres slot. The child's eighty-six litres is drawn beside it as eighty-six litre bottles, a crowd far larger than the card, and the child's answer stays in the slot.
- writes eight litres and stops, leaving the six hundred millilitres out. → the millilitres slot holds six hundred in green, lit, beside the eight litres. The two slots are put back together through the converter, eight thousand and six hundred, and the card is whole again. The child's eight litres stays in the slot, six hundred millilitres short.
- reads three thousand and eighty millilitres as three litres eight hundred millilitres, putting the eight in the first place. → the millilitres slot writes the remainder as a number, eighty, and the place board shows the card's zero in its hundreds box, holding that place. The child's three litres eight hundred millilitres is rebuilt beside it, three thousand eight hundred, seven hundred and twenty more than the card, and both stay drawn.
**Misconception to break:** Reading the last three digits, 090, as 900 mL, or dividing by a hundred and writing 60 L 90 mL.

## Delight
The second phase, the moment both rows are poured back. The small glasses tip into the empty jug one after another, and the water climbs to the brim line and stops exactly on it; the jug is emptied into the large glasses and they are tipped back in turn, fewer of them, and the water climbs to the very same line. The two counts stand side by side under one jug. It can be poured back and forth as often as a finger likes, nothing is marked, and the voice says nothing at all while it happens.

**General miss line:** Pour it again and watch the level, or read the one number on the converter.

## Answer key
Capacity is the quantity of liquid a container can hold, measured in millilitres and litres. Counting how many glasses a full container fills gives its capacity in glasses, and glasses of different sizes give different counts, so the exact capacity is measured in litres and millilitres. A thousand millilitres make a litre, and a thousand is the only number that joins them. Litres become millilitres by multiplying by a thousand, with any millilitres added on. Millilitres become litres and millilitres by dividing by a thousand: the quotient is the litres and the remainder stays as millilitres. Beside litres, millilitres take three places, and a zero holds any place they leave empty.

## Student controls
- **Tap**: fills a container to its brim
- **Containers**: a bucket, a bottle, a jug, a mug and a spoon, each taking a capacity tag when full
- **Pouring bay**: pours one full jug into small or large glasses, a glass at a time
- **Glass counter**: counts each glass filled
- **Measuring jug**: marked in millilitres, to measure what one glass holds
- **Converter**: joins millilitres and litres by a thousand only
- **Litres and millilitres slots**: take the quotient and the remainder after dividing by a thousand
- **Place board**: shows the millilitres in four places, with a zero in every empty one
