## What it is
**Description:** Add and take away weights in kilograms and grams, three places for the grams, carrying or breaking a kilogram as a thousand grams, and say which is heavier and by how much.
**Why:** Add and take away weights in kilograms and grams, keeping three places for the grams, and answer which is heavier and by how much.
**Look:** A kilograms and grams frame in the middle, a shelf of tagged bags at the left, and a balance with a two-blank answer card at the right.
**Context:** Grade 4, and the length columns again with one joining number. An adult sees the column work repeated. What is actually being taught is that grams take three places beside kilograms whatever their number. Whole kilograms are written with three zeros in the grams column before anything can be taken from them, as the page's Example 14 does. The last exercise question asks who weighs more and how much more, and the answer is two things, the heavier one and the difference; the book's own answer key gives both.
**For the child:** Fill all three grams boxes. Work the grams first. Carry a kilogram for each thousand grams. Open a kilogram when the grams are too few. Say which is heavier, and by how much.
**Teaching notes:** Weigh two or three packets on a kitchen scale before the tool is opened and write each weight in kilograms and grams. Example 13 says to write forty-five grams as zero four five. Example 14 writes zero zero zero under the grams of five hundred kilograms before it borrows. Both are the three places that grams take beside kilograms. Every borrow in this topic breaks one kilogram into a thousand grams, and the kilograms go down by one. In Exercise 11.4 several additions have grams that come to more than a thousand, and two subtractions take away from whole kilograms. Its last question asks who weighs more and how much more, which is two answers, and the book's key gives both; do not accept the difference on its own. To say which weight is heavier, compare the kilograms first and look at the grams only when the kilograms are equal. Say weight throughout.

## Delight
The fourth phase, the moment a weight of exactly the difference is dropped onto the lighter bag's pan. The beam, tipped since the bags went on, lifts, wobbles and settles dead level, and the two pans hang side by side at one height. Take the weight off and the beam tips back. It can be tried with any two bags as often as a finger likes, nothing is marked, and the voice says nothing at all while it happens.

**General miss line:** Read the question again, fill all three grams boxes, and work the grams first.

## Answer key
Beside kilograms, grams take three places, and a short number of grams is written with zeros in front, so 45 g is 045. Add or take away the grams first. Each full thousand grams is carried as one kilogram. When the grams on top are fewer than the grams below, one kilogram is broken into a thousand grams. The kilograms on top go down by one and the grams on top grow by a thousand. A weight of whole kilograms is written with 000 in the grams column. To say which of two weights is heavier, compare the kilograms first, and the grams only when the kilograms are equal. A question that asks which is heavier and by how much needs both answers.
