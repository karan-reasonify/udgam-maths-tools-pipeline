## What it is
**Description:** Split the multiplier into place cards, open one rose row for each card, watch the holding zeros arrive under the columns, and add the rows.
**Why:** Use a multiplier of two or three digits one place at a time, and put the partial products back together into one answer.
**Look:** The multiplier standing whole above a row of empty place cards, with rose rows opening beneath them and a rule waiting under those.
**Context:** Grade 4, the heart of the chapter and its longest stretch. An adult sees long multiplication. What the page is actually doing is the same single digit multiplication the child already owns, done once for each place of the multiplier and then added up, with two special cases printed beside it: a place holding a zero is skipped rather than worked, and a round number is handled by its zeros rather than split. A partial product row with its zero missing is the most expensive slip in this chapter, because everything after it is added correctly onto a wrong number.
**For the child:** Tap the multiplier to split it into place cards before you multiply anything. Open one row for each card and let the zeros arrive as the row is built. If a card holds a zero, grey it and close its row without working it. Then close the rule and add the rows. If one of the two numbers is round, try the swap first.
**Teaching notes:** This is the longest stretch of the chapter and it is one method, not four. Lay the place cards down before anything is multiplied, every time, so the number of rows is decided before the first digit is worked. Use the chapter's own examples in order. The two digit one multiplies 5632 by 23 as 5632 x 3 = 16,896 and 5632 x 20 = 1,12,640, which add to 1,29,536. The three digit one multiplies 658 by 151 as 658, then 32,900, then 65,800, which add to 99,358. Then the two that decide whether the method has been understood rather than copied. Multiplying 3134 by 205 has a zero in the tens place of the multiplier, so that step is skipped and not worked: two rows only, 15,670 and 6,26,800, adding to 6,42,470. And 7000 multiplied by 140 comes to 9,80,000 with no place split at all, because a round number is handled by its zeros. Make them point at the zero holding the ones column of the tens row and say in their own words why the zero has to be there. A row with its zero missing is the slip that costs the most here, because everything after it is added correctly onto a wrong number.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · A long multiplier is used one place at a time
**Screen:** a two digit multiplier sitting whole with empty place cards below it
**Does:** splits the multiplier into its tens and its ones and lays the cards out
**Win:** the cards hold the tens and the ones and the whole multiplier stays lit above them
**Feels:** the multiplier looks like the easy number and the multiplicand like the awkward one, and it is the easy one that has to come apart
**Goal:** the multiplier is split by place into cards before anything is multiplied
**Observe:** The multiplier came apart into cards and the number of rows was settled before a single digit had been multiplied.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- splits the multiplicand instead of the multiplier, breaking 5632 into 5000, 600, 30 and 2 and leaving 23 standing whole. → the two splits are laid out side by side on the chart and both are worked to the end. Splitting the multiplicand opens four rows, 1,15,000 and 13,800 and 690 and 46, and every one of them still wanted a two digit multiplier, which is the job that was too big to begin with. Splitting the multiplier opens two, 16,896 and 1,12,640, and each of those is a single digit multiplication the child already owns. Both sets add to 1,29,536, both are left standing, and neither is marked.
- reads the digits of the multiplier as they are printed, so 23 becomes a card holding 2 and a card holding 3, and the rows come to 11,264 and 16,896 for a total of 28,160. → the place names slide up under the two cards and the tens card is read aloud as two tens; its face rewrites itself from 2 to 20 while the ones card is left exactly as it is. The row that card had opened empties and is built again from its right hand end, the grey zero walking into the ones column first, and it comes to 1,12,640. The child's answer stays in the slot.
**Misconception to break:** Treating the 3 of 32 as three ones, so the tens row is worked as 4213 x 3 with no zero at its end.

### 2 · Each place gives one row, and the rows are added
**Screen:** the workshop with one rose row open for each place card and a rule under them
**Does:** works each place into its own row, then closes the rule and adds the rows
**Win:** every row fills in rose with a zero holding each of its right hand columns open and the rows add to the product
**Feels:** a row that ends in two zeros looks like a mistake, and then the two zeros turn out to have come from two different places
**Goal:** one partial product per place, and the partial products are added together
**Observe:** The tens row of 658 x 151 came out as 32,900, which ends in two zeros, and the row above it ended in none.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- leaves the holding zero off the tens row, writing 5632 x 2 as 11,264 under the ones row, and adds 16,896 and 11,264 to hand in 28,160. → the chart headings light one at a time from the ones and the tens row is put back where it belongs, its right hand digit standing in the tens column rather than in the ones. The grey zero then walks into the ones column underneath it, holding open the one place that row was never going to fill, and the row reads 1,12,640. Nothing is cleared and the answer stays in the slot.
- rubs a zero off the tens row of 658 x 151, because the rule heard was that a tens row ends in one zero and this row ended in two, so 32,900 is handed in as 3290 and the three rows add to 69,748. → the tens row is drawn again from its right hand end with its two zeros put down a moment apart. The grey one walks into the ones column first, because that column is being held open and nothing is going into it. Then the short fact is worked above the row, 658 x 5 = 3290, and its own zero comes down in rose and lands beside the grey one. Two zeros stand side by side in two colours, and the headings are read across so the row is seen to begin in the tens column, which is the only thing the rule was ever about.
**Misconception to break:** Leaving the zero off the tens row and adding 9372 and 6248, which gives 15,620.

### 3 · A zero place is a step you skip
**Screen:** a three digit multiplier with a zero in its tens place and three empty rows waiting
**Does:** greys the zero card, closes its row and works only the rows that are left
**Win:** the zero card greys, its row closes unworked and the rows that remain add to the product
**Feels:** a step you are allowed to leave out feels like cheating, right up until you look at what that step would have added
**Goal:** a place of the multiplier that holds a zero is skipped rather than worked
**Observe:** The card holding a zero greyed, its row closed without being worked, and the total did not move.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- works the zero place as a row anyway, writing 3134 x 0 as a line of zeros with a holding zero behind it, and adds it in with the rest. → the two workings stand side by side with a rule closed under each, and both add to 6,42,470. The row of zeros is then greyed and closed to a thin line with the word skipped printed along it, and the shorter working is the one left standing. The rows below the closed line do not move up. Neither working is marked.
- skips the zero place and then moves the hundreds row up into the tens column, so 3134 x 200 is written as 62,680 with one holding zero instead of 6,26,800 with two, and the total comes to 78,350 instead of 6,42,470. → the places of the multiplier are named along the chart headings and the two hundred is read out as two hundreds, which is still a hundreds place whether or not the tens above it was ever worked. The hundreds row is built again from its right hand end, two grey zeros walking into the ones and the tens columns before the short fact 3134 x 2 = 6268 is written in above them, and the row reads 6,26,800 beginning in the hundreds column. The skipped line stays closed where it was.
**Misconception to break:** Writing a row of zeros for the tens place and adding it in, which lengthens the work without changing the answer.

### 4 · A round multiplier is better swapped than split
**Screen:** a round number standing where the multiplier goes, with the swap control lit beside it
**Does:** rearranges the two numbers and uses the zeros instead of splitting by place
**Win:** the swap lands, one short fact is worked and the zeros follow it without a single row being opened
**Feels:** swapping the two numbers feels as though it has to change something, and the only thing it changes is how much writing there is
**Goal:** a round number is handled by its zeros rather than split into places, and swapping is allowed because it leaves the product alone
**Observe:** 7000 went into the multiplier position and one short fact with its zeros finished the whole thing in a single row.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- splits the round number by place anyway, laying four cards for 7000 and opening four rows, three of which hold nothing. → the four cards go down and three of them grey the instant their place names are read, because those places hold nothing; the fourth card turns out to be 7000 itself, which is the whole number and not a part of it. The rows close back to one line, the short fact 140 x 7 = 980 is worked in it, and the three zeros of 7000 open to its right one at a time, leaving 9,80,000. Nothing is cleared.
- reads the swap as getting the round number out of the multiplier position, so 7000 goes on top and 140 is split into a hundreds card and a tens card. → the two arrangements are worked side by side to the end. With 7000 on top, the multiplier 140 still comes apart by place and two long rows, 2,80,000 and 7,00,000, have to be written and then added. With 140 on top, the multiplier 7000 is never split at all: one short fact, 140 x 7 = 980, with the three zeros of 7000 opening after it, finishes the job in a single row. Both land on 9,80,000, and the zeros are only able to do that work from where the multiplier stands.
**Misconception to break:** Splitting 3000 into three separate places and working three rows, two of which hold nothing.

## Delight
The second phase, the moment the tens row of 658 x 151 is built. The grey zero walks into the ones column and stands there alone for a beat with the rest of the row still empty, because that column is only being held open. Then the short fact is worked above it, 658 x 5 = 3290, and its digits come down into the row from the right, so its own zero lands in rose immediately to the left of the grey one. Two zeros end up side by side in two colours, arriving from two different places a moment apart, and the row reads 32,900. Nothing else moves and the voice says nothing while it happens.

**General miss line:** Lay the cards down again and read which column each row begins in.

## Answer key
Split the multiplier by place before anything is multiplied, so the number of rows is settled first. Each place gives one partial product, and each row starts one column further left than the row above it: the ones row starts in the ones column, the tens row starts in the tens column with a zero holding its ones column open, and the hundreds row starts in the hundreds column with zeros holding its ones and its tens columns open. Read those zeros off the columns rather than by counting the zeros at the end of a row, because a row can finish on a zero of its own as well. Where a place of the multiplier holds a zero, that step is skipped rather than worked, since taking the number no tens of times adds nothing. The partial products are added together at the end to give the product. Where one of the two numbers is round, it is handled by its zeros and never split into places, and the two may be rearranged because swapping them never changes the product.

## Student controls
- **Split the multiplier**: breaks the multiplier into one card for each of its places
- **Open a row**: gives a place card its own rose row in the working area
- **Holding zeros**: writes the zeros that hold a row's right hand columns open as the row is built
- **Name the place**: labels each rose row with the place of the multiplier that made it
- **Grey a zero card**: dims a place holding a zero and closes its row without working it
- **Swap**: rearranges the two numbers so a round one can be handled by its zeros
- **Add the rows**: closes the rule under the rose rows and adds them into the product

## Textbook (this topic)
**Pages:** 44, 45, 46, 47
**Key terms:** partial product, expanded form, multiplier, place
A multiplier of two or three digits is split by place, and the partial products are added. Split it by place. A multiplier of tens and ones becomes a tens part and a ones part, and each part is used separately. You already know how to multiply by a single digit, so each part is work you can do. Each one is a partial product, and they are added together to give the product. The tens row holds a zero in the ones column and the hundreds row holds zeros in the ones and the tens, so each row starts one column further left. Nothing at all, because taking the number no tens of times adds nothing. So that step is skipped rather than worked, and the rows that remain are the only ones added. There is no need. A round number is handled by its zeros, never split. Swapping leaves the product alone, so make the round one the multiplier and use its zeros in a single step.
