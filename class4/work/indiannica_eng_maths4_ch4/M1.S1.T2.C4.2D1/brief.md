## What it is
**Description:** A place value mat where one digit multiplies every column in turn, and the digit that will not fit waits in a ring above the next column.
**Why:** Multiply a long number by a single digit, one column at a time, and find out where the part that will not fit has to wait.
**Look:** A ruled column mat with a heading and a small empty ring over every column, one long number across it and a single digit below its ones.
**Context:** Grade 4, the first working topic of the multiplication chapter. An adult sees a long multiplication being filled in, which a child could do on squared paper. What the page is actually doing is printing the carries as small ringed digits above the columns they join, and that position is the lesson. The carried digit is added after the next column has been multiplied, never before it, and a child who reverses those two steps gets a wrong answer by a method that looks right from the outside.
**For the child:** Name the three rows before you start. Tap the ones column first and work leftwards. When a column passes nine, only one digit stays and the rest goes into the ring above the next column. Multiply that next column first, then add what is waiting in its ring. Open the doubling mat at the end and check the last digit.
**Teaching notes:** Say the three names before any working starts, and keep saying them: the multiplicand, the multiplier, the product. The vocabulary list near the end of the printed chapter asks for them back, and a child who has only ever called them the top number and the bottom number has nothing to give. Work the chapter's own chart first, the one that multiplies 2613 by 8 and comes to 20,904, and read its rings off the page before anything is tapped: the circled digits sit above the tens, the hundreds and the thousands columns, and nothing sits above the ones or above the ten thousands. Then spend the time where this method actually fails. Make them say the order out loud on every column, in these words: multiply this column, then add what is waiting above it. A child who adds first and multiplies afterwards gets an answer that is wrong by a method that looks right, and a cross on a page never tells them which of the two steps slipped. Keep the doubling mat open for the last minute of every session. It is the second thing the self assessment asks for, and the even last digit is a check a child can run alone.

## Opening (first frame of every mount)
Every mount comes up already furnished for its phase with nothing in front of it. On the first phase a four digit number lies across the mat in deep blue with one amber digit under its ones column, the row beneath the rule empty, and three naming plates waiting on the ledge below. On the second the mat carries a multiplier small enough that no column will pass nine, so every ring above the headings stands empty and will stay empty. On the third a column has already come to more than nine and one vermilion digit is sitting in the ring above the column on its left, with that column not yet multiplied. On the fourth the doubling mat stands open beside the main one, the same number written twice with a plus between them and nothing under either rule. The running count at the top left holds the columns worked against the columns the mat is ruled for, and the answer slot and the pad stand empty down the right. ## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Every multiplication has three names
**Screen:** the mat holding a four digit number with a single digit under its ones column and the row under the rule still empty
**Does:** drops a label on each of the three rows and reads them back
**Win:** all three labels land on the right rows and the naming strip reads them out
**Feels:** the same three words fit any multiplication on the mat, whatever the two numbers in it are
**Goal:** the three parts of a multiplication can be pointed at and named
**Observe:** The one amber digit was used once on every column of the mat, and never once on the whole number at a stroke.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- names the single digit as the multiplicand, because the long number looks too big to be the one being handled. → the plates the child laid stay exactly where they were put, and the mat reads its own three rows aloud from the top: the number lying across the columns is the one being multiplied, the digit sitting under its ones column is the one it is multiplied by, and the empty row under the rule is where the answer will stand. Nothing is moved for the child and nothing is cleared.
- lays the third plate on the multiplier's row, because the row under the rule is still empty and looks like nowhere to put a name. → the row under the rule keeps its empty squares and is outlined on its own while the rule above it is drawn heavier, then the mat works a single column so that one green digit lands in that row. The plate the child put on the multiplier lifts a little and both rows are read out again, the one that was named and the one that now holds part of an answer.
**Misconception to break:** Calling 6 the multiplicand because it is the smaller of the two numbers.

### 2 · One digit multiplies every column in turn
**Screen:** the mat with a four digit number and a multiplier small enough that no column passes nine
**Does:** taps the ones column, then each column to its left, and counts the taps
**Win:** every column is worked from the right and the product stands complete under the rule
**Feels:** one small digit, used over and over, and the long number worked a column at a time
**Goal:** the single digit is used once on every column, starting at the ones
**Observe:** On the chapter's own chart, 2613 by 8, rings filled above the thousands, the hundreds and the tens, and none above the ones or the ten thousands.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- multiplies the leftmost column first and works rightwards, the way the number is read. → every digit the child wrote stays under the rule exactly where it was put. The ones first control then locks itself, the same two numbers are worked again from the right with one column lighting at a time, and the two runs stand one under the other with the order of work numbered beside each column. Nothing is cleared.
- multiplies the ones column and copies the rest of the multiplicand straight down into the product row. → the multiplier card lifts out of its place and travels slowly along the foot of the mat, stopping under each column in turn and lighting the pair it makes with the digit standing above it, then returns home. The copied digits stay exactly where they are while that happens, and each one is stood beside the product of the pair it should have been.
**Misconception to break:** Multiplying only the first digit of the multiplicand and copying the other digits straight down.

### 3 · The carried digit is added, not multiplied
**Screen:** a column that has come to more than nine, with the ring above the column on its left already filled
**Does:** multiplies the next column on its own, then adds the digit waiting in the ring above it
**Win:** the column closes on the right digit and the ring empties into it
**Feels:** the digit in the ring waits, and what it waits for is the multiplying to finish
**Goal:** the next column is multiplied first and the carried digit is added afterwards
**Observe:** Multiplying the column first and adding the ring afterwards gave one answer, and adding the ring first gave another, from the same two numbers.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- adds the digit waiting in the ring to the column's own digit and multiplies that total. → the ring lifts out of its place and hangs clear, still holding its digit, while the column underneath it is multiplied on its own and that product is written in the open where the ring was; only then does the ring come down and add itself in. Both readings are then left standing together on the tens column of 2613 by 8: multiplying first gives 1 x 8 = 8, and the waiting 2 makes 10, so 0 stays in the column and 1 goes up into the next ring, while adding first gives 1 + 2 = 3 and then 3 x 8 = 24, so 4 stays and 2 goes up. Nothing is cleared and nothing is marked.
- writes the whole column total inside the column, both of its digits in the one square. → the column narrows to a single square that will hold one digit only, the right hand digit of the total settles into it, and the rest rises visibly into the ring above the column on its left, the ring lighting as it arrives. The child's two digits stay drawn faintly where they were put, so the one that left can be seen leaving.
**Misconception to break:** Adding the carried 2 to the 6 first and multiplying the total, which puts 40 in the tens instead of 32.

### 4 · Doubling is multiplying by two
**Screen:** the doubling mat holding the same number twice with a plus between them, beside the main mat
**Does:** adds the number to itself on one mat and multiplies it by two on the other
**Win:** both mats land on the same number and its last digit lights as an even one
**Feels:** adding it to itself and taking it twice turn out to be one move under two names
**Goal:** adding a number to itself and multiplying it by two are the same move
**Observe:** The doubling mat and the main mat landed on the same number, and that number ended in an even digit every time.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes the number down twice side by side and reads the pair as its double. → the two numbers stay where they were written and a rule is drawn under the pair as it stands, so the whole run of digits is read out as one very long number and its size is spoken; then the second number slides down under the first, the columns are worked from the right, and the two readings stand one above the other.
- doubles only the leading digit of the number and copies the rest of it down unchanged. → the number is laid out along the mat as equal groups, one group under each column, then a second identical row of groups is laid under the first, and the two rows are counted column by column from the right so that every place is seen to be taken twice. The child's answer stays in the slot beside the one the mat builds, and the first column from the right where the two part is lit amber and stays lit.
**Misconception to break:** Writing 3416 twice side by side instead of adding it to itself.

## Delight
The third phase, the moment a child reaches for the filled ring before the column under it has been multiplied. Nothing is refused and nothing is marked. The ring lifts out of its place and hangs there, still holding its vermilion digit, while the column beneath it is multiplied on its own in the space the ring has left, the product of that pair written out in the open where the ring was sitting. Only when that is done does the ring come down and add itself in. The ring can be reached for early as often as the child likes and it does the same thing every single time, unhurried, with no line spoken over it.

**General miss line:** Lift the ring clear and multiply the column underneath it on its own.

## Answer key
Name the three parts first: the multiplicand is the number being multiplied, the multiplier is the number you multiply it by, and the product is the answer under the rule. Start at the ones column and work leftwards, using the single digit on every column in turn. When a column comes to more than nine, only its right hand digit stays in the column and the rest goes into the ring above the column on its left. That carried digit is added afterwards, once the next column has been multiplied, and never before it. Doubling is adding a number to itself, which is the same as multiplying it by two, and a doubled number always ends in an even digit.

## Student controls
- **Name the parts**: labels the three rows as the multiplicand, the multiplier and the product
- **Tap a column**: multiplies the tapped column and writes its digit under the rule
- **Ones first**: locks the working order so the rightmost column lights first
- **Carry ring**: takes the digit that will not fit and holds it above the next column
- **Hold the ring**: keeps the ring clear until the column beneath it has been multiplied
- **Doubling mat**: opens a second mat that adds the number to itself beside multiplying it by two
- **Even check**: lights the last digit of a doubled number and sorts it as odd or even

## Textbook (this topic)
**Pages:** 40, 41
**Key terms:** multiplicand, multiplier, product, doubling
The parts of a multiplication are named, and a long number is multiplied by a single digit. The number being multiplied is the multiplicand. The number you multiply by is the multiplier. The answer is the product. Naming them makes it easier to say what a question is asking for. Once for every column. The single digit multiplies the ones, then the tens, then the hundreds, and so on leftwards. The work starts at the ones because anything that spills over moves to the left. It is written above the next column to the left. When that column is reached, it is multiplied first and the carried digit is added afterwards. Adding it before multiplying gives the wrong answer. They are the same, and adding a number to itself is called doubling. Every doubled number ends in an even digit, because the ones column has been added to itself.
