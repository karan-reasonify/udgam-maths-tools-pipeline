# Time Converter: the owner's description (2026-10-28). Read with brief.md and ../NOTES.md "Owner decisions".

Develop an interactive tool that helps students convert hours into minutes and days into hours, calculate how long an activity takes, and determine the ending time of an activity.

Use familiar situations such as a train journey, a school activity or a holiday. Display a wall clock, a stopwatch, a row of minute tiles, a day strip containing 24 hour tiles and a movable bar representing the duration of an activity.

Divide the activity into three stages.

Stage 1: Converting Hours into Minutes. Students turn the minute hand around the clock and watch twelve groups of five-minute tiles fill one by one. Show that one complete round represents 60 minutes or one hour. Allow students to enter a number of hours and calculate the equivalent number of minutes by multiplying by 60.

Stage 2: Converting Days into Hours. Display a day wheel or a strip of 24 hour tiles. Students turn the wheel once for each day and watch the total number of hours increase. Allow students to convert one or more days into hours by multiplying the number of days by 24.

Stage 3: Calculating Duration and Ending Time. Display a starting time and an activity duration on a timeline. Students drag a bar from the starting time to the ending time and calculate how long the activity lasts or when it finishes. Include activities that cross noon. For these, divide the timeline into the part before noon and the part after noon, then combine the two durations.

Provide a number pad for entering answers and show the calculation step by step after each response. If students add the hours and minutes as though they were one number, or forget to convert hours into minutes, highlight the relevant tiles and give a simple hint. Allow students to try again.

Visual and Interaction Requirements: Keep the clock, conversion tiles and timeline clear and large enough to use easily. The number of filled tiles must match the displayed quantity. The duration bar should move when dragged, and the ending time should update accordingly.

Include new examples for practice, a reset option and a free-exploration mode. Keep the activities aligned with the chapter and use simple explanations so Grade 3 students understand why they are multiplying by 60 or 24 and how a duration is counted across noon.
