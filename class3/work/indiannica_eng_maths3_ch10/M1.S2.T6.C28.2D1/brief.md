## What it is
**Description:** Flip a real wall calendar, say the months along two fists, find the leap year, and hop seven days at a time to find a day or a date.
**Why:** Count the days of a week and the months of a year on a real wall calendar, say the days of each month by the knuckle method, find the leap year as every fourth year, and hop seven days at a time to find a day, a date and the weeks between two dates.
**Look:** A classroom wall with a real calendar page of 2026 at the left, the month cards, the two fists, the four-year strip and the week counter at the right as each phase needs them, a pencil, flip arrows, a Grid view button, and the number pad down the right of the canvas.
**Context:** Grade 3, the last working topic of the time chapter. An adult sees a calendar being flipped, which a paper calendar can do. What is being settled is that a week is always seven days so the same weekday returns every seventh day, that months take turns on the knuckles and in the dips, and that a leap year comes every fourth year and not at random. A child who counts the days between two dates by counting both ends, or who counts forward without taking away the first day, will be one day out and will not see why.
**For the child:** Flip the pages of the calendar. Say the months along the knuckles. Find the leap year. Hop seven days to find the same day.
**Teaching notes:** Hang a real wall calendar in the room before the tool opens, and let the class find their own birthdays and the first day of the year. Say the knuckle months along fists together, and ask each child to count the days of one month for themselves. Fold a paper strip into four years and mark the leap year to show why one February is longer. Insist on counting the days between two dates by taking the first date away, and wait for a child to say why the same day comes back after seven days, rather than saying it for them. If a child says a year is exactly fifty-two weeks, ask how many days that is. Use fresh dates: the printed exercise asks about the calendar of the year, and the child's own answers come from the page in front of them.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Days, weeks, months and a year
**Screen:** the 2026 wall calendar open at its first month with a row of twelve month cards beside it and the week counter blank
**Does:** taps a row to count its days, flips through the twelve pages, then counts the weeks in a year and the one day left over
**Win:** the twelve pages stack into a year and one extra day sits after the last full week
**Feels:** the pages of the year flipping past, and one day left over after the last full week
**Goal:** a week has seven days, a year has twelve months and is about fifty-two weeks and one more day
**Observe:** Every seventh date down one column lit in the same colour on the calendar page.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- says a year is exactly fifty-two weeks → the week counter fills with fifty-two full rows of seven, and one lone day is left lit amber on the last page, labelled Left, with the count of days written beside it
- counts the days of the year by adding the twelve months and forgetting that they are not equal → each month card shows its own number of days when it is tapped, and the counts are added in turn as the pages are flipped
**Misconception to break:** Adding 4 and 7 to get 11 days instead of making 4 groups of 7 days.

### 2 · How many days in a month: the knuckle method
**Screen:** two drawn fists with four knuckles and three dips on the first and three knuckles and two dips on the second, and twelve month cards waiting
**Does:** says each month along the fists, drops the card on a knuckle or a dip, and reads the number of days
**Win:** the card lands and its number of days appears, and February is left alone with its two numbers
**Feels:** the months falling onto knuckles and dips in turn, with February left alone
**Goal:** months take turns on the knuckles and in the dips: a knuckle month has thirty-one days and a dip month thirty
**Observe:** A card dropped on a knuckle read thirty-one and on a dip read thirty.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- says a dip month has thirty-one days → the month card is dropped on the dip and its number reads thirty, and the knuckle months light in turn along the fist with thirty-one beside each
- counts the knuckles and dips as if the months sat in two separate rows → the months are said along the first fist and then, without a gap, along the second, so July and August both sit on a knuckle and the count carries on
**Misconception to break:** Calling July a dip month because it comes after June, which is a dip, and forgetting to count along the knuckles from January.

### 3 · Leap year: every fourth year has 366 days
**Screen:** a four-year strip of small wall calendars with their Februaries side by side, and a year ruler from nineteen hundred to two thousand ninety-nine
**Does:** counts in fours on the year ruler from a leap year, taps the February that grows a twenty-ninth square, and finds a year from the last and the next leap years
**Win:** the twenty-ninth square drops into the leap February and the year ruler lights every fourth year
**Feels:** the extra day dropping into one February in four
**Goal:** every fourth year is a leap year, and its February has an extra day and the year has one day more
**Observe:** One February on the four-year strip grew a twenty-ninth square, once in four years.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- says every year that ends in an even number is a leap year → the year ruler lights every fourth year only, and the years between it stay dark even when their numbers are even
- counts in threes or fives instead of fours from a leap year → the marker steps four years at a time along the ruler, and the February on the strip grows its extra square only at every fourth step
**Misconception to break:** Saying February always has 28 days, whatever the year.

### 4 · Finding a day and a date on a calendar
**Screen:** a calendar page with a pencil, a seven-day hop and a week counter, and one date already circled
**Does:** circles a date, hops seven days at a time down the column, then counts on to the date asked for or counts the weeks between two circled dates
**Win:** the hops light the same weekday down the column and the week counter shows the weeks between the two dates
**Feels:** the same weekday lighting down the column, one week at a time
**Goal:** the same day of the week comes back every seven days, so a day or a date is found by hopping in sevens
**Observe:** The week counter counted fifty-two weeks in a year and one day was left over.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- counts the days between two dates including the first date → the first date is ringed near-black and left out of the count, the days are counted from the next date on, and the week counter shows the total
- hops six days or eight days instead of seven → each hop lands on a date lit in one colour, and a hop of six or eight leaves a different weekday lit, so the seventh is seen to be the one that stays
**Misconception to break:** Counting 31 days and forgetting to take away the first 3 days.

## Delight
On the fourth phase, when the pencil circles a date, the seven-day hop jumps down the calendar one week at a time, lighting the same weekday in the same colour with a small tick each time it lands. On the third phase, the leap February squeezes in its twenty-ninth square with a soft click, and the other three Februaries stay as they were.

**General miss line:** Count again, one week or one month at a time.

## Answer key
A week has seven days and a year has twelve months. A year is about fifty-two weeks and one more day, because fifty-two weeks is three hundred and sixty-four days and a year has three hundred and sixty-five. A month has twenty-eight, twenty-nine, thirty or thirty-one days. On the knuckles, a knuckle month has thirty-one days, a dip month has thirty days, and February has twenty-eight, or twenty-nine in a leap year. Every fourth year is a leap year, with twenty-nine days in February and three hundred and sixty-six days in the year. Adding seven days to a date gives the same day of the week, so the days between two dates are changed into weeks by counting in sevens, and a date a few days after a known day is found by counting on from that day.
