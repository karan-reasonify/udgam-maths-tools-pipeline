# Read the Clock: the owner's description (2026-10-28). Read with brief.md and ../NOTES.md "Owner decisions".

Develop an interactive clock-reading tool that helps students understand the hour hand, minute hand and different ways of reading time. Display a large wall clock with a short red hour hand and a long blue minute hand. Place a digital clock beside it so students can see how the same time appears in both formats. Students should be able to drag the clock hands and observe the digital time changing automatically.

Divide the activity into three stages.

Stage 1: Understanding the Clock Hands. Students drag the short and long hands separately and identify which hand shows hours and which shows minutes. Highlight the corresponding part of the digital clock when a hand moves.

Stage 2: Understanding Minutes. Allow students to move the minute hand from one number to the next while counting in steps of five. Show the minutes increasing as the hand moves around the clock. Demonstrate that twelve groups of five minutes make 60 minutes, which is one hour.

Stage 3: Reading Time in Different Ways. Students practise reading times using minutes past, minutes to, half past, quarter past and quarter to. Include a plain clock face, a minutes view and a past-and-to view. Ask students to read a displayed time and set the hands to match a given time.

Use new practice times rather than repeating only the examples from the concept videos.

Provide immediate feedback after each response. If a student confuses the hour hand with the minute hand or reads the number on the minute hand as the number of minutes, highlight the relevant hand and demonstrate the correct reading. Allow students to try again.

Visual and Interaction Requirements: Use a clean, easy-to-read clock face with twelve numbers and clearly visible minute markings. The hour hand must move gradually as the minute hand turns. Keep the digital clock synchronised with the hands at all times. Use familiar clock visuals and simple instructions suitable for Grade 3 students. Include a reset button and a free-exploration mode where students can set their own times. The tool should make learning active by allowing students to move the hands, observe changes and practise reading time independently.
