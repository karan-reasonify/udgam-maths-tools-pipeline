# Morning or Night: the owner's description (2026-10-28). Read with brief.md and ../NOTES.md "Owner decisions".

Develop an interactive tool that helps students understand a.m. and p.m., the 24 hours in a day, and the meanings of noon and midnight.

Display a horizontal day strip that begins at midnight, passes through noon and ends at the next midnight. Use a sky-blue section for the time before noon and an amber section for the time after noon. Include a wall clock, a sun, a moon and familiar scenes such as a school morning and a street at night.

Divide the activity into three stages.

Stage 1: Understanding the Length of a Day. Students turn the hour hand around the clock and observe that it makes two complete rounds during one day. Show the hours accumulating on the day strip until all 24 hours are covered.

Stage 2: Understanding Noon and Midnight. Students move a sun or moon marker along the day strip. When the marker reaches 12 o'clock in the daytime, identify the time as noon. When it reaches 12 o'clock at night, identify it as midnight. Help students understand that both are shown as 12 o'clock but refer to different parts of the day.

Stage 3: Using a.m. and p.m. Display everyday situations, such as going to school in the morning, eating lunch, playing in the evening and sleeping at night. Students select the correct time and choose a.m. or p.m. using interactive buttons. Include activities in which students complete a time card by entering the time and selecting the correct label.

Show the correct answer with a clear visual explanation. If students select the wrong label, highlight the time's position on the day strip and provide a simple hint. Explain that noon and midnight are named separately and should not be labelled with a.m. or p.m. in this tool.

Visual and Interaction Requirements: Keep the day strip, clock and sun or moon visible and easy to understand. When students move the time marker, update the clock and the a.m./p.m. section together. Use simple labels, large controls and uncluttered visuals.

Include a free-exploration mode where students can select different times of day and observe whether they belong to a.m. or p.m. The tool should connect the 24-hour day to familiar daily routines rather than relying only on memorising rules.
