## What it is
**Description:** Sweep the hour hand twice round a day, find noon and midnight, and drop each time on its morning or evening half.
**Why:** See that a day is two rounds of the hour hand and so has 24 hours, name 12 o'clock in the day noon and 12 o'clock at night midnight, and write a time with a.m. before noon and p.m. after it.
**Look:** A day strip from midnight to midnight with a sky blue morning half and an amber evening half, a wall clock above it, a sun and a moon, picture cards, a time card with a.m. and p.m. buttons, and the number pad down the right of the canvas.
**Context:** Grade 3, the second working topic of the time chapter. An adult sees a rule being learnt, a.m. before noon and p.m. after it, which a printed pair of clocks states. What is being settled is that one clock face shows two different times of a day, that a day has twenty-four hours because the hour hand goes round twice, and that the twelve of the day and the twelve of the night have names of their own. A child who reads a small number as a.m. will call five in the evening a.m., and a child who writes 12 with p.m. has not yet seen that noon stands alone.
**For the child:** Turn the hour hand round the clock twice. Find noon and midnight on the day strip. Drop each time on its half. Write each time with a.m. or p.m.
**Teaching notes:** Give each child a long paper strip before the tool opens. Ask them to mark noon in the middle, colour the morning half sky blue and the evening half amber, and stick picture cards of their own day on it, writing each time with a.m. or p.m. Ask which things happen before noon and which after, and wait for a child to say why noon and midnight take no label rather than saying it for them. If a child calls a small number a.m., ask whether the sun is up before or after noon. Use fresh times: the printed clocks and sentences are the child's to read and write.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · A day has twenty-four hours
**Screen:** a day strip from one midnight to the next with a wall clock above it and its hour hand waiting at the twelve
**Does:** turns the hour hand once round, then a second time, and counts the hours of each round
**Win:** the second round ends, the day strip fills from end to end and a green tag reads one whole day
**Feels:** the hour hand sweeping round twice while the sun crosses the whole strip
**Goal:** the hour hand goes round the clock twice in a day, twelve hours and twelve hours, so a day has twenty-four hours
**Observe:** The hour hand went once round and the sun was only at noon, and it went round again before the sun reached the far end.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- says a day has 12 hours because the clock face shows 12 numbers → the hour hand is turned a second time while the sun is still crossing the strip, and the second round is counted in amber beside the first in vermilion until the strip is full
- counts only the hours of the first round and stops at noon → the strip stays unfilled from the middle to the far end, a gap labelled Still to come, and the hand is turned on until the gap closes
**Misconception to break:** Saying 12 hours, because the clock face only shows 12 numbers.

### 2 · Noon and midnight
**Screen:** the day strip with a high sun over the middle and a moon over a street lamp at each end, and both hands at the twelve
**Does:** slides the sun to the middle and to each end and names the twelve o'clock there noon or midnight
**Win:** the middle of the strip lights as noon and the two ends light as midnight, with no label beside them
**Feels:** the two twelves looking the same on the clock and different in the sky
**Goal:** twelve o'clock in the day is noon and twelve o'clock at night is midnight, and neither takes a.m. or p.m.
**Observe:** The twelve of the middle was lit as noon and the twelve of the ends as midnight, and neither had a label.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- calls both twelves noon → the sun is dragged to the end of the strip and the sky goes dark, the moon comes out over the street lamp, and the label changes to midnight
- writes a.m. or p.m. after noon or midnight → the label buttons grey out on noon and midnight, and the card reads noon or midnight alone with a small note that it stands alone
**Misconception to break:** Saying midnight because both hands point to the 12, without looking at the sun.

### 3 · a.m. and p.m.
**Screen:** the day strip with its left half sky blue and its right half amber, and picture cards of an alarm clock, a school bag, a lunch box, a football and a dinner plate
**Does:** drops a time or a picture card on the sky blue half or the amber half and says a.m. or p.m.
**Win:** the card settles in its half and the label a.m. or p.m. lights beside it
**Feels:** the strip split at noon, the sky blue half on one side and the amber half on the other
**Goal:** from midnight to noon is a.m. and from noon to midnight is p.m.
**Observe:** A card dropped left of the middle took a.m. and a card dropped right of it took p.m.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- chooses a.m. for any small number and p.m. for any big number → the card is carried along the strip from midnight to its place, so the child sees which half it lands in, and the clock shows the same hour in both halves
- puts a lunch box time on a.m. because lunch comes after the morning meal → the strip is cut at the noon mark in the middle, and a lunch time is carried to the right of that mark and lands on the amber half
**Misconception to break:** Choosing a.m. because 5:40 looks like a small number.

### 4 · Writing a time with a.m. or p.m.
**Screen:** the day strip with a picture card, a time card, the number pad, an a.m. button, a p.m. button and a Noon and midnight button
**Does:** types the time for a picture card, chooses a.m. or p.m., or chooses noon or midnight, and reads it back
**Win:** the time card is written in full beside the picture and the day strip lights the half it belongs to
**Feels:** the time card finishing only when a label or a word is chosen
**Goal:** a time is written with a.m. or p.m. after it, and noon and midnight are written as words with no label
**Observe:** A time typed on the card was not finished until a.m. or p.m. or a word was chosen.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes the time with no a.m. or p.m. → the time card stays open with a small gap after the number and the strip lights both halves, asking which half, until a label is chosen
- writes a.m. for an evening time that has a small number → the card is carried along the strip and lands on the amber half, and the label p.m. lights beside it
**Misconception to break:** Leaving off a.m., so nobody can tell whether it is the morning or the evening.

## Delight
On the first phase, as the Sun slider crosses the day, the hour hand sweeps round the wall clock and starts again at the twelve in the middle of the strip, and when the sun reaches the far end the sky changes from morning to evening light and then to a moon over the street lamp. On the second phase the sun settles at the middle of the strip with both hands at the twelve and the whole sky glows for a moment.

**General miss line:** Look at where the sun is. Is it before noon or after noon?

## Answer key
A day has 24 hours, because the hour hand goes round the clock twice and 12 plus 12 is 24. 12 o'clock in the day is noon and 12 o'clock at night is midnight. The time from midnight to noon is a.m. The time from noon to midnight is p.m. A time is written with a.m. or p.m. after it, but noon and midnight take neither.

## Student controls
- **Sun slider**: moves the sun along the day strip while the hands of the wall clock turn with it
- **Hour hand**: turns round the wall clock, a second round starting where the first ends
- **Picture cards**: an alarm clock, a school bag, a lunch box, a football, a dinner plate and a bed, dropped on the strip
- **a.m. and p.m. buttons**: write the label a.m. or p.m. after the time on the time card
- **Noon and midnight button**: writes the word noon or midnight, which takes no label
- **Plain clock button**: redraws the wall clock as a bare dial
- **Number pad**: types the hours and the minutes of a time
