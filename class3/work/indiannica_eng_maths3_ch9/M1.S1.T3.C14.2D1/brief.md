## What it is
**Description:** Break a metre rod into centimetres, slide kilometre posts along a road, and add and subtract lengths in columns.
**Why:** Change metres into centimetres and back, change kilometres and metres into each other, and add and subtract lengths in columns, carrying and borrowing where a column needs it, with the unit written next to every number.
**Look:** A metre rod in ten tens across the top, a kilometre road with posts under it, a column board for adding lengths, a trek path of five days, blank boxes for missing numbers, and the number pad down the right of the canvas.
**Context:** Grade 3, the second working topic of the metric measures chapter. An adult sees a rule, multiply by a hundred, which a child can say and not understand. What is being settled is that a metre really is a hundred centimetres, so changing the unit changes how many are counted and not how long the thing is, and that a column of centimetres can reach a hundred and must hand a metre across. A child who adds the metres and the centimetres as plain numbers writes a total that has no length at all.
**For the child:** Break the metres into centimetres. Gather the centimetres back into metres. Slide the post along the road. Add and subtract lengths in columns, small unit first.
**Teaching notes:** Give each pair a paper strip one metre long to mark every ten centimetres before the tool opens, and let them count the marks and say how many centimetres make a metre. Insist that the metres are changed first and the centimetres added after, and that the unit is written next to every number. Say that the last two digits of a length in centimetres are the centimetres. Use a real road, a rope or a ribbon for each question, never a bare number. If a child adds metres and centimetres as plain numbers, ask which unit the answer would be in. If a child leaves a hundred or more in the centimetres column, ask how many metres a hundred centimetres make. Use fresh numbers: the exercise's lengths are the child's to convert.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Metres into centimetres: multiply by 100
**Screen:** a length in metres and centimetres in a deep blue tag, the metre rod above it, and an empty answer tag
**Does:** breaks each metre into its centimetre marks, adds the extra centimetres, and types the length in centimetres
**Win:** the metre rods break into marks inside the vermilion ring and the total lands in the green tag
**Feels:** a metre rod snapping into a hundred marks and the extra marks joining them
**Goal:** one metre is a hundred centimetres, so metres are changed into centimetres by multiplying by a hundred and adding the centimetres
**Observe:** The six metres broke into six lots of a hundred marks, and the centimetres of the length stayed beside them.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- adds the metres and the centimetres as plain numbers → the metres break first into their marks inside the vermilion ring, and the centimetres of the length are carried over in amber, labelled Second, so the two parts are seen to be counted in the same unit before they are joined
- multiplies only the centimetres by a hundred → the Unit fact ring lights over the metre rod only, and the centimetres of the length stay unchanged in the amber tag
**Misconception to break:** Adding the metres and the centimetres as plain numbers, 6 plus 37, instead of changing the metres first.

### 2 · Centimetres into metres: divide by 100
**Screen:** a length in centimetres in a deep blue tag, a row of centimetre marks, and an empty tag for metres and centimetres
**Does:** gathers the marks into metre rods, reads the last two digits as the centimetres, and types the metres and the centimetres
**Win:** the marks gather into whole metre rods and the leftover marks stay beside them in amber
**Feels:** a long row of marks gathering into whole metres with a few left over
**Goal:** centimetres are changed into metres by dividing by a hundred, and the last two digits are the centimetres
**Observe:** The last two digits of the length in centimetres were ringed, and they were the marks left over after the metres gathered.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- takes the last digit only as the centimetres → the last two digits are ringed together in amber, and the marks gather into groups of a hundred so that both digits are seen to be left over
- writes the whole number as the metres → the gathered metre rods are counted in deep blue beside the leftover marks in amber, so the number of rods and the number of leftover marks are seen apart
**Misconception to break:** Taking only the last digit as the centimetres and writing 83 m 4 cm.

### 3 · Kilometres and metres: use 1000
**Screen:** a long road with a kilometre post, a length in kilometres and metres in a deep blue tag, and an empty answer tag
**Does:** slides the post along the road a thousand metres at a time, adds the extra metres, or gathers metres back into kilometres, and types the answer
**Win:** the post stops at each kilometre mark and the total lands in the green tag
**Feels:** a post sliding along a long road, one kilometre mark at a time
**Goal:** one kilometre is a thousand metres, so kilometres change into metres by multiplying by a thousand and back by dividing
**Observe:** The post stopped at every thousand metres along the road, and the metres left over were counted in amber.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- uses a hundred instead of a thousand for kilometres → the vermilion ring changes to the unit fact 1 km = 1000 m, the post slides ten stretches of a hundred metres each to reach one kilometre, and the child counts them
- writes the metres left over in the kilometres place → the kilometre posts are counted in deep blue and the leftover metres in amber, labelled Left, so the two numbers are seen to be different units
**Misconception to break:** Multiplying by a hundred instead of a thousand, because a hundred is the number used with centimetres.

### 4 · Adding and subtracting lengths
**Screen:** a column board with two lengths one under the other, a carry mark above the metres column, and an empty answer row
**Does:** adds or subtracts the centimetres or metres first, carries or borrows into the next column, and types the answer in both units
**Win:** the carried amount hops into the next column and the answer row fills green
**Feels:** a hundred centimetres hopping across the board as one metre
**Goal:** lengths are added or subtracted in columns, the small unit first, carrying or borrowing where a column needs it
**Observe:** A total of a hundred or more centimetres hopped into the metres column as one metre, and the rest stayed.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- leaves a hundred or more centimetres in the centimetres column → the hundred marks gather into one small rod and hop up into the metres column, and the amber carry mark lights above it
- subtracts the smaller number from the larger in every column → a top amount that is too small makes the metres column lend one metre, which breaks into a hundred marks and falls into the centimetres column, and the borrow mark lights in amber
**Misconception to break:** Writing 143 cm in the centimetres column and leaving the metres column at 3 m.

## Delight
On the first phase, when the Break button is pressed, a metre of the rod snaps into a hundred marks with a soft click and the marks rain gently into the tray in ten neat tens. On the fourth phase, when a total reaches a hundred centimetres, the hundred marks gather into a small rod and hop up into the metres column all at once.

**General miss line:** Break the big unit into the small one first, then count again.

## Answer key
One metre is 100 cm, so to change metres into centimetres multiply by 100. A length in metres and centimetres is changed by changing the metres and adding the centimetres. To change centimetres into metres divide by 100: the last two digits are the centimetres and the digits before them are the metres. One kilometre is 1000 m, so to change kilometres into metres multiply by 1000 and to change metres into kilometres divide by 1000. To add or subtract lengths, write them in columns with the small unit on one side, work the small unit first, carry a hundred centimetres or a thousand metres into the next column, and borrow a metre or a kilometre when the top amount is too small. Keep the unit written next to every number.

- **Kilometre road**: slides a post along the road a thousand metres at a time

- **Column board**: takes two lengths one under the other, metres and centimetres or kilometres and metres
- **Carry and borrow marks**: show a hundred centimetres moving into the metres column, or a metre moving down

## Textbook (this topic)
**Pages:** 104, 105, 106, 107
**Key terms:** convert, multiply, divide, centimetre, metre, kilometre, column
Metres change into centimetres by multiplying by 100, centimetres change back by dividing by 100, kilometres and metres use 1000, and lengths are added and subtracted in columns. To change metres into centimetres, multiply by 100. To change centimetres into metres, divide by 100, and the last two digits are the centimetres. To change kilometres into metres, multiply by 1000, and divide to go back. Lengths in one unit are added and subtracted like ordinary numbers, and the unit is kept. For metres and centimetres, write two columns. Add or subtract the centimetres first. If the centimetres reach 100, carry one metre. If there are too few centimetres, borrow one metre. One metre is 100 cm. Seven metres is seven hundreds. Multiply the metres by 100. So 7 m is 700 cm. Divide by 100. The last two digits are the centimetres. The rest are the metres. So 640 cm is 6 m 40 cm. One kilometre is 1000 m. So 4 km is 4000 m. To change km into m, multiply by 1000. To change m into km, divide by 1000. So 9000 m is 9 km. Add the numbers and keep the unit: 8 m and 6 m make 14 m. For metres and centimetres, write two columns. Add the cm first, then the m. So 2 m 30 cm plus 1 m 45 cm is 3 m 75 cm.
