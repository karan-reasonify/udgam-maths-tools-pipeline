## What it is
**Description:** Pour a kilogram of salt out as a thousand grams, gather grams back into kilograms, and add and subtract weights in columns.
**Why:** Change kilograms into grams and grams into kilograms by seeing a kilogram bag break into a thousand grams and gather back, change a weight in kilograms and grams, and add and subtract weights in columns with a carry or a borrow.
**Look:** A kitchen scale with a kilogram dial and a gram dial, a real bag of salt that pours out a thousand gram marks inside a vermilion ring, a Given tag, an Answer tag, a column board with a kilograms column and a grams column, and two shopping baskets.
**Context:** Grade 3, the second weight topic of the metric measures chapter. An adult sees a rule being applied, multiply by one thousand and divide by one thousand. What is actually being settled is what one kilogram is made of and what a weight written in two units means: a child who has only the rule will move the digits the wrong way, will write a weight such as two kilograms ninety grams as twenty ninety grams, and will compare 1 kg 620 g with 1700 g by looking at the first digit. The tool breaks a real kilogram into its grams and lays the columns out.
**For the child:** Set the kilograms and press Pour. Press Gather to turn grams back into kilograms. Work the grams column first. Change both weights to one unit before you compare them.
**Teaching notes:** Weigh a kilogram of rice on a kitchen scale before the tool opens and share it into small bags of a hundred grams by hand, counting ten of them, so that a thousand grams is a kilogram that has been counted. Say the unit with every number, a number of kilograms and a number of grams, and never a bare number. For kilograms and grams together, write the two parts with a gap between them and ask for the grams in three places, so that a weight such as two kilograms ninety grams keeps its zero. In the columns, ask which column to work first and why: the grams, because they might reach a thousand. When two weights are in different units, ask what must be done before they are compared. Use fresh numbers: the exercise's weights and its three fruits are the child's to change and add.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Kilograms into grams: multiply by 1000
**Screen:** a kitchen scale with a bag of salt on the pan, the kilogram stepper at two, and a closed bag with a vermilion ring waiting
**Does:** sets the kilograms, presses Pour, watches the thousands of grams fall out, and types the grams
**Win:** each bag pours out its gram marks inside the vermilion ring and the Answer tag names the grams in green
**Feels:** a bag of salt tipping out ten small bags, each splitting into ten tens, until the gram dial has climbed to a thousand
**Goal:** one kilogram is one thousand grams, so kilograms change into grams by multiplying by one thousand
**Observe:** One kilogram bag poured out the same number of gram marks every time, and three bags poured out three times as many.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- multiplies by one hundred → the bag pours out only a hundred gram marks and stops far short of the full kilogram bag, and the Unit fact ring lights round one thousand
- gives the number of kilograms as the answer → the grams dial stays at zero until the Pour is pressed, and then climbs to the full number of grams
**Misconception to break:** Multiplying by one hundred, as for metres and centimetres, and writing two hundred grams.

### 2 · Grams into kilograms: divide by 1000
**Screen:** a heap of gram marks on the scale, the Gather button lit, and empty kilogram bags waiting beside it
**Does:** presses Gather, watches every thousand grams flow into a kilogram bag, and types the kilograms
**Win:** the gram marks gather into whole kilogram bags and the Answer tag names the kilograms in green
**Feels:** a heap of gram marks flowing into kilogram bags, one bag for every thousand, until the bags are full
**Goal:** every thousand grams gather into one kilogram, so grams change into kilograms by dividing by one thousand
**Observe:** Gather made one kilogram bag for every thousand gram marks, and what was left stayed as grams.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- divides by one hundred → the gram marks gather into far too many bags, and the Unit fact ring lights round one thousand
- takes away only one zero → the heap gathers into the true number of bags and the Answer tag shows it beside the number typed
**Misconception to break:** Writing 170 kg, because only one zero was taken away instead of three.

### 3 · Kilograms and grams together
**Screen:** a scale reading some kilograms and some grams, a Given tag, a vermilion ring waiting, and an empty amber Second tag
**Does:** changes the kilograms in the ring, adds the grams in the amber tag, and types the total in grams
**Win:** the kilograms pour out their thousands, the grams land in the amber tag, and the Answer tag names the total in green
**Feels:** the kilograms pouring out their thousands inside the ring and the grams landing on the end in an amber tag
**Goal:** a weight in kilograms and grams is changed by changing the kilograms first and then adding the grams
**Observe:** A weight in kilograms and grams came out as the kilograms' thousands with the grams added on the end.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- pushes the two numbers together → the kilograms pour out inside the vermilion ring first and the grams are added in the amber tag, and the total is built in two visible steps
- drops a zero in the grams → the grams land in the amber tag in three places and the empty tens place is drawn as a small zero that stays
**Misconception to break:** Writing 4 kg 700 g as 47000 g, by pushing the two numbers together with a zero too many.

### 4 · Adding and subtracting weights
**Screen:** a column board with kilograms and grams columns, two weights written one under the other, and two baskets of fruit beside it
**Does:** adds or subtracts the grams first, then the kilograms, carrying or borrowing where the column needs it, and types the answer
**Win:** the carry rises above the kilograms column or the borrowed kilogram breaks into a thousand grams, and the Answer tag names the weight in green
**Feels:** a column of grams filling up to a thousand and spilling one kilogram over to the next column, or a kilogram breaking into a thousand grams to lend
**Goal:** weights are added and subtracted in two columns, the grams first, with a carry or a borrow when the column needs it
**Observe:** The grams column was worked before the kilograms column, and a thousand grams carried over as a kilogram.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- works the kilograms column first → the grams column lights first and the carry or the borrow is drawn above the column it reaches, and only then does the kilograms column light
- compares two weights by their first digits → both baskets are shown on the scale one after the other, and each weight is changed to grams before the scale says which is more
**Misconception to break:** Adding all four numbers as one sum, which mixes grams and kilograms.

## Delight
On the first phase, the moment Pour is pressed, the bag of salt tips and the grams run out in ten small bags that each split into ten tens, inside the vermilion ring, and the kilogram dial turns to zero while the gram dial climbs. On the fourth phase a borrowed kilogram snaps into a thousand gram marks that slide across into the grams column.

**General miss line:** Look at the grams again. How many thousands are there? Then the grams that are left.

## Answer key
One kilogram is one thousand grams. To change kilograms into grams, multiply by one thousand. To change grams into kilograms, divide by one thousand. A weight in kilograms and grams is changed to grams by changing the kilograms first and then adding the grams, and a zero in the hundreds place of the grams must be kept, so 2 kg 90 g is 2090 g. Weights are added and subtracted like ordinary numbers with the unit kept, and weights in kilograms and grams are written in two columns and worked grams first. When the grams add to a thousand or more, a kilogram is carried to the kilograms column, and when the top grams are fewer than the grams taken away, one kilogram is broken into a thousand grams. Two weights are compared only when they are in the same unit.

## Textbook (this topic)
**Pages:** 108, 109, 110
**Key terms:** convert, multiply, divide, kilogram, gram, column
Kilograms change into grams by multiplying by 1000, grams change back by dividing by 1000, and weights are added and subtracted in columns. To change kilograms into grams, multiply by 1000. To change grams into kilograms, divide by 1000. For kilograms and grams together, change the kilograms first and then add the grams. Weights in one unit are added and subtracted like ordinary numbers, and the unit is kept. For kilograms and grams, write two columns. Add or subtract the grams first. If the grams reach 1000, carry one kilogram. If there are too few grams, borrow one kilogram. One kilogram is 1000 g. Three kilograms is three thousands. Multiply the kilograms by 1000. So 3 kg is 3000 g. Divide by 1000. So 4000 g is 4 kg. Change the kilograms first: 2 kg is 2000 g. Then add the 300 g. So 2 kg 300 g is 2300 g. Write two columns. Add the grams first, then the kilograms. So 1 kg 250 g plus 2 kg 400 g is 3 kg 650 g. To subtract, take the grams first, then the kilograms.
