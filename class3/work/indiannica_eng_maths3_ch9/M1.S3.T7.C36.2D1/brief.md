## What it is
**Description:** Empty a litre jug into its millilitre drops, change litres and millilitres into each other, and add and subtract capacities on a column board.
**Why:** Change litres into millilitres by multiplying by one thousand and millilitres into litres by dividing, change litres and millilitres together, judge a total, and add and subtract capacities in columns with a carry and a borrow, and find how many small cups fill a larger container.
**Look:** A one litre jug that empties into millilitre drops at the left, a bucket with a litre scale and a millilitre scale beside it, a column board at the right with a litres column and a millilitres column, a row of small cups beside a larger container, and a drop count at the top left of the canvas.
**Context:** Grade 3, the last working topic of the metric measures chapter. An adult sees sums with zeros. What is being settled is that a litre is a thousand millilitres, so changing a big unit into a small one multiplies and changing back divides, and that two capacities in litres and millilitres are added with the millilitres first and a full thousand carried into the litres. A child who writes the litres and the millilitres side by side as one number will write 13 L 90 mL as 1390 mL, and will add the two columns as if they were the same unit.
**For the child:** Tap the jug to empty it into drops. Change litres and millilitres into each other. Then add and subtract on the column board.
**Teaching notes:** Pour a litre jug into a hundred millilitre cup ten times before the tool opens, and say how many millilitres are in a litre. Make the count of one thousand by tens: ten cups of a hundred millilitres, and each hundred is ten tens. To change litres into millilitres multiply by one thousand, and to change back divide by one thousand: ask which unit is bigger, and which way the number must go. For litres and millilitres together, say change the litres first, then add the millilitres, and never write the two numbers side by side as one number. In a column sum, start with the millilitres, carry a litre when they reach one thousand, and borrow a litre when the top millilitres are fewer. Use the true or false question to say why a total is right or wrong, and give the right one. Use fresh numbers: the exercise's sums, differences and conversions are the child's to try.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Litres into millilitres: multiply by 1000
**Screen:** a water tank drawn beside the one litre jug, a deep blue tag with a number of litres, and the Unit fact ring waiting
**Does:** taps the jug to empty it into drops, counts the thousand, then types the litres times one thousand
**Win:** the drops of every litre pile up and the green tag shows the millilitres
**Feels:** a litre jug tipping out a thousand drops, and the count climbing by a hundred for each cup
**Goal:** litres are changed into millilitres by multiplying by one thousand, because every litre is one thousand millilitres
**Observe:** The litre jug emptied into the same thousand drops every time, whatever amount the tag showed.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes the litres with a few zeros, such as one hundred or ten times the litres → the drops are counted in tens, then hundreds, then thousands, and the vermilion Unit fact ring pulses once at each thousand
- divides the litres by one thousand instead of multiplying → the answer slot shows one litre beside one thousand millilitres, so the millilitres are seen to be the bigger number
**Misconception to break:** Writing 190 mL or 1900 mL, with too few zeros, instead of multiplying by one thousand.

### 2 · Millilitres into litres: divide by 1000
**Screen:** a bucket marked in millilitres with a deep blue tag showing a number of millilitres and a row of empty litre jugs
**Does:** pours the millilitre drops into the litre jugs a thousand at a time and counts the jugs filled, then types the litres
**Win:** the jugs stand full in a row and the green tag shows the litres
**Feels:** a bucket of drops pouring into a row of litre jugs, each filling at a thousand
**Goal:** millilitres are changed into litres by dividing by one thousand, because one thousand millilitres make one litre
**Observe:** When litres were changed into millilitres the number grew, and when millilitres were changed into litres it shrank.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- multiplies the millilitres by one thousand again → the drops are poured into the litre jugs a thousand at a time, and the jugs filled are counted, so the number of litres is seen to be smaller than the millilitres
- gives the number of jugs but forgets the unit → the answer slot writes the unit beside the number and the green tag reads it back aloud with its unit
**Misconception to break:** Multiplying by one thousand again, which makes the number bigger when the unit is bigger.

### 3 · Litres and millilitres together
**Screen:** a jar with a litre scale and a millilitre scale and a deep blue tag with litres and millilitres
**Does:** changes the litres into millilitres first, adds the millilitres on, and types the total; or judges a total a child has written
**Win:** the two scales read the same water and the green tag shows one amount in millilitres
**Feels:** the litres turning into a stack of thousands and the millilitres joining them to make one amount
**Goal:** litres and millilitres together are changed by changing the litres first and then adding the millilitres
**Observe:** For litres and millilitres together the two scales read the same water in two ways.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes the litres and the millilitres side by side as one number → the two scales on the jar read the same water, one in litres and millilitres and one in millilitres only, and the two readings are seen not to match
- changes only the litres and forgets to add the millilitres → the green tag shows the thousands first and then the millilitres joining them, so the two parts are seen to make one amount
**Misconception to break:** Writing the litres and the millilitres side by side as 13470 L, or as 1347 mL.

### 4 · Adding, subtracting and filling cups
**Screen:** the column board with a litres column and a millilitres column, two amounts written under each other, and a row of cups beside a jar
**Does:** adds or subtracts the millilitres first, carries or borrows one litre, then does the litres; or changes both amounts to millilitres and divides to count the cups
**Win:** the carried or borrowed litre slides across the board and the green tag shows the answer in litres and millilitres
**Feels:** a full thousand millilitres lifting out of its column and sliding across to join the litres
**Goal:** capacities are added and subtracted in columns, carrying or borrowing a thousand millilitres, and the cups that fill a container are found by dividing
**Observe:** A column that reached one thousand millilitres sent a litre across to the litres column, and a short column borrowed one back.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- takes the smaller millilitres from the larger when the top millilitres are fewer → the top millilitres turn amber and a litre slides down to join them as a thousand, so the top number becomes bigger than the bottom one
- forgets to carry the litre when the millilitres pass a thousand → the column shows one thousand millilitres lifting out and sliding over the top of the litres column to join it, with the carried litre drawn in an amber tag
**Misconception to break:** Taking the smaller millilitres from the larger, 460 mL minus 120 mL, instead of borrowing.

## Delight
Two small surprises. On the first phase, when the jug is tapped, the thousand drops pour out in ten cups of a hundred, each cup tipping out ten tens, and the vermilion Unit fact ring pulses once for each cup, so the count climbs by a hundred every time. On the fourth phase, when the millilitres reach a full thousand, a whole litre lifts out of the millilitres column and slides over the top of the litres column to join it.

**General miss line:** Look at the jug. How many millilitres is one litre? Try the step again.

## Answer key
One litre is one thousand millilitres. To change litres into millilitres, multiply by one thousand, and to change millilitres into litres, divide by one thousand. A capacity in litres and millilitres is changed by changing the litres first and adding the millilitres, so 13 L 470 mL means 13000 mL and then 470 mL more. To add two capacities, write a litres column and a millilitres column, add the millilitres first, and carry one litre when the millilitres reach one thousand. To subtract, take the millilitres first, and borrow one litre, which is one thousand millilitres, when the top millilitres are fewer. To find how many small cups fill a larger container, change both to the same unit and divide.

## Textbook (this topic)
**Pages:** 111, 112, 113, 114
**Key terms:** convert, multiply, divide, litre, millilitre, column
Litres change into millilitres by multiplying by 1000, millilitres change back by dividing by 1000, and capacities are added and subtracted in columns. To change litres into millilitres, multiply by 1000. To change millilitres into litres, divide by 1000. For litres and millilitres together, change the litres first and then add the millilitres. For litres and millilitres, write two columns. Add or subtract the millilitres first. If they reach 1000, carry one litre. If there are too few, borrow one litre. To count the cups that fill a bucket, change both to millilitres and divide. One litre is 1000 mL. Seven litres is seven thousands. Multiply the litres by 1000. So 7 L is 7000 mL. Divide by 1000. So 9000 mL is 9 L. Change the litres first: 3 L is 3000 mL. Then add the 250 mL. So 3 L 250 mL is 3250 mL. Change 4 L to 4000 mL. Divide 4000 by 500. It takes 8 mugs. To add, write two columns: 2 L 300 mL plus 1 L 450 mL is 3 L 750 mL.
