# Weigh It (M1.S2.T4.C16.2D1), class 3: Hindi and Gujarati draft for the TR table

Status: DRAFT for the builder's language pass. Keys are the English byte for byte. A native speaker must read the Gujarati first.
Rows are in file order; a string that appears twice (a choice label that is also a tag, a unit that is also a label) is listed once, at its first place.

## Terms settled once (same as Measure It where it has the word)

- kg, short, beside a digit, on labels, tags, the slot, thumbnails: Hindi किग्रा, Gujarati કિગ્રા
- g, short: Hindi ग्राम, Gujarati ગ્રામ (the same word as the long form; fallback for tight pills: ग्रा / ગ્રા)
- kilograms, long, a word on its own or in a sentence: Hindi किलोग्राम, Gujarati કિલોગ્રામ
- balance, pan, beam, needle, post: तराज़ू, पलड़ा, डंडी, काँटा, खंभा / ત્રાજવું, પલ્લું, દાંડી, કાંટો, થાંભલો
- iron weight (the object) and weight (how heavy): लोहे का बाट, वज़न / લોખંડનું કાટલું, વજન
- level (the beam is level): बराबर / સમતોલ
- heavier, lighter, more than, less than: भारी (ज़्यादा भारी), हल्का, ज़्यादा, कम / ભારે (વધુ ભારે), હળવું, વધુ, ઓછું
- half and quarter of a kilogram: आधा किलोग्राम, चौथाई किलोग्राम / અડધો કિલોગ્રામ, પા કિલોગ્રામ
- tap, unit, Answer: छुओ, इकाई, उत्तर / સ્પર્શ કરો, એકમ, જવાબ
- packet, sugar, tray, loaf: पैकेट, चीनी, ट्रे, ब्रेड / પૅકેટ, ખાંડ, ટ્રે, બ્રેડ
- rice, pillow, brick, mango, watermelon, school bag: चावल, तकिया, ईंट, आम, तरबूज़, स्कूल बैग / ચોખા, ઓશીકું, ઈંટ, કેરી, તડબૂચ, સ્કૂલ બૅગ

Rule used: a digit takes the short unit ("1 किग्रा", "200 ग्राम", "1 કિગ્રા"); a unit named as a word takes the long one ("किलोग्राम में", "કિલોગ્રામમાં").

## Notes for the builder and the visual reviewer

LONG rows (the unit word "ग्राम" / "ગ્રામ" is two letters wide where "g" is one, so a line full of grams grows most; expect up to about +25% on screen, check on `shots --lang hi|gu`):
- Level again: 200 g and 100 g make 300 g.
- Add 200 g and 100 g.
- Yes, 200 g and 50 g make 250 g for one packet.
- 1 kg is 1,000 g, and the pan had 500 g, 200 g and 200 g before.
- The 500 g weight balances them, and 1 kg is 1,000 g.
- Yes, 900 g and 100 g make 1,000 g, the same as 1 kg.
- The longest English line is 76 characters (The parcel is 1 kg ...); its Hindi and Gujarati are shorter by letter count, so the bubble should stay within 3 lines.

TIGHT places (a fixed-width tag, pill or key; the tool's `wiTag` is at most 262 wide and the stamped pill on an iron weight is 84 to 108 wide):
- the correction chip values "1 किग्रा से अधिक" / "1 किग्रा से कम" / "1 કિગ્રાથી વધુ" / "1 કિગ્રાથી ઓછું" (2 to 3 times the letters of "over 1 kg"); the shorter forms "अधिक" and "વધુ" were already chosen here, nothing shorter is faithful.
- the stamped labels "500 ग्राम", "500 ગ્રામ" on the iron weights (fs 32 in a 92 to 116 wide box: `wiIron` measures with `wiW`, which counts every Indic code point as 0.62 em, so it will over-estimate and may shrink the font).
- the choice key "चौथाई किलोग्राम" / "પા કિલોગ્રામ" are shorter than the English "a quarter of a kilogram"; nothing to fit.

Not translated, deliberately: "+" and "=" (printed between tokens), digits, "1,000" (kept in comma form). Digits are not turned into Hindi or Gujarati numerals.

To confirm with a native speaker (Gujarati first):
- "કાટલું" (iron weight), "દાંડી" (beam), "કાંટો" (needle), "થાંભલો" (post), "ગૂણ" (rice sack), "તડબૂચ" (watermelon); "પા કિલોગ્રામ" / "અડધો કિલોગ્રામ" for a quarter / half (the shop and school word) where Measure It says "ચોથો ભાગ" for a quarter of a metre.
- Hindi "चौथाई किलोग्राम" (same word as Measure It's "चौथाई मीटर"); the shop word is "पाव किलो", change all "चौथाई किलोग्राम" rows if the textbook says "पाव".
- Hindi "बाट" (weight, the iron piece) against "वज़न" (how heavy); "डंडी" (beam), "काँटा" (needle), "खंभा" (post), "बोरी" (sack), "ब्रेड" for a loaf.
- Short unit forms: Hindi "किग्रा" and "ग्राम", Gujarati "કિગ્રા" and "ગ્રામ" (so "g" and "grams" are the same word); the textbook abbreviation for gram is "ग्रा" / "ગ્રા" if a shorter form is wanted for the iron weights' pills.
