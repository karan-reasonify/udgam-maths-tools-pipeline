## What it is
**Description:** Measure real things with a handspan, a ruler, a tape and a metre rod, and choose the unit that fits each one.
**Why:** See that a handspan is not fair and a fixed unit is, choose centimetres, metres or kilometres for a real thing, read a length at a mark on a ruler or a tape that starts at 0, and tell a thing longer than a metre from one shorter, with half a metre and a quarter of a metre.
**Look:** A workbench with real things on it, a ruler, a tape and a metre rod beside them, three unit bins with benchmark chips, a My list board, and the number pad down the right of the canvas.
**Context:** Grade 3, the first working topic of the metric measures chapter. An adult sees a ruler held against a pencil, which a real ruler does well. What is being settled is why a fixed unit is needed at all, and how big each unit feels: a centimetre for a clip, a metre for a rope, a kilometre for a road. A child who has never seen two handspans disagree thinks the desk has two lengths, and a child with no feel for the units will write the height of a door in kilometres without a worry.
**For the child:** Measure the desk with a handspan strip. Put a thing in the bin that fits its unit. Lay a thing on the ruler from 0. Compare things with the metre rod.
**Teaching notes:** Let two children measure the same desk with their own handspans before the tool opens, and let them argue about it before anyone says why. Then measure it with one ruler and let them see the same answer. Say about every time a benchmark is used: a finger is about a centimetre and a door handle is about a metre, never exactly. Keep to unmistakable things when sorting against the metre rod, and do not ask for the exact length of a benchmark thing. If a child counts a ruler from the 1 mark, ask where the thing starts. If a child writes the height of a door in kilometres, ask which would be a sensible road. Use fresh things: the exercise's own objects are the child's to find.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Hands and feet are not fair: we need fixed units
**Screen:** a desk with a small handspan strip and a bigger handspan strip beside it, and a ruler waiting under the bench
**Does:** lays each strip along the desk, reads the two different counts, then lays the ruler along the desk and reads the one length
**Win:** the two counts light up side by side, then both strips fade and the ruler shows one length for everyone
**Feels:** two honest counts for one desk, and then one ruler making them agree
**Goal:** a handspan changes from person to person, so it gives different counts, and a fixed unit gives one answer for everyone
**Observe:** The small strip and the bigger strip gave two different counts for one desk, and the ruler gave one length.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- says the desk has two different lengths → the desk stays one desk in the middle of the bench, each strip's count lights up beside the strip that made it, and the ruler laid along the desk gives one length that both strips agree on
- says the bigger strip counted wrongly → the two strips are laid side by side on the bench and the bigger strip is seen to cover more of the desk with each lay, so fewer lays are needed
**Misconception to break:** Thinking one child counted wrongly, when both counted right with hands of different sizes.

### 2 · Centimetres, metres and kilometres
**Screen:** a row of real things on the bench with three bins below, cm, m and km, and a benchmark chip above each
**Does:** drags each real thing into the bin whose unit fits it, reading the benchmark chips for help
**Win:** the thing settles in its bin and a mark on the bin counts how many things fit that unit
**Feels:** each thing finding a bin where its number is easy to say
**Goal:** centimetres fit short things, metres fit longer things and kilometres fit far places
**Observe:** The rope across the playground sat in the m bin and the paper clip in the cm bin.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- puts the rope across the playground in the km bin → the benchmark chip above the km bin lights, saying about a quarter of an hour of travel, and the rope is stood beside the road to show it is much shorter
- puts the paper clip in the m bin → the benchmark chip above the cm bin lights, saying a finger is about 1 cm, and the paper clip is laid beside a finger to show they are alike
**Misconception to break:** Choosing km because a playground feels big to a child.

### 3 · Measuring with a ruler and a tape
**Screen:** a ruler that starts at 0 and a measuring tape lying across the bench, and a paper clip, a pencil, a ribbon and a shelf waiting
**Does:** lays a thing along the ruler or the tape from the zero mark, reads the number at its other end and types it
**Win:** the centimetre marks light one by one up to the end of the thing and the length lands in a deep blue tag
**Feels:** the marks lighting one by one from the edge of the ruler to the end of the thing
**Goal:** to measure, put one end of the thing at 0 and read the number at its other end, taken at a mark
**Observe:** The ribbon began at the zero mark and its far end lit the number to type.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- starts counting from the 1 mark → the zero mark is lit at the edge of the ruler and the thing is slid back to it, and the marks are counted aloud from 0
- reads the number at the end of the thing when it does not start at 0 → a second ruler tag shows the starting mark in deep blue and the ending mark in deep blue, and the marks between them are counted in amber, labelled Left
**Misconception to break:** Counting the marks from the 1 mark and so giving a length one too short or one too long.

### 4 · Longer or shorter than one metre
**Screen:** the metre rod standing from the floor to a door handle beside a classroom door, two empty bins, and a Fold button
**Does:** drags real things into longer than the rod or shorter than the rod, then folds the rod in half and in half again
**Win:** the rod folds in half and in half again, and each folded rod is labelled half a metre and a quarter of a metre
**Feels:** the metre rod bending in the middle and each fold making a shorter piece
**Goal:** a thing is longer than one metre if it goes past the top of the metre rod, and half a metre and a quarter of a metre are the rod folded
**Observe:** The metre rod folded in half and again, and the second fold was shorter than the first.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- puts a long thin thing in shorter than the rod because it is thin → the thing is stood beside the rod from the same floor line and its top is carried across to the rod, so the length is seen past the top of the rod
- calls the rod folded twice half a metre → the first fold is labelled half a metre and the second fold is labelled a quarter of a metre, and the two folded rods are laid side by side
**Misconception to break:** Judging by how thick or how heavy a thing is instead of how long it is.

## Answer key
A handspan or a foot changes from person to person, so it gives different counts for the same thing, and a fixed unit gives the same answer for everyone. Centimetres measure short things, metres measure longer things and kilometres measure far places. One metre is 100 cm and one kilometre is 1000 m. A centimetre is about the width of a finger, a metre is about the height of a door handle from the floor and a kilometre is about a quarter of an hour of travel, always about. To read a ruler, put one end of the thing at 0 and read the number at its other end. A thing is longer than a metre if it goes past the top of the metre rod and shorter if it stops before. Half a metre is half of the rod and a quarter of a metre is half of that.

## Student controls
- **Handspan strips**: a small strip and a bigger strip are laid along the desk and counted, and each count lights up
- **Ruler**: starts at 0 and takes a paper clip, a pencil or a ribbon laid along it from the zero mark
- **Tape**: takes a longer thing, such as a shelf, laid along it from the zero mark
- **Unit bins**: take a real thing dragged into cm, m or km, with a benchmark chip above each bin
- **Metre rod**: stands from the floor to a door handle so a thing can be compared with it
- **Fold button**: folds the metre rod in half for half a metre and again for a quarter of a metre
- **My list board**: holds four things longer than the rod and four things shorter, chosen by the child
- **Number pad**: types a reading or a count
