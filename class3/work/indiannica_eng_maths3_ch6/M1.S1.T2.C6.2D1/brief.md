## What it is
**Description:** Cut a chapati, a pizza or a cake into equal parts, lift some onto the plate, and write the fraction that names what you took.
**Why:** Cut a real thing into equal parts and name them, take some parts and write the fraction with a top and a bottom number, see that equal top and bottom numbers make one whole, and write a fraction of a group of real things.
**Look:** A kitchen table with a shelf of wholes along the top, an amber board with the chosen whole and a Cut dial, a deep blue plate beside it, a fraction card and a words card at the right, Put together, Shape view and Group view buttons, and the number pad down the right of the canvas.
**Context:** Grade 3, the first working topic of the fractions chapter. An adult sees a pizza being sliced and a plate being filled, which a real pizza and a crayon can do. What is being settled is that a fraction needs equal parts, and which of the two written numbers counts what. A child who reads the top and bottom as two ordinary numbers puts the taken count underneath, and a child who thinks any cut gives halves will name unequal parts without a worry.
**For the child:** Pick a whole from the shelf. Cut it into equal parts. Lift some parts onto the blue plate. Count all the parts, then count the parts you took. Put the parts back together.
**Teaching notes:** Give each child a roti or a paper plate pizza to fold and cut before the tool opens, one into two parts, one into three and one into four, and ask them to say the name of one part aloud. Insist on equal parts and wait for a child to say why a torn roti cannot be named, rather than saying it for them. Always count the cuts before the parts taken, and say the bottom number first. Use a real row of mangoes, laddoos or counters for the Group view. If a child writes the taken count underneath, ask which number tells how many parts the whole was cut into. Use fresh numbers: the exercise's pictures are the child's to colour and write.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Equal parts: halves, thirds and quarters
**Screen:** a chapati, a pizza and a cake on the shelf with their cuts drawn and nothing taken, and a torn roti at the end
**Does:** turns the Cut dial to two on the chapati, three on the pizza and four on the cake, reads the name card, then tries the torn roti
**Win:** the cuts settle into equal parts and the name card reads one-third on the pizza
**Feels:** the pieces becoming equal and a name arriving only when they do
**Goal:** equal parts of one whole have names: two are halves, three are thirds, four are quarters
**Observe:** The torn roti made one piece bigger, and the words card would not name any piece.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- calls every part a half, whatever the number of cuts → the Cut dial runs from two up to four on the pizza and the cake with the parts counted aloud in amber, and the name card changes its name each time the count changes
- names the pieces of the torn roti → the name card stays blank and says in words that the pieces are not equal, and the slider slides back so the roti closes into equal parts
**Misconception to break:** Calling it a half, because half is the only part name the child knows.

### 2 · Coloured parts: the top and bottom numbers
**Screen:** a chocolate bar, a jug of juice and a pizza on the shelf, the blue plate empty, and a card with a line and two empty boxes
**Does:** lifts pieces onto the blue plate, or fills the jug mark by mark, then types the taken count above the line and all the parts below it
**Win:** the card shows the stacked fraction and its two boxes light up as numerator and denominator
**Feels:** the card filling from two counts: all the parts below the line, the parts taken above it
**Goal:** the bottom number counts all the equal parts and the top number counts the parts taken
**Observe:** The bottom number stayed the same while more pieces went onto the blue plate.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- types the taken count at the bottom and the count of all the parts on top → the card turns the numbers round once, and the parts are counted in amber: first all of them, which light the bottom box, then the taken ones on the blue plate, which light the top box
- counts the parts left on the board as the top number → the parts on the blue plate are numbered in turn in deep blue, and the parts left on the amber board stay unnumbered and labelled Left
**Misconception to break:** Writing the six on top and the four below, so the fraction is upside down.

### 3 · All the parts make one whole
**Screen:** a pizza cut into equal slices, every slice on the blue plate, an empty pizza box on the board and the card empty
**Does:** puts the slices back into the box one by one, or taps Put together, and types the missing top or bottom number
**Win:** the last slice clicks in, the pizza closes inside the green outline and the card shows the same number twice
**Feels:** the slices clicking back one by one until the pizza closes with no gap
**Goal:** when the top and bottom numbers are the same, every part is taken and together they make one whole
**Observe:** The last slice clicked into the pizza box and the pizza closed with no gap left.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes the number one on top when every slice is taken → every slice is numbered in turn as it clicks into the box, the last number lands on the top box, and the card shows the same number above and below
- calls the fraction a whole while a slice is still missing → the box stays open with a gap where the missing slice belongs, the slices still on the plate are lit amber and labelled Left, and the gap closes only when the last slice is put back
**Misconception to break:** Writing 1 on top and 7 below because one whole means the number one goes on top.

### 4 · A fraction of a group of things
**Screen:** Group view with a shelf of mangoes, some already in the blue basket, and an empty fraction card
**Does:** counts all the things on the shelf and in the basket, counts the things in the basket, types both, then reads the name in words
**Win:** the card shows the stacked fraction and the words card names it, such as three-sevenths
**Feels:** the same card that named part of a pizza naming part of a shelf of things
**Goal:** a group is counted like a whole: all the things go below the line and the things taken go above it
**Observe:** The same card named part of a pizza and part of a shelf of mangoes.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- puts the things in the basket over the things left on the shelf → all the things on the shelf and in the basket light amber and are counted in turn, the count lands in the bottom box, and the things in the basket are counted into the top box
- counts only the things in the basket and leaves the bottom box empty → the whole shelf stays in the frame, the things left on it are lit amber, labelled Left, and the bottom box waits lit until every thing has been counted
**Misconception to break:** Writing the yellow laddoos over the orange ones, 6 over 4, instead of over all of the laddoos.

## Answer key
A whole cut into equal parts gives fractions. Two equal parts are halves, three equal parts are thirds and four equal parts are fourths, or quarters. The parts must be equal, or there is no fraction to name them. The bottom number, the denominator, counts all the equal parts in the whole. The top number, the numerator, counts the parts that are coloured or taken. When the top and bottom numbers are the same, every part is taken and the fraction is one whole. A group works like a whole: the bottom number counts all the things and the top number counts the things taken.

## Student controls
- **Shelf**: chooses the whole to work on: chapati, pizza, cake, chocolate bar, jug or the torn roti
- **Cut dial**: cuts the whole into two to ten equal parts, the cut lines drawn first
- **Tap a part**: lifts it onto the deep blue plate, or puts it back when tapped again
- **Uneven cut slider**: tears the roti or moves one cut off the middle, and the words card refuses every name
- **Put together button**: clicks the slices back into the pizza box one by one until it closes into one whole
- **Shape view button**: redraws the same cut whole as a plain shape with the taken parts coloured deep blue
- **Group view**: swaps the whole for a shelf of up to twenty separate real things that are lifted into the basket
- **Number pad**: types the top and bottom numbers of the fraction card

## Textbook (this topic)

**Key terms:** fraction, half, one-third, quarter, numerator, denominator, whole
One whole cut into equal parts gives halves, thirds and quarters, and a fraction is written with a top number and a bottom number. The bottom number is the denominator. It counts all the equal parts of the whole. The top number is the numerator. It counts the parts that are taken. When more parts are taken, the top number grows and the bottom number stays the same. When every part is taken, the two numbers are the same. A whole cut into equal parts gives halves, thirds and quarters. The parts must be equal. The bottom number of a fraction counts all the equal parts. The top number counts the parts that are coloured or taken. When the two numbers are the same, the fraction is one whole. A group of things is named the same way. Two equal parts make halves. Three equal parts make thirds. Four equal parts make fourths, or quarters. One part of a whole is a fraction. The parts must be equal. 3/5. The bottom number, the denominator, tells how many equal parts there are in all. The top number, the numerator, tells how many parts are coloured or taken. One whole. Three thirds make one whole, and so do four quarters. When the top and bottom numbers are the same, the fraction is one whole. 3/7. A group works like a whole. The bottom number counts all the mangoes. The top number counts the ripe mangoes.
