## What it is
**Description:** Share a tray of real things into equal baskets, plates or pouches, take the groups the top number names, and then do the same sharing under a cloth with numbers only.
**Why:** Find a half, a third, a quarter or another fraction of a collection of real things by sharing it into the equal groups the bottom number names and taking the groups the top number names, first with baskets, plates and pouches and then under a cloth with numbers only.
**Look:** A market tray of real things at the top left, a row of matching holders under it with a dial and an object picker beside them, a fraction card, a counter of what the taken holders hold, and on the last phase number boxes behind a cloth.
**Context:** Grade 3, the last working topic of the fractions chapter. An adult sees sums of dividing. What is actually being settled is what the two numbers of a fraction do to a collection of real things: the bottom number says how many equal groups to make and the top number says how many of them to take. A child who reads the pair as two ordinary numbers will give the number of baskets as the answer, or the size of one group when two groups were asked for.
**For the child:** Pick the thing to share. Set the groups. Press Share. Tap the groups the top number names. Count the things in them. Then cover the tray and try with numbers.
**Teaching notes:** Share a heap of real things before the tool opens: pencils into pouches, small fruit into bowls, bottle caps onto plates, one at a time so the groups stay equal, and say the fraction as groups: one pouch out of four. The bottom number is how many groups, and the top number is how many groups to take. When a heap will not share equally, say so out loud: the groups are not equal, so this way of finding a fraction does not work on it. Wait for the child to say what one group has before they say what the fraction is. On the last phase, put a cloth over a bowl of marbles or a tray of fruit, say how many there are, and ask the child to say the two steps in words before they type, then lift the cloth. Use fresh numbers: the exercise's rows of flowers, stars, erasers and pencils are the child's to colour and count.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Share into equal groups: half of a collection
**Screen:** a tray of 14 mangoes, two empty baskets and the Share button lit
**Does:** taps Share, watches the mangoes roll into the two baskets, then taps one basket
**Win:** the tapped basket takes a deep blue rim and the counter reads 7
**Feels:** a tray of mangoes rolling into two baskets, one at a time, until both are the same
**Goal:** half of a collection is what one of two equal groups holds
**Observe:** After Share, every basket, plate or pouch held the same number of things.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- says the number of baskets as the answer → the baskets lift off the tray one at a time and the mangoes in one basket are counted aloud, one number for each mango
- takes both baskets → the basket not taken keeps its amber rim with the word Left under it, and the counter reads only the basket that took the deep blue rim
**Misconception to break:** Giving 2, the number of baskets, instead of the number of mangoes in one basket.

### 2 · Take one group: a third or a quarter
**Screen:** a tray of 28 pencils, the dial at four pouches, and the fraction card showing one over four
**Does:** sets the dial to the bottom number, taps Share, watches the pencils slide into the pouches, and taps one pouch
**Win:** one pouch takes a deep blue rim, three keep amber rims, and the counter reads 7
**Feels:** the same tray of pencils sliding into three pouches, then into four, each pouch holding fewer than before
**Goal:** a third or a quarter is what one holder has when the things are shared into three or four equal holders
**Observe:** The bottom number of the fraction card was always the number of holders.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- uses two pouches for a third or a quarter → the dial lights amber at the bottom number of the fraction card, and the word third or quarter is written beside the pouches
- gives the total of all the pouches instead of one pouch → the pouches that were not tapped keep their amber rims with the word Left under them, and the counter reads only the pouch that took the deep blue rim
**Misconception to break:** Using two pouches for a quarter, because half was just done with two baskets.

### 3 · Take more than one group: two thirds
**Screen:** a tray of 25 marbles, the dial at five bowls, and the fraction card showing two over five
**Does:** shares the marbles into the bowls, then taps two bowls and watches the counter
**Win:** two bowls take deep blue rims, three keep amber rims, and the counter reads 10
**Feels:** two bowls taking deep blue rims and the counter growing by the same amount each time
**Goal:** two fifths, two thirds and the like mean taking as many equal groups as the top number names
**Observe:** Each extra holder tapped made the counter grow by the same amount.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- takes only one bowl for two thirds or two fifths → the top number on the fraction card is lit, and a small number two is written above the bowls, so the bowls to take are counted against it
- gives the size of one bowl as the answer → the two taken bowls are rimmed deep blue in turn, and the counter adds the second bowl to the first
**Misconception to break:** Giving 5, the size of one bowl, which is one fifth and not two fifths.

### 4 · A fraction of a collection without the picture
**Screen:** a cloth over a tray of laddoos with a tag that reads laddoos, and number boxes for the total, the size of one group and the groups taken
**Does:** types the size of one group, then the number of things taken, then lifts the cloth to check
**Win:** the cloth lifts, the laddoos settle on their plates, and the groups match the numbers typed
**Feels:** the same sharing done with numbers under a cloth, and then the cloth lifting to show it was right
**Goal:** a fraction of any collection is found by dividing by the bottom number and then taking the groups the top number names
**Observe:** Under the cloth, the numbers gave the count the holders gave when the cloth came off.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- takes the groups before sharing, using the top number first → the cloth lifts for one beat and the plates are shown filled before any are taken, then it settles again
- stops after dividing and gives the size of one group → the top number is lit, and the number of plates to take is counted out loud one plate at a time
**Misconception to break:** Giving 10, the size of one plate, and forgetting to take three of the plates.

## Delight
Two small surprises. When the last thing lands in the first sharing, the holders give one slow rock like a balanced scale and settle level, because every holder has the same. On the fourth phase, the moment the cloth lifts, the laddoos or bananas slide back onto their plates and trays one at a time to match the numbers the child typed, and the counter reads the same count.

**General miss line:** Look at one group. How many are in it? Then count the groups you take.

## Answer key
To find a fraction of a collection, share the collection into as many equal groups as the bottom number says, then take as many of those groups as the top number says. Half is one group out of two, a third is one group out of three, and a quarter is one group out of four. Two thirds is two groups out of three. Every group must hold the same number, so the total has to share equally. Without the picture, divide the total by the bottom number to get the size of one group, then multiply that size by the top number to get the number of things taken.

## Student controls
- **Object picker**: chooses what is on the tray, from mangoes, laddoos, bananas, pencils, marbles, flowers, books and bangles, and matches the holders to it
- **Holders dial**: sets how many baskets, plates, pouches or bowls there are, from two to six, to match the bottom number
- **Share button**: deals the things out one at a time round the holders, so every holder ends equal
- **Tap a holder**: gives it a deep blue rim and the label Taken, or back to amber and Left
- **Cover the tray button**: lays a cloth over the tray and leaves number boxes to fill with the pad
- **Number pad**: types the size of one group and the number of things taken

## Textbook (this topic)
**Pages:** 76, 77, 78, 79
**Key terms:** fraction, collection, equal groups, numerator, denominator
A fraction of a collection is found by sharing the collection into equal groups and taking the groups the numerator names. To find a fraction of a collection, share it into equal groups. The bottom number says how many groups to make. The top number says how many groups to take. We can also divide the collection by the bottom number to find the size of each group. Share the books into two equal groups. Each group has 5 books. Half of 10 is 5. Share the pencils into three equal groups. Each group has 7 pencils. One third of 21 is 7. For a quarter, share into four equal groups and take one. Share the marbles into three equal groups of 10. Two thirds means take two groups. Two thirds of 30 is 20. Divide 40 by the bottom number, 5. Each group has 8. One fifth of 40 is 8. The bottom number says how many groups to make. The top number says how many groups to take.
