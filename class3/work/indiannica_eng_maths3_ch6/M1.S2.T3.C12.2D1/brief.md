## What it is
**Description:** Sort fraction cards, each with a pizza, a chocolate bar or a jug beside it, into unit, proper and improper, check whether two cards are like or unlike, and line up like fractions to see which is bigger.
**Why:** Read the kind of a fraction from its two numbers, tell like from unlike by the bottom numbers, and say which of two like fractions is bigger by comparing the top numbers, with a real pizza, bar or jug beside every card to check the reading.
**Look:** A tray of fraction cards at the left, each beside a real object cut or filled to show it, two labelled bins at the right, a mat for two cards, and on the last phase two identical objects from one edge and a line-up row.
**Context:** Grade 3, the second working topic of the fractions chapter. An adult sees fractions being circled in a list, which a pencil does well. What is being settled is that the kind of a fraction is read from its two numbers: one on top, the top smaller than the bottom, or the bottoms matching. A child who watches only the size of the numbers will call 9/10 improper because nine and ten are big, or will call two fractions like because their top numbers match. A real pizza, bar or jug beside each card is what lets the child check a reading against something they can picture eating or pouring.
**For the child:** Read the top number and the bottom number of each card. Look at the pizza, bar or jug. Drop the card in its bin. Then line up like things from the smallest to the biggest.
**Teaching notes:** Cut a paper pizza and draw a chocolate bar of ten squares before the tool opens, and colour paper strips of ten squares and line them up from the fewest squares coloured to the most. Ask the child to read the top number and the bottom number of every card aloud before it is dropped in a bin. Wait for them to say which number they looked at. For improper cards ask how many pizzas it takes to hold all the slices. For like and unlike, cover the top numbers with a finger, so only the bottom numbers can be read. On the last phase say the rule in the child's words: the parts are the same size, so more parts taken is bigger. Do not compare two fractions with different bottom numbers, even when the child can guess; say that it needs more steps and has not been learned yet.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Unit fractions: the top number is one
**Screen:** a tray of six cards, each with its pizza, bar, jug, cake, laddoos or kites, and two empty bins, Top number is one and Not one
**Does:** reads the top number of each card, drags it to a bin, then taps Check
**Win:** the unit cards sit in their bin, each object showing a single part on its blue plate
**Feels:** the cards with one slice, square or mark taken gathering in one bin, however big the pizza or bar was
**Goal:** a fraction with one as its top number is a unit fraction
**Observe:** A card with a one on top showed a single slice, square or mark on the blue plate, however many parts the pizza or bar had.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- puts a card in the Top number is one bin because of its bottom number → the object's parts on the blue plate are counted: the taken slices light amber in turn, and the count is written beside the top number
- leaves a card with one slice taken out of the bin, because the pizza has many slices → the pizza's other slices dim to half strength, so the single slice on the blue plate stands alone beside the one on top
**Misconception to break:** Looks at the bottom number, so the chocolate bar card 2/5 goes in the bin with the pizza card 1/5 because both are fifths.

### 2 · Proper and improper: is the top number smaller?
**Screen:** a tray of cards and two bins, Proper and Improper, with a second pizza, bar or cake beside any card that needs one
**Does:** reads both numbers of a card, drags it to a bin, and watches the pizzas and bars
**Win:** an improper card shows one whole pizza on the blue plate and more slices from a second pizza
**Feels:** a second pizza sliding in beside a card exactly when its top number gets past the bottom
**Goal:** a fraction is proper when its top is smaller than its bottom, and improper when it is bigger or the same
**Observe:** A card whose top number was bigger than its bottom number slid a second pizza in beside the first.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- calls a card proper because its top number is a big number → the two numbers are set side by side and the slices on the plate are counted, and a second pizza slides in only where the first pizza is full
- calls a card with the same number top and bottom proper → the whole pizza is ringed in green and labelled Whole, with the words top number the same as bottom number beside it
**Misconception to break:** Calls it proper because it looks like any other fraction, without comparing the top and bottom numbers.

### 3 · Like and unlike: do the bottom numbers match?
**Screen:** a mat with room for two cards and their two objects, and a pair of cards waiting beside it
**Does:** puts two cards on the mat, reads the lit bottom numbers, and taps Like or Unlike
**Win:** the two bottom numbers light together, the two objects show parts of one size, and the mat reads Like
**Feels:** the two bottom numbers lighting up together on the mat, and the top numbers staying quiet
**Goal:** fractions with the same bottom number are like, and fractions with different bottom numbers are unlike
**Observe:** The mat lit the two bottom numbers together, and the top numbers stayed dark.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- says like because the two top numbers are the same → the two bottom numbers light amber together and the top numbers dim to half strength, and the two objects show parts of different sizes
- says unlike because the two cards show different amounts taken → the two objects are shown cut into the same number of equal parts, with the bottom number lit on both, and the words same bottom number appear beside them
**Misconception to break:** Says like because both top numbers are 4 and four parts are on the blue plate in each picture.

### 4 · Comparing like fractions
**Screen:** two identical objects cut into equal parts, side by side from one common edge, and a line-up row
**Does:** taps the object with more parts taken, then drags objects into a row from the smallest to the biggest
**Win:** the bigger one gets a vermilion ring and is labelled Bigger
**Feels:** the longer taken stretch getting its vermilion ring, with all the parts the same size
**Goal:** between two like fractions the one with the bigger top number is bigger
**Observe:** The chocolate bar with more squares taken reached further from the left edge, and the squares were all the same size.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- says the bar with the bigger bottom number is bigger, when the bottom numbers match → both bottom numbers light amber as the same number, and the squares are counted by the ones taken, the longer taken stretch glowing
- tries to compare two cards that have different bottom numbers → the mat says in words that fractions with different bottom numbers need more steps that are not met yet, and offers a pair of like cards
- lines the objects up from the biggest → the first slot is marked Smallest in words, and the object placed there is checked against the object beside it
**Misconception to break:** Looks at the bottom numbers, which match, and then cannot choose, instead of comparing the top numbers.

## Delight
On the fourth phase, when the line-up row is finished in the right order, the chocolate bars slide together into one staircase, each step one square longer than the one before, and the biggest gets its vermilion ring. On any jug card, the juice rises one mark at a time to the top number as the card is picked up.

**General miss line:** Look at the picture again. Count the parts on the plate, then all the parts.

## Answer key
A unit fraction has one as its top number. A proper fraction has a top number smaller than its bottom number, so it is less than one whole pizza, bar or cake. An improper fraction has a top number bigger than its bottom number, or the same, so it is one whole or more. Like fractions have the same bottom number, and unlike fractions have different bottom numbers, whatever the top numbers are. Between two like fractions the one with the bigger top number is bigger, because the parts are the same size and there are more of them. Fractions with different bottom numbers are not compared here.
