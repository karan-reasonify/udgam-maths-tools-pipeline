## What it is
**Description:** Fill the rupees box and the paise box of a price tag, put the dot between them, and write each amount in figures and in words.
**Why:** Name the rupees and the paise of a price and see that a hundred paise make a rupee, write an amount with a dot between the rupees and the paise, keep the paise to two digits, and turn an amount from words into figures and from figures into words.
**Look:** A price tag on a real shop thing with a deep blue Rupees box, a dot and a vermilion Paise box of two cells, a bar of ten blocks beside a Rs 1 coin, a words card, the Words and figures switch, and the number pad down the right of the canvas.
**Context:** Grade 3, the first working topic of the money chapter that writes an amount. An adult sees an amount being written with a dot, which a paper price tag does well. What is being settled is which part of an amount is the rupees and which is the paise, that the dot is what keeps them apart, that the paise always take two digits, and that an amount said in words and an amount written in figures are one amount. A child who writes 5 paise as one digit after the dot writes 50 paise without knowing it, and a child who reads the number after the dot as a smaller number of rupees will say that 9.05 and 9.5 are the same.
**For the child:** Fill the boxes of the price tag. Put the dot between the rupees and the paise. Give the paise two digits. Say each amount aloud.
**Teaching notes:** Give each child a paper price tag with two boxes and a dot drawn on it before the tool opens, and ask them to write a few prices for classroom things and read them aloud. Always ask which part is the rupees and which is the paise before a digit is typed, and say each amount aloud as the rupees and then the paise, as in twelve rupees sixty paise. Wait for a child to say that the paise take two digits, rather than saying it for them. If a child writes a single digit of paise, ask how many digits paise take and how many cells the paise box has. Use fresh amounts: the printed amounts are the child's to write.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Rupees and paise: a hundred paise make a rupee
**Screen:** a price tag on a ruler on a shop shelf, with a Rs 1 coin and a bar of ten blocks under it
**Does:** taps the rupees part and the paise part of the tag, and fills the bar block by block to the coin
**Win:** the rupees box lights deep blue, the paise box lights vermilion, and the full bar settles on the coin
**Feels:** the bar filling block by block until it settles on the coin, one rupee made of a hundred paise
**Goal:** a price has a rupees part and a paise part, and a hundred paise make one rupee
**Observe:** The rupees box lit deep blue beside the whole part of the price and the paise box lit vermilion beside the small part, and the bar of ten blocks filled the Rs 1 coin.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- reads the paise as the bigger part because its number is bigger → the bar of ten blocks fills the coin and the paise box shows that the paise are only a part of one rupee, while the rupees box counts whole coins
- says the number before the dot is the paise → the rupees box lights deep blue and the paise box vermilion, and the words card says the rupees first and the paise after
**Misconception to break:** Thinking 35 is worth more than 7 because 35 is the bigger number.

### 2 · The dot between the rupees and the paise
**Screen:** a price tag on a notebook with two empty boxes, and the dot waiting between them
**Does:** types the rupees, then moves to the paise and types them, and watches the dot sit between the boxes
**Win:** the dot lights between the boxes, the words card says the amount aloud and a green tag holds it
**Feels:** the dot dropping into its place between the two boxes as soon as both are filled
**Goal:** an amount is written Rs, the rupees, a dot and the paise, and the dot keeps the two parts apart
**Observe:** A dot lit between the two boxes once both were filled, and the words card said the rupees and then the paise.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes the rupees and the paise side by side with no dot → the two boxes are shown apart with the dot lit between them, and the words card reads the amount as one number and then as rupees and paise
- writes the word paise at the end of the amount → the Paise heading stays over the second box, so the word is already written there, and the amount on the green tag ends with its last digit
**Misconception to break:** Leaving out the dot and writing 1680, or writing the word paise at the end.

### 3 · Two digits for paise
**Screen:** a price tag on a pencil box whose paise box has two cells, one filled and one waiting
**Does:** types a paise number of one digit, and watches a 0 slide into the empty cell in front of it
**Win:** the 0 slides in, both cells glow vermilion, and the words card says the amount
**Feels:** the 0 sliding in from the left until both paise cells are full
**Goal:** the paise of an amount always take two digits, so a single digit has a 0 in front of it
**Observe:** A single digit typed into the paise box had a 0 slide in front of it, so both cells were always full.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- types a single digit of paise and leaves the second cell empty → a 0 slides into the first cell and both cells fill vermilion, and the words card says the amount aloud with its paise
- drops the 0 at the end of the paise, writing forty paise as 4 → the second cell stays filled with its 0, and the words card says forty paise and not four paise
**Misconception to break:** Writing 4.9, which means 4 rupees and 90 paise.

### 4 · Saying and writing an amount in words and in figures
**Screen:** a shop board holding an amount in words beside an empty price tag, and a filled tag beside an empty board
**Does:** writes an amount given in words as figures on the tag, and turns a filled tag into words on the board
**Win:** the tag fills, the words and the figures agree, and a green tag holds the amount
**Feels:** the words and the figures agreeing, the same amount said on the board and written on the tag
**Goal:** an amount said in words and an amount written in figures are one amount, even for whole rupees and for less than a rupee
**Observe:** The tag and the shop board showed the same amount, one in figures and one in words, and an amount of whole rupees showed two zeros after the dot.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes an amount under one rupee with no rupees digit, as .85 → the Rupees box shows a 0 and the dot stays lit, and the words card says no rupees and eighty-five paise
- thinks a tag with two zeros after the dot is a different price from the whole rupees → the tag and the shop board show the same amount, and the bar shows the paise part empty, so the price is whole rupees
**Misconception to break:** Writing 48.1 and dropping the 0 of the ten paise.

## Delight

On the fourth phase, when the tag is filled, it swings once on its string and the shop board beside it fills with the same amount in words.

## Answer key
An amount of money has two parts. The rupees are the whole part and the paise are the small part, and 100 paise make 1 rupee. In figures an amount is written Rs, then the rupees, then a dot, then the paise, and the word paise is not written at the end. Paise always take two digits, so 5 paise is written 05 and the amount is Rs 9.05 and not 9.5. A whole number of rupees may be written with two zeros after the dot, as Rs 8.00, and an amount under one rupee is written with 0 rupees, as Rs 0.07. An amount is said as the rupees and then the paise, so Rs 12.60 is twelve rupees sixty paise.

## Textbook (this topic)
**Pages:** 130, 131
**Key terms:** rupees, paise, Rs, dot, amount
An amount of money is written as the rupees, a dot and exactly two digits of paise, and it is read and written both in figures and in words. A hundred paise make one rupee. We write an amount as Rs, then the rupees, then a dot, then two digits of paise. For example, 14 rupees and 30 paise is Rs 14.30. An amount can be said in words and written in figures. We use paise for a part of a rupee. 100 paise make 1 rupee. Rs is short for rupees. Books also use a special sign for rupees. Write Rs, then the rupees, then a dot, then the paise. Rs 12 and 60 paise is Rs 12.60. The dot keeps the rupees and the paise apart. We do not write the word paise at the end. Paise always take two digits. 5 paise is written 05, so Rs 9 and 5 paise is Rs 9.05. 40 paise is written 40, so Rs 3 and 40 paise is Rs 3.40. Say the rupees first, then the paise. Rs 27 and 50 paise is Rs 27.50, said as twenty-seven rupees fifty paise. A whole number of rupees has no paise, so Rs 8 is Rs 8.00. An amount under a rupee has 0 rupees, so 7 paise is Rs 0.07.
