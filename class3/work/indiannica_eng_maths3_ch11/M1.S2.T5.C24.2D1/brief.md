## What it is
**Description:** Set amounts in a Rupees column and a Paise column, carry or borrow a rupee for a hundred paise, and find the total, what is left and the change.
**Why:** Set two amounts one under the other with the dots in a line, add them by carrying one rupee for a hundred paise, subtract them by borrowing one rupee as a hundred paise, and turn a money story into one of the two sums.
**Look:** A board ruled into a deep blue Rupees column and a vermilion Paise column, an amber shelf of price tags, a green answer row, the Carry, Borrow and Plain columns buttons, and the number pad down the right of the canvas.
**Context:** Grade 3, the fourth working topic of the money chapter. An adult sees a column sum, which a worksheet shows well. What is being settled is that the dot, not the right edge, is what the numbers line up on, that a hundred paise are exactly one rupee and cross from the paise column to the rupees column as a single one, and that borrowing a rupee gives the paise a hundred, not ten. A child who lines the amounts up on the right will add rupees to paise with great confidence, and a child who borrows ten paise will take away the wrong amount.
**For the child:** Set the two amounts in the columns. Make the dots stand in one straight line. Work the paise first. Carry or borrow one rupee when you need to.
**Teaching notes:** Give each pair two paper price tags and a sheet ruled into a Rupees column and a Paise column before the tool opens, and ask them to set the tags one under the other and say what is under what. Always ask where the dots are before any digit is added, and have the paise worked first with a finger on the Paise column. Wait for a child to say that a hundred paise are one rupee, rather than saying it for them. Say carry when a hundred paise go over to the rupees and borrow when a rupee comes over to the paise, every time. If a child lines the numbers up on the right edge, ask which part of each amount is the rupees. Use fresh amounts: the printed sums are the child's to set out and work.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Rupees under rupees, paise under paise
**Screen:** the empty board at a fair stall with two price tags waiting on the shelf and the dot line drawn between the columns
**Does:** drags each part of both amounts into its column, checks that the dots line up, and writes two zeros after a whole amount
**Win:** the dots fall into one line, the two headings light in their own colours, and a whole amount shows its two zeros
**Feels:** the digits sliding into their columns until every dot stands on the same line
**Goal:** rupees stand under rupees, paise under paise, the dots make one straight line, and a whole number of rupees takes two zeros
**Observe:** When the amounts were set one under the other, the dots stood in one straight line only when the rupees were under the rupees and the paise under the paise.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- lines the amounts up on the right edge so the paise sit under the rupees → the Rupees and Paise headings stay lit, the dot line is drawn through the board, and the digits are slid back until every dot stands on it
- leaves out the zeros of a whole amount, so the paise column is empty → the paise cells of that amount blink once and two zeros are written in, so the whole amount is read as rupees and no paise
**Misconception to break:** Lining the numbers up on the right edge, so the 85 paise sit under the 3 rupees.

### 2 · Adding: a hundred paise carry one rupee
**Screen:** the board with two amounts set, a plus sign at the left of the second row, and a round carry cell above the rupees
**Does:** adds the paise, presses Carry when they reach a hundred, adds the rupees with the carried one, and types the answer
**Win:** a bundle of a hundred paise slides across and lands as one rupee, and the answer row lights green
**Feels:** a hundred paise sliding across the board and landing as a single rupee
**Goal:** the paise are added first, and a hundred paise or more send one rupee across to the rupees column
**Observe:** When the paise made a hundred or more, a bundle of a hundred paise slid over to the rupees column and landed as exactly one rupee.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes all the paise of the sum under Paise, so the paise column has three digits → the Paise column holds only two cells, so the extra hundred paise rise out of it as a bundle and slide over to the carry cell as one rupee
- adds the rupees and forgets the carried rupee → the carry cell glows deep blue until it is added, and the rupees are counted again with the carried one included
**Misconception to break:** Writing 115 under Paise, so the answer has three digits of paise.

### 3 · Subtracting: one rupee borrowed is a hundred paise
**Screen:** the board with a bigger amount above a smaller one, a minus sign at the left, and a borrow strip above the top row
**Does:** looks at the paise, presses Borrow when they are too few, takes away the paise and then the rupees, and types the answer
**Win:** one rupee breaks into a hundred paise, the rupees drop by one, and the answer row lights green
**Feels:** one rupee breaking into a hundred paise and the rupees column dropping by one
**Goal:** the smaller amount goes below the bigger, and when the top paise are too few one rupee breaks into a hundred paise
**Observe:** When the top paise were too few, one rupee broke into a hundred paise and the rupees column dropped by exactly one.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- takes the smaller paise from the bigger paise the wrong way round → the paise are shown in a row with the top amount first, the borrow strip opens, and the paise to take away are counted from the top amount only
- borrows ten paise instead of a hundred → the borrowed rupee breaks into a hundred small paise, counted in tens across the cells, so ten tens fill the paise column
**Misconception to break:** Taking the smaller paise from the bigger paise the wrong way round, 35 minus 10.

### 4 · Money problems: how much in all, how much left, how much change
**Screen:** a shop counter with a bill, a note and a purse beside the board, and a short money story written above it
**Does:** reads the story, chooses to add or to subtract, sets the amounts in columns, works them and types the answer
**Win:** the amounts settle into the columns, the answer lights green, and the bill and the note show the same amounts
**Feels:** the story settling into the columns and the right sign taking its place
**Goal:** a story that puts money together is added, and a story about what is left, the change or how much more is subtracted
**Observe:** A story that put money together was set as an adding sum, and a story about what was left or the change was set as a taking away sum.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- adds the note and the bill to find the change → the note and the bill are laid side by side on the counter, the bill is taken from the note, and the change is what is left
- subtracts the two prices when the story asks how much in all → the two prices slide together onto one bill slip and the board switches to an adding sum, with the plus sign at the left
**Misconception to break:** Adding the note and the bill instead of taking the bill from the note.

## Delight
On the second phase, as the paise are added, the bundle of a hundred paise slides across the board so slowly that it seems to hesitate, and when it lands in the carry cell it turns into a single rupee coin and the answer row lights green. On the third phase, one rupee coin breaks into a hundred small paise that rain softly into the paise cells.

**General miss line:** Look at the dots again. Do the rupees and the paise stand in their own columns?

## Answer key
To add or subtract money, set the amounts one under the other so that rupees stand under rupees, paise under paise and the dots make one straight line. If an amount has no paise, write two zeros after the dot. To add, add the paise first. If they make 100 paise or more, write the paise over 100 and carry 1 rupee to the rupees column, then add the rupees. To subtract, put the smaller amount below the bigger one and take the paise first. If the top paise are too few, borrow 1 rupee from the rupees and count it as 100 paise, then take the rupees. Adding finds how much in all. Subtracting finds how much is left, the change or how much more.

## Student controls
- **Digit cells**: take the digits of an amount, so each stands in its own column
- **Carry button**: gathers a hundred paise into a bundle that slides to the Rupees column as one rupee
- **Borrow button**: breaks one rupee into a hundred paise and lowers the rupees by one
- **Show tags button**: slides each amount onto an amber price tag on the shelf
- **Plain columns button**: redraws the same board with no scene
- **Number pad**: types the rupees and the paise of the answer, with a dot key and a clear key

Amounts of money are set in a rupees column and a paise column with the dots in a line, added by carrying one rupee for a hundred paise, subtracted by borrowing one rupee as a hundred paise, and the same two moves solve money problems. Rupees go under rupees and paise go under paise, with the dots in one straight line. A whole number of rupees takes two zeros after the dot. Add the paise first. When they make a hundred or more, carry one rupee to the rupees. Put the smaller amount under the bigger one and take the paise first. When the top paise are too few, borrow one rupee, which is a hundred paise. Add to find how much in all. Subtract to find how much is left, the change or how much more. Rupees go under rupees. Paise go under paise. The dots stand in one straight line. If an amount has no paise, write two zeros after the dot. So Rs 9 is written Rs 9.00. Add the paise first. 60 paise plus 70 paise is 130 paise. A hundred paise make one rupee, so 130 paise is Rs 1 and 30 paise. Write 30 under Paise. Carry Rs 1 to the rupees. Then add the rupees. Rs 28.60 plus Rs 14.70 is Rs 43.30. Put the smaller amount below the bigger one. If the top paise are too few, borrow Rs 1 from the rupees. Rs 1 is 100 paise. 100 paise plus 30 paise is 130 paise. 130 paise minus 45 paise is 85 paise. The rupees fall to Rs 51. Rs 51 minus Rs 18 is Rs 33. So Rs 52.30 minus Rs 18.45 is Rs 33.85. Put money together and add to find how much in all. Rs 38.50 plus Rs 12.25 is Rs 50.75. Take money away and subtract to find how much is left, the change, or how much more. Rs 100 minus Rs 50.75 is Rs 49.25.
