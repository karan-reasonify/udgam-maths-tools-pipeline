## What it is
**Description:** Put a part over its whole, both in one unit, and make it a per cent with the bracketed rule, the % sign last.
**Why:** Write one number as a per cent of another by putting the part over the whole, with the bracketed rule, both amounts in one unit first, and tell the matches not won from the matches counted against a team.
**Look:** A whole bar labelled 100% over a part bar at the same scale. The fraction card and the Rule button stand beside them, with the Unit fact card, and on the last phase a row of match tiles.
**Context:** Grade 5, the pages on one number as a per cent of another. An adult sees a formula. What the pages are doing is naming a part against its whole: the part over the whole, made into hundredths. Both amounts are written in the same unit first, a match not won may have been a draw, and the opening question can be tried here, with its answer left to the child.
**For the child:** Name the whole. Put the part over it. Make both amounts one unit first, and write the % sign after the brackets.
**Teaching notes:** Count a group of the class's own before the tool opens, and write the part over the number asked. Name the whole first every time. Write the brackets: the first number over the second, times 100, then the % sign. Put both amounts in the same unit first, and say the unit fact aloud. A match not won may be a draw, so ask which the question wants. The opening question can be tried now, and its answer is the child's own. Use fresh numbers: the exercise's pairs are the child's to work.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · The part over the whole
**Screen:** a whole bar of 25 beads with 14 red, 8 blue and 3 yellow, and the fraction card blank
**Does:** taps Part over whole for each colour, grows each fraction to hundredths, and reads the bands
**Win:** the bands read 56%, 32% and 12%, and together they fill the whole bar
**Feels:** the coloured bands filling the whole bar exactly
**Goal:** the part over the whole, made into hundredths, is the per cent. The parts of one whole make 100% together
**Observe:** The coloured bands of one whole always filled the whole bar.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- puts the part over the other parts instead of over the whole → the whole bar lights end to end in amber and its count is written under it, beside the child's fraction, with the part's band kept
- types band per cents that add to more than the whole → the bands are laid end to end along the whole bar, and the one that runs past its end is lit amber
**Misconception to break:** Writing 23/27, the marigolds over the other plants, instead of over all 50.

### 2 · The rule with brackets
**Screen:** the question card asking what per cent 18 is of 30, and the Rule button lit
**Does:** taps Rule, places each number in the formula, and places the % sign last
**Win:** the line reads (18/30 x 100)% = 60%, with the % sign after the closing bracket
**Feels:** the brackets closing, and the sign landing last
**Goal:** the first number over the second, times 100, in brackets, then the % sign
**Observe:** The % sign could only be placed after the closing bracket.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- puts the second number over the first → the part bar and the whole bar are drawn for the child's order, the part longer than its whole, lit amber
- writes the % sign inside the brackets → the sign tile goes back to its tray, and the closing bracket is lit amber until the sign is placed after it
**Misconception to break:** Putting the second number over the first, 40/26, which gives more than one whole for a part.

### 3 · Same unit first
**Screen:** a whole bar of 2 kg and a part bar of 600 g, too long to lie against it
**Does:** picks the Unit fact card, watches the whole relabel in grams, and finds the per cent
**Win:** the whole bar reads 2000 g in violet, and the card reads 600 g is 30% of 2000 g
**Feels:** two amounts in two units becoming one bar in one unit
**Goal:** first write both amounts in the same unit, and state the unit fact used
**Observe:** The bars would not lie against each other until both amounts were in one unit.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- puts the two numbers over each other without changing a unit → the part bar is drawn to the child's numbers, far longer than the whole, and the two unit words are lit amber
- picks a unit fact that does not join the two units → the chosen fact is written in violet beside the bar, and the bar redraws to it, so the part is seen not to fit
**Misconception to break:** Writing 1200/5, which mixes millilitres with litres and gives far more than one whole.

### 4 · Matches not won
**Screen:** thirty match tiles, twenty-one of them marked won and three turned to show a draw
**Does:** drags the tiles that were not won into their own row, and finds that row's per cent
**Win:** nine tiles in the Not won row, and the card reads 30% not won
**Feels:** a draw tile flipping over and staying in the Not won row
**Goal:** not won is the whole take away the matches won, and it counts as lost only when every match was either won or lost
**Observe:** A tile turned to show a draw stayed in the Not won row.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- gives the per cent won when the question asks for not won → the Not won row is lit rose and counted tile by tile, beside the Won row's count
- counts every Not won tile as a loss, draws too → the draw tile turns over to show the word Draw and stays in the Not won row, set apart from the others and lit amber
**Misconception to break:** Giving 68%, the per cent won, when the question asks for the matches not won.

## Delight
On the third phase, the moment the Unit fact card is dropped, the whole bar's label rewrites itself in violet, and the part bar shrinks to its true length and settles against the whole with a soft click.

**General miss line:** Name the whole again, then put the part over it.

## Answer key
Name the whole first. Write the part over the whole and make the denominator 100, or use the rule: the first number over the second, times 100, in brackets, then the % sign. The parts of one whole, as per cents, make 100% together. First write both amounts in the same unit, and say the unit fact used, such as 1 kg = 1000 g. A match that was not won may have been a draw, so not won counts as lost only when every match was either won or lost. The question in the opening box is the child's to answer.

## Student controls
- **Part over whole button**: writes the part over the whole as a fraction
- **Times buttons**: grow both terms until the denominator is 100
- **Rule button**: builds the first number over the second times 100 in brackets, then the % sign
- **Unit fact card**: rewrites an amount so both are in one unit
- **Match tiles**: are dragged into Won and Not won, and can be turned to show a draw
- **Number pad**: types a number, an amount or a per cent
