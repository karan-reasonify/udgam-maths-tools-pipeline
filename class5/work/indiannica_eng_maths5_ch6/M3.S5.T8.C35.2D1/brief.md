## What it is
**Description:** Shade a shape and name the shaded part three ways, as a fraction, a decimal and a per cent, all one amount.
**Why:** Name a shaded part as a fraction, a decimal and a per cent, see all three as one amount, accept any equivalent fraction unless lowest terms are asked, start from a per cent, and say the chapter's words in the child's own terms.
**Look:** A shape at the left, cut into equal parts and labelled Whole and 100%. The three name cards stand at its right, and on the last phase the word cards and the can-do cards.
**Context:** Grade 5, the closing page on percentage. An adult sees three answer lines under each shape. What the page is doing is naming one amount three ways: a fraction, a decimal and a per cent, each read through hundredths. Any equivalent fraction is right unless lowest terms are asked, and the self-assessment and the words are the child's own.
**For the child:** Shade the shape. Name the shaded part as a fraction, a decimal and a per cent, and check that all three are one amount.
**Teaching notes:** Shade shapes on squared paper before the tool opens, cut into two, four, five, ten, twenty or twenty-five equal parts. Name every shaded part three ways and check all three through hundredths. Accept any equivalent fraction unless lowest terms are asked, and 0.3 or 0.30 alike. Ask for a picture beside each word: a hundred grid for per cent, two equal bars shaded alike for equivalent fractions. The can-do list is the child's own, ticked honestly. Use fresh shapes: the closing page's shapes are the child's to work.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Three names for one part
**Screen:** a circle cut into 20 equal parts with 11 shaded, and the three cards blank
**Does:** counts the shaded parts, types the fraction, taps Hundredths, and reads the decimal and the per cent
**Win:** the cards read 11/20, 0.55 and 55%, and all three turn green together
**Feels:** three cards lighting green together for one shading
**Goal:** a shaded part is named as a fraction, a decimal and a per cent, and all three are one amount
**Observe:** All three cards turned green at the same moment.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- puts the shaded parts over the unshaded parts → every part of the shape is numbered in turn, shaded and unshaded, and the last number is lit amber under the Fraction card
- writes the decimal from the numerator alone, without making hundredths → the Hundredths button pulses, and a second copy of the shape shades what the child's decimal names beside the first
**Misconception to break:** Writing 11/9, the shaded parts over the unshaded parts instead of over all 20.

### 2 · Lowest terms or not
**Screen:** a bar of 50 equal cells with 22 shaded, and the Fraction card reading 22/50
**Does:** taps Simplify, watches the shading stay still, and compares the two fractions
**Win:** the Fraction card shows 22/50 and 11/25 side by side, with 0.44 and 44% under both
**Feels:** the fraction changing while the shading holds still
**Goal:** any equivalent fraction is right unless lowest terms are asked, and the HCF gives lowest terms
**Observe:** Simplify changed the Fraction card, and the shading stayed exactly as it was.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- calls a fraction wrong because it is not in lowest terms → both fractions stand side by side over the same shading, each grown to the same hundredths, and both are lit green
- divides the two terms by different numbers → the shape beside the child's fraction is shaded to it, plainly a different amount, and the two divisors are lit amber
**Misconception to break:** Calling 22/50 wrong because it is not in lowest terms, when the question did not ask for them.

### 3 · Starting from a per cent
**Screen:** a square of 25 equal small squares, empty, and the Per cent card reading 36%
**Does:** watches the hundredths shade, taps Simplify, and types the decimal
**Win:** nine small squares shaded, and the cards read 9/25, 0.36 and 36%
**Feels:** the shape filling to the per cent, then the fraction shrinking to lowest terms
**Goal:** a per cent is written over 100 and simplified for the fraction, and its hundredths give the decimal
**Observe:** Typing a per cent shaded that many hundredths of the shape, whatever its number of parts.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes the per cent over ten instead of one hundred → a second copy of the shape shades what the child's fraction names beside the per cent's own shading, far more, and the denominator is lit amber
- stops at the fraction over one hundred when lowest terms are asked → the Simplify button pulses once, and the words lowest terms in the question are lit amber
**Misconception to break:** Writing 45/10 or 4.5, which reads 45% as tenths instead of hundredths.

### 4 · Words to know
**Screen:** three word cards, a hundred grid, two equal bars shaded alike, and the can-do cards
**Does:** drags each word card onto its picture, says the word in its own words, and ticks each can-do card
**Win:** each word card sits on its picture, and every can-do card carries the child's own tick
**Feels:** each word landing on the picture that shows it
**Goal:** per cent means out of a hundred, a percentage is an amount written as a per cent, and equivalent fractions name the same amount
**Observe:** Each word card fitted one picture best.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- swaps the per cent card and the percentage card → both pictures are lit in turn, and the meaning of each word is read under its picture, with the swapped cards kept where the child put them
- puts the equivalent fractions card on bars shaded to different amounts → the two bars are laid one above the other and their shaded lengths compared, the difference lit amber
**Misconception to break:** Reading 37% as the whole number 37, which leaves out the out of a hundred.

## Delight
On the first phase, the moment the last card is right, the three cards chime one after another and turn green together, and a thin green outline runs once around the shaded part.

**General miss line:** Count the shaded parts and all the parts again.

## Answer key
Count the shaded parts and the equal parts of the whole, and write the fraction. Grow both terms until the denominator is 100: the hundredths give the decimal, and the number of hundredths is the per cent. All three name one amount. Any equivalent fraction is right unless lowest terms are asked; for lowest terms, divide both terms by their HCF. From a per cent, write it over 100, then simplify. 0.3 and 0.30 are both right. Per cent means out of a hundred. A percentage is an amount written as a per cent. Equivalent fractions name the same amount.

## Student controls
- **Shape switch**: picks a bar of cells, a circle or a square of small squares
- **Parts buttons**: cut the shape into 2, 4, 5, 10, 20, 25 or 50 equal parts
- **Tap to shade**: shades a part deep blue, or clears it when tapped again
- **Hundredths button**: grows both terms until the denominator is 100
- **Simplify button**: divides both terms by their HCF, the shading unchanged
- **Word cards**: are dragged onto pictures of per cent, percentage and equivalent fractions
- **Can-do cards**: are ticked Yes, Almost or Not sure by the child
- **Number pad**: types a fraction, a decimal or a per cent
