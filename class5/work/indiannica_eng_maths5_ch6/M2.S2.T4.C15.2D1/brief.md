## What it is
**Description:** Read a decimal as hundredths on hundred grids, and write the number of hundredths as the per cent with the % sign kept.
**Why:** Write a decimal as a per cent by reading it as hundredths, with the % sign kept, including tenths, decimals above one and whole numbers.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Hundredths are per cents
**Screen:** the card showing 0.62 over one empty grid, and the % sign tile beside the per cent card
**Does:** taps Read as hundredths, reads the counter, types the per cent and places the % sign
**Win:** the counter stops at sixty-two, and the cards read 62/100 and 62%
**Feels:** the counter and the per cent card landing on the same number
**Goal:** a decimal with two places is a number of hundredths, and that number is the per cent
**Observe:** The Hundredths counter stopped at the number the per cent card showed.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- puts the % sign straight onto the decimal → a second grid beside the first shades what the child's answer names, less than a single cell, and the counter's own count is lit amber
- types the count of hundredths without the % sign → the per cent card shows that many whole grids faded to half strength, and the sign tile pulses once in its tray
**Misconception to break:** Writing 0.39%, which puts the sign on the decimal and names less than one hundredth.

### 2 · Tenths as per cents
**Screen:** the card showing 0.5 over one grid with five columns shaded
**Does:** taps Split tenths, watches each column cut into ten, and types the per cent
**Win:** the counter reads fifty, and the cards read 50/100 and 50%
**Feels:** each column breaking into ten small cells
**Goal:** each tenth is ten hundredths, so a number of tenths is ten times as many hundredths
**Observe:** Each split tenth became a column of ten counted cells.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- counts the tenths as hundredths, giving a per cent ten times too small → Split tenths cuts each shaded column into ten cells, and the cells are counted one by one in amber beside the child's count
- counts the unshaded columns → each shaded column is numbered in turn, and the unshaded ones stay warm white and unnumbered
**Misconception to break:** Writing 9%, which counts the tenths as if they were hundredths.

### 3 · Decimals greater than one
**Screen:** the card showing 1.7 over two grids, the first whole and the second empty
**Does:** taps Read as hundredths, counts the whole grid and the columns of the second, and types the per cent
**Win:** the counter reads one hundred and seventy, and the per cent card reads 170%, past 100%
**Feels:** the count rolling past one hundred into the next grid
**Goal:** each whole is one hundred hundredths. So an amount above one whole is above 100%
**Observe:** Once a grid was full, the count went on into the next grid, past one hundred.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- skips the zero that holds the tenths place → the grey 0 is ringed in amber, and the part grid shows single cells with no full column, the columns counted as none
- counts a full grid as one, not one hundred → the full grid's cells are counted in a quick amber sweep, and the counter climbs with them to one hundred
**Misconception to break:** Writing 280%, which skips the zero holding the tenths place.

### 4 · Whole numbers as per cents
**Screen:** the card showing 5 and a row of empty whole grids
**Does:** fills one whole grid for each whole, counts the lots of one hundred, and types the per cent
**Win:** five full grids, and the line reads (5 x 100)% = 500%
**Feels:** row after row of full grids, each one a hundred per cent
**Goal:** a whole number is that many lots of 100%
**Observe:** Until the sign tile was placed, the per cent card showed whole grids.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- types the whole number with the % sign straight after it → each whole grid is labelled one hundred per cent in turn, and a second row shades what the child's answer names, only a few cells of one grid
- counts one hundred once instead of once for each whole → the grids fill one at a time, each ringed amber as it is counted, and the counter climbs one hundred for each
**Misconception to break:** Writing 7%, which is seven hundredths of one whole, not seven wholes.

## Delight
On the third phase, the instant the first grid fills, it gives a small bounce and the count rolls on into the second grid without stopping, the per cent card ticking past one hundred.

**General miss line:** Read it as hundredths again, one column at a time.

## Answer key
Read the decimal as hundredths, and the number of hundredths is the per cent, with the % sign kept. Tenths become hundredths as ten times as many: each tenth is 10 hundredths. Each whole is 100 hundredths. So an amount above one whole is above 100%. A whole number is that many lots of 100%, and the same comes from (whole number x 100)% with the brackets written. The route is always decimal, then hundredths, then per cent. Without its % sign the answer would mean wholes.

## Textbook (this topic)
**Pages:** 93
**Key terms:** hundredths, tenths, whole number, % sign
A decimal becomes a per cent through its hundredths, with the % sign kept every time. A whole number is that many lots of 100%. Read the decimal as hundredths, and the number of hundredths is the per cent. Tenths become hundredths first, and each whole is a hundred hundredths, so an amount more than one whole is more than a hundred per cent. Keep the % sign every time. 0.47 means 47 hundredths, so 0.47 = 47/100. Per cent means out of a hundred, so 47 hundredths is 47%. Keep the % sign: 47 without it would mean 47 wholes. 0.3 means 3 tenths. Each tenth is 10 hundredths, so 3 tenths is 30 hundredths. So 0.3 = 30/100, which is 30%. 1 whole is 100 hundredths, so 1.64 is 164 hundredths. So 1.64 = 164/100, which is 164%. It is more than 100%, because 1.64 is more than one whole. One whole is 100%. As hundredths, 4 = 400/100, which is 400%. The same comes from multiplying by 100 and writing the % sign: (4 x 100)% = 400%.
