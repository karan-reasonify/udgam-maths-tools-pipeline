## What it is
**Description:** Shade a hundred grid, read the shading as a fraction over 100 and as a per cent, and fold the grid into a strip of ten equal cells.
**Why:** Read and shade a per cent on a hundred grid as a count out of a hundred, write it as a fraction over 100 and with the % sign, and see a strip of ten equal cells as the same whole in columns.
**Look:** A full ten by ten grid at the left, the Columns and cells counter under it, a fraction card and a per cent card at its right, and on the last phase a strip of ten equal cells.
**Context:** Grade 5, the first pages on per cent. An adult sees a new sign. What the pages are doing is counting out of a hundred: a per cent is the number of cells shaded when the whole is cut into one hundred equal cells. Any cells of the right number are correct, and a strip of ten equal cells is the same idea in columns.
**For the child:** Count full columns first, then single cells. Shade any cells of the right number. Fold the grid into a strip of ten.
**Teaching notes:** Shade a hundred grid on squared paper before the tool opens, and say per cent means out of a hundred every time the sign is written. Count full columns of ten first and single cells after. Any cells of the right count are correct, so accept a scattered shading as readily as a tidy one. Fold a paper strip of ten equal cells beside the grid so each cell is seen to be one column. A percentage is an amount written as a per cent. Use fresh numbers: the exercise's fractions are the child's to shade.
**Placement:** Explore

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Per cent means out of a hundred
**Screen:** a grid with five full columns and three more cells shaded, and the cards blank
**Does:** counts the full columns, then the single cells, and types the count over one hundred
**Win:** the fraction card reads 53/100 and the per cent card 53%, both green
**Feels:** the count out of a hundred turning into a per cent in one step
**Goal:** a number of shaded cells out of the 100 cells of a hundred grid is that many per cent
**Observe:** Scatter moved the shaded cells about, and the per cent card did not change.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- counts the unshaded cells instead of the shaded ones → each shaded cell gets a small number in turn, column by column, and the unshaded cells stay warm white and unnumbered
- counts each full column as one cell → each full column lights amber in turn with its ten cells numbered down it, and the counter adds ten for it
**Misconception to break:** Giving 37%, which counts the cells left unshaded instead of the cells shaded.

### 2 · Hundredths as per cents
**Screen:** the question card showing 31/100 and an empty grid
**Does:** shades as many cells as the numerator says, and types the per cent
**Win:** three columns and one cell shaded, and the per cent card reads 31%
**Feels:** the fraction card and the per cent card saying the same number
**Goal:** a fraction over 100 is written as a per cent by its numerator, with the % sign
**Observe:** Every full column held exactly ten cells.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- types the numerator with no % sign → the grid's count is written under it with the word hundredths, and the empty place after the child's number in the answer slot is lit amber
- types the denominator as the per cent → a second grid beside the first shades what the child typed, every cell of it, so the two shadings are seen side by side
**Misconception to break:** Writing 71 with no % sign, which would mean 71 wholes rather than 71 hundredths.

### 3 · Shading a per cent
**Screen:** an empty grid and the question card asking for 27%
**Does:** shades full columns first, then single cells, then taps Scatter and watches the cards
**Win:** the cells are scattered to new places, and the per cent card still reads 27%
**Feels:** the cells scattering across the grid while the per cent card holds still
**Goal:** any cells of the right count show a per cent, full columns of ten first
**Observe:** The numerator on the fraction card and the number on the per cent card were always the same.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- swaps the tens and the ones, shading the ones digit as full columns → the shaded columns are numbered ten, twenty and on in amber, and the question's per cent is written beside the running count so the two are read together
- clears a scattered shading, believing the cells must sit in one block → Scatter spreads the shading again, and the per cent card and the Columns and cells counter read the same count as before
**Misconception to break:** Shading four full columns and three more cells, 43 in all, which swaps the columns and the single cells.

### 4 · A strip of ten
**Screen:** the grid with its ten columns outlined in amber and the Fold button lit
**Does:** taps Fold, counts the cells of the strip, and shades some of them
**Win:** four cells shaded, and the per cent card reads 40% under the words four tenths
**Feels:** a whole grid folding into ten wide cells
**Goal:** a strip of ten equal cells is the whole, and each cell is ten hundredths of it
**Observe:** After folding, one cell of the strip covered what a whole column had.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- counts each cell of the strip as one per cent → Fold runs back once, the grid opening out so each strip cell is seen to cover a whole column of ten, then it closes again
- counts the unshaded cells of the strip → the shaded cells are numbered in turn in amber, and the unshaded ones stay warm white and unnumbered
**Misconception to break:** Saying 3%, which counts each cell as one hundredth as if the strip had a hundred cells.

## Delight
On the fourth phase, the moment Fold is tapped, the ten columns glide together into one long strip, the shaded columns staying shaded, and the per cent card does not change at all.

**General miss line:** Count the full columns first, then the single cells.

## Answer key
Per cent means out of a hundred. A hundred grid has 100 equal cells, so each cell is one hundredth, and a number of shaded cells over 100 is that many per cent. A fraction over 100 is written as a per cent by its numerator, with the % sign. To show a per cent, shade that many cells: full columns of ten first, then single cells, and which cells does not matter, only how many. A strip of ten equal cells is the whole, 100%. Each of its cells is one tenth of it, ten hundredths. A percentage is an amount written as a per cent.

## Student controls
- **Drag to shade**: shades cells deep blue, or clears them when dragged back
- **Columns and cells counter**: reads the shading as full columns of ten and single cells
- **Scatter button**: moves the shaded cells to other places without changing their number
- **Fold button**: slides the ten columns together into a strip of ten equal cells
- **Number pad**: sets a count of cells, or types a fraction or a per cent

## Textbook (this topic)

**Key terms:** per cent, percentage, hundred grid, hundredths, strip
Per cent means out of a hundred, so a fraction over 100 names a per cent, shaded on a hundred grid or a strip of ten equal cells. Per cent means out of a hundred. On a hundred grid, the number of shaded cells is the per cent, and which cells are shaded does not matter. On a strip of ten equal cells, each cell is ten per cent. 37 of the 100 cells are shaded, so 37/100 of the grid is shaded. Per cent means out of a hundred, so this is also 37 per cent. It is written 37%, with the sign % for per cent. The denominator is already 100. The numerator counts the hundredths, so 64/100 is 64%. In the same way, 8/100 is 8%. A percentage is an amount written as a per cent. 23% means 23 out of 100, so shade 23 of the 100 cells. Two full columns make 20 cells, and 3 more make 23. Any 23 cells will do. Which cells does not matter, only how many. The whole strip is 100%. Each cell is one tenth of the strip, which is 10 hundredths. So each cell is 10%, and 7 cells make 70%.
