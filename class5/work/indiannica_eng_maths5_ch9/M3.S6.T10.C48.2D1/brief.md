## What it is
**Description:** Write one real measure three ways, put a group's measures in order, add a group's weights, and measure the water a group drinks with a measuring jug.
**Why:** Write one real measure in two units, in its smallest unit and as a decimal in the larger unit, put a group's measures in order in one unit, add a group's weights, and measure the water a group drinks with a measuring jug.
**Look:** A class record chart with a row for each child, record cards that flip through three forms, and a measuring jug marked in millilitres.
**Context:** Grade 5, the math lab page that closes the chapter. An adult sees a class chart of measurements. What the page is doing is bringing every idea of the chapter to real measures: writing each one three ways, ordering them in one unit, and adding them.
**For the child:** Enter your measure and flip it through three forms. Put the group's measures in one unit, then in order. Pour each drink from the jug and add them.
**Teaching notes:** Write every measurement three ways: in two units, in the smallest unit and as a decimal in the larger unit. Put all the measures in one unit before ordering them. Use a measuring jug or a marked bottle for the water step, and order the capacities too. Weigh bags or books rather than children, so nobody's weight is read aloud. Enter the measures from packet labels the class has collected. Close by running through the five can-dos of the self-assessment with the group's own measures.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · One measure, three ways
**Screen:** a long jump entered on a record card in metres and centimetres
**Does:** flips the card and types each form in turn
**Win:** the three forms stand side by side, all green, all one jump
**Feels:** three different-looking forms standing side by side, all green, all one jump
**Goal:** one measure can be written in two units, in its smallest unit and as a decimal in the larger unit
**Observe:** The three forms on the card looked different but were all one jump.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- flips to the decimal form and writes the centimetres straight after the point with no place holder → the child's form stays, and the other two forms are laid beside it so the one that differs stands out, lit amber
- writes the jump in centimetres by putting the metres and centimetres side by side → the child's form stays, the other two forms are laid beside it, and the one that differs is lit amber
**Misconception to break:** Leaving out the zero that holds the tenths place, which makes the jump longer than it was.

### 2 · Ordering real measures
**Screen:** four jumps on the chart, each written in a different form
**Does:** flips every card to centimetres, then drags the rows into ascending order
**Win:** the rows settle in order, and the jumps rise like steps
**Feels:** the rows settling into order and the jumps rising like steps
**Goal:** put every measure in one unit, then order them
**Observe:** The rows only fell into a clear order once every jump was in centimetres.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- drags the rows into order by the first number on each card before flipping them → the rows stay where the child put them, and each row's jump is shown in the chart's one unit beside its neighbours so the one out of step is seen
- puts a jump written as a decimal below a mixed one because its digits after the point look small → the row stays where it was put, and its jump is shown in centimetres beside its neighbours, lit amber
**Misconception to break:** Reading 2.5 m as 2 m 5 cm, which puts that jump first instead of third.

### 3 · Weights of a group
**Screen:** three weights on the chart in kilograms and grams, and an empty total row
**Does:** writes each weight in grams, adds them, and writes the total in kilograms and grams
**Win:** the total turns green, in grams, in kilograms and grams, and as a decimal
**Feels:** three weights melting into one total that bundles back into kilograms
**Goal:** add weights in the smallest unit, then write the total back in the larger unit
**Observe:** The weights added in grams gave a total that bundled back into kilograms and grams.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- adds the grams parts and leaves a thousand or more in the grams part of the total → the total stays, and the thousand grams inside its grams part are lit amber and outlined as one bundle, ready to become a kilogram
- writes a weight in grams with a zero missing inside it → that row's card flips through its three forms beside the child's, and the one that differs is lit amber
**Misconception to break:** Writing 2 kg 85 g as if it were 2 kg 850 g, which adds grams that are not there.

### 4 · Water drunk by a group
**Screen:** a measuring jug marked in millilitres beside the water column of the chart
**Does:** pours each drink from the jug, reads its marks, enters it, and adds the drinks
**Win:** the water column fills, and the total turns green in litres and millilitres
**Feels:** the water column filling and the total landing in litres and millilitres
**Goal:** measure each drink with a measuring jug, add the millilitres, and write the total in litres and millilitres
**Observe:** Each drink read off the jug's marks was a real measure, not a guess.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- reads the jug at the nearest numbered mark instead of the water's level → the marks between the numbered mark and the water's level light one at a time, and the child's reading stays in the water column beside the jug's
- writes the total in millilitres as litres by splitting it after two digits → the child's total stays, and beside it the thousand millilitres are bundled into one litre in steps
**Misconception to break:** Guessing each drink by eye instead of reading a measuring jug, so the total cannot be trusted.

## Delight
On the fourth phase, as the last drink is entered, the water column of the chart fills like a glass from the bottom up, and the total lands at the top in litres and millilitres.

**General miss line:** Flip the card through all three forms. Do they all say the same measure?

## Answer key
One measure can be written in two units, in its smallest unit, or as a decimal in the larger unit, and all three are right when the question does not name a form. Put every measure in one unit before ordering. Add weights in the smallest unit and write the total back in the larger unit. Measure each drink of water with a measuring jug, add the millilitres, and write the total in litres and millilitres.

## Student controls
- **Record card**: takes a measure the group has taken or a label gives
- **Flip**: turns the card through its three forms
- **Row drag**: moves a row of the chart into order
- **Jug**: pours a drink out of a measuring jug marked in millilitres
- **Number pad**: types a measure, a form or a total

## Textbook (this topic)
**Pages:** 145
**Key terms:** measure, mixed measure, decimal, ascending order, measuring jug, total
Real lengths, weights and capacities are measured, written in three ways, put in order and added, bringing the chapter's units and conversions together. One measure can be written three ways: in two units, in its smallest unit, and as a decimal in the larger unit. Put measures in one unit before ordering or adding them. Measure each drink with a measuring jug, and add the drinks. In centimetres it is 246 cm. As a decimal in metres it is 2.46 m. So 2 m 46 cm is 246 cm, which is 2.46 m. Write them all in centimetres: 246 cm, 198 cm, 230 cm and 209 cm. 198 < 209 < 230 < 246. So 2 m 46 cm is the longest jump. In grams they are 2300 g, 1850 g and 2075 g. 2300 + 1850 + 2075 = 6225. So together they weigh 6225 g, which is 6 kg 225 g, or 6.225 kg. Measure each drink with a measuring jug. 240 + 310 + 185 + 265 = 1000. 1000 mL make 1 litre, so the four drank 1 litre in all.
