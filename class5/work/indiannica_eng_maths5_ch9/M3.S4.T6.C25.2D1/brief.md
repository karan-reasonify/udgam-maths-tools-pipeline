## What it is
**Description:** Add measures in unit columns with a carry, add them again as decimals, take away with a borrow, and walk a boundary once every side is in one unit.
**Why:** Add measures in unit columns with a carry, add them as decimals in the larger unit, take one measure from another with a borrow, and find a boundary once every side is in one unit.
**Look:** A frame of two unit columns with measures stacked in it, carry and borrow tiles that are dragged across, and on the last phase a shape with its sides in mixed units.
**Context:** Grade 5, the pages on adding and subtracting measures. An adult sees column sums. What the pages are doing is making the carry and the borrow happen between units, where one of the larger unit is 1000 or 100 or 10 of the smaller one, not always 10.
**For the child:** Work the smaller unit first. Bundle a carry or break a borrow and drag it across. For a boundary, put every side in one unit, then walk round.
**Teaching notes:** Add the smaller unit first and carry when it fills up; say how many of the smaller unit make one of the larger each time, because it is 1000, 100 or 10 depending on the units. When borrowing, change one larger unit into the smaller unit and say how many that is. Adding as decimals needs every measure in the larger unit with the points lined up, and the answer's decimal is in that unit. For a boundary, rewrite every side in the unit asked for before adding. Bring in labels from packets at home and add their measures here.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Adding in unit columns
**Screen:** two lengths in metres and centimetres stacked in the frame
**Does:** adds the centimetres, bundles a metre out of them, drags it across, then adds the metres
**Win:** the bundle lands in the metres column and the sum turns green
**Feels:** a full column wrapping itself into one bundle and hopping across
**Goal:** add the smaller unit first, and carry one of the larger unit across when the smaller column fills up
**Observe:** The smaller unit's column filled past one of the larger unit, and the Bundle button lit.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes the centimetres total, a hundred or more, as it stands in the centimetres column → the over-full total stays in its column lit amber, and the Bundle button beside it is lit until a bundle is made from it
- adds the metres column first and forgets the carry → the metres total stays, the centimetres column is lit amber, and once its bundle is made the carry tile waits beside the metres column
**Misconception to break:** Writing all of the centimetres in the centimetre column and never carrying the metre across, so the centimetres hold more than a metre.

### 2 · Adding as decimals
**Screen:** two capacities in litres and millilitres, and the decimal rows waiting
**Does:** writes each capacity as a decimal in litres, lines up the points, adds, and names the unit
**Win:** the decimal answer turns green, with the word litres beside it
**Feels:** the points lining up like buttons and the answer landing in litres
**Goal:** write each measure as a decimal in the larger unit, line up the points and add; the answer is in the larger unit
**Observe:** With the points in one line, the decimal sum ended in the larger unit.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes a capacity's millilitres straight after the point with no zero in an empty place → that row is read back in litres and millilitres beside it, showing more millilitres than the question gave, and the empty place is lit amber with a grey zero waiting
- names the decimal answer in millilitres → the answer waits uncoloured, and the unit the rows are in, litres, is lit amber above the frame
**Misconception to break:** Writing the decimal with mL after it, which turns more than seven litres into a few drops.

### 3 · Taking away with a borrow
**Screen:** a weight in kilograms and grams, and a smaller weight to take away that needs more grams than are there
**Does:** breaks one kilogram into grams, drags them across, then takes away column by column
**Win:** the rose part is taken away, and what is left turns green
**Feels:** one kilogram breaking into a heap of grams that makes the taking away possible
**Goal:** when there is too little of the smaller unit, change one of the larger unit into the smaller unit first
**Observe:** A broken larger unit filled the smaller column with far more than ten.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- takes the smaller grams number from the larger, whichever row it is in → the grams column is shown too short beside the part to be taken away, both lit amber, and the child's column total stays
- presses Break when there are already enough grams → the broken kilogram's tile waits beside the grams column, the two grams parts are laid side by side so the child sees there were enough, and the tile can be dragged back
**Misconception to break:** Taking 240 from 580 in the gram column because it is easier, which leaves more paint than is really there.

### 4 · A boundary in mixed units
**Screen:** a four-sided flower bed with its sides labelled in metres, centimetres and millimetres
**Does:** taps each side card to rewrite it in metres, then sets the walker going round
**Win:** the walker comes home, and the boundary turns green in metres
**Feels:** the walker coming home and the whole boundary turning green
**Goal:** write every side in the unit asked for, then add all the sides
**Observe:** The walker would not move past a side still in its own unit.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- sets the walker going while some side cards are still in their own units → the side left in its own unit stays on its card, lit amber, while the walker waits at that corner
- rewrites a side in millimetres by moving the point only two places → the side card reads back in the unit its point now sits after, and that side is drawn to scale beside the real side so the two lengths are seen apart
**Misconception to break:** Adding the four numbers as they stand, mixing metres, centimetres and millimetres, which gives a boundary thousands of metres long.

## Delight
On the first phase, when the Bundle button wraps the full column, the bundle is tied with a small violet ribbon carrying the unit fact and hops over into the larger column.

**General miss line:** Look at the smaller unit's column. Is there a whole larger unit in it?

## Answer key
Add the smaller unit first. When it reaches one of the larger unit, carry that one across: 1000 g make 1 kg, 100 cm make 1 m, 1000 mL make 1 litre. When there is too little of the smaller unit to take away, change one of the larger unit into the smaller unit first. To add or take away as decimals, write every measure in the larger unit and line up the points; the decimal answer is in that larger unit. For a boundary, write every side in the unit asked for, then add all the sides.

## Student controls
- **Bundle**: wraps one of the larger unit's worth of the smaller unit into a carry tile
- **Break**: turns one of the larger unit into the smaller unit for a borrow
- **Tile drag**: moves a carry or a borrow between the columns
- **Side card**: is tapped to rewrite a side in the unit asked for
- **Number pad**: types a column total, a decimal or an answer

## Textbook (this topic)
**Pages:** 140, 141, 142
**Key terms:** add, subtract, carry, borrow, unit column, boundary, decimal
Measures are added and taken away in unit columns, carrying or borrowing across units, or as decimals in the larger unit, and a boundary is found once every side is in one unit. Work the smaller unit first, and carry or borrow one of the larger unit across. The same sum can be done as decimals in the larger unit, with the points in a line. For a boundary, write every side in one unit, then add. Add the grams first: 650 + 480 = 1130. 1130 g is 1 kg 130 g, so write 130 g and carry 1 kg. Then add the kilograms: 3 + 2 + 1 = 6. Together they weigh 6 kg 130 g. 3 kg 650 g is 3.650 kg, and 2 kg 480 g is 2.480 kg. Line up the points and add: 3.650 + 2.480 = 6.130. So together they weigh 6.130 kg, which is 6.13 kg. The decimal is in kilograms, the larger unit. There are not enough grams to take 450 g from 120 g. So change 1 kg into 1000 g: 1000 + 120 = 1120, and 1120 - 450 = 670. That leaves 6 in the kilogram column, and 6 - 2 = 4. So 4 kg 670 g is left. First write every side in metres. 350 cm is 3.5 m, and 3500 mm is 3.5 m. Then add: 4 + 3.5 + 4 + 3.5 = 15. So the boundary is 15 m.
