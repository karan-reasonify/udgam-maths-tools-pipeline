## What it is
**Description:** Write the measure in its smallest unit, multiply or divide in whole numbers, write the answer back in the larger unit, and say what is left over when a sharing is not even.
**Why:** Multiply a measure by a number in its smallest unit, in whole numbers, and write the product back in the larger unit; divide a measure in its smallest unit, and say what is left over when a sharing is not even.
**Look:** A measure card above a strip drawn to scale, copied end to end or cut into equal pieces, with any part left over kept in view.
**Context:** Grade 5, the pages on multiplying and dividing measures. An adult sees a measure times a number. What the pages are doing is keeping every step in whole numbers by working in the smallest unit, and saying what is left over when a sharing is not even.
**For the child:** Write the measure in its smallest unit first. Copy the strip to multiply, or cut it to share. Say what is left over.
**Teaching notes:** Write the measure in its smallest unit before anything is multiplied or divided, so every step is in whole numbers; the chapters so far have not taught multiplying a decimal, and it is not needed here. After multiplying, write the answer back in the larger unit. When a sharing does not come out even, say what is left over in words, and check by multiplying back and adding it. The same working finds how many packets of one size fill a vessel: both in the smallest unit, then divide.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Multiply in the smallest unit
**Screen:** a length in metres and centimetres on its card, the Smallest unit slot empty
**Does:** types the length in centimetres, then taps Copy until the strip is laid the number of times asked
**Win:** the copies meet end to end and the total in centimetres turns green
**Feels:** copies of one strip clicking end to end into a long row
**Goal:** write the measure in its smallest unit, then multiply the whole number
**Observe:** Once the measure was in its smallest unit, it was one whole number.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- types only the centimetres part into the Smallest unit slot → the strip drawn from the child's number is far shorter than the card's strip, which is drawn beside it, and the metres part on the card is lit amber
- taps Copy one time too few → the running count shows the copies laid, and the place of the missing copy is outlined amber at the end of the row
**Misconception to break:** Multiplying only the centimetres by 4 and forgetting the metres, so the shelves come out far too short.

### 2 · Back to the larger unit
**Screen:** a total in grams from the copies, and the Regroup button
**Does:** regroups the grams into kilograms and grams, then reads it as a decimal in kilograms
**Win:** the bundles of a thousand grams stack up as kilograms, and the answer turns green
**Feels:** grams stacking up into whole kilograms with a small part beside them
**Goal:** write the product back in the larger unit, with its smaller part less than one of the larger unit
**Observe:** The copies laid end to end made the same length as the product.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- splits the total in grams after its last two digits, taking those as the grams → the child's answer stays, and beside it the bundles of a thousand grams are counted out again one at a time
- stops regrouping with a thousand grams or more still in the grams part → the grams part stays lit amber, and the next bundle it holds is outlined inside it, waiting to be stacked
**Misconception to break:** Dividing by 100 instead of by 1000 when writing the grams back in kilograms, which makes the packets ten times too heavy.

### 3 · Sharing equally
**Screen:** a length in metres and centimetres drawn as one strip, with cut marks in a tray
**Does:** writes the length in centimetres, drags the cut marks on, and reads one piece
**Win:** the pieces slide apart, all the same, and one piece turns green
**Feels:** the pieces sliding apart, all the same length
**Goal:** write the measure in its smallest unit, then divide the whole number; each piece is the quotient
**Observe:** The Regroup bundles stacked up as whole larger units, with a small part beside them.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- types a piece that is too long → the pieces from the child's answer are laid on the strip, and the last one runs past its end, in amber
- types a piece that is too short → the pieces from the child's answer are laid on the strip, and more than a piece's worth is left over, in amber
**Misconception to break:** Trying to share the 4 metres among 6 pieces and getting stuck, because the metres were not written in centimetres first.

### 4 · Some left over
**Screen:** a capacity in litres and millilitres drawn as a column of water, with cups waiting
**Does:** writes the capacity in millilitres, shares it among the cups, and types what each gets and what is left
**Win:** the cups fill level, the small part left over stays rose, and both numbers turn green
**Feels:** the cups filling level and a small rose part staying behind
**Goal:** when a sharing does not come out even, say how much each part gets and how much is left over, in words
**Observe:** Some sharings came out even, and some left a small rose part at the end.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- types the part left over after a point, as if it were a decimal → the answer stays, the rose part left over is drawn at the bottom of the column, and a line in ink beside the slot names each cup's share and then the part left over
- types what each cup gets and leaves the Left over slot empty → the rose part at the bottom of the column is lit amber until its number is typed
**Misconception to break:** Leaving out the 2 mL that is left over, so the cups and the can no longer add back to the whole.

## Delight
On the fourth phase, when the cups fill level, the small rose part left over gives a tiny wobble at the bottom of the column, as if it is waiting to be named.

**General miss line:** Is the measure in its smallest unit? Start there.

## Answer key
Write the measure in its smallest unit first, so every step is in whole numbers. Multiply or divide the whole number. Then write a product back in the larger unit, with its smaller part less than one of the larger unit. When a sharing does not come out even, say how much each part gets and how much is left over, in words. Check a quotient by multiplying it back and adding what was left over.

## Student controls
- **Smallest unit slot**: takes the measure written in its smallest unit
- **Copy**: lays the strip end to end once more
- **Regroup**: bundles a total in the smallest unit back into the larger unit
- **Cut marks**: are dragged onto the strip to share it into equal pieces
- **Number pad**: types a product, a quotient and what is left over

## Textbook (this topic)
**Pages:** 141, 142
**Key terms:** multiply, divide, smallest unit, whole numbers, remainder, left over
A measure is multiplied or divided by a number in its smallest unit, in whole numbers, then written back in the larger unit, and a sharing that does not come out even says what is left over. Write the measure in its smallest unit, then multiply or divide in whole numbers. Write the answer back in the larger unit. When a sharing is not even, say how much each part gets and how much is left over. Write the length in the smallest unit: 2 m 35 cm is 235 cm. Multiply whole numbers: 235 x 6 = 1410. So 6 tiles are 1410 cm long, which is 14 m 10 cm. 1 kg 275 g is 1275 g. 1275 x 8 = 10,200, so 8 bags hold 10,200 g. 1000 g make 1 kg, so 10,200 g is 10 kg 200 g. As a decimal in kilograms that is 10.2 kg. Write the length in the smallest unit: 4 m 20 cm is 420 cm. 420 divided by 5 is 84. So each piece is 84 cm long. 2 L 350 mL is 2350 mL. 2350 divided by 4 is 587, with 2 left over. So each glass gets 587 mL, and 2 mL is left over in the jug.
