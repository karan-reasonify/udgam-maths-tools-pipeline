## What it is
**Description:** Find how long, an end time or a start on a time line by taking clock times after midnight and by jumping, across noon and 12 midnight, and see why a slow clock shows an earlier time.
**Why:** Find how long an activity lasts by taking clock times after midnight, find an end time and a start by jumping along a time line, and carry a journey past 12 midnight into the next day.
**Look:** One time line with a mark for every hour, 12 noon and 12 midnight marked on their own, and violet jumps that are dragged along it.
**Context:** Grade 5, the pages on how long an activity lasts, when it ends and when it began. An adult sees subtraction and addition of times. What the pages are doing is making a time of day a length of time after midnight, so that starts, ends and lengths can be taken apart, and making a journey that passes 12 midnight go on into the next day.
**For the child:** Set the times on the line. Jump the whole hours first and then the minutes, and stop at 12 midnight before going on into the next day.
**Teaching notes:** Draw a time line for every start, end and length of time, with 12 noon and 12 midnight as marks of their own. Write both clock times as the hours and minutes after midnight, and say so: 1:35 p.m. is 13 h 35 min after midnight. Jump the whole hours first, then the minutes, and check an end time by jumping back to the start. When a jump passes 12 midnight, stop at 12 midnight, carry on, and say the next day. A clock that runs slow shows a time behind the true time: find how far behind, then jump back. Three sessions one after another are totalled by adding their lengths, or by finding how long from the first start to the last end.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · How long, through the 24-hour clock
**Screen:** the time line with a start and an end set on it in deep blue, and the hours and minutes after midnight beneath each
**Does:** reads each time as hours and minutes after midnight, takes the earlier from the later, and finds the length on the line
**Win:** the length between the two marks turns green
**Feels:** the two marks joined by a length that lights up on the line
**Goal:** how long is the later time minus the earlier time, both written as the hours and minutes after midnight
**Observe:** Each clock time sat on the line with its hours and minutes after midnight beneath it.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- takes the earlier time from the later as they stand on the 12-hour clock, so the afternoon time looks smaller → the answer stays in the slot, both times are drawn again with their hours and minutes after midnight beneath them, and the smaller of the two lengths after midnight lights amber
- takes the minutes from the minutes without regrouping when the minutes on top are too few → the answer stays, the length between the two marks is shown on the line, and the minutes column is lit amber with 1 h broken into 60 min
**Misconception to break:** Taking the times apart as they stand on the 12-hour clock, so the afternoon time looks smaller than the morning one.

### 2 · Jumps to an end time
**Screen:** the time line with a start set on it and a length of time to jump
**Does:** drags a violet jump of whole hours, then a jump of minutes, and reads the mark reached
**Win:** the mark reached turns green, labelled End
**Feels:** the jumps landing one after another on the line, the hours first and then the minutes
**Goal:** an end time is found by jumping from the start, whole hours first and then the minutes
**Observe:** The jump of whole hours was dragged before the jump of minutes.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- adds the minutes to the start and stops, even when they pass the next hour → the child's time stays, the jump of minutes is lit amber where it passes the next hour mark, and the hour is moved on in steps
- types the length of the jump as the end time → the jump stays, the mark it reaches is lit amber and read out as a clock time in green, labelled End, beside the child's answer
**Misconception to break:** Adding the minutes and not moving on to the next hour when they pass 60.

### 3 · Working back to a start
**Screen:** the time line with an end set on it, a length of time to jump back, and a slow clock beside the line
**Does:** drags the jumps back along the line, then checks by jumping forward again, and sets the slow clock behind the true time
**Win:** the start turns green, and the forward jump lands on the end
**Feels:** the jump back and the jump forward meeting at the same mark
**Goal:** a start is found by jumping back from the end; a slow clock shows a time behind the true time
**Observe:** A start found by jumping back could be checked by jumping forward again.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- jumps forward from the end to find a start → the child's start stays, it is drawn later than its own end on the line, and the end it was worked from is lit amber beside it
- takes the time on a slow clock for the true time → the slow clock is set beside the true time, the gap between them is drawn as a jump in violet, labelled Jump, and the child's answer stays
**Misconception to break:** Jumping forward instead of back, so the start comes out later than the end.

### 4 · Past midnight
**Screen:** the time line with the 12 midnight mark drawn on its own and the words the next day waiting after it
**Does:** drags a jump to the 12 midnight mark, drags a second jump on from it, and adds the two lengths
**Win:** the two jumps are added, the words the next day light up, and the length turns green
**Feels:** the line folding at 12 midnight and the two jumps joining into one length
**Goal:** a journey that passes 12 midnight is jumped to 12 midnight first, then on, and is written with the next day
**Observe:** The jump stopped at 12 midnight, and the words the next day lit up after it.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- takes a night time from a morning time as they stand, without going through 12 midnight → the two marks are drawn on two days of the line, the 12 midnight mark between them is lit amber, and the words the next day are written in ink beside the second day
- adds the two jumps but leaves out the words the next day → the words the next day are lit amber over the line after 12 midnight, and the end time is drawn there
**Misconception to break:** Taking the night time from the morning time as they stand, without going through 12 midnight into the next day.

## Delight
On the fourth phase, as the second jump crosses 12 midnight, the words the next day rise over the far side of the line and the two jumps slide together into one length.

**General miss line:** Look along the line again. Which jump have you not made yet?

## Answer key
A clock time is the hours and minutes after midnight, so 1:35 p.m. is 13 h 35 min after midnight. How long is the later time taken from the earlier time, both after midnight. An end time is found by jumping, whole hours first and then the minutes: 1 h 55 min after 11:10 a.m. is 1:05 p.m. A start is found by jumping back the same way. A journey that passes 12 midnight stops at 12 midnight first, then goes on, and is written with the next day: from 10:20 p.m. to 4:35 a.m. the next day is 6 h 15 min. A clock that runs slow shows a time behind the true time, so jump back from the true time by the minutes it is behind.

## Student controls
- **Time marker**: is placed on the line at a start, an end or a time given
- **Jump**: is dragged along the line by whole hours, then by minutes, forwards or back
- **Hours after midnight**: shows a time as hours and minutes after midnight
- **Slow clock**: is set behind the true time on the third phase
- **Number pad**: types a time, a length of time or a day

## Textbook (this topic)
**Pages:** 155, 156, 157
**Key terms:** duration, start time, end time, after midnight, the next day
How long an activity lasts, when it ends and when it began, found through the 24-hour clock and by jumps along a time line, across noon and midnight. To find how long, write both times as a length of time after midnight, then take the smaller from the larger. To find an end time, jump the hours forward from the start, then the minutes. To find a start time, jump back from the end. If the time passes 12 midnight, jump to 12 midnight first and say the next day. 1:20 p.m. is 13 h 20 min after midnight, and 9:45 a.m. is 9 h 45 min after midnight. 13 h 20 min - 9 h 45 min is 3 h 35 min. So from 9:45 a.m. to 1:20 p.m. is 3 h 35 min. Jump 2 h from 10:40 a.m. to 12:40 p.m. Then jump 50 min from 12:40 p.m. to 1:30 p.m. So 2 h 50 min after 10:40 a.m. is 1:30 p.m. Jump back 1 h from 3:10 p.m. to 2:10 p.m. Then jump back 25 min from 2:10 p.m. to 1:45 p.m. So 1 h 25 min before 3:10 p.m. is 1:45 p.m. Check it: 1 h 25 min after 1:45 p.m. is 3:10 p.m. Jump to 12 midnight first. From 9:40 p.m. to 12 midnight is 2 h 20 min. Then from 12 midnight to 3:15 a.m. is 3 h 15 min. 2 h 20 min + 3 h 15 min is 5 h 35 min. So from 9:40 p.m. to 3:15 a.m. the next day is 5 h 35 min.
