## What it is
**Description:** Line up the points, add place by place with carries across the point, give whole numbers their point, and see that order and adding zero change nothing.
**Why:** Add decimals, whole numbers among them, with the points in one line and carrying across the point, and use the order and zero properties to check a sum.
**Look:** A column frame with one fixed point line down its middle, decimal cards lined up on it, and an answer row under a ruled line.
**Context:** Grade 5, the pages on adding decimals. An adult sees steps to follow. What the pages are doing is keeping every place in its own column by lining up the points, then adding as with whole numbers, carrying across the point. Zeros after the point help keep the columns tidy; the sum is the same without them.
**For the child:** Line up the points. Add each column from the right. Carry ten of one place to the next place.
**Teaching notes:** The points go in one line, one below the other; that is what keeps each place in its own column. Writing zeros after the point to make like decimals keeps the columns tidy, and the sum is the same without them. A carry from the tenths goes into the ones, straight across the point. A whole number has its point after the ones digit; write it there before lining up. Check a sum by adding in another order.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Line up the points
**Screen:** the cards 3.46 and 2.3 in the tray, and the empty frame
**Does:** drags both cards into the frame, watches them slide to the point line, and taps Add
**Win:** the columns add to 5.76 in green, its point straight below the others
**Feels:** the cards clicking into line by their points
**Goal:** decimals are added with their points in one line, as whole numbers, the point placed straight below
**Observe:** Every card slid until its point sat on the one line.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- lines up the last digits instead of the points → the card slides back until its point sits on the point line, and the column it had covered lights amber
- leaves the point out of the sum → the point line runs down through the answer row and a point drops onto it, the child's digits kept
**Misconception to break:** Lining up the last digits instead of the points, which adds the 6 tenths to the 3 hundredths.

### 2 · Carrying across the point
**Screen:** 4.68 above 1.57 on the point line, and the Add button lit
**Does:** adds the hundredths, then the tenths, and watches each carry roll left
**Win:** a vermilion one rolls into the tenths, another rolls across the point into the ones, and the sum reads 6.25
**Feels:** a carry hopping across the point
**Goal:** ten of one place is carried as one of the next place, across the point too
**Observe:** A carry from the tenths crossed the point line into the ones.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- writes the whole column total under the column → the total's tens digit lifts off, turns vermilion and rolls to the next column, and only the ones digit stays
- forgets the carry in the next column → the vermilion 1 at the top of the column pulses and joins the column's sum as it is added again
**Misconception to break:** Forgetting the carried 1 tenth, which gives 6.23, one tenth too small.

### 3 · Adding whole numbers and decimals
**Screen:** the cards 6, 0.35 and 2.4 in the tray, the 6 card with no point
**Does:** tries the 6 card, taps Point first, lines up all three and adds
**Win:** the 6 takes a violet point after its ones digit, all three line up, and the sum reads 8.75
**Feels:** a whole number getting its point and sliding into line
**Goal:** a whole number has its point after the ones digit before it is lined up
**Observe:** A whole number card would not line up until its point was written.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- lines the whole number up under the last digit of a decimal → the whole number card will not settle, and the Point first button pulses once
- adds a zero straight onto the whole number before its point → the card shows the number ten times as big in words beside it, and the zero slides back off
**Misconception to break:** Lining the 9 up under the last digit of 1.75, which adds 9 hundredths instead of 9 ones.

### 4 · Order and zero in adding
**Screen:** 1.35 above 4.2 with their sum 5.55 in the answer row
**Does:** taps Swap and adds again, then drops the Add zero card on 7.08
**Win:** the swapped sum is 5.55 again, and 7.08 + 0 = 7.08
**Feels:** the sum staying put while the cards swap places
**Goal:** changing the order of the addends does not change the sum, and adding 0 changes nothing
**Observe:** Swapping the cards and adding zero left the sum as it was.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- thinks the greater number has to go on top in adding → Swap reverses the cards and the sum lands again in the same place, green
- thinks adding zero adds a zero digit to the end → the Add zero card lines up as its own row with 0 in the ones place, and the sum is written unchanged
**Misconception to break:** Thinking the greater number must come first, and adding again only in that order, though either order gives the same sum.

## Delight
On the second phase, when the tenths make ten or more, the vermilion carry hops over the point line like a frog over a stream and lands on top of the ones column with a soft plop.

**General miss line:** Look down the point line. Is every point on it?

## Answer key
Write the decimals one below the other with the points in one line, so each place is in its own column. Add as whole numbers, from the right, carrying ten of one place as one of the next, across the point too. Put the point in the sum straight below the other points. A whole number has its point after the ones digit. Zeros written after the point to make like decimals keep the columns tidy; they are a help, not a rule. Changing the order of the addends does not change the sum, and adding 0 changes nothing.

## Student controls
- **Decimal cards**: drag into the frame and slide until their points sit on the point line
- **Like button**: writes zeros after the point on a shorter card, as a help
- **Add button**: adds one column at a time from the right and rolls each carry to the next column
- **Point first button**: writes a point after the ones digit of a whole number card
- **Swap button and Add zero card**: reverse the order of the cards, or add 0
- **Number pad**: sets any decimal with up to three places, or types a sum

## Textbook (this topic)
**Pages:** 83, 84, 85
**Key terms:** decimal point, like decimals, carry, addends, sum, whole number
Decimals are added with their points in one line, as whole numbers, with the point placed straight below; the order of the addends and adding zero change nothing. Write 2.3 as 2.30, so both have two decimal places. Write one below the other with the points in one line. Add as whole numbers, then put the point straight below the others. So 3.46 + 2.30 = 5.76. Hundredths: 8 + 7 = 15, so write 5 and carry 1 tenth. Tenths: 6 + 5 + 1 = 12, so write 2 and carry 1 one. Ones: 4 + 1 + 1 = 6. So 4.68 + 1.57 = 6.25. Write 6 as 6.00 and 2.4 as 2.40, so every point lines up. Then 6.00 + 0.35 + 2.40 = 8.75. A whole number always has its point after the ones digit. Yes: 1.35 + 4.2 = 5.55 and 4.2 + 1.35 = 5.55. Changing the order of the addends does not change the sum. Adding 0 changes nothing: 7.08 + 0 = 7.08.
