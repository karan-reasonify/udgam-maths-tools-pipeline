## What it is
**Description:** Add and take away measures in one unit, carry a written answer into the next step, total a list of parts two ways, and pour water in litres.
**Why:** Add and subtract measures in one unit, carrying each answer into the next step, and list the parts of a plan with their measures and find and check the total.
**Look:** One measuring scene at a time, a tape, a scale or a jug, with a column strip at the right writing each step, an Answer card on the second phase and a List panel on the third.
**Context:** Grade 5, the closing pages. An adult sees a day's planning, a self-assessment, word cards and a water activity. What the pages are doing is using decimals as measures: every measure in one sum is in one unit, and when one question builds on another, its answer is written down and carried forward. Water is measured in litres.
**For the child:** Keep every measure in one unit. Write each answer down before the next step. Check a total by adding in another order.
**Teaching notes:** Every measure in one sum must be in one unit; say the unit with every number. In a chain of questions, write each answer down and use the written total, so one slip does not carry through. A list of parts with a total is checked by adding it again in another order. Pour and measure water in litres with a jug marked in tenths of a litre, not a dropper, and keep every reading in litres. The printed day out is the child's own work on the page; the examples here use other measures.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Adding measures in one unit
**Screen:** rope pieces of 2.4 m, 1.35 m and 0.8 m over the tape, each with its unit tag
**Does:** drags the pieces end to end and reads the tape, then writes the sum in the strip
**Win:** the tape reads 4.55 m and the strip shows 2.40 + 1.35 + 0.80 = 4.55 in green
**Feels:** the pieces snapping end to end and the tape reading their total
**Goal:** measures in one unit are added like any decimals, with the unit said with every number
**Observe:** The Add button only worked when every unit tag matched.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- drags a piece tagged in another unit into the sum → the Add button stays dim, and the mismatched tag lights amber beside the others
- lines up the last digits instead of the points → the column strip slides the row until its point sits in line with the others, the child's own row kept above
**Misconception to break:** Lining up the last digits instead of the points, which adds the 2 tenths of 3.2 to the 5 hundredths of 2.45.

### 2 · One answer feeds the next
**Screen:** a 6 kg bag on the scale, and two scoops of 1.65 kg and 0.7 kg waiting
**Does:** adds the two scoops, drags the Answer card into the second step, and takes it away
**Win:** the Answer card reads 2.35 kg, and the second step shows 6 - 2.35 = 3.65 kg left
**Feels:** the Answer card carrying one step's result into the next
**Goal:** in a chain of steps, each answer is written down and the next step uses the written number
**Observe:** The second step would not start until the Answer card was dragged into it.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- takes only the first scoop from the bag → the second scoop lights amber on the scale, and the Answer card waits for the two scoops' total
- does the second step from memory without the written answer → the second step stays closed until the Answer card is dragged into it, and the card's number lights
**Misconception to break:** Taking only 1.25 kg from 5 kg and forgetting the second day, which leaves 3.75 kg.

### 3 · A list of parts and its total
**Screen:** the List panel with three garden beds of 2.6 m, 3.25 m and 1.8 m
**Does:** taps Total, then taps Check, and compares the two totals
**Win:** both totals read 7.65 m, side by side
**Feels:** two totals, added two ways, landing on the same number
**Goal:** the parts of a plan are listed with their measures, totalled, and checked in another order
**Observe:** The list totalled top to bottom and bottom to top gave the same number.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- leaves one part out of the total → the Total button lights each line as it is added, and the missing line stays amber
- adds the list only once and stops → the Check button pulses once, and its total fills in beside the first for the two to be compared
**Misconception to break:** Adding the list once and stopping, so a slip in one column can go unseen.

### 4 · Pouring and measuring in litres
**Screen:** the jug holding 1.4 L of juice, and a cup of 0.35 L and a bottle of 0.8 L beside it
**Does:** pours out the cup, reads the jug, then pours in the bottle and reads again
**Win:** the jug reads 1.05 L, then 1.85 L, and both steps are written in litres
**Feels:** the water level sliding down the marks and back up again
**Goal:** water is poured and measured in litres, taken away and added as decimals
**Observe:** The jug's level matched the working written in the strip.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- adds the water poured out instead of taking it away → the jug's level drops as the pour runs, and the strip's row turns to a take away with the poured part rose
- reads the level at the nearest whole litre → the tenths marks between the long lines light one by one up to the water level
**Misconception to break:** Adding the water poured out instead of taking it away, which gives 2.35 L, more than the jug had.

## Delight
On the fourth phase, as water pours out of the jug, the level slides down past the tenths marks one by one with a soft glug for each, and the reading in the strip ticks down with it.

**General miss line:** Say the unit with every number. Did you write the first answer down?

## Answer key
Measures are added and taken away like any decimals, with the points in one line, and every measure in one sum is in one unit, said with every number. When one step uses the answer to another, write that answer down and use the written number. A list of parts is totalled, and the total is checked by adding again in another order. Water is measured, added and taken away in litres.

## Student controls
- **Measure pieces**: rope pieces, bags or water, each tagged with its measure and unit
- **Add and Take buttons**: join, weigh or pour, working only when every unit tag matches
- **Answer card**: catches a step's result and must be dragged into the next step
- **List panel with Total and Check**: totals a list of parts top to bottom, then bottom to top
- **Jug**: fills and empties as water is poured, read off marks in tenths of a litre
- **Number pad**: sets any measure with up to two places, or types an answer

## Textbook (this topic)
**Pages:** 87, 88, 89
**Key terms:** measure, total, left, litre, list
Measures in one unit are added and taken away like any decimals, and in a chain of steps each answer is written down before the next step uses it. All three are in metres, so they can be added straight away. As like decimals, 2.40 + 1.35 + 0.80 = 4.55. So the rope is 4.55 m long in all. First find the flour used: 1.65 + 0.7 = 2.35 kg. Write that answer down, because the next step uses it. Then 6 - 2.35 = 3.65 kg is left. List each part with its measure, then add. 2.60 + 3.25 + 1.80 = 7.65, so 7.65 m of edging is needed. Check the total by adding the list again in another order. Take away first: 1.40 - 0.35 = 1.05 L. Then add: 1.05 + 0.80 = 1.85 L. Every measure here is in litres, so they combine directly.
