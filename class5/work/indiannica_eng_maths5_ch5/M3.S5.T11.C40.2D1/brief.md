## What it is
**Description:** Put the greater number on top with the points in one line, trade across the point where a place is short, and undo a mistaken addition in two steps.
**Why:** Subtract decimals, from whole numbers too, with the points in one line and trading where a place is short, read the three wordings of subtraction, and undo a mistaken addition in two steps.
**Look:** A column frame with one fixed point line, two cards lined up on it, trades drawn in vermilion above the columns, and a Wording card and an Undo panel on the later phases.
**Context:** Grade 5, the pages on subtracting decimals. An adult sees the steps of adding run again. What the pages are doing is taking away place by place with the greater number on top and the points in one line, trading across the point where a place is short. A mistaken addition is undone by taking the number away, and the wanted subtraction takes it away again.
**For the child:** Put the greater number on top. Line up the points. When a place is short, trade from the place to its left.
**Teaching notes:** The greater number goes on top, with the points in one line. When a place is short, trade one of the place to its left for ten of this place, across the point too, and say each trade aloud. A whole number gets its point and zeros before anything is taken from it. Subtract from, take away and how much less all ask for the same difference; say which number starts on top. A wrong addition is undone by taking the number away, and the wanted subtraction takes it away again. Check a difference by adding it back.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Greater number on top
**Screen:** the cards 2.63 and 7.4 in the tray, and the Top check unlit
**Does:** puts 7.4 on top, writes it as 7.40, and trades twice while taking away
**Win:** two vermilion trades are drawn, one across the point, and the difference reads 4.77
**Feels:** a block breaking into ten so a short place can be taken from
**Goal:** the greater number goes on top, the points in one line, and a short place trades with the place to its left
**Observe:** The frame would not start until the greater number was on top.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- takes the smaller digit from the larger in every column → the short column lights amber and the block to its left pulses, and the child's own row is kept beside the answer row
- puts the smaller number on top because it was named first → the Top check stays lit around the upper card, and the two cards' bars are drawn to scale beside the frame
**Misconception to break:** Taking the smaller digit from the larger in each column, 6 - 0 and 5 - 3, which gives 4.26 instead of trading.

### 2 · Taking a decimal from a whole number
**Screen:** the whole number card 5 on top and 1.28 below, the 5 with no point
**Does:** taps Point first, then trades one one into tenths and one tenth into hundredths
**Win:** the top reads four ones, nine tenths and ten hundredths, and the difference reads 3.72
**Feels:** a whole number opening up into ones, tenths and hundredths
**Goal:** a whole number gets its point and zeros before anything is taken from it
**Observe:** Every trade broke one block into exactly ten of the next place.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- brings the decimal's digits down as they are because the top has nothing there → the Point first button writes the point and zeros on the whole number, and the first empty place lights amber
- trades straight from the ones into the hundredths → the trade runs one step at a time: one one into ten tenths, then one tenth into ten hundredths, each drawn in vermilion
**Misconception to break:** Lining the 7 up under the 5 of 2.45, which takes 2.45 from 7 hundredths instead of 7 ones.

### 3 · Three ways to ask for a difference
**Screen:** the Wording card with three wordings of one subtraction of 2.5 and 8.1
**Does:** taps each wording, puts the lit number on top, and takes away
**Win:** all three wordings end in 8.1 - 2.5 = 5.6, and 4.09 - 4.09 = 0 sits beside them
**Feels:** three different sentences landing on one column
**Goal:** subtract from, take away and how much less all ask for the same difference, and a number minus itself is 0
**Observe:** A whole number needed its point and zeros before any trade could start.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- puts the first number said on top → the Wording card lights the number that starts, and the Top check lights amber around the upper card
- thinks how much less asks for a different sum → all three wordings are set side by side and each builds the same column, ending in the same difference
**Misconception to break:** Putting 1.8 on top because it is named first in the question, which makes the frame refuse to start.

### 4 · Undoing a mistaken addition
**Screen:** the Undo panel showing a number that had 2.5 added to it by mistake, giving 9.1
**Does:** takes 2.5 away once, watches the starting number appear, then takes 2.5 away again
**Win:** 6.6 appears as the start, and the right answer 4.1 lands green, the number taken away twice
**Feels:** the starting number reappearing, then the right answer below it
**Goal:** a mistaken addition is undone by taking the number away, then taking it away again
**Observe:** Taking the added number away once brought back the starting number.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- takes the number away only once and stops → the Undo panel's first step lights the starting number, and its second step waits with the added number beside it
- adds the number again to fix the mistake → the Undo panel shows the amount growing, the start drawn below it, and the take away step lit
**Misconception to break:** Taking 1.6 away only once, which gives 6.3, the starting number and not the answer.

## Delight
On the first phase, when a place is short and the child taps the column to its left, one block cracks open like a pod and ten small vermilion blocks tumble into the short column.

**General miss line:** Which number is on top? Is any place too short?

## Answer key
Put the greater number on top, with the points in one line. Subtract place by place from the right. When a place is short, trade 1 of the place to its left for 10 of this place; it works across the point too. A whole number gets its point and zeros before anything is taken from it. Subtract 2.5 from 8.1, take away 2.5 from 8.1, and how much less 2.5 is than 8.1 all ask for 8.1 - 2.5. A number minus itself is 0. A mistaken addition is undone by taking the number away, and the subtraction that was wanted takes it away again, so it is taken away twice.

## Student controls
- **Decimal cards**: drag into the frame and slide until their points sit on the point line
- **Top check**: lights the upper card until it holds the greater number
- **Point first button**: writes a point and zeros after the ones digit of a whole number card
- **Take button and column tap**: work one column at a time, and break one block of the next place into ten where a place is short
- **Wording card**: shows one subtraction worded three ways and lights which number goes on top
- **Undo panel**: takes an added number away once, then once more
- **Number pad**: sets any decimal with up to three places, or types a difference

## Textbook (this topic)
**Pages:** 85, 86
**Key terms:** difference, trade, subtract, take away, less than, whole number
Decimals are subtracted with the greater number on top and the points in one line, trading across the point where a place is short. Write 7.4 as 7.40 and put the greater number on top, points in one line. Hundredths: 3 cannot be taken from 0, so trade 1 tenth for 10 hundredths, and 10 - 3 = 7. Tenths: 3 are left, and 6 cannot be taken from 3, so trade 1 one for 10 tenths, and 13 - 6 = 7. Ones: 6 - 2 = 4. So 7.40 - 2.63 = 4.77. Write 5 as 5.00. Trade 1 one for 10 tenths, then 1 tenth for 10 hundredths. That leaves 4 ones, 9 tenths and 10 hundredths to take from. Then 5.00 - 1.28 = 3.72. Subtract 2.5 from 8.1 starts at 8.1 and takes 2.5 away. Take away 2.5 from 8.1, and how much less 2.5 is than 8.1, ask the same. Each time 8.1 - 2.5 = 5.6. A number minus itself is 0, as in 4.09 - 4.09 = 0. First undo the mistake: 9.1 - 2.5 = 6.6, the starting number. Then do the subtraction that was wanted: 6.6 - 2.5 = 4.1. So 2.5 is taken away twice.
