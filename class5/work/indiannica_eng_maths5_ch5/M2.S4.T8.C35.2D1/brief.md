## What it is
**Description:** Count decimal places, sort like from unlike, and write zeros after the point to make decimals like, giving a whole number its point first.
**Why:** Tell like decimals from unlike decimals by counting decimal places, and change unlike decimals, whole numbers included, into like decimals by writing zeros after the point.
**Look:** Decimal cards with their points in one line, Like and Unlike trays under them, a tray of violet zero chips at the right, and a hundred grid under a card on the third phase.
**Context:** Grade 5, the pages on like and unlike decimals. An adult sees a rule about adding zeros. What the pages are doing is counting the places after the point, and writing zeros at the end, after the point, so decimals line up without changing value. A whole number gets its point first. Making decimals like is a help for comparing and adding, not a rule.
**For the child:** Count the digits after each point. Add zeros after the point until the counts match. A whole number gets its point first.
**Teaching notes:** Count decimal places by counting the digits after the point, never the digits of the whole number part. Zeros written at the end, after the point, change nothing; show it on a hundred grid, where eight tenths and eighty hundredths shade the same squares. A whole number gets its point first: a zero straight onto a whole number makes it ten times as big. Making decimals like is a help for comparing and for lining up sums, not a rule that must come first.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Like decimals
**Screen:** the cards 4.17, 0.36 and 12.05 with their points in one line, nothing lit
**Does:** taps Count places and reads the count at the right of each card
**Win:** every card shows two places, and the set settles on the Like decimals tray
**Feels:** the digits after the point lighting up and being counted
**Goal:** decimals with the same number of decimal places are like decimals
**Observe:** The count of places never included the digits before the point.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- counts every digit of the decimal, the whole number part too → the digits before the point fade to half strength and only the digits after it light and are counted
- sorts by the size of the numbers instead of their places → each card's count of places is written large at its right end, and the tray lights the count it takes
**Misconception to break:** Counting every digit, so 13.47 seems to have four places, though the 1 and 3 are before the point.

### 2 · Unlike decimals
**Screen:** the cards 3.8 and 3.456, their points in one line
**Does:** counts the places of each and drags the pair to a tray
**Win:** the counts one and three stand side by side, and the pair settles on the Unlike decimals tray
**Feels:** two counts standing side by side and not matching
**Goal:** decimals with different numbers of decimal places are unlike decimals
**Observe:** Cards with different counts would not settle on the Like tray.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- calls two decimals like because they start with the same digits → the counts of places are written at the right of both cards, side by side, and the Like tray does not take the pair
- calls two decimals unlike because their whole number parts differ → the whole number parts fade to half strength, and the matching counts of places light together
**Misconception to break:** Calling them like because both start with 7.1, though the number of places after the point is what decides.

### 3 · Zeros after the point
**Screen:** 3.8 and 3.456 again, the zero chips waiting, and a hundred grid under 3.8
**Does:** drags zero chips onto the end of 3.8 until both have three places
**Win:** 3.8 becomes 3.800 with two violet zeros, and the grid under it keeps its shading
**Feels:** zeros settling at the end while the shading does not move
**Goal:** zeros written at the end, after the point, make decimals like and do not change the value
**Observe:** The grid under the card kept its shading as each zero was added after the point.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- drops a zero chip inside the decimal, before its last digit → the chip slides back to the end after the last digit, and the grid shows what the other spot would have done to the shading
- adds zeros to the longer decimal as well → the longer card's count of places is lit as the target, and extra chips on it slide back to the tray
**Misconception to break:** Writing the zeros in front of the 4, as 2.004, which puts them inside the number and makes it much smaller.

### 4 · Whole numbers get a point first
**Screen:** the whole number 5 beside 2.64, and the Point first button unlit
**Does:** tries a zero chip on 5, watches it slide off, taps Point first, then adds the zeros
**Win:** a violet point appears after the five, two zeros settle, and the card reads 5.00 beside 2.64
**Feels:** the point appearing first, and then the zeros settling after it
**Goal:** a whole number gets its point first, and zeros are added only after the point
**Observe:** A zero chip dropped straight onto a whole number made the card jump, and slid off.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- drops a zero chip straight onto the whole number → the card jumps to a number ten times as big, written in words beside it, the chip slides back off, and the Point first button pulses once
- writes the point before the whole number → the point slides to its place after the ones digit, lining up with the other cards' points
**Misconception to break:** Writing two zeros straight onto 8, which makes 800, a hundred times as big.

## Delight
On the third phase, as each violet zero chip settles after the point, the hundred grid under the card gives a small shiver and its cells redraw finer, yet the shaded part stays exactly where it was.

**General miss line:** Count only the digits after the point.

## Answer key
The decimal places of a decimal are the digits after its point. Decimals with the same number of decimal places are like decimals, and decimals with different numbers of decimal places are unlike decimals. To make them like, write zeros at the end, after the last digit to the right of the point, until they have the same number of places; the value does not change. A whole number has its point after the ones digit: write the point first, then the zeros. Zeros are added only after the point. Making decimals like is a help, not a rule.
