## What it is
**Description:** Stack layers of cubes to see length x breadth x height, check whether all its edges are equal, and multiply an edge in tenths by whole numbers.
**Why:** See that the cubes in a layer times the number of layers give the count, so the volume of a cuboid is length x breadth x height; tell a cube by all its edges equal; and multiply an edge in tenths by whole numbers.
**Look:** A base layer of violet cubes with the Height handle beside it, the Edge checker on the third phase and the Tenths lens on the last.
**Context:** Grade 5, the pages on the volume of a cuboid and a cube and Exercise 8.4. An adult sees two formulas. What the pages are doing is shortening a count: the cubes in a layer times the number of layers give the count, so the volume of a cuboid is length x breadth x height, and a cube, a cuboid with all its edges equal, is side x side x side.
**For the child:** Count one layer, then stack the layers. Check the edges: a cube has all its edges equal.
**Teaching notes:** Build one layer of unit cubes, count it, then stack more layers by hand before the tool opens. Layers times the cubes in a layer is the volume. Name the longer side of the base the length. A cube has all its edges equal; a solid with any two edges unequal is a cuboid. An edge in tenths is multiplied as tenths by whole numbers, then written back. A cube whose edge has a decimal part multiplies a decimal by a decimal, a step taught later, so leave that number to a later chapter. The printed boxes are the child's to work.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Layers of cubes
**Screen:** a base layer 7 cubes long and 5 cubes wide, and the Height handle
**Does:** counts the base layer, drags the handle up two layers, and types the count
**Win:** the line reads 2 x 35 = 70, and the running count reads 70
**Feels:** the count jumping a whole layer at a time as the stack grows
**Goal:** one layer has length x breadth cubes, and the layers times that give the count
**Observe:** Every layer stacked added the same number of cubes.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- types the three edge counts added together → the layers are stacked again in steps, one layer's worth at a time, and the running count is shown beside the child's number
- types the cubes in one layer as the answer → the layers above the base are lit amber one after another while the child's number stays in the slot
**Misconception to break:** Adding the three counts, which gives the cubes along three edges, not the cubes that fill the cuboid.

### 2 · Length times breadth times height
**Screen:** a box 11 cm long, 6 cm wide and 4 cm high, and the Units switch on centimetres
**Does:** stacks the layers, reads the product, and types the volume with its unit
**Win:** the line reads 11 x 6 x 4 = 264, and 264 cu. cm lands green
**Feels:** the box filling up to its lid as the three numbers multiply
**Goal:** the volume of a cuboid is length x breadth x height
**Observe:** The Edge checker lit all three edges together only for some solids.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- multiplies only the length and the breadth → the base layer is lit amber alone, and the layers above it stack in steps until the box is full
- stops the Height handle one layer short of the height → the empty space under the lid line is lit amber, and the missing layer is drawn there as a ghost while the child's layers stay
**Misconception to break:** Multiplying only the length and the breadth, which gives one layer, not the whole box.

### 3 · A cube has equal edges
**Screen:** a solid with edges of 9 cm, 9 cm and 9 cm, and the Edge checker
**Does:** runs the Edge checker along the three edges, then types the volume
**Win:** all three edges light together, the label reads Cube, and 9 x 9 x 9 = 729 gives 729 cu. cm
**Feels:** three edges lighting at once, and the word Cube appearing
**Goal:** a cube is a cuboid with all its edges equal, so its volume is side x side x side
**Observe:** Through the lens, an edge with a point in it became a whole number of tenths.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- types side x side x side for a solid with a square top but a different height → the Edge checker's bead runs up the height in steps, and the edge that differs is lit amber while the label reads Cuboid
- types side x side as the volume → the bottom layer of the cube is lit amber, labelled Layer, and the layers above it stack in steps until the cube is full
**Misconception to break:** Calling any box with a square face a cube, though a cube has all its edges equal.

### 4 · An edge in tenths
**Screen:** a box 8 cm long, 3.5 cm wide and 2 cm high, and the Tenths lens
**Does:** multiplies the whole-number edges, holds the lens over the edge in tenths, and types the volume
**Win:** the lens reads thirty-five tenths, the line reads 35 x 16 = 560 tenths, and 56 cu. cm lands green
**Feels:** an awkward edge turning into a whole number of tenths
**Goal:** an edge in tenths is multiplied as a count of tenths by the whole-number edges
**Observe:** The lens stayed dark when two edges had tenths.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- types the count of tenths times the other edges as the volume in cu. cm → the word tenths is lit amber under the product, and the answer is written back in steps beside the child's number
- holds the lens over a second edge in tenths → the lens stays dark over the second edge, and the card Taught later is lit amber
**Misconception to break:** Forgetting that 35 counted tenths, and giving an answer ten times too big.

## Delight
On the third phase, when all three edges light together, the solid gives a little spin on the spot and settles with the word Cube under it.

**General miss line:** Count one layer, then count the layers again.

## Answer key
One layer has length x breadth cubes, and the layers stack up the height, so the volume of a cuboid is length x breadth x height; lengths in cm give cu. cm and lengths in m give cu. m. A cube is a cuboid with all its edges equal, so its volume is side x side x side; a solid with any two edges unequal is a cuboid, not a cube. An edge in tenths is a count of tenths: multiply the whole-number edges first, then the count of tenths, and write the answer back. When two edges both have tenths, the multiplying needs a step taught later.

## Student controls

- **Units switch**: gives the edges cm or m, and writes length x breadth x height with a cubic unit

- **Tenths lens**: rewrites an edge in tenths of a centimetre as a count of tenths
- **Number pad**: types a count or a volume, with cu. cm and cu. m keys and a point key
