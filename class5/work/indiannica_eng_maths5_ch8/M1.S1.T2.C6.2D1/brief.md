## What it is
**Description:** Walk an ant round a figure and add each side as it is walked, in one unit, then fold a rectangle's four sides into 2 x (length + breadth).
**Why:** Find a perimeter as the length of a boundary by adding every side once, in one unit, name the longer side of a rectangle its length, and fold its four sides into 2 x (length + breadth).
**Look:** A figure on squared paper with its sides labelled, an ant at one corner, a Sum strip along the foot of the working area, and on the later phases the Length and Breadth tags and the Two lots button.
**Context:** Grade 5, the recap and the pages on the perimeter of a rectangle. An adult sees a formula. What the pages are doing is measuring a boundary: the perimeter is the length of the boundary, every side is added once and in one unit, and a rectangle's two equal pairs of sides let the sum be written as two lots of one length and one breadth.
**For the child:** Walk the ant once round the edge. Every side goes into the sum. Put every side in one unit first.
**Teaching notes:** Run a finger, then a string, once round a real edge before the tool opens. Say that the perimeter is the length of the boundary every time, so it is never taken for the edge itself. Put every side in one unit first and say the unit fact aloud. Name the longer side of a rectangle its length before any formula, and check the formula against all four sides added. Use beds, cards, frames and gardens for the figures; the printed exercises' own lengths are the child's to work.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Perimeter is a length
**Screen:** a five-sided flower bed with its sides labelled 14 m, 9 m, 11 m, 7 m and 10 m, and the ant at one corner
**Does:** drags the ant once round the boundary, watching each side drop into the Sum strip, and types the total
**Win:** the whole boundary glows vermilion, the strip reads 14 + 9 + 11 + 7 + 10 = 51, and the answer slot reads 51 m in green
**Feels:** the ant closing the loop and the whole edge glowing
**Goal:** the perimeter is the length of the boundary, found by walking every side once and adding
**Observe:** Every side lit vermilion as the ant walked it, and its length joined the sum.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- walks one side twice before closing the boundary → the repeated tile on the Sum strip is lit amber, and the ant replays the walk in steps beside the strip with each side lighting once
- types the number of sides as the perimeter → the Sum strip is shown under the answer slot with its lengths, and the word Length is written in ink over the strip
**Misconception to break:** Counting the sides instead of adding their lengths, and giving the number of sides as the answer.

### 2 · One unit for every side
**Screen:** a mat with one side labelled 1 m and three labelled 85 cm, 90 cm and 75 cm, and the Write in cm tile
**Does:** drags the Write in cm tile onto the side in metres, then walks the ant round and types the total
**Win:** the side reads 100 cm beside the unit fact, and the strip reads 100 + 85 + 90 + 75 = 350 with 350 cm in green
**Feels:** a small-looking side growing into a big number the moment it becomes centimetres
**Goal:** write every side in the same unit before adding, saying the unit fact
**Observe:** A side in metres looked small on the strip until the Write in cm tile rewrote it.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- walks the ant round before using the Write in cm tile → the tile for the side in metres is lit amber on the Sum strip, and the unit fact is written beside it in ink while the rest of the child's sum stays
- drops the Write in cm tile on a side already in centimetres → that side keeps its number, the tile slides back to its place, and the side in metres is lit amber
**Misconception to break:** Adding the four numbers as they stand, which treats the metre as one centimetre and gives a perimeter far too short.

### 3 · Length and breadth
**Screen:** a rectangle with one side labelled 11 cm and the side next to it 6 cm, and the Length and Breadth tags
**Does:** drags Length onto the longer side and Breadth onto the shorter, then walks the ant round
**Win:** the tags sit on the right sides, the opposite sides copy their labels, and the strip reads 11 + 6 + 11 + 6 = 34
**Feels:** the tags clicking onto the right sides and their opposites lighting to match
**Goal:** the longer side of a rectangle is its length and the shorter is its breadth, and opposite sides are equal
**Observe:** Once Length went on one side, the opposite side copied its label.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- drags the Length tag onto the shorter side → both sides are measured again by a ruler drawn along them in steps, and the longer one is lit amber while the child's tag stays where it was put
- walks only the two labelled sides and types their sum → the two unlabelled sides copy their opposite sides' labels in steps, and the ant walks on from where the child stopped
**Misconception to break:** Adding only the two sides that are labelled, which walks only half of the boundary.

### 4 · Two lots of length and breadth
**Screen:** a rectangle 15 m long and 8 m wide with its tags on, and the Two lots button lit
**Does:** taps Two lots, watches one length and one breadth slide together and double, and types the perimeter
**Win:** the line reads 2 x (15 + 8) = 46 above the strip, and the four sides added give the same 46 m
**Feels:** one length and one breadth sliding together and doubling into the whole edge
**Goal:** length + breadth + length + breadth is two lots of one length and one breadth, 2 x (length + breadth)
**Observe:** The Two lots line and the four sides added ended on the same perimeter.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- builds a line that doubles only the length and then adds one breadth → the brackets are drawn round length + breadth in steps, the missing breadth is lit amber on the rectangle, and the child's line stays above for comparison
- adds one length and one breadth and stops there → the ant walks the first half of the boundary, stops at the far corner, and the second half is lit amber
**Misconception to break:** Doubling only the length and then adding one breadth, which leaves one breadth out of the walk.

## Delight
When the ant closes the boundary on the first phase, it gives a small hop at the corner where it started, and the whole vermilion path glows once before the sum lands.

**General miss line:** Walk it again with the ant. Did every side go in once?

## Answer key
The perimeter of a figure is the length of its boundary: add every side once, in one unit. Sides in two units are written in one unit first, and 1 m = 100 cm. The longer side of a rectangle is its length and the shorter side is its breadth, and opposite sides are equal. So the perimeter of a rectangle is length + breadth + length + breadth, which is 2 x (length + breadth). A perimeter is a length, in cm or m.

## Textbook (this topic)
**Pages:** 120, 121, 122
**Key terms:** perimeter, boundary, length, breadth, same unit, formula
Perimeter is the length of the boundary: the sides are added in one unit, and a rectangle's perimeter is two times its length and breadth together. The perimeter of a figure is the length of its boundary, so it is measured in units of length. Add every side once, with every side in the same unit. The longer side of a rectangle is its length and the shorter is its breadth, and its perimeter is 2 x (length + breadth). It walks along every side once. 12 + 9 + 11 + 6 = 38, so it walks 38 cm. The perimeter of a figure is the length of its boundary. It is measured in units of length, such as cm or m. Not yet, because the sides are in two units. First write every side in the same unit. 1 m = 100 cm, so 2 m is 200 cm. Then 200 + 175 + 140 + 95 = 610, so the perimeter is 610 cm. The longer side of a rectangle is its length, and the shorter side is its breadth. Opposite sides of a rectangle are equal. So the perimeter of a rectangle 9 cm long and 4 cm wide is 9 + 4 + 9 + 4 = 26 cm. Add one length and one breadth, then double it. So the perimeter of a rectangle is 2 x (length + breadth). The perimeter of a rectangle 8 m long and 6 m wide is 2 x (8 + 6) = 28 m. Check it: 8 + 6 + 8 + 6 = 28.
