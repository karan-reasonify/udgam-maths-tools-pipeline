## What it is
**Description:** Fold a square's four equal sides into 4 x side, add a triangle's three sides, halve a perimeter to work back to a side, and count laps of a side in tenths.
**Why:** Find the perimeter of a square as 4 x side and of a triangle as the sum of its sides, work back to a rectangle's length by halving its perimeter, and find the distance of several laps, with a side in tenths read as tenths.
**Look:** A square, a triangle or a rectangle on squared paper, a Sum strip under it, a perimeter string on the third phase, and a square path with a lap counter and the Tenths lens on the last.
**Context:** Grade 5, the pages on the perimeters of squares and triangles and Exercise 8.1. An adult sees two formulas and two harder questions. What the pages are doing is adding equal sides quickly and working back: a square's four equal sides make 4 x side, a triangle's three sides are simply added, and half a rectangle's perimeter is one length and one breadth together.
**For the child:** Copy one side of the square onto the others. Halve a perimeter to find one length and one breadth. Find one lap before counting laps.
**Teaching notes:** Chalk a square on the ground, measure one side and walk a lap before the tool opens. A square's four equal sides make 4 x side; check it once by adding all four. A triangle has no formula to learn: add its three sides in one unit. To work back to a side, halve the perimeter first, and say that the half is one length and one breadth together. For laps, find one lap first. Read a side in tenths as a count of tenths, multiply by a whole number, and write the answer back in metres; a decimal is never multiplied by a decimal here. The printed sides and parks are the child's to work.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Four equal sides
**Screen:** a square tile with one side labelled 19 cm and the other three blank, and the Copy side button
**Does:** taps Copy side to label the other three, then Times 4, and types the perimeter
**Win:** all four sides read 19 cm, the line reads 4 x 19 = 76, and 76 cm lands green in the slot
**Feels:** four equal strips lining up and folding into one short line of working
**Goal:** a square has four equal sides, so its perimeter is 4 x side
**Observe:** Copying one side of the square onto the other three made four equal lengths.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- types two lots of the side → the ant walks the square, stopping after two sides, and the two sides not yet walked are lit amber
- taps Times 4 before copying the side onto the other three → the Copy side button pulses, and the three blank sides fill in one at a time while the child's line stays
**Misconception to break:** Taking two lots of the side, as for one length and one breadth, which walks only half way round.

### 2 · Three sides of a triangle
**Screen:** a triangle with sides labelled 13 cm, 15 cm and 17 cm, and the ant at one corner
**Does:** walks the ant round the three sides and types the sum
**Win:** the strip reads 13 + 15 + 17 = 45, and the triangle's boundary glows vermilion
**Feels:** the third side closing the triangle and the sum landing
**Goal:** the perimeter of a triangle is the sum of its three sides, in one unit
**Observe:** The halved string lay exactly along one length and one breadth.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- walks two sides and types their sum → the ant walks on along the third side in steps, and that side is lit amber while the child's sum stays on the strip
- adds a side in metres to sides in centimetres → the side in the other unit is lit amber, and the unit fact is written beside it in ink
**Misconception to break:** Adding only two of the sides, which leaves part of the boundary out.

### 3 · Working back to a side
**Screen:** a rectangle with its breadth labelled 7 cm and its length blank, and a perimeter string of 36 cm beside it
**Does:** taps Halve, watches the half lie along one length and one breadth, then drags Take away onto the breadth
**Win:** the half reads 18 cm, the breadth lifts off, and the length reads 11 cm in green; the check line reads 2 x (11 + 7) = 36
**Feels:** the folded string fitting one length and one breadth exactly
**Goal:** half the perimeter is one length and one breadth together, so the length is half take away the breadth
**Observe:** Each tap of Lap added the same amount again.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- drags Take away onto the whole string before tapping Halve → the string is laid round the rectangle in steps, showing that the whole string still holds two lengths and a breadth, and the Halve button is lit amber
- types the half of the perimeter as the length → the half is laid along the rectangle, one length and one breadth, with the breadth part lit amber
**Misconception to break:** Taking the breadth away from the whole perimeter instead of from half of it, which leaves two lengths and a breadth mixed together.

### 4 · Laps and tenths
**Screen:** a square pond path with each side labelled 3.4 m, the Tenths lens and the Lap button
**Does:** holds the Tenths lens over a side, finds one lap in tenths, then taps Lap for each lap and types the distance
**Win:** the lens reads thirty-four tenths, one lap reads 136 tenths, which is 13.6 m, and five laps read 68 m in green
**Feels:** the lap counter ticking up by the same amount each time
**Goal:** several laps are one lap multiplied, and a side in tenths is multiplied as a count of tenths
**Observe:** Through the lens, a side with a point in it became a whole number of tenths.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- types the count of tenths as metres → the lens is shown again over the side, with the word tenths lit amber under the number, and the child's answer stays in the slot
- multiplies the side by the number of laps and skips one lap's four sides → the ant walks one full lap in steps, lighting all four sides, and the lap counter reads one before the laps are counted
**Misconception to break:** Forgetting that 34 counts tenths of a metre, and giving an answer ten times too big.

## Delight
On the third phase, when the halved string settles along one length and one breadth, the folded half gives a small wave along its whole length and then lies still.

**General miss line:** Walk it once more. Did every side, or every lap, go in?

## Answer key
A square has four equal sides, so its perimeter is 4 x side. A triangle's perimeter is the sum of its three sides, all in one unit. Half a rectangle's perimeter is one length and one breadth together, so the length is half the perimeter take away the breadth; check by 2 x (length + breadth). Going round several times is one lap multiplied by the number of laps. A side in tenths is a count of tenths: multiply the count by a whole number, then write the answer back in metres.

## Textbook (this topic)
**Pages:** 122, 123
**Key terms:** square, triangle, perimeter of a square, missing side, tenths
A square's perimeter is four times its side and a triangle's is the sum of its three sides; a missing side is worked back from a perimeter, and laps are counted. The perimeter is the length of the boundary, and a square has four equal sides, so its perimeter is 4 x side. The perimeter of a triangle is the sum of its three sides, all in the same unit. Half the perimeter of a rectangle is one length and one breadth together, so take away the side you know to find the missing side. A side in decimals can be read as tenths, and laps are counted by multiplying. All four sides of a square are equal. So its perimeter is side + side + side + side, which is 4 x side. The perimeter of a square of side 13 cm is 4 x 13 = 52 cm. The perimeter of a triangle is the sum of its three sides. 7 + 9 + 13 = 29. So the perimeter of a triangle with sides 7 cm, 9 cm and 13 cm is 29 cm. All three sides must be in the same unit first. Half the perimeter is 15 cm. That half is one length and one breadth together. So the length is 15 - 5 = 10 cm. Check it: 2 x (10 + 5) = 30. Think of 2.3 m as 23 tenths of a metre. 4 x 23 = 92, so one lap is 92 tenths, which is 9.2 m. 3 laps are 3 x 92 = 276 tenths, which is 27.6 m. So 3 x 9.2 = 27.6, and 3 times round is 27.6 m.
