## What it is
**Description:** Sort measures into length, surface and space with their units, build every cuboid of a set of cubes once, and compare volumes by rising water, floaters pushed fully under.
**Why:** Tell perimeter, area and volume apart by what they measure and by their units, find every cuboid of a set of cubes once, and compare volumes by rising water with every object fully under.
**Look:** Three measure bins with their units on the first phase, a cube tray and a cuboid shelf on the second, and equal jars of water with the Push stick on the last two.
**Context:** Grade 5, the closing pages. An adult sees a checklist, a vocabulary list, an open investigation and a water activity. What the pages are doing is bringing the three measures together: a length, a surface and a space, each in its own unit; every cuboid of a set of cubes found once; and a volume shown by the water a solid pushes aside when it is fully under.
**For the child:** Sort each thing into length, surface or space. Turn a cuboid: it stays the same cuboid. Push floaters fully under.
**Teaching notes:** Sort measures by what they measure, a length, a surface or a space, before naming any unit. Build cuboids with real cubes and turn each one to see that it stays the same cuboid; count each shape once. Lower a stone into a jar of water before the tool opens. Push every object fully under the water before reading the rise; a floating object shows its volume only when it is held fully under. The self-assessment is the child's own, so let each can-do be ticked honestly. The printed set of cubes and the printed objects are the child's to work.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Which measure, which unit
**Screen:** the three bins, and cards for a fence round a field, the grass inside it and the water in a tank
**Does:** drags each card into its bin and a unit tile onto each
**Win:** the fence sits under Length with m, the grass under Surface with sq. m, and the water under Space with cu. m
**Feels:** each card dropping into the bin that fits it
**Goal:** a perimeter is a length in cm or m, an area a surface in square units, and a volume a space in cubic units
**Observe:** Every card went into exactly one bin, and each bin had its own kind of unit.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- drags the grass card into the Length bin → the grass card is shaded over its whole surface in steps, and the Surface bin is lit amber while the card stays where the child put it
- drags the sq. cm tile onto the water card → the water card fills up like a box in steps, and the Space bin's units are lit amber
**Misconception to break:** Giving the grass in m, a unit of length, though grass covers a surface.

### 2 · The same cuboid, turned
**Screen:** a heap of eighteen unit cubes, the tray, the Turn button and an empty shelf
**Does:** builds cuboids, turns each one, and sets on the shelf only the ones not already there
**Win:** the shelf holds four cuboids, and every turned copy set on it has slid onto its twin, labelled Same cuboid
**Feels:** a turned copy sliding home onto its twin
**Goal:** every cuboid of a set of cubes is found once, and a turned cuboid is the same cuboid
**Observe:** A turned cuboid set on the shelf slid onto a cuboid already there.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- sets a turned copy on the shelf as another cuboid → the copy slides along the shelf onto its twin in steps, and the label Same cuboid is lit amber while the rest of the shelf stays
- builds a cuboid that leaves some cubes out of the heap → the cubes still in the heap are lit amber, and the count on the tray is set beside the count in the heap
**Misconception to break:** Counting a cuboid again after standing it on another face, though a turned cuboid is the same cuboid.

### 3 · Water makes room
**Screen:** two equal jars filled to one line, a key and a stone
**Does:** lowers the key into one jar and the stone into the other, taps Mark level on each, and taps the greater volume
**Win:** the stone's jar shows the higher green rise, and the stone lights green, labelled Greater volume
**Feels:** the water climbing the glass as the object goes in
**Goal:** a solid fully under water raises the water by as much space as it takes up
**Observe:** The water rose more for the bigger object.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- taps the longer object as the greater volume → both new levels are marked again in steps, and the higher rise is lit amber
- marks a level before the object is fully in the water → the object sinks the rest of the way in steps, and the new line is drawn above the child's mark
**Misconception to break:** Choosing the key because it is longer, though the rise shows the space an object takes up, not its length.

### 4 · Push it fully under
**Screen:** a jar filled to its line with a cork bobbing at the top, and the Push stick
**Does:** marks the level with the cork floating, then pushes it fully under with the stick and marks it again
**Win:** the second mark stands above the first, and the rise from the line to the second mark is lit green
**Feels:** the water rising again as the floater goes fully under
**Goal:** a floating object is pushed fully under before the rise is read, in equal jars with equal water
**Observe:** The cork's rise grew when it was pushed fully under.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- marks the level while the cork floats, not yet under, and reads that rise → the part of the cork above the water is lit amber, and the Push stick pulses beside it while the child's mark stays
- pushes the cork only part of the way under → the part still above the water is lit amber, and the level line waits unmarked
**Misconception to break:** Reading the rise while the cork floats, which shows only the part under the water.

## Delight
On the second phase, when a turned copy slides onto its twin on the shelf, the two give a little bump together like old friends meeting.

**General miss line:** Look again. Is it a length, a surface or a space?

## Answer key
A perimeter is a length, in cm or m. An area is a surface, in square units such as sq. cm or sq. m. A volume is a space, in cubic units such as cu. cm or cu. m. Every cuboid of a set of cubes is found by looking for three whole numbers that multiply to the count, and each is counted once: standing a cuboid on another face does not make a new cuboid, it is the same cuboid, turned. A solid lowered fully under water raises the water by as much space as it takes up. A floating object is only partly under, so push it fully under with a thin stick before reading the rise, and use equal jars holding equal water.
