## What it is
**Description:** Stack rows of squares on a rectangle to see length x breadth, make a square for side x side, and split a length into three to find a breadth given as one third of it.
**Why:** See that rows times the squares in a row give the count, so the area of a rectangle is length x breadth and a square's is side x side, and find a breadth from a clue before multiplying.
**Look:** A rectangle on squared paper with one row laid, the Rows handle up its breadth and the record card at the right; the Square switch on the third phase and the length strip with the Split button on the last.
**Context:** Grade 5, the pages on the area of a rectangle and a square and Exercise 8.2. An adult sees two formulas. What the pages are doing is shortening a count: rows of squares times the squares in a row give the count, so the area of a rectangle is length x breadth, with the longer side as the length, and a square's is side x side.
**For the child:** Stack the rows. Rows times squares in a row is the count. The longer side is the length.
**Teaching notes:** Count one rectangle square by square, then count its rows and the squares in a row, before the tool opens. Name the longer side the length before multiplying, so the words match the picture. A square is a rectangle whose length and breadth are equal. Say one third of the length, and find the breadth before multiplying. The screen question in the opening box can be tried now and stays the child's; its measures are rough, so any answer is an estimate, said with about. The printed rectangles and squares are the child's to work.

## Phases (what each mount shows and does; the words are in tool.tsx)

### 1 · Rows times squares in a row
**Screen:** a rectangle 9 squares along its length and 5 along its breadth, with one row of nine laid
**Does:** drags the Rows handle up one row at a time, then types the count and the product on the record card
**Win:** five rows fill it, the running count reads 45, and the record card reads 9 x 5 = 45 beside the count
**Feels:** the rows stacking up and the count jumping a whole row at a time
**Goal:** squares along the length x squares along the breadth gives the count
**Observe:** Each row stacked added the same number of squares to the count.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- types the squares along the length added to the squares along the breadth → the rows are stacked again in steps, one row's worth at a time, and the running count is shown beside the child's number
- stops the Rows handle one row short → the empty strip at the top is lit amber, and the ghost of the missing row is drawn in it while the child's rows stay
**Misconception to break:** Adding the squares along the length and the breadth, which counts one row and one column, not the whole surface.

### 2 · Length times breadth
**Screen:** a garden 16 m long and 7 m wide, its length along the bottom, and the Units switch on metres
**Does:** stacks the rows, reads the product as length x breadth, and types the area with its unit
**Win:** the line reads 16 x 7 = 112 and the slot reads 112 sq. m in green
**Feels:** the product and the count agreeing the first time they are set side by side
**Goal:** the area of a rectangle is length x breadth, with the longer side as the length
**Observe:** The product on the record card always matched the count.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- types length + breadth as the area → the sum is drawn as a single line along the edge in steps, labelled Length, while the rows fill the surface beside it
- gives the area in m, not sq. m → one unit square with sides of one metre is drawn in a corner of the rectangle, and the unit key sq. m is lit amber
**Misconception to break:** Adding the length and the breadth, which gives a length in metres, not a surface.

### 3 · Side times side
**Screen:** a board 15 cm long and 9 cm wide, and the Square switch
**Does:** taps the Square switch, watches the breadth grow to 15 cm, and types the area
**Win:** the board becomes a square of side 15 cm, the line reads 15 x 15 = 225, and 225 sq. cm lands green
**Feels:** the rectangle stretching into a square and the two numbers matching
**Goal:** a square is a rectangle whose length and breadth are equal, so its area is side x side
**Observe:** When the Square switch pulled the breadth up, the two numbers in the product became the same.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- types four lots of the side as the area → the four sides light vermilion with the word Length beside them in steps, and then the rows fill the square beside them
- types two lots of the side as the area → rows are stacked up the square in steps until it is full, and the row count is shown beside the child's number
**Misconception to break:** Taking four lots of the side, which gives the perimeter, a length, not the area.

### 4 · A breadth from a clue
**Screen:** a length strip labelled 27 cm, a clue card reading one third of the length, and the Split button
**Does:** taps Split, drags one part down as the breadth, then stacks rows and types the area
**Win:** one part reads 9 cm and becomes the breadth, and the line reads 27 x 9 = 243 with 243 sq. cm in green
**Feels:** one part of the split strip dropping into place as the breadth
**Goal:** find the breadth from a clue such as one third of the length, then multiply
**Observe:** The split part stood beside the rectangle, much shorter than the length.
**Wrong (each is a traceback on the working area):**
- multiplies the length by three to find the breadth → a breadth three times the length is drawn standing beside the rectangle in steps, much longer than the length, and the clue card is lit amber
- multiplies before finding the breadth → the Split button pulses, and the empty breadth side of the rectangle is lit amber
**Misconception to break:** Multiplying the length by three to find the breadth, which makes the breadth longer than the length.

## Delight
On the fourth phase, when the split part drops down beside the rectangle, the other two parts give a small nod, as if to say they are the same size.

**General miss line:** Count the rows again. How many squares are in each row?

## Answer key
Squares along the length x squares along the breadth gives the count, the same as counting one by one. So the area of a rectangle is length x breadth, with the longer side as the length; lengths in cm give sq. cm and lengths in m give sq. m. A square is a rectangle whose length and breadth are equal, so its area is side x side. A breadth given as one third of the length is found first: one third of a length is the length split into three equal parts. Then multiply. The screen in the opening box has rough measures, so any area worked from them is an estimate, said with about.

## Student controls
- **Rows handle**: dragged up the breadth; stacks one row of squares at a time
- **Record card**: fills Along the length, Along the breadth, Product and Count as the child works

- **Square switch**: pulls the breadth up to equal the length, for side x side
- **Split button**: cuts the length strip into three equal parts; one part becomes the breadth
- **Number pad**: types a count or an area, with sq. cm and sq. m keys
